# mod-105 — Enterprise Regulatory Change Management

**Estimated effort:** 14 hours
**Position on the track:** the fifth module — the discipline that
turns a moving regulatory landscape into a running program the
enterprise can actually operate.

## What this module is for

Regulatory change is the load-bearing input to the head of AI
governance's job that the head cannot commission, cannot delay,
and cannot negotiate. Everything else on the head's operating
calendar can be sequenced by the head; regulatory change cannot.
The head therefore has to run change as a discipline — a
scanning cadence that produces awareness before decisions are
needed, an interpretation workflow that turns awareness into
operating decisions, a change-management plan shape that turns
decisions into landed change, a compliance-date posture that
adjudicates the tension between the regulator's timing and the
enterprise's readiness, and a cross-function coordination pattern
that keeps six adjacent functions moving in step.

This module builds that discipline and rehearses it against the
specific pending changes on the horizon as of the research
window. It presupposes:

- The scope statement and leadership shape from
  [[mod-101-role-scope-and-leadership-shape]] — the head is a
  second-line leader, not a first-line operator, and not a
  substitute for GC.
- The running program from [[mod-102-running-the-program]] — the
  AIMS operating calendar, the risk register cadence, the AI
  system inventory, and the third-party audit interface are
  where changes actually land.
- The reporting shape from
  [[mod-103-board-and-audit-committee-reporting]] — regulatory-
  change status appears in the pack clusters and material
  changes are briefed to the audit committee.
- The regulator engagement discipline from
  [[mod-104-regulator-engagement]] — the head packages
  obligations for GC and takes the technical position; GC opines;
  the head does not deliver legal opinions.

## Learning objectives (from the plan)

- Design the enterprise horizon-scanning cadence for AI
  regulation — inputs, cadence, escalation thresholds —
  coordinating with `ai-governance-analyst` (level 15) on the
  tracking legwork and with legal counsel on legal-opinion
  interpretation.
- Design the interpretation-and-prioritisation workflow — when a
  new obligation lands, how the head triages priority across the
  enterprise portfolio, how the impact assessment is scoped, how
  the delta is handed to `senior-ai-governance-architect` (level
  50) for the crosswalk update.
- Design the enterprise change-management plan for a regulatory
  change — communications cadence (from CEO letter to team-level
  runbook update), training refresh, exception window management,
  board-report inclusion, regulator-facing notification where
  relevant.
- Manage the tension between speed (the regulator's compliance
  date) and safety (the enterprise's operational readiness) —
  where the head has to argue for a slower rollout, or defend a
  fast one, with defensible facts.
- Lead the coordination between senior-ai-governance-architect
  (level 50, updated crosswalk), product / engineering
  (implementation), legal (interpretation), procurement (vendor-
  contract updates), HR (training refresh), and communications
  (public trust posture).
- Rehearse specific pending changes as of the research window —
  EU AI Act GPAI obligations timelines, CEN-CENELEC JTC 21
  harmonised-standards publication dates, state AI act
  enforcement dates (Colorado 2026, Texas TRAIGA), Korea AI
  Basic Act, OSFI E-23 2027 effective date, evolving AISI
  methodology.

## How to work through this module

The module is designed to be worked linearly. Chapters 01 and 02
build the intake and interpretation half of the discipline —
without them, later chapters have no supply of interpreted
obligations to plan against. Chapter 03 sets the seven-lane plan
shape; chapter 04 adds the compliance-date posture layer that
turns the plan into an aligned decision; chapter 05 adds the
cross-function coordination discipline that carries the plan
into operation. Chapters 06 and 07 rehearse the discipline
against six specific in-flight items so the head has muscle
memory when the items land.

Exercises follow the same order. Exercises 1 through 5 map to
chapters 1 through 5 respectively and produce the deliverables
the head carries into operation. Chapters 6 and 7 are rehearsal
scaffolding; their exercise-equivalents are the specific plan-
authoring drills the head performs when a rehearsed item
becomes an active-tier item.

By the end of the module, a working head-of-role has produced:

- A written scanning-system design (sources, cadence,
  escalation, memory) with named ownership and SLA.
- A written interpretation-and-prioritisation workflow with the
  seven-step intake-to-disposition shape and the four-axis
  priority framework.
- A change-management plan template with the seven lanes fully
  specified.
- A written compliance-date posture framework with the five-move
  aligned-posture logic and the four-room defense discipline.
- A cross-function coordination pack template with the six lanes
  fully specified.
- Rehearsed plan sketches against at least three of: EU AI Act
  GPAI, JTC 21 harmonised standards, evolving AISI methodology,
  Colorado SB 24-205, Texas TRAIGA, OSFI E-23, Korea AI Basic
  Act.

These artefacts feed forward into every subsequent module in the
track — [[mod-106-external-positioning-and-voluntary-codes]] on
the sign-on decisions the enterprise faces,
[[mod-107-program-measurement-and-maturity]] on the KRIs the
regulatory-change discipline produces,
[[mod-110-incident-leadership]] on the change-management pattern
when a regulatory change is triggered by an incident, and
[[mod-111-sector-program-leadership]] on the sector-specific
regulatory-change patterns.
