# mod-111 — Sector Program Leadership

**Estimated effort:** 20 hours
**Position on the track:** the eleventh module — the depth pass on
seven specific sectors that turns the generic leadership shape from
[[mod-101-role-scope-and-leadership-shape]], the running-program
discipline from [[mod-102-running-the-program]], the board-reporting
cluster shape from [[mod-103-board-and-audit-committee-reporting]],
the regulator-engagement pattern from [[mod-104-regulator-engagement]],
the change-management cadence from
[[mod-105-regulatory-change-management]], the external-positioning
discipline from [[mod-106-external-positioning-and-voluntary-codes]],
the cross-CxO coordination pattern from
[[mod-109-cross-enterprise-leadership]], and the incident-response
shape from [[mod-110-incident-leadership]] into the sector-specific
operating package a head at a bank, an insurer, a health system, a
pharma, a federal agency or contractor, a frontier lab, or a critical-
infrastructure operator actually signs into on day one.

## What this module is for

The head of AI governance is a sector-specific role at the point of
execution and a sector-invariant role in leadership shape. Every
chapter of this track before this one built the sector-invariant
shape: how the head runs a program, how the head reports to a board,
how the head engages a regulator, how the head positions the
enterprise externally, how the head coordinates with peer CxOs, how
the head leads an incident response. Those disciplines land the same
way at every enterprise. The details of *what* those disciplines
attach to — which regulator, which instrument, which counterpart,
which board committee, which industry body, which cadence — vary
sharply by sector.

This module is the sector overlay. It walks seven sectors — banking,
insurance, health system, pharmaceutical, public sector and federal
contractor, frontier lab, and critical infrastructure — one at a
time, at leadership depth, so the head can:

- Read the sector's specific supervisory instruments against the
  enterprise's AI portfolio without paraphrasing them.
- Locate the head's placement relative to the sector's incumbent
  senior functions (the Chief Model Risk Officer at a bank, the
  Chief Actuary and Chief Underwriting Officer at an insurer, the
  Chief Medical Officer and Chief Medical Informatics Officer at a
  health system, the Head of Regulatory Affairs and the Head of
  Quality at a pharma, the Federal CAIO designation itself in
  federal service, the Responsible Scaling Officer or Preparedness
  Committee equivalent at a frontier lab, the CISO and Chief
  Engineering Officer at a critical-infrastructure operator).
- Adapt the generic board-reporting cluster shape to the sector's
  actual board conventions (Quality Committee for a health system;
  Risk Committee for a bank; R&D or Science and Technology
  Committee for a pharma; Safety and Security Committee for a
  frontier lab; AI Governance Board under M-25-21 for a federal
  agency).
- Adapt the generic external-positioning portfolio to the sector's
  expected industry-body surfaces (Bank Policy Institute and IIF at
  a bank; ACLI, NAMIC, APCIA at an insurer; AHA and specialty
  societies at a health system; PhRMA and BIO and EFPIA at a
  pharma; Federal CAIO Council in federal service; Frontier Model
  Forum at a frontier lab; sector ISACs at a critical-infrastructure
  operator).

The module closes with chapter 08 — the sector-adaptation
methodology itself — that turns the seven sector chapters into
seven instantiations of the same leadership discipline rather than
seven parallel bodies of specialist knowledge.

## What this module answers

1. Banking — how does the head lead an AI program inside the
   enterprise's Model Risk Management framework under SR 11-7 / OCC
   Bulletin 2011-12, coordinate SR 23-4 third-party AI oversight,
   overlay OSFI E-23 and PRA SS1/23 for Canadian and UK entities,
   and carry Regulation B / ECOA and UDAAP obligations on consumer-
   facing AI without owning the compliance function itself.
2. Insurance — how does the head run a state-by-state two-axis
   regulatory map across 50+ jurisdictions and the NAIC AI Model
   Bulletin adoption cycle, fit-check against Colorado Regulation
   10-1-1 and NYDFS Circular Letter No. 7 (2024), coordinate with
   the Chief Actuary and CUO across ASOP-governed pricing and
   underwriting, and defend a state-DOI examination-ready evidence
   pipeline that survives any single state's market-conduct
   examination.
3. Health system — how does the head navigate the four-layer stack
   (FDA device / HIPAA PHI / clinical / state medical-AI) across
   every clinical AI in the inventory, interface with the CMO and
   medical staff without owning clinical judgement, run the HIPAA
   / BAA discipline for third-party AI vendors touching PHI, and
   ready the enterprise for a TJC or DNV accreditation survey.
4. Pharmaceutical — how does the head lead the AI-specific overlay
   on the pharma regulatory-submission lifecycle across FDA, EMA,
   MHRA, and PMDA, interface with GxP (GLP, GCP, GMP, GVP, GDP)
   without becoming the person who submits, own pharmacovigilance
   AI validation the QPPV can rely on, and prepare inspector-facing
   evidence for every regulated site.
5. Public sector / federal contractor — how does the head operate
   as a Federal CAIO under OMB M-25-21 (chair the AI Governance
   Board's operating cadence, run the AI Use Case Inventory,
   execute the high-impact minimum practices, front IG relations,
   ready congressional-oversight materials), or as a federal
   contractor's head serving CAIO-designated agencies (produce
   CAIO-consumable AI product characterisation, align contract
   terms with M-25-22, maintain FedRAMP posture, and run the CAIO
   relationship map).
6. Frontier lab — how does the head operationalise a public safety
   framework (Anthropic RSP, OpenAI Preparedness, DeepMind FSF)
   with capability-threshold evaluation, mitigation-standard
   implementation, RSO / Preparedness / FSF Committee governance,
   and a public-transparency cadence; coordinate White House and
   US / UK AISI engagement; and operate through the Frontier Model
   Forum and adjacent industry bodies.
7. Critical infrastructure — how does the head extend the operator's
   existing safety-and-reliability framework to cover AI as a
   modality (not as a separate stack), map to CISA / NCSC / ENISA
   cross-sector cyber-and-AI guidance, coordinate with the sector-
   specific regulator (FERC / NERC for electricity; FCC for
   telecom; EPA for water; FAA / DOT for transportation; SEC /
   CFTC / ESMA / FCA for financial-market infrastructure), keep
   the IT / OT boundary clean in inventory and controls, and stay
   incident-notification-ready across CIRCIA, NIS2, sectoral
   directives, and SEC Form 8-K Item 1.05.
8. Sector-adaptation methodology — how does the head separate the
   architect's blueprint work (level 50) from the head's own
   adaptation work (regulator-engagement pattern per regulator,
   board-reporting cadence adapted to the sector's committee
   conventions, external-positioning posture adapted to the
   sector's expected surfaces), reconcile the adaptations in a
   multi-sector enterprise, and refresh each surface on the cadence
   that matches its pace of change.

## Learning objectives

- Banking — lead AI-governance in the model-risk-management
  context: Federal Reserve + OCC + FDIC exam coordination on AI/ML
  model risk under SR 11-7 + OCC 2011-12 + SR 23-4; OSFI E-23 for
  Canadian FIs; PRA SS1/23 for UK banks; interfacing with the
  enterprise Head of Model Risk Management (where the head-of-AI-
  governance sits as a peer or a specialist under MRM); consumer-
  facing AI obligations (CFPB) and fair-lending (Reg B) shape.
- Insurance — lead AI-governance in the state-DOI + NAIC context:
  NAIC AI Model Bulletin adoption cycle by state DOI, Colorado
  Insurance Regulation 10-1-1, NYDFS Circular Letter No. 7 (2024),
  state DOI exam coordination, external actuarial coordination on
  AI-driven pricing / underwriting; consumer-facing AI obligations.
- Health system — lead AI-governance in the FDA + HHS OCR context:
  FDA GMLP + PCCP submission leadership on SaMD; clinical-governance
  interface with the Chief Medical Officer / medical staff; HIPAA
  breach notification for AI-triggered PHI events; state medical-AI
  regulation; hospital-accreditation body coordination.
- Pharmaceutical — lead AI-governance in the FDA + MHRA + EMA +
  PMDA context: regulatory-submission leadership on AI-enabled
  clinical trials, pharmacovigilance, manufacturing; GxP interface;
  inspector engagement; regulatory-submission cadence coordination.
- Public sector / federal contractor — lead AI-governance as the
  Federal Chief AI Officer designation under OMB M-25-21 (or as
  the head-of-AI-governance for a federal contractor that services
  agencies with CAIO designations); AI Governance Board seat under
  M-25-21; IG relations; agency-head coordination; Federal CAIO
  Council participation; FedRAMP + NIST SP 800-53 + NIST AI RMF
  interface; congressional-oversight interface where the agency is
  subject to it.
- Frontier lab — lead an RSP / Preparedness / FSF program
  (Anthropic RSP, OpenAI Preparedness Framework, Google DeepMind
  FSF, Frontier Model Forum member charter); White House
  engagement; US AISI + UK AISI evaluation-access negotiation;
  industry-body chair roles; public-transparency cadence (annual
  updates, capability-threshold publications, incident-summary
  publications).
- Critical infrastructure — lead AI-governance for a critical-
  infrastructure operator: CISA + NCSC engagement under Secure AI
  System Development Guidelines; ENISA engagement for EU operators;
  sector-specific regulator coordination (electricity, telecom,
  water, transportation).
- Design the sector-adaptation methodology at leadership scope —
  the enterprise operating design comes from senior-ai-governance-
  architect (level 50) sector blueprints; the head adapts the
  sector-specific regulator-engagement pattern, board-reporting
  cadence, and external-positioning posture.

## How to work through this module

The module is designed to be worked one sector chapter at a time,
in the order the chapters appear. Every sector chapter follows the
same internal shape: motivation grounded in the sector's distinctive
supervisory reality, the sector's core-concept framing, the specific
instruments and regulators the head reads line by line, the head's
placement relative to the sector's incumbent senior functions, the
sector's examination or inspection or evaluation cadence at
leadership scope, the head's board-level story adapted to the
sector's board conventions, and a summary. Reading a chapter is not
enough; the chapter's exercise is where the shape becomes an
operating package.

Chapter 08 is the methodological close. After the seven sector
chapters, chapter 08 names the four-step sector-adaptation
methodology that separates the architect-designed blueprint work
(level 50) from the head-adapted work at level 60, walks the
multi-sector enterprise's reconciliation problem, and specifies the
refresh cadences that keep the adaptation live. Reading chapter 08
before the sector chapters produces a methodology with no content
to methodologise about; reading it after produces the leadership
skill the module exists to build.

The module extends every prior module in the track. The regulator-
engagement pattern from [[mod-104-regulator-engagement]] chapter 04
is the pattern each sector chapter instantiates. The board-reporting
cluster shape from [[mod-103-board-and-audit-committee-reporting]]
is the shape each sector chapter adapts to a specific board
convention. The external-positioning portfolio from
[[mod-106-external-positioning-and-voluntary-codes]] is the
portfolio each sector chapter populates with sector-specific
industry bodies. The incident-response shape from
[[mod-110-incident-leadership]] is the shape each sector chapter's
incident-notification overlay attaches to. The cross-CxO coordination
pattern from [[mod-109-cross-enterprise-leadership]] is the pattern
each sector's peer-executive coordination protocol (Chief Model Risk
Officer, Chief Actuary, Chief Medical Officer, Head of Regulatory
Affairs, Responsible Scaling Officer, Chief Engineering Officer)
instantiates.

The module feeds
[[mod-112-certifications-career-path-community-leadership]], where
the sector-specific professional communities the head participates
in (RMA and PRMIA for banking; CAS and SOA for insurance; ACHE and
AMIA for healthcare; RAPS and DIA for pharma; Federal CAIO Council
for public sector; Frontier Model Forum for frontier labs; sector
ISACs for critical infrastructure) become the standing external
engagement portfolio the head builds a career inside. It also feeds
[[mod-113-budget-vendor-p-and-l-and-investment-case]], where the
sector's supervisory instruments shape the enterprise's AI-related
investment case and vendor-selection posture.
