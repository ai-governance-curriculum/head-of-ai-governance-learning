**Estimated effort:** 16 hours

## What this module is for

The head of AI governance does not run the AI-security SOC, the
AI platform, the enterprise privacy program, the enterprise risk
framework, third-line audit, legal opinion authority, the
enterprise budget, HR policy, the enterprise compliance program,
or enterprise communications. Every one of those functions has
an accountable executive. The head *extends into AI* on top of
each of them.

Extension without coordination is the failure mode this module
is written against. When the head extends into AI security
without a written interface to the CISO, the SOC gets red-team
findings it cannot triage. When the head extends into AI privacy
without a written interface to the CPO / DPO, the AIA and DPIA
either duplicate each other or leave gaps that surface in the
next regulatory examination. When the head chairs the AI
governance council without discipline, decisions do not stick,
dissent is not captured, and the escalation path to the board is
improvised in the moment.

The [[mod-101-role-scope-and-leadership-shape]] chapter 06
"CxO adjacency positioning" is the *boundary map* — three lines
per adjacency, ratified once, referenced when the boundary is
challenged. This module is the working *coordination pattern*
underneath the boundary map: what interface each peer relies on,
what cadence the coordination runs at, what artefacts are
co-authored versus owned, what happens when the pattern breaks.
Boundary maps prevent turf war; coordination patterns run the
work.

This module answers twelve connected questions.

1. How does the head design the interface with the CISO — the AI
   security handoff, the red-team-finding routing into the SOC,
   the AI-specific detection engineering — so the CISO's SOC has
   what it needs and the head does not attempt to run a shadow
   SOC.
2. How does the head design the interface with the CIO / CTO
   and CDO — the AI-platform ownership handoff, the model-
   registry integration, the data-governance interface, the
   MLOps runtime the governance runtime plugs into.
3. How does the head design the interface with the Chief Privacy
   Officer / DPO — where the AIA meets the DPIA, where the ROPA
   picks up AI processing, how GDPR / CPPA / DPDPA obligations
   divide between AI-scope and enterprise-scope.
4. How does the head design the interface with the Chief Risk
   Officer — how the AI risk portfolio composes with the parent
   ERM framework, how the reporting line runs where the head
   reports into the CRO, how appetite / tolerance / limit
   language is shared with the enterprise risk register under
   ISO 31000 / ISO/IEC 27005 / COSO ERM.
5. How does the head coordinate with the Chief Audit Executive
   on the third-line AI audit programme under the IIA Three
   Lines Model, IIA CAE guidance, and the IIA International
   Professional Practices Framework (IPPF).
6. How does the head coordinate with the General Counsel on
   regulator-obligation interpretation (head packages; GC
   delivers legal opinion), litigation posture, whistleblower
   and regulator-complaint intake, SAR / MOU / MRA management.
7. How does the head coordinate with the CFO on governance-
   function budget, ROI reporting, capital-planning discipline,
   and contingency-reserve planning for regulator fines and
   remediation programs.
8. How does the head coordinate with the CHRO on AI-in-HR
   governance — hiring-tool bias auditing under NYC LL 144,
   EEOC guidance, and Illinois HB 3773; performance-management
   AI governance; workforce AI-training strategy.
9. How does the head coordinate with the Chief Compliance
   Officer on the parent-compliance framework, where the AI-
   governance function reports up through compliance, where
   compliance owns the enterprise policy library the AI-policy
   taxonomy plugs into.
10. How does the head coordinate with the Chief Communications
    Officer on public trust posture, media strategy, and the
    crisis-communications runbook for AI-incident public
    disclosure — head owns the substance; CCO owns the delivery.
11. How does the head *chair* the AI governance council with
    agenda discipline, decision-forcing, dissent-capture,
    minute-keeping, decision-log discipline, and the escalation-
    to-board pathway.
12. How does the head manage the *conflict* pattern with product
    owners — stop-shipping decisions, delayed-release decisions,
    feature-scope-reduction decisions — with the escalation
    ladder rehearsed so the ambiguity does not become political.

The module produces the working coordination artefacts and
rehearses the discipline against the standing CxO adjacency
surface as of the research window.

## How to work through this module

The module is designed to be worked in adjacency order. Chapters
01–02 open with the *technical adjacencies* — CISO, CIO / CTO /
CDO — because the coordination there is the most under-invested
in most enterprises and because the AIMS runtime physically
plugs into these functions' systems. Chapter 03 turns to the
*privacy adjacency* — CPO / DPO — where the AIA / DPIA interface
is the single most contested boundary in the field. Chapters
04–05 turn to the *risk-and-assurance adjacencies* — CRO, CAE —
where the head's reporting line and third-line assurance model
usually live. Chapter 06 turns to *General Counsel* — the
engagement-versus-legal-opinion boundary the head crosses every
regulator engagement. Chapter 07 turns to *CFO* — budget, ROI,
contingency reserve. Chapter 08 turns to *CHRO* — the AI-in-HR
programme that is now a first-class regulatory surface. Chapter
09 turns to *Chief Compliance Officer* — the parent compliance
framework the AI-policy taxonomy plugs into. Chapter 10 turns
to *Chief Communications Officer* — public trust posture and
crisis comms. Chapter 11 turns *inward*: how the head chairs the
AI governance council itself. Chapter 12 closes with the
*conflict pattern* with product owners — the stop-ship, delayed-
release, and feature-reduction escalation the head has to run
without letting the conflict become political.

Exercises consolidate the chapters into working coordination
artefacts, grouped so the head produces one signed interface
document per exercise. Exercises 1–4 each produce a co-signed
interface document with the named peers. Exercise 5 produces the
governance-council charter and the rehearsed decision-forcing
drill. Exercise 6 rehearses the escalation ladder through the
three product-owner conflict scenarios.

By the end of the module, a working head-of-role has produced:

- Co-signed interface documents with the CISO, the CIO / CTO /
  CDO, the CPO / DPO, the CRO, the CAE, the General Counsel,
  the CFO, the CHRO, the Chief Compliance Officer, and the
  Chief Communications Officer — each ratified in the audit-
  committee-visible boundary map from
  [[mod-101-role-scope-and-leadership-shape]] chapter 06.
- A ratified AI Governance Council charter with membership,
  quorum, agenda cadence, decision-forcing discipline, dissent-
  capture, minute-keeping, decision-log format, and escalation-
  to-board pathway.
- A rehearsed escalation ladder for the three product-owner
  conflict scenarios — stop-ship, delayed-release, feature-
  scope-reduction — with the "asked-and-answered" pattern that
  keeps the conflict on merits rather than politics.

These artefacts extend the CxO adjacency map from
[[mod-101-role-scope-and-leadership-shape]] with the running
coordination pattern the head uses every quarter. They feed the
incident-leadership discipline in
[[mod-110-incident-leadership]] (where the CISO, GC, and CCO
coordination patterns become live under time pressure), the
sector-program overlays in [[mod-111-sector-program-leadership]]
(where sector-specific coordination adjacencies like the Head of
Model Risk Management or the Chief Medical Officer are added),
and the P&L discipline in
[[mod-113-budget-vendor-p-and-l-and-investment-case]] (where
the CFO coordination pattern becomes an operating budget cycle).
