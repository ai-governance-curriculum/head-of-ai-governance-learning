# exercise-05: Governance Council Chair Drill

**Estimated effort:** 4 hours

## Objective

Author the **AI Governance Council charter** and rehearse the
**decision-forcing drill** the head runs as chair, per chapter
[11-chairing-the-governance-council](../11-chairing-the-governance-council.md).
Deliverables are the ratified charter, the standing-agenda
template, the pre-read format, the minute template, the
decision-log format, the action-item register format, the
dissent-capture convention, the escalation-to-board pathway
document, the effectiveness-review template, and a *rehearsal
transcript* for one worked contested-policy-ratification
drill through the four decision-forcing moves.

The charter has to survive three stress tests. First, the
audit-committee secretariat reads the charter and confirms
that the escalation-to-board pathway is unambiguous — an
improvised pathway that requires interpretation fails the
audit-committee readability standard. Second, the CAE reads
the charter and confirms the decision-log discipline is
audit-ready — a decision log that lives in the chair's
personal notes fails the third-line-assurance standard.
Third, the rehearsal transcript survives review by a
peer head-of-role or an external governance advisor without
revealing improvisation on the four moves (frame, solicit,
test, capture and close) — a transcript where the chair
drifts into advocacy or where dissent is not captured fails
the craft standard.

## Steps

1. **Enterprise context one-pager.** Author the half-page
   context: sector, C-suite shape, board-committee
   architecture, current material AI program dimensions the
   council will actually work on. Every subsequent artefact
   references this.

2. **The council charter.** Author the ratified charter (5
   to 10 pages) with the twelve chapter-11 sections:
   - Purpose.
   - Membership (voting members by role, standing invitees,
     rotating attendees, secretariat).
   - Quorum threshold for substantive decisions and other
     business.
   - Voting model (consensus, formal vote, or hybrid).
   - Written-resolution rules.
   - Agenda-setting authority and item-proposal process.
   - Pre-read discipline (lead time, format, unprepared-item
     rule).
   - Decision-log and minute discipline (document-control
     location, review-and-ratification cadence, retention).
   - Dissent-capture discipline (named-dissent convention,
     48-hour written-dissent window, escalation on
     structural dissent).
   - Escalation-to-board pathway (standing report cadence,
     material-single-decision escalation, emergency
     escalation).
   - Effectiveness-review cadence.
   - Charter-review cadence.

3. **The standing-agenda template.** Author the template
   every council meeting agenda uses:
   - Standing items (decision-log review from prior
     meeting; action-item status; regulatory-change
     surfacing; incident and near-miss review; KRI / KPI
     portfolio dashboard).
   - Rotating items with named decisions.
   - Time allocations per item.
   - Named decision or named informational outcome per
     item.
   - Named accountable presenter per item.
   - Named ratifying pattern (consensus, vote, deferral).

4. **The pre-read format.** Author the template every
   substantive pre-read follows: substantive summary,
   options-analysis, recommendation, decision requested,
   named substantive author, distribution date, pre-read-
   due-back-date.

5. **The minute template.** Author the minute template the
   secretariat uses: attendance record (voting members
   present, invitees present, secretariat present), material
   discussion per item, decision taken (referenced to the
   decision log), dissent recorded, action items assigned,
   time-of-adjournment. The template is document-controlled
   with named review-and-ratification cadence.

6. **The decision-log format.** Author the format the
   cumulative decision log uses: decision identifier, date,
   decision text (verbatim from the minute), material
   context, named dissent, current status (in force,
   superseded, revoked). The log is a separate document
   from the minutes; it is the reference the audit committee
   reads.

7. **The action-item register format.** Author the format:
   action item text, assignee, due date, status, standing-
   review cadence (top-of-next-meeting).

8. **The dissent-capture convention.** Author the convention
   in one to two pages: what named dissent looks like in the
   minute; the 48-hour written-dissent window and how a
   member submits; the escalation-on-structural-dissent
   discipline (peer conversation with the dissenting
   member; escalation to the CEO or audit-committee chair
   where the disagreement is genuinely irresolvable at the
   council).

9. **The escalation-to-board pathway document.** Author the
   two- to four-page pathway document with the three
   pathways from chapter 11:
   - Standing report to the audit committee on the reporting
     cadence from [[mod-103-board-and-audit-committee-
     reporting]].
   - Material-single-decision escalation from the chair.
   - Emergency escalation on material risk.
   For each pathway, name the timing, the participants, the
   materials the head takes to the audit committee, and the
   audit-committee's decision options.

10. **The effectiveness-review template.** Author the
    template the council uses on its annual self-review:
    - Attendance discipline (per-member attendance rate).
    - Decision-forcing discipline (ratio of items closed
      with named decisions to items that drifted).
    - Dissent-capture discipline (dissent rate per
      substantive decision).
    - Action-item completion (rate closed on time).
    - Escalation-pathway usage (count of standing and
      emergency escalations).
    - Member feedback (structured feedback round with the
      chair; feedback confidential to the chair and the
      secretariat unless the chair chooses to share).

11. **The rehearsal transcript.** Author a specific rehearsal
    transcript for one contested-policy-ratification drill.
    The scenario: the head brings the AI Third-Party
    Standard v1.3 for ratification. Two voting members are
    prepared to dissent if the standard is ratified as
    drafted (chapter 11's worked example, adapted). Write
    the transcript covering:
    - The chair's opening frame (chapter 11 move 1).
    - The named-member solicitation in a considered order
      (move 2).
    - The chair's proposed amendment.
    - The test for consensus (move 3).
    - The dissent capture and close (move 4).
    - The action-item and re-review-date assignment.
    - The secretariat's minute entry.
    - The decision-log entry.
    - The action-item register entry.
    The transcript is a rehearsal artefact — a rehearsal for
    the head's own craft, and a walkthrough the head can
    use to onboard a new voting member or secretariat.

12. **The failure-mode audit.** Author a short section — one
    paragraph per failure mode — naming how the charter
    detects and recovers from each of the seven chapter-11
    anti-patterns:
    - Status-meeting drift.
    - Unanimous decision that hid dissent.
    - Wandering agenda.
    - Chair-as-author.
    - Minute as chair's notes.
    - Improvised escalation.
    - Council that has never reviewed itself.

## Acceptance criteria

- The charter covers all twelve chapter-11 sections; a
  missing section fails the criterion. Voting members and
  standing invitees are named by role, not by incumbent (so
  the charter survives turnover). A charter that names
  incumbents in the membership list fails.
- The standing-agenda template covers all five standing
  items and provides a slot for rotating items with named
  decisions and accountable presenters. An agenda template
  that permits informational-drift items without a name
  change fails.
- The decision-log format is a *cumulative* log distinct
  from the minute — a decision log that is a copy of the
  latest minute fails.
- The dissent-capture convention names the 48-hour written-
  dissent window and the escalation-on-structural-dissent
  discipline explicitly. A convention that treats dissent
  as anonymised or aggregated fails.
- The escalation-to-board pathway document covers all three
  pathways with named timings and named participants; an
  improvised pathway fails.
- The rehearsal transcript exercises all four decision-
  forcing moves (frame, solicit, test, capture and close)
  and captures the named dissent explicitly with the
  substantive reason. A transcript where the chair drifts
  into advocacy or where dissent is not named fails.
- The failure-mode audit names, for each of the seven
  anti-patterns, at least one detection signal and one
  recovery action.
- Any factual claim about a specific parliamentary rule, a
  specific board-committee convention, a specific standards
  clause, or a specific enterprise's charter carries a
  `<!-- needs-research: verify [specific fact] against
  [official source] -->` marker where not verifiable from
  cited primary sources.

## Notes and starter guidance

The most common first-cut failure on this exercise is the
charter that reads as a job description for the chair rather
than a decision-making architecture. A charter that says
"the chair convenes the council quarterly" is not a charter;
a charter that says "the council convenes quarterly, with
quorum at 2/3 of voting members, decisions taken by
consensus with formal-vote fallback for contested items,
dissent captured by name in the minute and in the decision
log, with a 48-hour written-dissent window and structural-
dissent escalation to the CEO where irresolvable at the
council" is. Force the architecture depth.

The second common failure is the rehearsal transcript that
reads as a script rather than as a working transcript. A
script has the chair speaking in polished paragraphs and
the members responding cooperatively; a working transcript
has the chair framing the decision explicitly, being
interrupted, returning the frame, testing for consensus,
capturing dissent by name. Write the transcript for the
working case, not the polished one. The rehearsal artefact
is only useful if it rehearses the pressure moves, not the
easy ones.

The third common failure is the escalation-to-board pathway
written in a way that discourages use. A pathway that says
"emergency escalation is available for exceptional matters"
is a pathway no chair will use in the moment because the
threshold is not named; a pathway that says "emergency
escalation to the audit-committee chair is triggered within
24 hours where the council surfaces a material AI risk not
carried in the enterprise's risk register, or a live incident
escalating past named tolerance, or regulator correspondence
changing the enterprise's obligation set" is a pathway with
named triggers. Force the trigger-naming.

On dissent-capture — the temptation is to soften the named-
dissent convention because it "makes members uncomfortable".
Do not soften. Named dissent is what makes the record
trustable to the audit committee, the CAE, and the head's
own future self. A member who dissents on the record is a
member the enterprise wants at the council; a member who
dissents privately in the parking lot after the meeting is
a member the enterprise cannot govern with. Force the
convention; the CAE will read the effectiveness review's
dissent-rate metric and a dissent rate of zero across a year
of material items is a signal, not a triumph.

The charter is a governance artefact. Ratify it at the
audit committee at inception; refresh it at least annually
at the council's own review cycle; store it in the CCO
(compliance)'s enterprise policy library
([[chapter-09]](../09-chief-compliance-officer-coordination.md)).
