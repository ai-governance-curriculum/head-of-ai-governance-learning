# exercise-06: Product Conflict Escalation Rehearsal

**Estimated effort:** 4 hours

## Objective

Author the enterprise's **product-conflict escalation-ladder
document** and rehearse the five-step ladder through *three
tabletop scenarios* — one per conflict type (stop-ship,
delayed-release, feature-scope-reduction) — per chapter
[12-product-conflict-management](../12-product-conflict-management.md).
Deliverables are the co-signed escalation-ladder document, the
asked-and-answered template, the three tabletop scenario
packs, the three rehearsal transcripts (one per scenario), the
after-action review template, and the escalation-record
discipline the head carries into the live case.

The ladder has to survive three stress tests. First, the CEO
or CAIO (co-signatory on the ladder document) reads the ladder
and confirms the thresholds for Step 4 escalation are named
tightly enough that the head is not escalating routine
council-adjudicated items — a ladder with a Step 4 trigger
phrased as "when the head feels the council was wrong" fails
the discipline standard. Second, the product-organisation
lead for the enterprise's largest AI-anchored product reads
the ladder and confirms the Step 2 conversation is a
substantive conversation on the merits, not an authority-
assertion exercise — a ladder that reads as "head overrides
product" fails the operating standard. Third, the CAE reads
the escalation-record discipline and confirms every ladder
step produces an evidence trail the third line can audit — a
ladder that leaves Step 1 and Step 2 off the record because
"they are informal" fails the audit-readability standard.

The rehearsal transcripts are not polished scripts. They are
working transcripts where the asked-and-answered discipline is
tested under the three characteristic failure modes: the
reframe attempt, the end-run attempt, and the request to
accept a council decision the head cannot accept in silence.
A transcript that only rehearses the easy case where the
product owner concedes at Step 1 fails the rehearsal purpose.

## Steps

1. **Enterprise context one-pager.** Author the half-page
   context the ladder operates against: sector, C-suite shape
   (CEO reporting line for the ladder's Step 4; whether a
   CAIO exists and holds the reporting line for the AI-
   governance function; which product organisation runs the
   largest AI-anchored product line), the AI Governance
   Council's membership shape (from exercise-05's deliverables
   if available), the current audit-committee cadence, and
   the enterprise's current release-decision architecture
   (release boards, launch committees, go/no-go rituals).
   Every subsequent artefact references this one-pager.

2. **The escalation-ladder document.** Author the co-signed
   document (6 to 12 pages) with the eight required sections
   from chapter 12:
   - The three conflict types (stop-ship, delayed-release,
     feature-scope-reduction) with the framing template and
     remediation shape per type.
   - The asked-and-answered discipline with the template for
     naming a finding as a substantive question with a
     substantive answer, and the head's obligation to carry
     both unmodified through the ladder.
   - The five ladder steps: Step 1 head-to-product-owner
     working session; Step 2 head-to-product-organisation-
     lead conversation; Step 3 AI Governance Council
     decision; Step 4 CEO / CAIO escalation; Step 5 audit-
     committee escalation under the audit-committee-access
     invariant. Named participants, named timings, named
     outcomes per step.
   - The council pathway reference to chapter
     [[11-chairing-the-governance-council]] — pre-read
     format, decision options, dissent-capture discipline.
   - The audit-committee-access invariant reference to
     [[mod-101-role-scope-and-leadership-shape]] chapter 07
     — Step 5 trigger discipline, named for what it is used
     for and what it is not used for.
   - The tabletop-rehearsal cadence (annual at minimum, plus
     named triggers for additional rehearsal).
   - The record-keeping discipline — how every escalation is
     logged (substantive question, answer, ladder steps,
     timing, outcome) for council effectiveness review and
     CAE audit scope.
   - The review-and-ratification cadence for the document
     itself.

3. **The asked-and-answered template.** Author the one-to-two-
   page template every finding uses when entering the ladder.
   The template fields:
   - Substantive question (one sentence, framed as a *policy
     question*, not an *authority question*).
   - Substantive answer (one to three sentences, with the
     specific policy clause, standard section, or regulatory
     obligation cited by name).
   - Evidence anchor (the evaluation finding, risk-register
     row, or AIA tier that produced the answer, with
     reference to the AIMS inventory ID from
     [[mod-102-running-the-program]] chapter 06).
   - Remediation shape (named substantive remediation with
     a named criterion for release readiness).
   - Conflict type classification (stop-ship, delayed-
     release, or feature-scope-reduction, with reason the
     head chose that classification over the alternatives).
   - Material-event evaluation flag (whether the conflict
     has disclosure, regulator-notification, or customer-
     notification implications that trigger the CFO, GC, or
     CCO coordination patterns from chapters 06, 07, 10).

4. **Three tabletop scenario packs.** Author three scenario
   packs — one per conflict type. Each pack has the specific
   AI system, the specific substantive finding, the product
   organisation's substantive counter-position, the time
   pressure (release date, customer commitment, regulator
   deadline, or earnings-cycle dependence), and the material-
   event flag. The scenarios have to be plausibly grounded in
   the enterprise's actual portfolio, not generic.
   - **Scenario A — stop-ship.** A production AI system
     where the head's evaluation function surfaces a
     substantive finding severe enough that the system
     cannot release in its current form. The scenario names
     the specific finding class (fairness, safety, security,
     privacy, regulatory), the specific policy clause or
     standard section the finding breaches, and the specific
     remediation criterion the head requires.
   - **Scenario B — delayed-release.** A production AI
     system where the head's evaluation function surfaces a
     finding that is not stop-ship-severe but is not within
     the standard's threshold at the current release scope.
     The scenario names the required remediation, the named
     timeline, and the re-evaluation gate.
   - **Scenario C — feature-scope-reduction.** A production
     AI system where the head's evaluation function
     surfaces a finding tied to a specific feature,
     population, or use case while the rest of the system is
     release-ready. The scenario names the specific scope
     reduction (feature-flag disable, population exclusion,
     use-case restriction), the named re-scoping trigger, and
     the audit trail on the reduction.

5. **Three rehearsal transcripts.** For each scenario, write
   a working rehearsal transcript (not a polished script)
   covering Steps 1 through 3 at minimum, and Steps 4 and 5
   where the scenario warrants. Each transcript exercises
   one of the three characteristic failure modes.
   - **Scenario A transcript — the reframe attempt.** The
     product owner tries to reframe the policy question as a
     calibration question ("is the standard's threshold even
     applicable to this system class"). The head's level-50
     architect returns the frame at Step 1. At Step 2, the
     product-organisation lead tries the same reframe. The
     head returns it again. At Step 3, the council runs the
     policy question on the merits, upholds the head's
     finding with a compressed remediation timeline co-
     scoped with the product owner, and logs the
     calibration question as a *separate* standing agenda
     item for the next council. The transcript captures the
     specific words the head uses to return the frame — the
     substantive-question-answer discipline under live
     pressure, not in retrospect.
   - **Scenario B transcript — the end-run attempt.** The
     product owner is dissatisfied with the Step 1 outcome
     and escalates directly to the CEO without taking Step 2.
     The CEO's office contacts the head to understand the
     situation. The head writes a short brief (one page)
     explaining that the ladder has not been exhausted and
     requests the CEO route the item to the council at Step
     3 rather than deciding bilaterally. The CEO accepts.
     The council decides. The head follows up on the end-run
     pattern — if it is a one-off, the head records the
     pattern and does not escalate; if it is recurrent, the
     head raises the pattern as a named structural item at
     the audit committee under the audit-committee-access
     invariant. The transcript captures the head's response
     to the end-run — substantive at the CEO level,
     structural at the audit-committee level — without the
     head becoming defensive about the end-run itself.
   - **Scenario C transcript — the council decision the
     head cannot accept in silence.** The council decides
     against the head's recommended feature-scope-reduction
     and permits the full-scope release with a weaker
     remediation. The head's professional judgement is that
     the remediation is insufficient and the finding is
     material to AI safety or regulatory exposure. The head
     captures named dissent in the minute per chapter 11's
     dissent-capture discipline. Within 48 hours the head
     files a written-dissent memo. Within one week the head
     triggers Step 4, prepares the one-to-three-page brief
     for the CEO / CAIO, and — if Step 4 does not resolve —
     prepares the Step 5 escalation to the audit-committee
     chair. The transcript captures the professional-
     obligation language the head uses at Step 4 and Step 5
     without framing the escalation as a political move,
     and captures the head's acceptance posture if the CEO
     / CAIO upholds the council.

6. **The after-action review template.** Author the template
   the head uses at the conclusion of every live ladder
   execution (and every tabletop rehearsal) to produce the
   institutional learning. The template fields:
   - Substantive question and answer carried through the
     ladder (verbatim).
   - Steps taken, timing per step, participants per step.
   - Decision at each step (resolved / escalated).
   - Final outcome and remediation status.
   - Named dissent captured.
   - What the head would do differently (per step).
   - Ladder-design failures surfaced (step whose timing was
     unrealistic; participant who could not be reached;
     outcome that was not anticipated).
   - Composition with other chapters' coordination patterns
     (did the CFO's material-event evaluation run; did the
     CCO's crisis-communications runbook activate; did GC
     deliver a legal-authority determination).
   - Updates to the ladder document, the scenario packs, or
     the escalation-record discipline that the review
     produces.

7. **The escalation-record discipline.** Author the one-to-
   two-page discipline that governs how every ladder
   execution is recorded. Record fields:
   - Record identifier, date opened, date closed.
   - Affected AI system (AIMS inventory ID).
   - Substantive question, substantive answer, evidence
     anchor.
   - Conflict type.
   - Ladder step history (per step: participants, date,
     outcome).
   - Final decision and remediation.
   - Named dissent on the record.
   - Record owner (the head's secretariat or level-50
     architect delegate; owner handles document control and
     retention, not substantive content).
   - Audit-committee visibility flag (whether the record is
     routed to the audit committee as part of the standing
     report cadence or as a material-single-decision
     escalation).
   The discipline specifies: Step 1 and Step 2 are on the
   record even where they resolve without council
   involvement; Step 3 is on the record via the council
   decision log; Step 4 and Step 5 are on the record via
   written brief and audit-committee minute. A ladder
   execution that is "informal" because it stayed below
   Step 3 is still an escalation and still produces a
   record.

8. **The anti-pattern audit.** Author a short section — one
   paragraph per anti-pattern — naming how the ladder
   detects and recovers from each of the seven chapter-12
   anti-patterns:
   - The unilateral stop-ship at Step 1.
   - The reframe accepted.
   - The end-run without response.
   - The council-decision that the head cannot accept in
     silence.
   - The escalation as routine.
   - The improvised ladder.
   - The finding that never enters the ladder.
   For each anti-pattern, name at least one detection signal
   the ladder surfaces (an attendance gap, a missing record,
   a dissent-rate anomaly, a Step 4 usage frequency) and one
   recovery action (a specific ladder step, a specific
   adjacency chapter's protocol, an audit-committee
   escalation under the invariant).

## Acceptance criteria

- The ladder document covers all five steps with named
  participants, named timings, and named outcomes per step;
  a step with an unnamed participant or an undefined timing
  fails the criterion. Step 4 and Step 5 trigger thresholds
  are named tightly — a ladder with Step 4 phrased as "when
  the head disagrees with the council" fails; a ladder that
  reserves Step 4 for *material substantive disagreements the
  head's professional obligation requires the head to raise*
  and names what "material" means (named policy breach,
  named regulatory implication, named safety / fairness
  threshold exceeded) passes.
- The asked-and-answered template produces a *policy
  question*, not an *authority question*. A finding entered
  into the template as "the head has authority to stop this
  release" fails; a finding entered as "the enterprise's AI
  Third-Party Standard section 4.2 threshold on protected-
  class exposure permits / does not permit this release"
  passes.
- Each scenario pack names a specific AI system (not a
  generic placeholder), a specific finding class, a specific
  policy clause or standard section, and a specific
  remediation criterion. A scenario authored at the "a
  system has a fairness problem" level fails.
- The three rehearsal transcripts each exercise their named
  failure mode (reframe, end-run, dissent-in-silence) on the
  record. A transcript where the chair, the head, or the
  product owner cooperates through the easy path fails the
  rehearsal purpose. The transcripts capture the specific
  language the head uses — the words, not the gist — at the
  pressure points.
- The after-action review template produces inputs to the
  ladder document, the scenario packs, or the escalation-
  record discipline. A template that produces a narrative
  the head keeps privately fails the institutional-learning
  purpose.
- The escalation-record discipline includes Step 1 and Step
  2 on the record even where they resolve without council
  involvement. A discipline that treats Step 1 and Step 2 as
  "informal and off the record" fails the audit-readability
  standard the CAE applies.
- The anti-pattern audit names, for each of the seven
  anti-patterns, at least one detection signal and one
  recovery action.
- Any factual claim about a specific regulator obligation, a
  specific standards clause, a specific case or incident
  precedent, or a specific negotiation-literature citation
  carries a `<!-- needs-research: verify [specific fact]
  against [official source] -->` marker where not verifiable
  from cited primary sources.

## Notes and starter guidance

The most common first-cut failure on this exercise is the
ladder written in a way that reads as the head's authority
assertion rather than as a decision-forcing process. A
ladder that says "the head decides stop-ship; the product
organisation decides scope reduction" has not written a
ladder, it has written a RACI with no escalation discipline.
A ladder that says "the substantive finding enters at Step
1 as a named policy question; the ladder runs the question
through named steps with named participants and named
timings; the council adjudicates at Step 3; Step 4 and Step
5 are reserved for material disagreements that engage the
head's professional obligation" has written a ladder. Force
the decision-forcing architecture.

The second common failure is the rehearsal transcript that
rehearses only the cooperative case. The product owner
agrees at Step 1; the council upholds unanimously; the head
walks away satisfied. That transcript rehearses nothing.
The rehearsal that *is* worth writing is the one where the
pressure moves land — the reframe attempt, the end-run
attempt, the council decision the head cannot accept in
silence — and the head's craft shows in the specific words
used to return the frame, to respond to the end-run without
defensiveness, and to escalate under professional obligation
without framing the escalation as political. Write the
transcript for the pressure moves. The cooperative case is
not where the ladder earns its keep.

The third common failure is the escalation-record
discipline that treats Step 1 and Step 2 as "informal". The
CAE reads this and asks, "where are the records for the
substantive findings that resolved below the council?" If
the answer is "they are in people's inboxes and in verbal
agreements", the head's function has handed the third line
an audit finding on third-line-assurance grounds — not on
the merits of the governance decisions, but on the
discipline of the record. Record everything that moves
through the ladder, including the Step 1 working session
and the Step 2 leadership-level conversation. "Informal" is
not an audit-defensible classification for a release-gate
substantive finding.

On the asked-and-answered discipline — the single most
important craft move is to make the question a *policy
question* rather than an *authority question*. "Does the
enterprise's AI Third-Party Standard permit this release"
is a policy question; it has a substantive answer that any
escalation level can read against the standard. "Does the
head have authority to stop this release" is an authority
question; it invites the political framing the ladder
exists to prevent. Force the policy-question frame; return
the authority reframe when it is attempted. The transcripts
make the craft visible; the ladder document makes it
required.

The ladder is a governance artefact. Co-sign it with the
CEO or CAIO; ratify it at the AI Governance Council;
refresh it at least annually; store it in the Chief
Compliance Officer's enterprise policy library (chapter
[[09-chief-compliance-officer-coordination]]). The audit
committee reads it as evidence that the head runs the
conflict pattern on the merits, not on the politics; the
record is what carries that evidence across turnover on
either side of the ladder.
