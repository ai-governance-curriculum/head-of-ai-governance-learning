# exercise-03: CAE + GC + CFO Coordination Authoring

**Estimated effort:** 4 hours

## Objective

Author the **assurance-legal-finance-adjacency interface set**
— three co-signed interface documents (head + CAE, head + GC,
head + CFO) that operationalise chapters
[05-cae-coordination](../05-cae-coordination.md),
[06-general-counsel-coordination](../06-general-counsel-coordination.md),
and [07-cfo-coordination](../07-cfo-coordination.md).
Deliverables are the three written interface documents plus
the supporting artefacts each chapter names: the CAE third-
line audit-plan-input template and the finding-response
convention; the GC engagement-versus-legal-opinion boundary
statement, the litigation-hold protocol for AI-adjacent
matters, and the SAR / MOU / MRA management playbook; and the
CFO governance-budget template, the AI-governance ROI
narrative, and the contingency-reserve model for regulator-
fines and remediation programs.

The interface set has to survive three stress tests. First,
the CAE reads the interface document and confirms that the
head's function operates as an audit *subject* under IIA
Three Lines / IPPF discipline — an interface that positions
the head as co-owner of the audit programme fails the test.
Second, the GC reads the engagement-versus-legal-opinion
boundary statement and confirms it preserves GC's exclusive
authority to issue legal opinions on regulator obligations —
a boundary statement that puts the head in the opinion seat
fails the test. Third, the CFO reads the ROI narrative and
confirms it uses the finance function's ROI methodology and
translates AI-governance value into terms the CFO can
present to the CEO and the board — an ROI narrative in AI-
governance-only vocabulary fails the CFO's readability
standard.

## Steps

1. **Enterprise context one-pager.** Author the half-page
   context: sector, CAE's reporting shape and independence
   posture, GC's reporting line, the enterprise's material
   regulator-obligation footprint (EU AI Act, sector-specific
   AI rules, US state law scope), any known active regulator
   engagements or pending litigation, and the CFO's operating
   discipline. Every subsequent artefact references this.

2. **The CAE interface document.** Author the co-signed
   interface document (6 to 10 pages) with the sections
   required by chapter 05:
   - Scope statement — head is audit subject, CAE runs the
     audit programme, no co-authorship of the audit plan.
   - The three surfaces (audit-plan input, evidence access
     and fieldwork support, finding response and
     remediation tracking) each with the artefacts, cadence,
     and joint owner.
   - The evidence-access SLA — how quickly the head's
     function delivers requested evidence to the CAE's
     fieldwork, and how the AIMS evidence-of-record store
     interacts with the CAE's evidence-collection
     discipline.
   - The finding-response taxonomy — accept, mitigate,
     dispute — with the mechanism for each and the
     remediation-tracking discipline.
   - The joint annual audit-committee appearance rhythm —
     annual programme review, annual audit-plan review,
     material-incident post-mortem.
   - Escalation ladder.
   - Review cadence.

3. **The audit-plan-input template.** Author the template
   the head uses to submit input to the CAE's annual audit-
   plan cycle. The template is *input*, not co-authorship —
   it proposes candidate audit areas the head's function
   would benefit from third-line assurance on, with the
   substantive reason and the AIMS-inventory reference. The
   CAE decides what enters the plan.

4. **The finding-response convention.** Author the
   convention document the head's function uses for every
   CAE finding: the accept / mitigate / dispute taxonomy,
   the timing (initial response within named window,
   detailed remediation plan within named window), the
   escalation path when the head disputes a finding, the
   remediation-tracking discipline through to closure.

5. **The GC interface document.** Author the co-signed
   interface document (8 to 12 pages) with the sections
   required by chapter 06:
   - Scope statement.
   - The engagement-versus-legal-opinion boundary — the
     head packages regulator engagements, evidence, and
     communications; GC delivers legal opinion on
     obligations.
   - Litigation posture — how the head coordinates with GC
     when the AI-governance function's artefacts are
     litigation-exposed (evidence preservation, privilege,
     testimony discipline).
   - Whistleblower and regulator-complaint intake —
     coordination on inbound AI-related complaints under
     the enterprise's whistleblower policy and directly to
     the head's function.
   - SAR / MOU / MRA management — Supervisory Action Requests
     (or the sector-specific equivalent), Memoranda of
     Understanding, Master Regulatory Agreements — how
     drafting, negotiating, and lifecycle-management run.
   - Regulator-correspondence workflow — every regulator-
     facing communication runs through the head-GC
     workflow.
   - Escalation ladder.
   - Joint annual audit-committee appearance.
   - Review cadence.

6. **The litigation-hold protocol for AI-adjacent matters.**
   Author the protocol that governs preservation, access,
   and privilege when litigation or regulator investigation
   is reasonably anticipated on an AI-adjacent matter. The
   protocol names:
   - Trigger criteria (when a hold is initiated).
   - Scope determination (which AIMS artefacts fall under
     the hold — training runs, evaluation results, incident
     records, model versions, evidence-store contents).
   - Preservation discipline (how the head's function
     preserves the artefacts without disrupting operations).
   - Privilege discipline (which artefacts are prepared in
     anticipation of litigation and receive work-product
     protection; which are ordinary-course records; GC's
     role in the determination).
   - Testimony discipline (how the head prepares to testify
     on the AI-governance function's substance under GC's
     preparation).

7. **The SAR / MOU / MRA management playbook.** Author the
   playbook for the head's coordination with GC on
   regulator-issued instruments. For each instrument type,
   the playbook names the drafting process (head's
   substantive input, GC's legal drafting), the negotiating
   process (head accompanies GC where the substance is AI-
   specific), the ratification process, the lifecycle
   management (renewal, amendment, closure), and the
   evidence-discipline requirements the instrument imposes
   on the head's function.

8. **The CFO interface document.** Author the co-signed
   interface document (6 to 10 pages) with the sections
   required by chapter 07:
   - Scope statement.
   - The governance-function budget (base OpEx, growth-
     scenario OpEx, CapEx for platform investments,
     contingency-reserve line).
   - The AI-governance ROI narrative in the CFO's
     methodology.
   - Capital-planning discipline for platform-and-tooling
     investments that require multi-year commitment.
   - Contingency-reserve planning for regulator-fine
     exposure and remediation-program costs, composed with
     the enterprise's material-event disclosure discipline.
   - Material-event financial disclosure interface — how
     the head informs the CFO when an AI event may trigger
     Item 1.05 8-K or adjacent disclosure obligations.
   - Escalation ladder.
   - Joint annual budget cycle appearance (with the CFO's
     enterprise budget cycle, not the head's own).
   - Review cadence.

9. **The governance-budget template.** Author the template
   the head uses in every budget cycle:
   - Headcount OpEx by role archetype and level (composed
     with [[mod-108-leading-the-governance-function]]
     multi-year hiring plan).
   - Non-headcount OpEx (tooling, certifications, external
     consulting, professional-body memberships, training-
     and-conferences).
   - CapEx for platform investments.
   - Contingency-reserve line with the derivation.
   - The three-scenario forecast (base, stretch, contingency)
     required by chapter 07.

10. **The AI-governance ROI narrative.** Author the ROI
    narrative in the CFO's methodology. The narrative
    composes the AI-governance value in the finance function's
    vocabulary — cost-avoidance from prevented incidents,
    prevented regulator-fine exposure, prevented remediation-
    program cost, prevented reputational and market-cap
    events, revenue-enablement through customer-facing
    trust posture, deal-enablement through B2B customer
    security-and-governance requirements, insurance-premium
    optimisation. Every value line has an underlying
    method (based on [[mod-107-program-measurement-and-
    maturity]] chapter 04 ROI modelling) with an honest
    read on the assumption strength.

11. **The contingency-reserve model.** Author the model the
    head co-owns with the CFO for regulator-fine and
    remediation-program contingency. The model names:
    - The exposure inventory (specific regulatory regimes
      with fine authority; specific known enforcement
      postures; specific pending investigations).
    - The likelihood-and-severity method (evidence-based
      where possible; scenario-modelled where not).
    - The reserve calibration (the reserve level as a
      function of exposure, with the CFO's methodology).
    - The disclosure discipline (when the reserve becomes
      material enough to disclose under accounting standards
      the CFO applies).

12. **The failure-mode audit.** Author a short section — one
    paragraph per failure mode — naming how the interface
    set detects and recovers from each of the anti-patterns
    across chapters 05, 06, and 07.

## Acceptance criteria

- The CAE interface document positions the head as audit
  *subject* under IIA Three Lines / IPPF; the head does not
  co-author the audit plan, edit audit reports, or negotiate
  findings off-line. Any language that positions the head as
  co-owner fails the criterion.
- The audit-plan-input template is *input*, not co-authorship
  — it proposes; the CAE decides. Language that assumes head
  approval on the audit plan fails.
- The GC interface document names the engagement-versus-
  legal-opinion boundary explicitly, with the head as
  packager and GC as opinion-issuer. Language that puts the
  head in the opinion seat on regulatory obligations fails
  the criterion.
- The litigation-hold protocol names trigger criteria, scope
  determination, preservation discipline, privilege
  discipline, and testimony discipline. A protocol missing
  any of these fails.
- The CFO interface document uses the CFO's ROI methodology
  and the CFO's budget-cycle discipline; the governance-
  budget template has the three-scenario forecast. An ROI
  narrative in AI-governance-only vocabulary fails.
- The contingency-reserve model names the exposure
  inventory, the method, the calibration, and the
  disclosure discipline. A model without exposure inventory
  fails.
- Any factual claim about a specific IIA standard clause, a
  specific ABA rule, a specific SEC or accounting standard,
  or a specific regulator-instrument convention carries a
  `<!-- needs-research: verify [specific fact] against
  [official source] -->` marker where not verifiable from
  cited primary sources.

## Stretch goals

- Author the **rehearsed audit-finding response** — a
  worked example: the CAE issues a finding on the head's
  function's evidence-completeness KPI trending down. Walk
  the finding-response convention end-to-end.
- Draft the **regulator-inquiry rehearsal** — a hypothetical
  regulator opens an inquiry on an AI system. Walk the head-
  GC coordination through packaging, opinion, response, and
  material-event disclosure to the CFO.
- Author the **budget-cycle presentation** — the specific
  materials the head takes to the CFO's budget-cycle review,
  composing the governance-budget template, ROI narrative,
  and contingency-reserve model into a coherent ask.
- Extend the failure-mode audit with a **finding-negotiation
  temptation drill** — the head sees a finding coming and
  is tempted to negotiate its scope off-line with the CAE's
  audit lead. Walk the interface's detection of the pattern
  and the recovery.
- Draft the **material-event disclosure coordination** — a
  live AI incident with material financial implications;
  how the head, GC, CCO (communications), and CFO
  coordinate the disclosure sequencing without stepping
  outside anyone's authority.

## Notes and starter guidance

The most common first-cut failure on this exercise is the
CAE interface document that reads as if the head and the CAE
are co-owners of the audit programme. They are not. The head
is a subject; the CAE runs the programme. An interface that
softens that boundary is an interface the CAE will not sign,
and — if signed — an interface the audit committee will
read as compromising the CAE's independence. Force the
subject-owner language; the head's craft is to be a good
subject (evidence-ready, transparent, responsive), not to
be a co-owner.

The second common failure is the GC engagement-versus-
opinion boundary that reads as a division of substantive
work rather than a division of legal authority. The head can
be substantive lead on the regulatory analysis; GC issues
the legal opinion. When a regulator asks the enterprise "is
your practice compliant with Article 6", the head packages
the substantive answer and GC delivers it as the enterprise's
legal position. The head does not answer the regulator on
their own authority; GC does not answer without the head's
substantive package. Force the division.

The third common failure is the ROI narrative that reads in
AI-governance vocabulary rather than in finance vocabulary.
The CFO carries the narrative into the CEO's office and the
board room; the narrative has to survive that translation.
"We prevented three regulator inquiries this year" is not
finance vocabulary; "cost-avoidance of $X across three
regulator-inquiry scenarios, based on the enterprise's own
inquiry-response cost history" is. Force the finance
translation without inflating the assumptions.

On the contingency-reserve model — the temptation is either
to over-model (specific line-items for every conceivable
regulator action) or to under-model (a single reserve line
without derivation). Neither survives the CFO's discipline.
Force a middle path: exposure inventory grounded in the
regulatory-obligation register from
[[mod-105-regulatory-change-management]], likelihood-and-
severity method with the assumption strength stated, and a
reserve calibration the CFO's methodology will accept.

The interface set is a governance artefact. Version it, date
it, refresh it annually (with the CAE interface refreshed
more frequently where audit-plan cycles run more than
annually), and distribute the current version to the CAE,
GC, CFO, CRO, CAIO, and audit-committee secretariat.
