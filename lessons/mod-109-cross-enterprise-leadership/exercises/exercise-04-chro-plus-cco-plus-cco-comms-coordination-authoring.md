# exercise-04: CHRO + Chief Compliance + Chief Communications Coordination Authoring

**Estimated effort:** 4 hours

## Objective

Author the **workforce-compliance-communications-adjacency
interface set** — three co-signed interface documents (head +
CHRO, head + Chief Compliance Officer, head + Chief
Communications Officer) that operationalise chapters
[08-chro-coordination](../08-chro-coordination.md),
[09-chief-compliance-officer-coordination](../09-chief-compliance-officer-coordination.md),
and [10-chief-communications-officer-coordination](../10-chief-communications-officer-coordination.md).
Deliverables are the three interface documents plus the
supporting artefacts each chapter names: the AI-in-HR policy
suite and the hiring-tool bias-audit protocol under NYC LL
144, EEOC guidance, and Illinois HB 3773; the AI-policy
taxonomy that plugs into the enterprise policy library
(named policy set with substantive-author and ratifying-body
per policy) and the AI-specific compliance-monitoring
convention; and the public trust posture, the approved- and
prohibited-language registers, and the AI-incident crisis-
communications runbook.

The interface set has to survive three stress tests. First, an
employment-law regulator (EEOC in the US, or the equivalent in
the enterprise's jurisdictions) reads the hiring-tool bias-
audit protocol and confirms it satisfies the named
jurisdictional obligations; a protocol that names LL 144
without the required annual-audit publication mechanism fails
the test. Second, the CCO (compliance) reads the AI-policy
taxonomy and confirms every AI policy composes with the
enterprise policy library on the CCO's document-control
discipline; a taxonomy of standalone documents outside the
library fails. Third, the CCO (communications) reads the AI-
incident crisis-communications runbook and confirms it
composes with the enterprise's parent crisis-communications
convention; a runbook that stands up a parallel bridge fails.

## Steps

1. **Enterprise context one-pager.** Author the half-page
   context: sector, workforce jurisdictional footprint
   (US states with automated-decision-tool laws in scope,
   EU / EEA workforce, UK, other), whether AI is used in
   hiring, performance management, workforce planning,
   employee monitoring, the current CCO (compliance)
   framework, and the CCO (communications) operating model.
   Every subsequent artefact references this.

2. **The CHRO interface document.** Author the co-signed
   interface document (8 to 12 pages) with the sections
   required by chapter 08:
   - Scope statement.
   - AI-in-HR governance surfaces (hiring tools, performance
     management, employee monitoring, workforce planning).
   - The hiring-tool bias-audit protocol (see step 3).
   - The performance-management-AI governance.
   - The workforce AI-training and literacy strategy.
   - The employee-monitoring boundary (where AI monitoring
     is used, and the transparency and works-council /
     collective-bargaining obligations that apply per
     jurisdiction).
   - Escalation ladder.
   - Joint annual audit-committee appearance on AI-in-HR.
   - Review cadence.

3. **The hiring-tool bias-audit protocol.** Author the
   protocol every AI-driven hiring tool follows under the
   enterprise's jurisdictional footprint. The protocol
   names:
   - Coverage inventory (every AEDT / hiring tool in scope
     for LL 144, HB 3773, EEOC guidance, and equivalent).
   - Bias-audit methodology (independent auditor
     engagement, the audit's substantive scope on protected-
     class and intersectional analysis, the audit cadence
     per jurisdiction).
   - Publication discipline (LL 144 requires publication of
     summary results on the employer's website; HB 3773's
     annual reporting; comparable disclosures per
     jurisdiction).
   - Candidate-notice discipline (LL 144's 10-business-day
     notice; comparable notice per jurisdiction).
   - Selection-alternative discipline (candidate's right to
     opt out or request alternative under HB 3773's design).
   - Remediation posture when a bias finding surfaces.
   Where the enterprise operates in more than one jurisdiction,
   the protocol names the highest-common-denominator posture
   that satisfies all in-scope obligations without weakening
   any of them.

4. **The AI-in-HR policy suite.** Author the suite of AI-
   in-HR policies co-owned by the head and the CHRO,
   composed into the CCO (compliance)'s enterprise policy
   library per chapter 09's discipline. Named policies:
   - AI-in-HR Policy (parent).
   - Hiring-Tool AI Policy (LL 144 / HB 3773 / EEOC
     compliance operationalised).
   - Performance-Management AI Policy.
   - Employee-Monitoring AI Policy.
   - Workforce AI-Training and Literacy Policy.
   Each policy has the head as substantive author, the CHRO
   as accountable executive for the workforce dimension,
   and the CCO (compliance) as accountable executive for the
   library's lifecycle.

5. **The CCO (compliance) interface document.** Author the
   co-signed interface document (8 to 12 pages) with the
   sections required by chapter 09:
   - Scope statement — the AI-policy taxonomy plugs into
     the enterprise policy library.
   - The three surfaces (AI-policy taxonomy composition,
     AI-specific compliance monitoring, reporting-line
     discipline).
   - The named AI-policy set (see step 6).
   - The compliance-monitoring composition (test authorship,
     execution, reporting, independent challenge).
   - Reporting-line specifics (solid-line, matrixed, peer
     — chapter 09).
   - Preserved audit-committee-access invariant
     ([[mod-101-role-scope-and-leadership-shape]] chapter
     07).
   - Escalation ladder.
   - Joint annual audit-committee appearance.
   - Review cadence.

6. **The AI-policy taxonomy.** Author the enumerated policy
   set that plugs into the CCO (compliance)'s enterprise
   policy library, with the fields chapter 09 requires:
   - Policy identifier and version.
   - Effective date and next-review date.
   - Substantive author (head's level-50 architect).
   - Accountable executive for content (head, or head +
     named CxO peer for co-owned policies).
   - Accountable executive for lifecycle (CCO compliance's
     policy-management lead).
   - Cross-references to parent enterprise policies.
   - Regulatory-obligation mapping (per the register from
     [[mod-105-regulatory-change-management]]).
   - Ratifying body.
   The taxonomy includes, at minimum, the ten named
   policies from chapter 09 (AI Policy root; AI Acceptable
   Use; AI System Development and Deployment; AI Third-
   Party Standard; AI Incident Response; AI Data Governance;
   AI-in-HR; AI Regulatory Engagement; AI Training and
   Literacy; AI Governance Council Charter).

7. **The AI-specific compliance-monitoring convention.**
   Author the convention that governs how AI-specific
   compliance tests are authored, executed, reported, and
   challenged. Chapter 09's discipline: head authors
   substantive tests; head executes on AIMS operating
   cadence; results feed the CCO (compliance)'s reporting
   pipeline; CCO retains independent-challenge sampling
   rights on any AI-specific control.

8. **The CCO (communications) interface document.** Author
   the co-signed interface document (6 to 10 pages) with
   the sections required by chapter 10:
   - Scope statement — substance / delivery split.
   - The four surfaces (public trust posture, proactive
     external communications, reactive external
     communications, AI-incident crisis communications).
   - The approved-language and prohibited-language registers
     (see step 9).
   - The named-executive spokesperson protocol.
   - The AI-incident crisis-communications runbook (see
     step 10).
   - Escalation ladder.
   - Joint annual audit-committee appearance on external-
     communications posture.
   - Review cadence.

9. **The approved-language and prohibited-language
   registers.** Author the two registers per chapter 10:
   - Approved: the specific phrasings the CCO
     (communications)'s spokespeople may use verbatim,
     each with the substantive AIMS-artefact basis (which
     evaluation, which AIA, which control provides the
     evidence for the phrasing).
   - Prohibited: the categorical claims ruled out because
     the enterprise's evidence does not support them (the
     "unbiased", "safe", "cannot hallucinate", "cannot
     cause harm" family), with the substantive
     substitutions ("we test against benchmarks X, Y, Z
     and here is what we found").
   The registers compose with the FTC misleading-AI-claims
   discipline.

10. **The AI-incident crisis-communications runbook.**
    Author the runbook per chapter 10 Surface 4:
    - Bridge convention (one bridge — CCO's — with the
      head joining, not a parallel bridge).
    - Roles at incident time (head substantive lead on AI
      classification; CCO delivery lead; GC legal review;
      CISO security-substantive review; DPO privacy review
      where personal data is in scope; CFO material-event
      review; named-executive spokesperson).
    - Sequencing discipline (internal cascade begins;
      regulator-notification obligations evaluated; public
      statement released; multi-audience statements
      synchronised).
    - Multi-audience workflow (regulator, customer,
      employee, investor, media, civil-society-stakeholder).
    - Post-incident narrative arc (initial disclosure,
      progressive updates, after-action summary, post-
      mortem publication where warranted).
    - Joint after-action review with the CCO
      (communications).
    The runbook composes with the incident-leadership
    discipline in [[mod-110-incident-leadership]].

11. **The failure-mode audit.** Author a short section — one
    paragraph per failure mode — naming how the interface
    set detects and recovers from each of the anti-patterns
    across chapters 08, 09, and 10.

## Acceptance criteria

- The hiring-tool bias-audit protocol names every AEDT /
  hiring tool in the enterprise's inventory, names the
  jurisdictions in scope, and satisfies each named
  jurisdictional obligation without weakening any other. A
  protocol that names LL 144 without the required
  publication mechanism, or that names HB 3773 without the
  required annual reporting, fails the criterion.
- The AI-policy taxonomy has every policy named with policy
  identifier, version, effective date, substantive author,
  accountable executives (content and lifecycle), cross-
  references, regulatory-obligation mapping, and ratifying
  body. A policy with any required field missing fails.
- The compliance-monitoring convention names test
  authorship (head's level-50 architect), execution
  (AIMS cadence), reporting (into the CCO's pipeline), and
  independent-challenge rights (CCO's sampling posture). A
  convention that positions the head as sole reporter
  without the challenge rights fails.
- The approved-language register has every phrasing cross-
  referenced to a substantive AIMS-artefact basis; the
  prohibited-language register has every categorical claim
  with the substantive substitution. A phrasing without
  basis, or a prohibition without substitution, fails.
- The crisis-communications runbook names the bridge
  convention (one bridge, the CCO's), the named roles at
  incident time, the sequencing discipline, and the multi-
  audience workflow. A runbook that stands up a parallel
  head-only bridge fails.
- Any factual claim about a specific jurisdiction's hiring-
  tool obligation, a specific EEOC guidance, a specific FTC
  guidance, or a specific SEC obligation carries a
  `<!-- needs-research: verify [specific fact] against
  [official source] -->` marker where not verifiable from
  cited primary sources.

## Stretch goals

- Extend the deliverable with the **jurisdictional-
  addendum** for a specific non-US-non-EU workforce
  jurisdiction the enterprise operates in — Canada (federal
  and provincial), Australia, Singapore, India, or Brazil
  — with the specific AI-in-HR obligations that apply and
  how the interface set adapts.
- Author the **rehearsed hiring-tool inquiry** — a hiring
  tool the enterprise uses is the subject of an EEOC inquiry
  or a private-plaintiff complaint. Walk the CHRO-head-GC
  coordination through the response.
- Draft the **policy-ratification cycle** — a specific
  policy in the taxonomy is up for annual review; walk the
  head-CCO (compliance) coordination through re-authoring,
  ratification, and communications-cascade to the workforce.
- Extend the crisis-communications runbook with a **specific
  scenario rehearsal** — an AI-in-HR tool produces a
  publicly visible bias finding; the enterprise is named in
  the story. Walk the runbook end-to-end with the CHRO in
  the room, the CCO (compliance) evaluating the workforce-
  policy dimension, and the CCO (communications) running
  the external voice.
- Author the **workforce AI-literacy roadmap** — the
  multi-year workforce AI-training strategy co-owned by
  the head and the CHRO, composed with the CCO (compliance)'s
  training curriculum, with quantitative targets per year.

## Notes and starter guidance

The most common first-cut failure on this exercise is the
hiring-tool bias-audit protocol that satisfies one
jurisdiction and leaves gaps in the others. LL 144's
requirements are specific and its penalties are specific;
HB 3773's requirements are specific and its penalties are
specific; EEOC guidance is federal and applies across US
jurisdictions. A protocol that satisfies LL 144 but is not
posture-ready for HB 3773's 2026 effective date will need
re-work under time pressure. Force the highest-common-
denominator posture — the protocol that satisfies each named
jurisdiction without weakening any other.

The second common failure is the AI-policy taxonomy that
duplicates enterprise-parent policies rather than composing
into them. The AI Data Governance Policy is not a replacement
for the enterprise Data Governance Policy; it is an
extension. The AI-in-HR Policy is not a replacement for the
enterprise Employee-Policy handbook; it is an extension.
Force the composition; every AI policy cross-references its
enterprise parent, and the parent references the AI
extension where AI-specific rules apply.

The third common failure is the approved-language register
that reads as marketing copy rather than as evidence-backed
phrasing. Every approved phrase has to trace back to a
specific AIMS artefact (a named evaluation, a named
control, a named AIA). A phrase without that trace is not
approved; it is aspirational. Force the trace even where the
phrasing is uncomfortable ("we test for bias on the
following benchmarks and here is what we found" is a hard
sentence to write and it is the one that survives an FTC
misleading-claims inquiry).

On the crisis-communications runbook — the temptation is to
write it as a set of PR talking points. Do not. It is a
runbook: named steps, named owners, named timing, named
outputs. When the incident is live at 2am, the runbook has
to be operable by the on-call person without a briefing
session. Write it for that reader.

The interface set is a governance artefact. Version it, date
it, refresh it annually, and distribute the current version
to the CHRO, CCO (compliance), CCO (communications), CAE,
GC, and audit-committee secretariat.
