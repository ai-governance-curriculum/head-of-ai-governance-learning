# CFO Coordination Pattern — Budget, ROI, and the Fine-and-Remediation Contingency Reserve

## Motivation

The Chief Financial Officer holds the enterprise's financial
discipline: the budget cycle, capital planning, management
accounting, financial reporting, treasury, and the finance
report line to the audit committee and the board. The head of
AI governance is a *cost centre* in that structure, unless the
head has built the ROI reporting shape (per
[[mod-107-program-measurement-and-maturity]] chapter 04) that
lets the function be read as an *investment centre* with
defensible avoided-loss, deal-enablement, and trust-margin
components. Either way, the CFO is the enterprise's arbiter of
whether the head's function is funded to the level the
program's ambition requires.

The failure mode is the head who arrives at the annual budget
conversation without discipline. Two variants. First, the head
who asks for an incremental budget increase without a defensible
ROI story and without a defensible cost-management track record;
the CFO reduces the ask on principle and the program's
trajectory (per [[mod-107-program-measurement-and-maturity]]
chapter 03 maturity assessment) misses its ratified targets.
Second, the head who under-asks because the head does not want
to appear grasping; the program runs on inadequate resources,
KPIs go red, and the audit committee reads the head as under-
resourced when the head could have made the case for adequate
resourcing at the outset.

The coordination pattern in this chapter is what avoids both.
It rests on a working finance-business-partner interface (the
CFO does not deal with the head directly on most operational
matters — a finance business partner does), a disciplined
budget cycle (candidate budget → CFO review → CFO ratification
→ head's execution against ratified budget), an ROI reporting
posture the CFO trusts (per
[[mod-107-program-measurement-and-maturity]] chapter 04), a
capital-planning discipline for multi-year investment (per
[[mod-113-budget-vendor-p-and-l-and-investment-case]]), and a
specific contingency-reserve discipline for regulator fines
and remediation programs.

## Core concept — the CFO buys signal, not activity

The load-bearing move is understanding what the CFO buys. The
CFO does not buy activity — "we ran twenty control tests this
quarter" is not what the CFO reads. The CFO buys *signal* —
signal that the enterprise's AI-governance program is
delivering, on defensible metrics, at a defensible cost, with a
defensible ROI, and with a clear line of sight to how the next
increment of investment produces the next increment of value.

Signal in the CFO's terms:

- **Cost per unit of governed AI.** The cost-per-AI-system-
  governed metric (KPI 10 in
  [[mod-107-program-measurement-and-maturity]] chapter 01) is
  the CFO's headline efficiency read. The head owns this
  metric; the CFO's finance business partner co-authors it.
- **ROI per component.** Avoided loss (fines avoided, incident
  costs avoided, defence costs avoided), deal enablement
  (trust-margin, regulator-approval-speed premium, tender-
  eligibility uplift), and trust-margin (customer retention,
  employee attraction). The head produces the pack; the
  finance business partner validates the modelling craft.
- **Cost variance to plan.** The head runs the seven cost
  categories (headcount, vendor spend, tooling depreciation,
  external-audit cost, training cost, certification cost,
  incident-response cost) at monthly variance against plan.
  A material variance without a defensible explanation is
  what the CFO reads as a discipline failure.
- **Contingency reserve utilisation.** For regulator fines
  and remediation-program costs, the reserve utilisation is
  what the CFO's treasury function tracks. Over-utilisation
  without warning is what the CFO reads as governance
  failure.
- **Multi-year investment case adherence.** Where the head
  has ratified a multi-year investment case (per
  [[mod-113-budget-vendor-p-and-l-and-investment-case]]),
  adherence to the case's milestones is what the CFO's
  capital-planning function reads.

The head produces all of the above; the finance business
partner co-authors, validates, and packages for the CFO's
consumption.

## The four coordination surfaces

### Surface 1 — the finance-business-partner interface

The CFO does not deal with the head directly on most
operational matters. The interface is a *finance business
partner* — a member of the CFO's finance function assigned to
the AI-governance program as their business-partnering
allocation. This is the head's working counterparty on the
CFO's side.

The interface specifies:

- **Named finance business partner.** A specific person, with
  a specific allocation percentage to the AI-governance
  program (typically 20–40% of the business partner's
  capacity for an enterprise-scope program; higher for
  frontier-lab or sector-heavy programs). The allocation is
  named in the finance business partner's role charter.
- **Monthly working cadence.** The head and the finance
  business partner meet monthly on operating cost variance,
  ROI backtest results, and forward-look on any material
  cost drivers. The meeting is a working session, not a
  status update.
- **Quarterly finance review preparation.** The head and the
  finance business partner co-author the quarterly finance
  review pack the CFO reads. The pack is not a monologue
  from either side; it is a joint artefact both sign.
- **Annual budget cycle joint work.** The head's candidate
  budget for the following year is authored jointly with the
  finance business partner, ahead of the CFO's budget review.
  The finance business partner brings the CFO's discipline
  (comparable ratios, cost benchmarks, treasury constraints)
  and the head brings the program's discipline (control-
  coverage plans, risk-portfolio movements, regulatory-change
  investment needs).
- **Escalation to CFO on material variance or investment
  decisions.** The finance business partner escalates to the
  CFO on material items; the head accompanies the
  escalation. Routine matters do not escalate.

Cadence: monthly operating, quarterly reporting, annual
budget. Joint owner: the head personally and the finance
business partner, with the CFO as the ultimate arbiter.

### Surface 2 — the annual budget cycle

The annual budget cycle for the AI-governance function has a
disciplined shape. The specifics vary by enterprise, but the
shape is common enough to codify.

**Timing.** The cycle runs on the enterprise's fiscal-year
calendar. For a January–December fiscal year, the cycle
typically opens in July with the CFO's guidance to functions
on assumptions and budget envelopes; runs through August–
October with candidate budget authoring; consolidates in
November with CFO review; ratifies in December with board
approval; executes from January.

**Head's authoring.** The head authors the candidate budget
against the seven cost categories (per
[[mod-107-program-measurement-and-maturity]] chapter 05), with:

- Base cost line per category, held from the prior year.
- Growth-plan investment per category (headcount increases
  per [[mod-108-leading-the-governance-function]], tooling
  upgrades, external audit or certification investments,
  training investments).
- One-time investments (major initiatives such as ISO/IEC
  42001 certification, an EU AI Act conformity-assessment
  cycle, a maturity-model-driven capability build).
- Reduction candidates (functions being sunsetted, tooling
  being consolidated).
- The multi-year investment case reference (per
  [[mod-113-budget-vendor-p-and-l-and-investment-case]]) that
  the current year's ask sits inside.
- The ROI-pack reference (per
  [[mod-107-program-measurement-and-maturity]] chapter 04)
  that supports the ask.
- The AI-portfolio scope forecast (per the AIMS inventory
  growth projection) that supports the per-unit cost
  discipline.

**CFO review.** The CFO reviews the candidate budget against
the enterprise's funding envelope, comparable functions'
budgets, and the multi-year investment case. The CFO returns
a ratified budget — typically with adjustments — that the
head executes against.

**Head's execution.** The head does not exceed the ratified
budget without CFO ratification of a re-forecast; the head
runs the monthly variance discipline; the head reports
variance to the finance business partner monthly and to the
CFO quarterly.

**Re-forecast.** Where material events require re-forecast —
a new regulatory requirement demanding investment mid-year, a
material AI incident requiring contingency reserve draw, a
material scope change — the head and the finance business
partner author a re-forecast for CFO ratification. Re-
forecasts are not routine; the discipline is that the
ratified budget holds unless the re-forecast case is
defensible.

Cadence: annual cycle plus monthly variance and quarterly
reporting. Joint owner: the head personally and the finance
business partner.

### Surface 3 — ROI reporting discipline

The ROI pack from
[[mod-107-program-measurement-and-maturity]] chapter 04 has
three components (avoided loss, deal enablement, trust-margin)
and a defensibility hierarchy (anchored, modelled,
illustrative). The CFO reads it at the frequency the CFO
requests, typically annually with a mid-year check-in.

The interface specifies:

- **Co-authorship with the finance business partner.** The
  ROI pack is co-authored, not head-authored-and-submitted.
  The finance business partner's role is modelling
  discipline; the head's role is substantive modelling
  inputs.
- **Defensibility labelling per component.** Every ROI figure
  carries its defensibility label (anchored / modelled /
  illustrative). The CFO reads the labels; the CFO does not
  treat illustrative figures as if they were anchored, and
  the head does not present them that way.
- **Annual backtest.** Every ROI model is backtested against
  the prior year's actuals. The backtest either validates
  the modelling assumptions or updates them. Modelling that
  does not backtest is modelling the CFO will stop trusting.
- **Explicit revision on backtest failure.** When a component
  misses on backtest (per
  [[mod-107-program-measurement-and-maturity]] chapter 06
  scenario B), the head presents the miss, the diagnosis,
  the model revision, and the revised projection together.
  The CFO reads the whole picture; the CFO does not read the
  revised projection alone.
- **No opportunism.** The head does not use a missed ROI
  target as an occasion to request budget increase. The
  budget conversation and the ROI conversation are separate
  and the head does not blur them.

Cadence: annual pack, mid-year check-in, incident-driven
supplementary if material. Joint owner: the head personally
on substance and defensibility labelling; the finance
business partner on modelling craft.

### Surface 4 — the fine-and-remediation contingency reserve

The AI-governance function is the enterprise's line of defence
against material regulatory fines and material remediation
program costs. The CFO's treasury and financial-reporting
functions carry a specific *contingency reserve* against these
events.

The reserve is not the head's budget; it is the enterprise's
treasury allocation. But the head's function is the
accountable executive for the underlying risk that the reserve
guards against, and the head coordinates with the CFO on the
reserve's sizing and utilisation.

The interface specifies:

- **Reserve sizing.** The reserve is sized against a
  defensible loss-model — expected regulator fines
  (probability-weighted against the enterprise's exposure to
  Article 99 EU AI Act penalties, sector-specific penalty
  regimes, and state-specific penalty regimes; frequency-
  informed by the enterprise's historical incident and
  regulator-action record; severity-informed by the specific
  regulatory penalty ceilings) plus expected remediation-
  program costs (consent-decree-driven remediation, class-
  action-settlement-driven remediation, regulator-mandated
  remediation programs).
- **The head's sizing input.** The head produces the loss
  model in coordination with GC (chapter
  [[06-general-counsel-coordination]]) on the legal-exposure
  inputs and with the CRO (chapter [[04-cro-coordination]])
  on the risk-appetite calibration. The finance business
  partner and the CFO's treasury lead determine the reserve
  quantum against the model.
- **The reserve-utilisation protocol.** When an event triggers
  reserve draw — a regulator settlement, a fine, a
  remediation program's cost profile — the head coordinates
  with GC (on the legal instrument), with the finance
  business partner (on the cost accounting), and with the
  CFO's treasury lead (on the reserve utilisation). The head
  does not draw against the reserve unilaterally.
- **Reserve-adequacy review.** Annually, the head and the
  finance business partner review reserve adequacy against
  the updated loss model. Where the reserve is inadequate
  against the model, the head presents the case for reserve
  increase; where the reserve is over-adequate, the head does
  not campaign to retain excess (the CFO's discipline
  reallocates excess).
- **Board reporting.** The reserve position (level, year-over-
  year change, utilisation history, adequacy against the
  loss model) is a standing item in the head's board pack
  (per [[mod-103-board-and-audit-committee-reporting]]) and
  in the CFO's finance report.

Cadence: reserve sizing at annual review; utilisation event-
driven with CFO ratification. Joint owner: the head, the CFO's
treasury lead, and GC.

## The interface document

Consolidated in a single interface document co-signed by the
head and the CFO, ratified in the CxO adjacency map. The
document has, at minimum:

1. **The signal-not-activity principle.** The CFO buys
   signal; the head produces signal on the CFO's terms.
2. **The four coordination surfaces.** Finance-business-
   partner interface (with the named partner and allocation),
   annual budget cycle, ROI reporting discipline, fine-and-
   remediation contingency reserve.
3. **The seven-category cost discipline.** Reference to the
   cost discipline from
   [[mod-107-program-measurement-and-maturity]] chapter 05.
4. **The multi-year investment case reference.** Reference to
   [[mod-113-budget-vendor-p-and-l-and-investment-case]] for
   the multi-year discipline the annual budget sits inside.
5. **The escalation ladder.** When the interface breaks — the
   CFO ratifies a budget that the head believes is materially
   inadequate to the program's ambition; the finance business
   partner disagrees materially on ROI modelling assumptions;
   the reserve utilisation is contested; the CFO does not
   approve a re-forecast case the head believes is defensible
   — how the head and the CFO escalate. Typically: peer
   conversation, then the CEO or the audit-committee chair
   (for material-adequacy disputes on function funding).
6. **The joint audit-committee appearance.** Once a year, the
   head and the CFO appear jointly at the audit committee on
   the AI-governance-program cost-and-ROI review — the seven
   cost categories, the ROI pack, the reserve position, the
   multi-year investment case adherence. Joint appearance is
   the discipline that keeps the interface honest.
7. **The review cadence.** Annual review of the interface
   document; ad-hoc on material enterprise financial events
   or CFO transitions.

## Anti-patterns the head does not permit

**The finance-business-partner bypass.** The head deals with
the CFO directly on operational matters the finance business
partner would handle, and the finance business partner is
side-lined. The finance business partner's function loses
context on the head's cost profile and the working
relationship deteriorates. The remedy is: the finance
business partner is the head's working counterparty on the
CFO's side; the CFO is engaged on material matters.

**The unbacked ROI figure.** The head presents an ROI figure
without the defensibility label and without the modelling
detail the finance business partner has validated. The CFO
challenges the figure at the audit committee; the head
cannot defend it; the ROI pack loses credibility for the
entire cycle. The remedy is: every ROI figure carries its
label and is co-authored with the finance business partner.

**The budget-opportunism after ROI miss.** The head uses a
missed ROI figure as an occasion to request budget increase.
The CFO reads this as opportunism; the head's future ROI
figures are read with more suspicion. The remedy is: the
budget and ROI conversations are separate; the head does not
blur them.

**The uncoordinated reserve draw.** The head draws against
the fine-and-remediation reserve for an event without
coordinating with GC on the legal characterisation, with the
finance business partner on the cost accounting, or with the
CFO's treasury on the reserve utilisation. The reserve
utilisation is misreported and treasury is surprised. The
remedy is: reserve utilisation follows the reserve-utilisation
protocol; no unilateral draw.

**The variance-explanation deferral.** A material cost
variance surfaces; the head defers the explanation to the
quarterly review, and the CFO reads about it for the first
time in the review. The CFO reads this as opacity. The
remedy is: material variance is escalated to the finance
business partner immediately (within the month it surfaces),
with the head's explanation and the corrective action.

## Summary

- The CFO buys signal, not activity. The head produces signal
  on the CFO's terms: cost per unit of governed AI, ROI per
  component with defensibility labelling, cost variance to
  plan on the seven categories, contingency reserve
  utilisation, multi-year investment case adherence.
- Four coordination surfaces carry the work: finance-
  business-partner interface (named partner with allocation,
  monthly cadence), annual budget cycle (candidate authored
  jointly, CFO ratifies, head executes against), ROI
  reporting discipline (co-authored, backtested, revised
  transparently on miss), fine-and-remediation contingency
  reserve (head sizes with GC and CRO inputs, CFO treasury
  quantifies and holds).
- The reserve is the enterprise's line-of-defence budget
  allocation against regulator fines and material
  remediation programs. The head coordinates on sizing and
  utilisation; the CFO's treasury holds the reserve; no
  unilateral draw.
- Five anti-patterns to refuse: finance-business-partner
  bypass, unbacked ROI figure, budget-opportunism after ROI
  miss, uncoordinated reserve draw, variance-explanation
  deferral.
- The interface document — co-signed by head and CFO — is
  ratified in the CxO adjacency map, reviewed annually, and
  the head and CFO appear jointly at the audit committee on
  the AI-governance program cost-and-ROI review once a year.
- Chapter [[08-chro-coordination]] turns to the CHRO
  adjacency — AI-in-HR governance under NYC Local Law 144,
  EEOC guidance, and Illinois HB 3773; performance-
  management AI governance; workforce AI-training strategy.
