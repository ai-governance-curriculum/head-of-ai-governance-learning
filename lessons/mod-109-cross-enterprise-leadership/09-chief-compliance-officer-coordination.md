# Chief Compliance Officer Coordination Pattern — Plugging the AI Policy Taxonomy Into the Enterprise Policy Library

## Motivation

The Chief Compliance Officer owns the enterprise compliance
program at parent scope — the compliance-monitoring function,
the enterprise policy library, the compliance report line to
the audit committee, the annual compliance testing plan, and
the compliance-training discipline. The head of AI governance
owns the *AI-specific* extension of the compliance program:
the AI regulatory-obligation register (EU AI Act, sector-
specific rules with AI extensions, voluntary codes), the AI-
policy taxonomy that plugs into the CCO's enterprise policy
library, and the AI-specific compliance-monitoring the head's
function runs on the AIMS operating cadence.

In many enterprises the head reports up through the CCO on the
compliance-adjacent dimension (with a matrixed reporting line
also to the CRO or the CAIO for the risk-and-strategy
dimension). Where the head reports through the CCO, the
coordination pattern in this chapter is also a reporting-line
pattern. Where the head does not report through the CCO, the
coordination pattern is a peer pattern between two officers
whose functions overlap on the AI-compliance surface.

The failure mode is a head who runs an AI-policy taxonomy that
does not compose with the CCO's enterprise policy library.
Two variants. First, the head who authors an AI-specific
policy suite (AI Policy, AI Acceptable Use, AI Third-Party
Standard, AI Incident-Response Policy) as standalone documents
outside the CCO's library; the CCO's compliance-monitoring
function does not test them; the compliance-training function
does not include them; the audit committee reads them as a
shadow policy set. Second, the head who defers all policy
authoring to the CCO's function, which then produces AI-generic
language that does not reflect the AI-specific obligations the
enterprise carries; the policies fail on the first regulator
examination.

The coordination pattern in this chapter is what avoids both.
It rests on a policy-composition discipline (the AI policy
taxonomy is a set of policies inside the CCO's enterprise
library, cross-referenced, jointly maintained), a compliance-
monitoring composition (the head's AI-specific compliance
monitoring feeds the CCO's enterprise compliance-monitoring
programme), and a reporting-line discipline where the head's
compliance-adjacent function reports up through the CCO to
the audit committee.

The pattern draws on the DOJ "Evaluation of Corporate
Compliance Programs" (September 2024 update), the FSGO
(Federal Sentencing Guidelines for Organizations) compliance-
program factors, ISO 37301:2021 (compliance management
systems), COSO Internal Control — Integrated Framework, the
IIA Three Lines Model on compliance as a second-line function,
and the enterprise-compliance-program guidance the CCO
typically follows in the enterprise's jurisdictions.

## Core concept — the AI policy taxonomy plugs into the enterprise policy library

The load-bearing move is the policy-composition discipline. The
head's AI-policy taxonomy is not a parallel policy set. It is a
set of policies that live *inside* the CCO's enterprise policy
library, under the CCO's document-control discipline, on the
CCO's review cadence, tested by the CCO's compliance-monitoring
function, and included in the CCO's compliance-training
curriculum.

The head is the substantive author of the AI-specific policies.
The CCO is the accountable executive for the library the
policies live in. The interface is the composition.

The AI-policy taxonomy — at a minimum, and adapted to the
enterprise — includes:

- **AI Policy (root).** The enterprise's overarching statement
  of AI-governance principles, aligned with ISO/IEC 42001
  clause 5.2 policy and the enterprise's board-endorsed AI
  ethics posture. This is the parent document from which the
  operational policies below derive.
- **AI Acceptable Use Policy.** What workforce use of AI is
  sanctioned, what is prohibited, what requires prior
  approval. Distinct from general acceptable-use / IT policy.
- **AI System Development and Deployment Policy.** The
  policy first-line teams follow when developing or deploying
  AI systems — AIA requirement, evaluation requirement,
  release-gate requirement, monitoring requirement.
- **AI Third-Party Standard.** The policy the third-party
  risk function follows for AI vendors — tiering, evidence
  depth, contract terms, monitoring cadence.
- **AI Incident Response Policy.** The policy the enterprise
  follows on AI incidents — classification, escalation,
  containment, disclosure. Composes with the enterprise
  incident-response policy.
- **AI Data Governance Policy.** The AI-specific extension of
  the enterprise data-governance policy — training-data
  provenance, evaluation-set curation, synthetic-data
  governance. Co-owned with the CDO (chapter
  [[02-cio-cto-cdo-coordination]]).
- **AI-in-HR Policy.** The AI-specific extension for HR uses
  of AI — hiring, performance management, employee
  monitoring. Co-owned with the CHRO (chapter
  [[08-chro-coordination]]).
- **AI Regulatory Engagement Policy.** How the enterprise
  engages regulators on AI matters. Co-owned with GC (chapter
  [[06-general-counsel-coordination]]).
- **AI Training and Literacy Policy.** The workforce AI-
  training strategy. Co-owned with the CHRO (chapter
  [[08-chro-coordination]]).
- **AI Governance Council Charter.** The charter of the AI
  Governance Council (per chapter
  [[11-chairing-the-governance-council]]). This is a governance
  document but it lives in the enterprise policy library so
  the CCO's audit-committee reporting picks it up.

The taxonomy is not a rigid template; enterprises adapt it to
their scope, sector, and jurisdictional footprint. But the
composition principle — every AI policy is in the CCO's
library — holds.

## The three coordination surfaces

### Surface 1 — the AI-policy taxonomy inside the enterprise policy library

The AI-policy taxonomy plugs into the enterprise library at the
policy-document-control layer. Each AI policy has:

- **The CCO's document-control metadata.** Policy identifier,
  version, effective date, next-review date, accountable
  executive, ratifying committee. The metadata format is the
  CCO's.
- **The head as substantive author.** The head's level-50
  architect drafts; the head's function is the accountable
  executive for the substantive content; the CCO's policy-
  management function is the accountable executive for the
  policy's presence in the library and its lifecycle
  discipline.
- **Cross-references.** The AI policies cross-reference the
  enterprise's parent policies (Information Security Policy
  under the CISO's ownership; Enterprise Risk Framework under
  the CRO's ownership; Privacy Policy under the DPO's
  ownership; Third-Party Risk Policy under the enterprise
  third-party function; and so on). The parent policies also
  reference the AI policies where AI-specific extensions
  apply — this is the discipline that keeps the taxonomy
  visible rather than orphaned.
- **Regulatory-obligation mapping.** Each AI policy maps to
  the specific regulatory obligations it operationalises (EU
  AI Act articles, sector-specific rules, voluntary-code
  commitments the enterprise has undertaken). The mapping is
  maintained by the head's level-50 architect against the
  regulatory-obligation register from
  [[mod-105-regulatory-change-management]].
- **Review cadence.** The CCO's library discipline sets the
  cadence (typically annual for policy documents, with
  supervisory-change-triggered reviews out of cycle). The
  head's function reviews at the CCO's cadence and additionally
  triggers a review when the regulatory-obligation register
  surfaces a material change.
- **Ratification.** Each AI policy is ratified by the body
  the CCO's policy discipline requires (typically the AI
  Governance Council for operational policies, the audit
  committee for the AI Policy root, and — where the CCO's
  discipline requires — the board for policies of highest
  material significance).

Cadence: annual review at the CCO's library cycle; ad-hoc on
regulatory change or material incident. Joint owner: the
head's level-50 architect on substance; the CCO's policy-
management lead on lifecycle.

### Surface 2 — AI-specific compliance monitoring

The CCO's compliance-monitoring function tests the enterprise's
compliance with the policies in the library. Where AI policies
require AI-specific testing depth, the head's function runs the
testing and the results feed the CCO's compliance-monitoring
programme.

The interface specifies:

- **AI-specific test authorship.** For AI policies, the tests
  are authored by the head's level-50 architect — because
  they require AI-specific technical depth (testing whether
  a model registry entry has the required metadata, whether
  an AIA is on file at the required tier depth, whether a
  bias audit was conducted at the required cadence, whether a
  human-in-the-loop discipline is enforced at the release
  gate). The CCO's compliance-monitoring function reviews
  the tests for compliance-methodology soundness.
- **AI-specific test execution.** The tests are executed on
  the head's function's cadence — typically the AIMS
  operating cadence from [[mod-102-running-the-program]]
  chapter 02 — with results feeding the KRI/KPI portfolio
  (specifically control-testing pass rate, KRI 1 in
  [[mod-107-program-measurement-and-maturity]] chapter 01).
- **Reporting composition.** AI-specific test results
  compose into the CCO's compliance-monitoring report to
  the audit committee. The CCO does not re-run the AI
  tests; the CCO's function accepts the head's function's
  test results, under the CAE's assurance discipline
  (chapter [[05-cae-coordination]]).
- **Independent challenge.** Because the head's function is
  authoring, executing, and reporting the same tests, the
  CCO's compliance-monitoring function retains challenge
  rights — the CCO's team can independently sample and
  re-test any AI-specific control. This is the safeguard
  against self-reporting bias.

Cadence: AI-specific tests on the AIMS operating cadence;
CCO's challenge sampling on the CCO's cadence; feed to
audit-committee reporting on the CCO's quarterly cycle.
Joint owner: the head's level-50 architect and the CCO's
compliance-monitoring lead.

### Surface 3 — the reporting-line discipline

Where the head reports up through the CCO on the compliance-
adjacent dimension, the reporting-line discipline governs how
the head's function's material events surface at the audit
committee via the CCO's report line.

The interface specifies:

- **Solid-line or matrixed.** Whether the head reports solid-
  line to the CCO, matrixed to the CCO (with a solid-line to
  CRO, CAIO, or CEO), or peer-to-CCO with a separate
  reporting line. The reporting-line specifics are named in
  the interface document and in the head's engagement
  contract with the enterprise (per
  [[mod-101-role-scope-and-leadership-shape]] chapter 07).
- **Material-event escalation.** The head's function's
  material events — material AI incident, material control
  failure, material regulatory correspondence — surface
  through the CCO's report line to the audit committee.
  Timing and format follow the CCO's escalation discipline.
- **Independent audit-committee access preserved.** Regardless
  of the reporting line, the head retains independent audit-
  committee access on second-line-material events —
  independence-impairment attempts, retaliation concerns,
  material dissent on compliance matters the CCO does not
  escalate. This is the invariant from
  [[mod-101-role-scope-and-leadership-shape]] chapter 07
  engagement-contract discipline.
- **Compliance-training composition.** The CCO's compliance-
  training curriculum includes the AI-specific training
  content (per the training-and-literacy policy). The head's
  function provides the substantive content; the CCO's
  training function delivers it.
- **Annual compliance-programme review.** The CCO's annual
  compliance-programme review (delivered to the audit
  committee) includes the AI-governance-programme section,
  authored jointly by the head and the CCO's compliance-
  programme lead.

Cadence: continuous escalation; annual programme review;
periodic reporting-line agreement review. Joint owner: the
head and the CCO, with the audit-committee chair as
arbitrator on any reporting-line disagreement.

## The interface document

Consolidated in a single interface document co-signed by the
head and the CCO, ratified in the CxO adjacency map. The
document has, at minimum:

1. **The policy-composition principle.** The AI-policy
   taxonomy is a set of policies inside the CCO's enterprise
   policy library; the head is substantive author, the CCO
   is accountable for the library's lifecycle.
2. **The three coordination surfaces.** AI-policy taxonomy
   composition, AI-specific compliance monitoring, reporting-
   line discipline — each with the artefacts, the cadence,
   and the joint owners.
3. **The named AI-policy set.** The specific list of AI
   policies in the enterprise library, with the substantive
   author and the ratifying body per policy.
4. **The compliance-monitoring composition.** How AI-specific
   test authorship, execution, reporting, and independent
   challenge run.
5. **The reporting-line specifics.** Whether solid-line,
   matrixed, or peer; the material-event escalation
   procedure; the independent audit-committee access
   invariant.
6. **The escalation ladder.** When the interface breaks — an
   AI policy is authored by the head but not included in the
   CCO's library on time; compliance-monitoring results are
   contested; reporting-line escalation is disputed — how
   the head and the CCO escalate. Typically: peer
   conversation, then the audit-committee chair.
7. **The joint audit-committee appearance.** Once a year,
   the head and the CCO appear jointly at the audit
   committee on the AI-compliance-programme review. Where
   the head reports through the CCO, the CCO's annual
   report includes an AI-governance-programme section the
   head co-authors.
8. **The review cadence.** Annual review of the interface
   document; ad-hoc on material regulatory changes or CCO
   transitions.

## Anti-patterns the head does not permit

**The shadow policy set.** The head's function maintains AI
policies in the head's own document store, outside the CCO's
library. The CCO's compliance-monitoring does not test them,
the compliance-training does not include them, and the audit
committee reads them as an unratified set. The remedy is:
every AI policy is in the CCO's library on the CCO's
lifecycle.

**The generic-language failure.** The head defers policy
authoring to the CCO's policy-authoring team, which produces
AI-generic language ("AI systems shall be governed
appropriately"). The policies fail on the first regulator
examination. The remedy is: the head's level-50 architect
authors the substantive content; the CCO's policy-management
function shepherds the lifecycle.

**The self-reported test-result overreach.** The head's
function reports control-test results to the CCO without
disclosing the independent-challenge sampling posture. The
CCO's function reports upward assuming the results are
independently validated when they are not. The remedy is:
the independent-challenge sampling is a standing discipline;
the interface makes the CCO's challenge rights explicit;
sampling is transparently reported.

**The reporting-line bypass.** The head, reporting through
the CCO, escalates to the audit-committee chair on issues
the CCO would have engaged constructively on. The remedy is:
the audit-committee-access invariant exists for genuinely
second-line-material events, not as a routine escalation
lever. Use of the invariant when the CCO would have engaged
damages the working trust the reporting line requires.

**The compliance-monitoring parallel-tracking.** The head's
function maintains its own compliance-monitoring dashboard
that reports different results from the CCO's dashboard.
The audit committee reads inconsistent numbers. The remedy
is: one set of AI-specific compliance-test results, run by
the head's function, reported through the CCO's
compliance-monitoring pipeline, cited consistently.

## Summary

- The AI-policy taxonomy plugs into the CCO's enterprise
  policy library. The head is substantive author; the CCO is
  accountable executive for the library's lifecycle.
- Three coordination surfaces carry the work: AI-policy
  taxonomy composition (the named policy set, cross-
  references, regulatory-obligation mapping, ratification),
  AI-specific compliance monitoring (test authorship,
  execution on AIMS cadence, composition into CCO reporting,
  independent challenge), reporting-line discipline (solid-
  line / matrixed / peer, material-event escalation,
  preserved audit-committee access, compliance-training
  composition).
- The AI-policy taxonomy — at a minimum, adapted to the
  enterprise — includes AI Policy (root), AI Acceptable Use,
  AI System Development and Deployment, AI Third-Party
  Standard, AI Incident Response, AI Data Governance, AI-
  in-HR, AI Regulatory Engagement, AI Training and Literacy,
  and the AI Governance Council Charter.
- Five anti-patterns to refuse: shadow policy set, generic-
  language failure, self-reported test-result overreach,
  reporting-line bypass, compliance-monitoring parallel-
  tracking.
- The interface document — co-signed by head and CCO — is
  ratified in the CxO adjacency map, reviewed annually, and
  the head and CCO appear jointly at the audit committee on
  the AI-compliance-programme review once a year.
- Chapter [[10-chief-communications-officer-coordination]]
  turns to the Chief Communications Officer adjacency —
  public trust posture, media strategy, and the crisis-
  communications runbook for AI-incident public disclosure.
