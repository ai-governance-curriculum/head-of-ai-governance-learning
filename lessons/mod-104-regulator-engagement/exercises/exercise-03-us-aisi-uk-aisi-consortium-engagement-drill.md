# exercise-03: US AISI / UK AISI / Consortium Engagement Drill

**Estimated effort:** 2 hours

## Objective

Design the enterprise's engagement stance across the AI Safety
Institute network — at minimum US AISI (and AISIC), UK AISI (and
Inspect), plus at least one non-US / non-UK counterpart the
enterprise should engage — and produce two artefacts the head
would put in front of an audit-committee chair:

- a one-page **annual engagement calendar** covering every AISI-
  family body the enterprise tracks, and
- a short **position paper** explaining why the enterprise engages
  each body.

The deliverable is not a reading list of AISIs and not a wish list
of convenings the head would attend if travel budget were
unlimited. It is a designed portfolio of stances — engage /
observe / co-sign via industry body / decline — each of which the
head can defend as a strategic choice.

## Prerequisites

- Chapter [03-ai-safety-institute-engagement](../03-ai-safety-institute-engagement.md)
  — Engaging the AI Safety Institute Network — read and
  internalised, especially the four engagement modes and the
  Bletchley / Seoul / San Francisco coordinating pattern.
- [[mod-102-running-the-program]] in view for the running-program
  evaluation discipline that consumes AISI outputs (Inspect and
  equivalents) into the enterprise's own pre-deployment testing.
- [[mod-106-external-positioning-and-voluntary-codes]] in view for
  the voluntary-code engagement pattern, which sits alongside AISI
  engagement and often overlaps at the working-group level.
- Chapter [06-public-consultation-submission-flow](../06-public-consultation-submission-flow.md)
  in view for the written-submission side of the discipline that
  the stretch goal exercises.
- A concrete enterprise in mind, with an assumed sector, an
  assumed jurisdictional footprint (which will drive which non-US
  / non-UK AISI-family bodies matter), and an assumed AI portfolio
  scale.

## Steps

1. **Portfolio scan.** List every AISI-family body the enterprise
   should track. At minimum: US AISI, AISIC, UK AISI, Canadian
   AISI (CAISI), Singapore's AI Verify Foundation and Singapore's
   AISI-equivalent, the EU AI Office scientific panel, and any
   EU Member-State AI-safety technical bodies the enterprise's
   footprint touches. Where the current organisational status,
   name, or mandate of a body is unclear (US AISI post-EO 14179,
   UK AISI post-rename, Singapore's AISI-equivalent, EU Member
   State bodies), mark the entry `needs-research` and record what
   would have to be verified. Do not fabricate current status.
2. **Stance decision per body.** For each body on the list,
   record one of four stances: **engage as member**, **observe
   only**, **co-sign via industry body**, or **decline**. Justify
   each stance in one paragraph that names the enterprise's
   reason — reputational signalling, technical-access value,
   voluntary-standards-shaping input, jurisdictional footprint,
   or explicit non-engagement rationale. "Engage with" without a
   named mode is not a stance; the drill is to force the choice.
3. **Engagement calendar.** Build the annual rhythm as a one-page
   calendar. Every entry has: the body, the specific convening,
   submission window, or consultation cycle; the named attendee
   (role, not person — the head, the level-50 architect, a
   nominated engineering lead, an industry-body delegate); and
   the specific deliverable (attend, submit written position,
   observe and readout, co-sign an industry-body response). Tie
   each calendar entry back to the stance recorded in step 2.
4. **Inspect and AISI-published evaluation adoption.** For UK
   AISI's Inspect framework and any other AISI-published
   evaluation output the portfolio scan surfaces, record whether
   the enterprise **adopts** the output into its own running-
   program evaluation discipline, **observes** it (tracks
   releases, does not run), or **declines** it (with a named
   reason). Adoption decisions cross-reference the running-
   program eval discipline in [[mod-102-running-the-program]].
5. **Rationale one-pager.** Write the position paper — one page,
   audit-committee-chair register — on why the enterprise engages
   the AISI network at all. The rationale distinguishes three
   motivations explicitly: **reputational** (the signalling value
   of being a named participant in the network), **technical-
   access** (methods, tooling, and evaluation science the
   enterprise consumes), and **voluntary-standards-shaping** (the
   enterprise's input into methodologies that later become the
   reference shape for regulators and auditors). The one-pager is
   defensible as a strategic choice, not as compliance-driven
   necessity — AISI engagement is voluntary and the head owns the
   argument for why the enterprise pays the staff-time cost.
6. **Separation discipline check.** Read the calendar and stance
   set back against the AISI-versus-enforcement separation from
   chapter 03. Flag any calendar entry where the attendee or the
   deliverable risks blurring an AISI technical conversation into
   a legally consequential regulator interaction. Correct the
   entry (change attendee, change deliverable, add a legal-
   privilege note) before finalising.

## Deliverables

- `portfolio-scan.md` — the enumerated list of AISI-family
  bodies, with `needs-research` markers where current status is
  unclear and a note on what would have to be verified.
- `stance-decisions.md` — one paragraph per body naming the
  stance (engage as member / observe only / co-sign via industry
  body / decline) and the justification.
- `engagement-calendar.md` (or `.pdf`) — the one-page annual
  calendar with body, convening, attendee role, and deliverable
  per entry.
- `inspect-and-eval-adoption.md` — the adoption / observe /
  decline decision for Inspect and other AISI-published
  evaluation outputs, cross-referenced to the running-program
  eval discipline.
- `rationale-one-pager.md` — the audit-committee-chair-register
  position paper on why the enterprise engages the AISI network,
  distinguishing reputational, technical-access, and voluntary-
  standards-shaping motivations.

## Acceptance criteria

- Every named AISI-family body on the portfolio scan has a
  specific stance from the four-mode set — not "engage with,"
  not "TBD," not "monitor" without a named mode.
- Every engagement on the calendar has a named attendee (a
  role, not a placeholder like "TBD" or "someone senior") and
  a specific deliverable (attend, submit written position,
  observe and readout, co-sign via industry body). Deliverables
  are named at the level a chief of staff could scan the
  calendar and staff each entry without further conversation.
- The Inspect / eval-adoption artefact makes an explicit
  adopt / observe / decline call for each AISI-published output
  in scope and names the cross-reference into the running-
  program eval discipline for anything adopted.
- The rationale one-pager reads at head-of-role register and is
  defensible to an audit-committee chair as a strategic choice
  — not as a compliance-driven necessity. The three motivations
  (reputational, technical-access, voluntary-standards-shaping)
  are named separately and weighted for the enterprise's
  specific sector and jurisdictional footprint.
- `needs-research` markers appear anywhere the current
  organisational status of a body is uncertain (US AISI post-EO
  14179, UK AISI's current sponsoring department and name,
  CAISI's mandate, Singapore's AISI-equivalent, EU Member State
  bodies) rather than fabricated current status.

## Stretch goals

- Draft a written submission to one specific AISI or AISI-
  network consultation the head has identified as a live
  engagement window. Follow the written-submission discipline
  from chapter [06-public-consultation-submission-flow](../06-public-consultation-submission-flow.md).
- Peer review the calendar and rationale one-pager with a
  colleague at industry-body or head-of-role level and
  incorporate one round of critique before finalising.
- Extend the engagement calendar into a two-year view showing
  the Bletchley-line summit cadence, the Seoul-line
  cross-recognition convenings, and the AISIC working-group
  rhythm together in one artefact, so that the head can see the
  attendance load across a full election-cycle horizon rather
  than a single annual snapshot.

## Notes and starter guidance

- The most common failure of a first-cut AISI engagement plan is
  that it lists bodies without stances — every AISI is "engaged
  with," which in practice means none of them is. The drill is
  to force the choice; observe-only and decline are legitimate
  stances if the justification names why.
- The second most common failure is under-weighting non-US /
  non-UK bodies. If the enterprise has an EU footprint, an EU
  Member State body or the EU AI Office scientific panel almost
  certainly belongs on the calendar; if the enterprise sells into
  Asia-Pacific, Singapore's AI Verify Foundation likely does.
  The portfolio scan is a jurisdictional-footprint exercise, not
  a generic AISI reading list.
- The rationale one-pager is where the drill lands or fails. If
  the paper reads as "we engage because everyone engages," the
  head has not done the work. Force the three motivations
  (reputational, technical-access, voluntary-standards-shaping)
  onto separate paragraphs so the trade-offs are visible.
- Keep the calendar to one page. A calendar that spans four
  pages is a calendar that no head reviews before the next cycle.
- Do not treat any AISI convening as equivalent to an NCA
  interaction. Chapter 03's separation discipline is the guardrail
  — read the calendar back through it before finalising, and
  correct any entry where a technical peer conversation risks
  being staffed as if it were a regulator engagement.
