# mod-104 — Direct Regulator Engagement

**Estimated effort:** 20 hours
**Position on the track:** fourth module — the external-engagement
module that carries the running program built in
[[mod-102-running-the-program]] and the reporting posture built in
[[mod-103-board-and-audit-committee-reporting]] out to the
regulators the enterprise actually answers to.

## What this module is for

The head of AI governance does not draft legal opinions; General
Counsel does. But the head is the accountable executive the
regulator hears from. Board members, audit-committee chairs, and
CEOs all read the head's regulator posture the same way: does the
enterprise engage the regulator as a cooperative counterparty, or
does it become a problem file the first time the regulator picks up
the phone.

This module is how the head builds the cooperative-counterparty
posture on purpose, across seven engagement surfaces:

1. The EU AI Act Chapter VII architecture — EU AI Office, European
   AI Board, national competent authorities, notified bodies — read
   as an engagement map rather than as legal text.
2. The Article 72 post-market monitoring cadence and the Article 73
   serious-incident reporting workflow at leadership scope — the
   filings the head personally signs, briefs the board on, and
   defends to the NCA.
3. The AI Safety Institute network — US AISI and AISIC, UK AISI and
   Inspect, CAISI, Singapore's AI Verify Foundation, EU Member
   State bodies — engaged as a voluntary technical peer relationship,
   never as an enforcement counterpart.
4. The sector-regulator engagement pattern — banking (Federal
   Reserve, OCC, FDIC, OSFI, PRA, ECB/EBA), insurance (NAIC and
   state DOIs, NYDFS), health (FDA, MHRA, EMA, Health Canada),
   employment (EEOC, DOL, NYC Local Law 144), consumer (FTC, CFPB),
   health privacy (HHS OCR), US state privacy and AI (CPPA,
   Colorado AG, Texas AG, other state AGs), UK data protection
   (ICO), and cyber (CISA, NCSC) — with the peer-fronting
   discipline that names, per regulator, who at the enterprise
   fronts the routine channel and when the head steps forward.
5. The regulator-examination runbook — six phases (pre-exam
   preparation, entrance conference, evidence-request handling,
   findings conference, remediation planning, post-exam
   instrument management) — that the head runs against a pre-
   designed shape.
6. The public-consultation submission flow — NIST AI RMF, US AISI
   evaluation methodologies, CEN-CENELEC JTC 21 via mirror
   committees, ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 42, CPPA ADMT rulemaking, FDA
   dockets, ICO consultations, state AG rulemakings, EU AI Office
   guidance drafts, EU Have Your Say — with the internal drafting
   cycle that turns a request-for-comment into a signed,
   defensible, on-time submission.
7. The head-and-General-Counsel boundary — the four-quadrant
   classification (Q1 technical no-exposure, Q2 technical with
   legal exposure, Q3 legal on technical facts, Q4 litigation
   posture), the packaging discipline, the technical-position
   discipline, and the disagreement-resolution protocol that
   keep head and GC from giving the regulator a split answer.

This module answers each of the seven, produces the operating
artefacts the head runs the engagements with, and feeds cluster 4
(regulator engagement) of the quarterly board pack in
[[mod-103-board-and-audit-committee-reporting]].

## Learning objectives (from the plan)

- Read EU AI Act Chapter VII (Articles 64–70 — EU AI Office, EU AI
  Board, national competent authorities) as the leadership-scope
  engagement map for EU-facing enterprises.
- Own the EU AI Act Article 72 post-market monitoring filing
  cadence and Article 73 serious-incident reporting workflow at
  leadership scope — the `senior-ai-governance-architect` designed
  the workflow; the head owns the filing.
- Design the engagement pattern with the US AI Safety Institute (US
  AISI) and its consortium (AISIC), the UK AISI (with Inspect), and
  equivalents in Canada / Singapore / EU Member States.
- Design the engagement pattern with sector regulators — Federal
  Reserve + OCC + FDIC (banking AI/ML under SR 11-7 + OCC 2011-12 +
  SR 23-4), OSFI (Canadian banks under E-23), NAIC and state DOIs
  (insurance under the AI Model Bulletin), FDA + MHRA + EMA +
  Health Canada (medical devices and pharma under GMLP + PCCP),
  EEOC + DOL (employment), FTC + CFPB (consumer), HHS OCR (HIPAA),
  CPPA (California ADMT), Colorado AG (Colorado AI Act), state AGs
  (state AI acts), UK ICO (data-protection interface for AI), CISA
  + NCSC (Secure AI System Development Guidelines).
- Design the regulator-exam runbook at leadership scope — pre-exam
  preparation, entrance conference, evidence-request handling,
  findings-conference posture, remediation planning, MOU / MRA
  management — with the sequenced hand-off to General Counsel and
  Government Affairs where legal opinion is required.
- Own the enterprise's public-consultation submission process —
  NIST AI RMF public comment, CEN-CENELEC JTC 21 harmonised
  standards, CPPA rulemaking, FDA docket submissions, EU AI Office
  GPAI Code work — with the internal drafting cycle across product
  / engineering / legal.
- Distinguish 'engaging a regulator' (this role's scope) from
  'delivering a legal opinion' (General Counsel's scope) — the head
  packages regulator obligations and takes the technical position;
  GC owns interpretation and litigation posture.

## How to work through this module

The module is designed to be worked linearly. Chapters 01 and 02
land the EU AI Act engagement architecture and the two filings the
head personally signs. Chapter 03 covers the AI Safety Institute
network as the voluntary-technical peer surface that sits next to
regulator engagement without being regulator engagement. Chapter
04 opens the sector-regulator surface — where most of the head's
regulator hours actually go — and hands depth on any single sector
to [[mod-111-sector-program-leadership]]. Chapter 05 is the exam
runbook that the artefacts from chapters 02 and 04 flow through
when a regulator arrives on-site. Chapter 06 is the written-
submission discipline that shapes rules before they bind. Chapter
07 closes the module on the head-and-GC boundary that every prior
chapter has quietly assumed works.

Exercises follow the same order. Exercise 1 builds the engagement
map that exercises 2, 4, and 5 refer back to. Exercise 3 stands
alone as an AISI-portfolio design and can be worked in parallel.
Exercise 4 (sector-regulator inventory) is a prerequisite for
exercise 5 (exam runbook), which selects one regulator from the
inventory and runs the six-phase runbook against it. Exercise 6
(consultation flow) is independent and can be worked in parallel
with the others. Exercise 7 (head-and-GC boundary drill) is the
capstone — it exercises the boundary against the interactions the
other six exercises produce.

By the end of the module, a working head-of-role has produced:

- A living EU AI Act engagement map covering Union bodies,
  Member-State NCAs, and notified bodies, with GC hand-off drawn
  on every rung of the escalation ladder.
- An executable Article 73 serious-incident filing runbook with
  three-timeline discipline, seven-question filing defence, named
  sign-off routing, and a four-stage NCA engagement plan.
- A designed AISI portfolio — engage-as-member / observe / co-sign
  / decline stance per body, an annual engagement calendar, and an
  audit-committee-ratifiable rationale one-pager.
- A sector-regulator inventory covering the enterprise's full
  footprint, with obligation-surface citations, peer-fronting
  decisions, cadence, and pre-decided escalation stance per
  regulator, directly droppable into cluster 4 of the board pack.
- A six-phase regulator-examination runbook against a specific
  named regulator, with GC and Government Affairs hand-offs
  annotated on every phase.
- A public-consultation submission flow with a portfolio of at
  least five surfaces, a locked internal drafting cycle, a lens
  discipline, an archive standard for four cold readers, and a
  worked draft against a real docket.
- A head-and-GC joint operating agreement — four-quadrant
  convention, packaging discipline, technical-position discipline,
  disagreement-resolution protocol — signed by head, GC, and the
  audit-committee chair, with adjacent-boundary annexes for
  Government Affairs, Communications, Investor Relations, and
  CISO.

These artefacts extend the running program from
[[mod-102-running-the-program]] outward to every regulator the
enterprise faces, and feed the regulator-engagement cluster of the
board pack in [[mod-103-board-and-audit-committee-reporting]]
directly. The regulatory-change discipline in
[[mod-105-regulatory-change-management]] keeps them current;
[[mod-106-external-positioning-and-voluntary-codes]] extends the
consultation flow into voluntary-code engagement; and
[[mod-110-incident-leadership]] carries the incident-leadership
discipline that Article 73 filings ride on top of.
