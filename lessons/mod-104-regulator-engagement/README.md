# mod-104-regulator-engagement: Direct Regulator Engagement

> Scaffolded by `aicg org execute-plan`. Lecture chapters and exercise content are authored on subsequent autonomous cycles.

**Estimated effort:** 20 hours

## Learning objectives

- Read EU AI Act Chapter VII (Articles 64-70 — EU AI Office, EU AI Board, national competent authorities) as the leadership-scope engagement map for EU-facing enterprises
- Own the EU AI Act Article 72 post-market monitoring filing cadence and Article 73 serious-incident reporting workflow at leadership scope — the senior-ai-governance-architect designed the workflow; the head owns the filing
- Design the engagement pattern with the US AI Safety Institute (US AISI) and its consortium (AISIC), the UK AISI (with Inspect), and equivalents in Canada / Singapore / EU Member States
- Design the engagement pattern with sector regulators — Federal Reserve + OCC + FDIC (banking AI/ML under SR 11-7 + OCC 2011-12 + SR 23-4), OSFI (Canadian banks under E-23), NAIC and state DOIs (insurance under the AI Model Bulletin), FDA + MHRA + EMA + Health Canada (medical devices and pharma under GMLP + PCCP), EEOC + DOL (employment), FTC + CFPB (consumer), HHS OCR (HIPAA), CPPA (California ADMT), Colorado AG (Colorado AI Act), state AGs (state AI acts), UK ICO (data-protection interface for AI), CISA + NCSC (Secure AI System Development Guidelines)
- Design the regulator-exam runbook at leadership scope — pre-exam preparation, entrance conference, evidence-request handling, findings-conference posture, remediation planning, MOU / MRA management — with the sequenced hand-off to General Counsel and Government Affairs where legal opinion is required
- Own the enterprise's public-consultation submission process — NIST AI RMF public comment, CEN-CENELEC JTC 21 harmonised standards, CPPA rulemaking, FDA docket submissions, EU AI Office GPAI Code work — with the internal drafting cycle across product / engineering / legal
- Distinguish 'engaging a regulator' (this role's scope) from 'delivering a legal opinion' (General Counsel's scope) — the head packages regulator obligations and takes the technical position; GC owns interpretation and litigation posture
