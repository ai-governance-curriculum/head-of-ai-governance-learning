# Reading EU AI Act Chapter VII as the Engagement Map

## Motivation

The head of AI governance does not read Chapter VII of Regulation
(EU) 2024/1689 the way General Counsel reads it. GC reads Chapter
VII as legal text — powers conferred, procedures prescribed,
remedies available. The head reads Chapter VII as an *engagement
map*: which bodies exist, what each one can do to the enterprise or
for the enterprise, who at the enterprise phones which body under
what circumstance, and where the boundary sits between the head's
executive engagement and GC's legal engagement.

If the head cannot draw that map on a whiteboard from memory, the
first serious information request from a national competent
authority becomes an internal scramble to work out who owns the
response. That scramble is visible to the regulator. Regulators
form durable impressions from first contact, and those impressions
determine whether the enterprise is treated as a cooperative
counterparty or as a problem file. The head owns not being a
problem file.

The EU AI Act entered into force on 2 August 2024 with a phased
application schedule running through 2026 and 2027. The governance
architecture in Chapter VII (Articles 64 to 70 of the consolidated
text) is the durable structure the head engages with for the life
of the regulation. Reading it as a map — not as text — is the
first move in this module. Chapter 02 turns to the specific
Article 72 and Article 73 filing regime; chapter 06 turns to the
GPAI Code of Practice work with the EU AI Office; chapter 07 turns
to the GC hand-off discipline. This chapter puts the bodies on the
board.

## Core concept: three layers, three engagement postures

The EU AI Act governance architecture has three layers, and the
head engages with each layer with a different posture. Confusing
the postures is how enterprises annoy regulators.

The **Union layer** sits inside the European Commission and around
it — the EU AI Office, the European Artificial Intelligence Board,
the Advisory Forum, the Scientific Panel of independent experts.
This layer sets Union-wide direction, drafts implementing and
delegated acts, coordinates enforcement, and produces the codes of
practice and guidance that shape what the whole market does. The
head's engagement here is *strategic and largely mediated* — via
industry bodies, formal consultations, and (for enterprises that
provide or deploy general-purpose AI models) the GPAI Code of
Practice process. The head is rarely on a call with the AI Office
alone; the head is on a call as part of a delegation.

The **Member-State layer** is where the enterprise actually gets
supervised. Each Member State designates one or more national
competent authorities, which include the market surveillance
authority for the AI Act and the notifying authority responsible
for notified bodies. Article 70 of the regulation is the head's
anchor citation for the NCA architecture. The head's engagement
here is *direct and executive* — the head, or a named delegate,
is the enterprise's accountable executive for correspondence with
the NCA about the enterprise's own systems and obligations. When
GC needs to be in the loop (and GC is often in the loop) the head
is still the one whose name is on the letter.

The **conformity-assessment layer** — notified bodies designated
under the Chapter III / Article 28-and-following regime — is a
supplier layer, not a regulator layer. Notified bodies conduct
third-party conformity assessment for those high-risk systems that
require it (a subset of Annex III systems, plus Annex I product
regulation cases). The head engages notified bodies the way the
enterprise engages any accredited assessor — through the
contracting, scoping, and evidence-provision disciplines that
sit under the CISO peer for security certification and under the
head for AI conformity. Confusing a notified body with a regulator
is a category error the head does not make.

The rest of this chapter walks each layer, names the specific
bodies, and lands the engagement stance for each.

## The Union layer — Commission-anchored bodies

### The EU AI Office

The EU AI Office was established within the European Commission
(DG CNECT) in early 2024 to serve as the Union-level centre of
expertise on AI, with particular responsibility for general-purpose
AI model supervision and for coordinating implementation of the
Act. Chapter VII of the regulation formalises its role.

Practically, the AI Office does four things the head cares about:
it supervises the general-purpose AI model provisions of the Act
(Chapter V); it convenes and secretariats the codes of practice for
GPAI providers; it prepares Commission implementing and delegated
acts; and it publishes guidance and templates that shape how NCAs
enforce and how deployers document. Its landing page on
digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu is the enterprise-facing entry
point for who to contact, what consultations are open, and which
documents have been published.

The head's engagement stance with the AI Office is strategic. Direct
bilateral engagement with the AI Office happens rarely for a
non-frontier enterprise and, when it does, it happens through
industry associations (the enterprise's sector body, the
cross-sector employer bodies such as BusinessEurope or DIGITALEUROPE,
or specialist bodies such as the Confederation of European Business).
The two exceptions are: participation in the GPAI Code of Practice
work if the enterprise itself provides a general-purpose AI model,
which chapter 06 covers; and responses to formal Commission
consultations, which the head coordinates with GC and Government
Affairs. Cold-calling the AI Office because a customer has asked a
hard question about high-risk classification is not a move the head
makes.

### The European Artificial Intelligence Board

Article 65 establishes the European Artificial Intelligence Board.
The Board is composed of one representative per Member State,
supported by the European Data Protection Supervisor as an observer
and with the AI Office providing secretariat functions. Its role is
to coordinate the NCAs across Member States, share supervisory
practice, contribute to Commission guidance, and generally keep
supervision consistent across the internal market.

The head does not engage the Board directly. The Board is a
regulator-to-regulator forum. The head's engagement with the Board
is *indirect via national representatives* — the enterprise's
positions on interpretive questions reach the Board through the
NCAs that sit on it, which in turn are influenced by the
enterprise's direct engagement with its lead NCA (see below) and by
industry-body positions channelled through Member-State ministries.
The head who understands this pathway can shape Board-level thinking
over time; the head who does not treats the Board as inert.

### The Advisory Forum

Article 67 constitutes an Advisory Forum with balanced
representation of industry, start-ups, SMEs, civil society, and
academia. It advises the AI Office and the Board on technical and
practical implementation questions. Fundamental-rights agencies and
standardisation bodies participate as permanent members.

For most enterprises the head does not sit on the Advisory Forum
personally. The forum matters because its outputs feed AI Office
guidance and because industry-body representatives on the forum
carry positions the enterprise has helped form. The head tracks
Advisory Forum outputs the way the head tracks EBA guidelines under
banking supervision — reference material that shapes what the NCA
will expect, six to twelve months before the NCA writes it down.

### The Scientific Panel of independent experts

Article 68 establishes a Scientific Panel of independent experts to
support enforcement of the general-purpose AI model provisions and
to advise on systemic risk. The panel is a specialised technical
body — the head engages with it (if at all) only through the AI
Office and only in the GPAI supervision context. For an enterprise
that is a deployer of GPAI models rather than a provider, the
panel's outputs are read as an early signal of what systemic-risk
determinations may look like, not as a bilateral engagement channel.

## The Member-State layer — where the enterprise gets supervised

### National competent authorities and market surveillance authorities

Article 70 requires each Member State to designate at least one
notifying authority and at least one market surveillance authority
as national competent authorities for purposes of the AI Act.
Member States were required to designate and publish these
authorities by 2 August 2025.

The NCA is the head's primary regulator counterpart for the AI Act.
Which authority a Member State designates varies — some designate
existing data-protection authorities (with the attendant tension
between GDPR-lineage and AI Act-lineage supervisory culture), some
designate ministry-level bodies, some create new AI-specific
authorities, some designate sector regulators as competent for
sectoral high-risk domains. The head who operates in multiple
Member States tracks each designation and builds a working
relationship with the *lead* NCA — typically the NCA of the Member
State where the enterprise has its primary EU establishment, or the
NCA that supervises the sector in which the enterprise operates the
majority of its high-risk systems.

The head's engagement stance with the NCA is direct and executive.
The head is the named enterprise counterpart on Article 26 deployer
obligations, on the Article 72 post-market monitoring regime, on the
Article 73 serious-incident reporting regime (chapter 02 covers the
filing mechanics), and on information requests under the market
surveillance powers of the Act (which mirror the general product
market surveillance regime under Regulation (EU) 2019/1020). GC is
in the room for anything that is a legal proceeding, a formal
enforcement action, or a matter where privilege is at issue. The
head is in the room for everything.

### Notifying authorities

The notifying authority is the Member-State body responsible for
setting up and running the procedures for assessment, designation,
and notification of conformity-assessment bodies (notified bodies)
under the Act. In most Member States the notifying authority is
distinct from the market surveillance authority, though both are
NCAs for AI-Act purposes.

The head does not routinely engage notifying authorities directly.
The head engages notifying authorities transitively — when a
notified body the enterprise relies on has its designation
challenged, extended, or revised, the notifying authority is the
counterparty. That situation is handled by the head's supplier-
management interface with the notified body itself, escalated when
material to the notifying authority via GC.

## The conformity-assessment layer — notified bodies

Articles 28 and following (referenced from Chapter VII's oversight
framework) govern notified bodies. Notified bodies are private or
public conformity-assessment bodies designated by a Member State's
notifying authority and formally notified to the Commission and
other Member States. For those high-risk AI systems that require
third-party conformity assessment — a subset defined by Annex III
categories and by the Annex I product-safety regulation regime —
the notified body is who conducts the assessment.

The head engages notified bodies as *suppliers to the enterprise*,
not as regulators. The head's stance:

- Selection: multiple notified bodies exist per assessment scope;
  the enterprise chooses on competence, sectoral fit, capacity, and
  responsiveness. GC handles the contract; the head owns the
  operating relationship.
- Scoping: the enterprise proposes the scope of assessment and the
  notified body confirms or challenges it; the head is accountable
  for the enterprise's scoping position being defensible.
- Evidence provision: the head governs the enterprise's evidence
  production pipeline (technical documentation under Annex IV,
  quality management system under Article 17, post-market monitoring
  plan) so that the notified body receives complete and consistent
  evidence packages.
- Findings response: when the notified body raises findings, the
  head is the enterprise's accountable executive for response and
  remediation.

Notified bodies operate under the accreditation frameworks
maintained by national accreditation bodies against ISO/IEC 17065
and adjacent standards, and they draw on the harmonised standards
produced by CEN-CENELEC JTC 21 (the joint technical committee for
AI standards work at European level). The head tracks JTC 21's
output — harmonised standards, once cited in the Official Journal,
create a presumption of conformity — as part of the regulatory-
change discipline covered in [[mod-105-regulatory-change-management]].

## Who does the head phone when

The map is only useful if the head can act on it under pressure.
The following organises the bodies discussed above by engagement
trigger, head-side counterpart, and GC hand-off point.

**A serious incident occurs involving a high-risk AI system placed
on the EU market.** Body to notify: the market surveillance
authority of the Member State where the incident occurred, under
Article 73. Head-side counterpart: the head of AI governance, with
the incident commander (see [[mod-110-incident-leadership]]) and
GC. GC hand-off: any communication that touches on liability,
enforcement exposure, or ongoing investigation goes through GC;
factual reporting under Article 73 is co-authored with GC but the
head signs.

**An NCA sends an information request under market surveillance
powers.** Body: the requesting NCA. Head-side counterpart: the
head, coordinating with the operating owner of the system in
question and with the relevant CxO peer (CISO for security items,
CPO for privacy items, CRO for risk appetite items — see
[[mod-101-role-scope-and-leadership-shape]] chapter 04). GC hand-off:
GC reviews the response before it goes out; escalation to GC-owned
posture happens when the request is framed as a formal
investigation, or when responsive material is potentially
privileged.

**The Commission opens a consultation on delegated or implementing
acts.** Body: the EU AI Office (via the Have Your Say portal or the
AI Office's own consultation channel). Head-side counterpart: the
head, coordinated with Government Affairs and GC, with substantive
positions drafted from the level-50 architect's operating knowledge.
GC hand-off: Government Affairs owns the submission strategy; the
head owns the technical substance; GC reviews for legal exposure.

**The enterprise provides a general-purpose AI model in scope of
Chapter V.** Body: the EU AI Office directly, plus participation in
the GPAI Code of Practice process. Head-side counterpart: the head,
typically with the chief scientist or the head of the model-
providing business unit. GC hand-off: GC on formal obligations
correspondence; the head on Code of Practice engagement and
technical dialogue. Chapter 06 covers this engagement.

**A notified body issues a finding during conformity assessment.**
Body: the notified body. Head-side counterpart: the head, with the
system's operating owner and the level-50 architect. GC hand-off:
GC only if the finding creates enforcement exposure or challenges
the enterprise's overall conformity position.

**A Member State designates or re-designates its NCA, or publishes
new supervisory guidance.** Body: the NCA. Head-side counterpart:
the head, monitoring via the regulatory-change discipline
(see [[mod-105-regulatory-change-management]]) and via industry-
body intelligence. GC hand-off: GC reviews new guidance for
obligation-shift implications; the head owns program response.

**A civil-society complaint, journalist inquiry, or third-party
audit request lands.** Body: not a Chapter VII body. These are not
routed through the AI Act architecture at all — they belong to
corporate communications, GC, and the head jointly. The point of
listing them here is that the head keeps them *off* the regulator
map so that Chapter VII engagements do not get diluted by inbound
that belongs elsewhere.

The regulator-engagement summary cluster in the quarterly board pack
(see [[mod-103-board-and-audit-committee-reporting]] chapter 01
cluster 4) reflects this map: engagements are listed by regulator,
by legal instrument at play, and by enterprise counterpart, so that
a director reading the pack can see who at the enterprise is on the
hook for each open item.

## The map as a durable artefact

The head maintains this map as a living artefact — a one-page
diagram or table, versioned, kept current as Member States finalise
NCA designations and as the AI Office publishes new guidance. The
map lives next to the regulatory-change register (chapter 03 of
[[mod-105-regulatory-change-management]]) and it is the first
artefact a new head reviews on onboarding. When the level-50
architect proposes an operating change that affects who at the
enterprise talks to which regulator, the map is updated *before* the
change ships.

Regulators notice organisations that answer the phone competently
on first contact. The map is how the head makes sure the phone is
answered competently.

## Summary

- Chapter VII of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 (Articles 64 to 70) is
  the head's engagement map for EU AI Act governance, not a legal
  text to interpret — legal interpretation belongs to GC.
- Three layers with three engagement postures: the Union layer
  (EU AI Office, European AI Board, Advisory Forum, Scientific
  Panel) engaged strategically and mediated; the Member-State
  layer (national competent authorities under Article 70) engaged
  directly by the head as accountable executive; the conformity-
  assessment layer (notified bodies under Article 28 and following)
  engaged as suppliers.
- The head's primary regulator counterpart is the lead NCA of the
  Member State of primary EU establishment (or the sector-competent
  NCA); GC is in the room for legal proceedings, the head is in the
  room for everything else.
- The EU AI Board is engaged indirectly via Member-State
  representatives, not directly; the AI Office is engaged directly
  only for GPAI providers (chapter 06) and for formal consultations,
  otherwise via industry bodies.
- Notified bodies are suppliers under Article 28 and following,
  operating against CEN-CENELEC JTC 21 harmonised standards; they
  are not regulators, and the head governs the relationship as a
  supplier-management interface.
- A "who does the head phone when" map — trigger, body, head-side
  counterpart, GC hand-off point — is a living artefact that the
  head maintains and that feeds the regulator-engagement cluster
  in the board pack ([[mod-103-board-and-audit-committee-reporting]]).
- Chapter 02 turns to the specific Article 72/73 NCA filing regime;
  chapter 06 turns to GPAI Code of Practice engagement with the AI
  Office; chapter 07 turns to the GC hand-off discipline in detail.
