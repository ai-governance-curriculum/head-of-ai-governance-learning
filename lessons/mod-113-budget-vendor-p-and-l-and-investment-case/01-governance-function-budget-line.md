# Owning the AI-Governance Function Budget Line — The Multi-Year Budget Model as a Whole

## Motivation

The head of AI governance is a P&L-carrying executive. Not in
the revenue sense — the function does not sell a product — but
in the cost-centre sense that carries every discipline a
revenue-carrying P&L carries: a multi-year plan, per-line
defence, a forecast-refresh cadence, a variance narrative, and
a substantive negotiating posture across the enterprise's own
annual cost-authoring calendar. The head who runs the function
as a queue of asks the CFO grants or refuses year by year is a
head who has misunderstood what the role is. The role is to
carry the function's cost — headcount, vendor spend across
four vendor categories, external assurance and audit,
capitalised tooling, training and certification, incident
reserve, external benchmarking, and community leadership — as
a coherent multi-year P&L that the CFO can pressure-test at
any point in the cycle and the audit committee can read as
evidence the function is being run as a business.

"Just fund the team" is the failure state. It is the disposition
of a head who believes headcount is the substantive request and
everything else — the GRC platform, the observability licences,
the runtime-security stack, the assurance-body fees, the
training budget, the incident retainer — is peripheral. It is
also the disposition the CFO reads as absence-of-thought and
compresses accordingly. The compression will not come at the
headcount line where the head has authored a specific
justification for each requisition; the compression will come
at the vendor lines the head has not defended, at the training
budget the head has treated as discretionary, at the incident
reserve the head has not named, and at the community-leadership
travel that reads as travel because the head has not named it
as anything else. The function survives at reduced capacity;
the head has ceded the substantive negotiation.

Annual line-item negotiation without a multi-year model is the
adjacent failure — the CFO trap. A head who presents each year
as a fresh ask, without a committed year-2 shape and a
year-3 planning shape, has consented to re-authoring the
function from a green field each cycle. The head trades the
strategic conversation ("here is the three-year trajectory this
function is on and the reasons the trajectory is defensible")
for the tactical negotiation ("here is what I need this year
and here are the reasons I need it"). The tactical negotiation
is one the CFO wins by attrition; the strategic conversation
is one the head has structural standing to hold if — and only
if — the head has authored the multi-year model.

This chapter is the multi-year budget model as a whole. Chapter
[02](02-multi-year-investment-case.md) authors the investment
case that sits on top of the model — the programme case, the
transformation case, and the incident-preparedness case.
Chapters [03](03-grc-for-ai-vendor-pnl.md) through
[06](06-assurance-body-relationship-pnl.md) run the vendor P&L
disciplines that populate the vendor lines this chapter
enumerates. Chapter [07](07-roi-case-modelling.md) authors the
ROI case that turns the model from a cost defence into a
modelled-value argument. Chapter
[08](08-caio-and-cxo-budget-envelope-coordination.md) puts the
model into proportion inside the enterprise-wide AI budget
envelope. This chapter is what all of those sit on top of.

## Core concept — the multi-year budget model shape

The head runs a multi-year rolling budget model. Three years is
the standard shape — a fully detailed year-1, a committed year-2,
and a planning year-3 — with the roll happening at each annual
cycle so that year-2 becomes year-1, year-3 becomes year-2, and
a fresh planning year is added. Some enterprises run four- or
five-year rolling models for infrastructure-intensive functions;
the AI-governance function's dependency on vendor contracts that
frequently sit on three-year terms and on ISO/IEC 42001
certification cycles that are three-year cycles makes three-year
the natural shape.

The model is not a single spreadsheet. It is a set of
interlocking artefacts: the line-item budget model itself
(rows are cost categories and named line items, columns are
years and quarters where the granularity matters); the
per-line defence-of-ask (the substantive justification each
line carries); the peer-benchmark reference set (where
comparable-enterprise benchmarks are available and defensible);
the growth-driver map (what changes in the AI portfolio, the
regulatory environment, or the incident-load history drives
line-item growth); and the compression-cost map (what capability
the function loses if the CFO takes a knife to each line).
Chapters [02](02-multi-year-investment-case.md) and
[07](07-roi-case-modelling.md) sit on top of this substrate;
this chapter is the substrate itself.

### The cost categories

The budget model groups line items into nine cost categories.
Each has a distinct commercial shape, a distinct escalation
surface, and a distinct compression-cost profile. The head
authors the model with the categories explicit so the CFO reads
the function's cost structure rather than an undifferentiated
line-item list.

- **Headcount** — salaries, employer taxes, benefits,
  equity/variable, recruiter fees, and backfill reserve.
- **GRC-for-AI vendor spend** — the platform that is the
  function's system of record.
- **AI-observability vendor spend** — model monitoring, drift
  detection, data-quality-for-AI, and evaluation-as-a-service
  overlays.
- **AI-runtime security vendor spend** — prompt-injection
  defence, model-firewall, red-team-as-a-service retainer,
  ML-BOM tooling, and model-supply-chain attestation.
- **External assurance and audit spend** — the ISO/IEC 42001
  certification-body fees, independent-assurance-body fees,
  Big Four AI-audit engagement fees, and specialist AI-audit
  firm fees.
- **Tooling depreciation** — the capitalised portion of platform
  implementation cost, amortised over useful life.
- **Training and certification** — cohort fees for the
  credentials that feed the function's talent pipeline.
- **Incident-response reserve** — contingent reserve funding the
  external retainers and legal / communications capacity the
  head unlocks in hours rather than weeks when the incident
  arrives.
- **External-benchmarking and community-leadership** — the dues,
  the travel, the speaking-slot offsets, and the peer-
  benchmarking-exchange fees that keep the function inside the
  community it draws its practice from.

Each category is walked in a subsection below. The head reads
the categories together, not in isolation — trade-offs across
categories are frequent (a heavier vendor spend can compress
headcount need; a heavier headcount can compress vendor spend;
a heavier training budget can compress recruiter fees), and
the multi-year model is where those trade-offs are made
substantively rather than by CFO fiat.

## Headcount

Headcount is the largest single line in almost every
AI-governance function's budget and the line the head has the
most direct authority to shape. The line-item detail composes
directly with the multi-year hiring plan authored in
[[mod-108-leading-the-governance-function]] chapter 02 — that
plan is the source of truth for what roles at what levels are
being hired in what quarter, and this budget line consumes it.

The headcount line-items themselves:

- **Salaries** — base compensation for each role on the plan.
  The head does not invent salary bands; the head consumes
  the enterprise's own compensation-band structure and works
  with HR / total-rewards on the specific bands for each role.
  <!-- needs-research: verify current enterprise band structure and market benchmarks for ai-governance-analyst L15, ai-risk-engineer L25, ai-evaluation-engineer L35, senior-ai-governance-architect L50, head-of-ai-governance L60 against the enterprise's compensation-benchmarking provider (Radford, Mercer, Willis Towers Watson, or equivalent) -->
- **Employer taxes and benefits** — the loaded-cost multiplier
  applied to base salary. The specific multiplier varies by
  jurisdiction and by the enterprise's benefit structure; the
  head consumes the multiplier finance publishes rather than
  authoring it.
- **Equity and variable compensation** — the annual bonus
  accrual, the equity-grant amortisation for roles that carry
  equity, and any long-term-incentive plan accrual. The head
  consumes the enterprise's own compensation-policy shape.
- **Recruiter fees** — the retained-search and contingent-search
  fees for the senior hires on the plan (`senior-ai-governance-
  architect` L50, `head-of-ai-governance` L60 where the head
  is hiring a peer or successor). The head budgets the fees
  against the specific requisitions in the hiring plan rather
  than as a general recruiter-fee bucket.
- **Backfill reserve** — a specific line for the departure the
  head cannot predict but can model. Attrition in the function
  is not zero, and the head models an expected-attrition
  backfill reserve for the year rather than absorbing the
  cost inside a rolling requisition process. <!-- needs-research: verify appropriate backfill-reserve percentage for governance/risk functions against enterprise HR analytics on functional attrition rates -->

The head reads the headcount line against the level ladder the
function is built on — the level-15 `ai-governance-analyst`, the
level-25 `ai-risk-engineer`, the level-35 `ai-evaluation-
engineer`, the level-50 `senior-ai-governance-architect`, and
the level-60 `head-of-ai-governance` — so that the CFO reads
the headcount cost not as a flat number of FTEs but as a
specific composition of levels. A budget with more level-15s
and fewer level-50s has a lower total salary cost and a lower
functional capability; a budget with the level-50s intact and
fewer level-15s has a higher hourly-cost profile and less
throughput capacity. The head owns that composition trade-off
explicitly rather than letting the CFO trade capacity for cost
implicitly.

## GRC-for-AI vendor spend

The GRC-for-AI platform is the function's system of record and
typically the largest single vendor line in the budget. Chapter
[03](03-grc-for-ai-vendor-pnl.md) runs the vendor P&L discipline
against the incumbent (or shortlist, where the function is
pre-platform). This chapter carries the line-items the vendor
P&L rolls up into.

- **Platform licence** — the recurring annual (or multi-year
  pre-paid) subscription fee. Multi-year contracts frequently
  carry a lower per-year rate than annual contracts; the head
  models both shapes so the year-2 and year-3 columns are
  populated at the actual contracted price where a multi-year
  contract is in force.
- **Implementation and integration** — the one-time (or
  amortised) cost of standing the platform up, integrating it
  with the enterprise's ISMS, connecting it to the AI system
  inventory as described in [[mod-102-running-the-program]]
  chapter 06, and wiring the evidence-store integrations. The
  head budgets these as capex or opex depending on the
  enterprise's accounting policy (see tooling depreciation
  below).
- **Professional services** — the vendor's implementation
  consultants, the enterprise's third-party systems integrator
  fees where the enterprise engages one, and the ongoing
  professional-services engagements the platform vendor charges
  for out-of-box customisation.
- **Growth-in-usage escalators** — the per-AI-system, per-user,
  per-model, or per-API-call charges that scale with the
  enterprise's AI portfolio growth. The head models the
  portfolio-growth trajectory (feeding out of
  [[mod-107-program-measurement-and-maturity]] chapter 01's
  KRI/KPI portfolio and the enterprise's own AI-strategy
  trajectory) and calibrates the year-2 and year-3 usage
  columns against it. Usage-growth surprise is the most
  frequent budget-cycle surprise in this category.

The head reads the GRC-for-AI vendor spend as a multi-year
commitment, not an annual renewal. Chapter
[03](03-grc-for-ai-vendor-pnl.md) is the P&L discipline; this
chapter's contribution is naming the shape of the line so it
appears in the model correctly and rolls into the multi-year
trajectory with the contract's actual term structure.

## AI-observability vendor spend

The observability stack is a smaller line than GRC-for-AI in
almost all cases but a distinct one. It is the tooling that
produces the drift, data-quality, and model-behaviour signals
the level-35 `ai-evaluation-engineer` reads and the level-50
`senior-ai-governance-architect` composes into the KRI
portfolio. Chapter [04](04-ai-observability-vendor-pnl.md)
runs the vendor P&L; this chapter carries the line-items.

- **Platform licence** — annual (or multi-year) subscription
  for the observability platform.
- **Data-volume escalators** — per-inference, per-model, or
  per-data-volume charges. Model-inference volume growth is
  the dominant growth driver in this category; the head models
  it against the AI portfolio's inference-load trajectory.
- **Integration cost** — the one-time or amortised cost of
  wiring observability into the enterprise's model-serving
  infrastructure and into the GRC-for-AI platform.
- **Evaluation-as-a-service overlays** — where the vendor
  offers an evaluation-as-a-service capability the function
  uses (LLM-as-judge, red-team evaluation panels, specialist
  evaluation harnesses), the per-engagement or per-evaluation
  cost.

The head co-authors the observability line with the level-35
evaluator, because the substantive requirement is one the
evaluator carries. The budget-line ownership is the head's;
the technical shape is co-authored.

## AI-runtime security vendor spend

The runtime-security stack sits alongside the observability
stack and is co-owned with the enterprise's security
organisation — the `ai-infra-security` level-35 engineer where
the enterprise has one, and the CISO's team at CxO scope where
the AI-runtime-security investment is coordinated with the
enterprise's ISMS. Chapter
[05](05-ai-runtime-security-vendor-pnl.md) runs the vendor
P&L; this chapter carries the line-items.

- **Prompt-injection defence and model-firewall licence** —
  the recurring subscription for the runtime-defence layer
  the enterprise's LLM deployments run behind.
- **Red-team-as-a-service retainer** — a distinct line for
  the retainer with a specialist red-team provider, whether
  as periodic engagements or a standing capacity.
- **ML-BOM tooling and model-supply-chain attestation** — the
  tooling that maintains the ML Bill of Materials (SPDX 3.0
  AI profile, CycloneDX ML-BOM) and the model-supply-chain
  attestation chain the function reads as its evidence of
  model provenance.
- **Runtime security integration** — the one-time and ongoing
  cost of integrating the runtime security stack with the
  enterprise's SOC, SIEM, and incident-response tooling.

The head runs this line in coordination with the CISO and with
the level-35 security engineer. Where the CISO's own budget
absorbs part of the runtime-security spend (as it frequently
does — an AI-runtime firewall is legitimately security-owned in
many enterprises), the head negotiates the split explicitly
rather than letting it drift into a bilateral cost that no one
carries end-to-end.

## External assurance and audit spend

The external-assurance line funds the independent-assurance
signals the audit committee and the market read as evidence
the function's discipline holds up under external inspection.
Chapter [06](06-assurance-body-relationship-pnl.md) runs the
per-body P&L; this chapter carries the aggregate line and its
component shape.

- **ISO/IEC 42001 certification-body fees** — the initial
  certification audit fee (a one-time cost in the
  certification year), the annual surveillance audit fee in
  the intervening years, and the three-year re-certification
  audit fee. The head models the three-year cycle explicitly
  so the model's year-1 / year-2 / year-3 columns carry the
  correct fee shape for whichever cycle year the enterprise
  is in. <!-- needs-research: verify current ISO/IEC 42001 CB fee ranges (initial certification, annual surveillance, three-year re-certification) against a specific accredited CB's published fee schedule (BSI, PECB, TÜV, DNV, DEKRA) -->
- **ForHumanity IAAIS audit fees** — where the enterprise
  carries an Independent Audit of AI Systems engagement. The
  fee shape depends on scope; the head models the fee against
  the specific IAAIS engagement scope authored with the CRO
  and audit-committee chair.
- **BABL AI audit fees** — where the enterprise engages BABL
  AI for an algorithmic-audit engagement against BABL's
  criteria. Similar scoped-engagement fee shape.
- **Big Four AI-audit engagement fees** — where the enterprise
  engages one of Deloitte, EY, PwC, or KPMG for an AI-focused
  external audit engagement, whether as an extension of the
  external financial audit or as a standalone engagement. The
  head coordinates the fee against the CFO's own external-
  audit budget (the Big Four fee for AI-specific work is
  typically an incremental line above the base external-audit
  fee).
- **Specialist AI-audit firm fees** — where the enterprise
  engages a specialist AI-audit firm (Trail of Bits, NCC
  Group's AI/ML practice, and adjacent specialist providers)
  for an assurance engagement scoped to a specific system or
  a specific technical risk (model-supply-chain security,
  red-team engagement on a foundation-model deployment, and
  peers).

The head reads the external-assurance line in aggregate as the
enterprise's independent-assurance-cost budget. The CFO
frequently pushes for consolidation ("do we really need the CB
audit and the ForHumanity audit and the Big Four engagement");
the head's response is not to defend all three reflexively but
to walk the CFO through what each signal purchases and where
the audit committee has ratified the specific mix. Chapter
[06](06-assurance-body-relationship-pnl.md) develops that
discipline.

## Tooling depreciation

Tooling depreciation is the accounting mechanism that spreads
capitalised platform-implementation cost across the useful life
of the platform. The head does not decide accounting policy —
the CFO's finance team and the enterprise's audit function
decide it — but the head does negotiate the timing of capex-vs-
opex classification with the CFO's team, because the
classification decision materially affects the shape of the
year-1 versus year-2 cost profile.

Under IFRS, IAS 38 governs the treatment of internally-
developed intangible assets and IFRS 16 governs the treatment
of certain SaaS arrangements that carry an in-substance lease
component. <!-- needs-research: verify current IFRS treatment of SaaS platform implementation costs under IAS 38 vs IFRS 16, and specifically whether GRC-for-AI platform implementation costs qualify for capitalisation as internally-generated intangible assets or must be expensed as SaaS service costs, against the IFRS Interpretations Committee's April 2021 agenda decision on configuration and customisation costs in a cloud computing arrangement -->

Under US GAAP, ASC 350-40 governs the treatment of
internal-use software including capitalisable implementation
costs for cloud-computing arrangements. <!-- needs-research: verify current US GAAP ASC 350-40 treatment of capitalisable implementation costs for hosted-arrangement (SaaS) software, and specifically the phases (preliminary, application development, post-implementation) that qualify for capitalisation vs the phases that must be expensed -->

The head's role in the tooling-depreciation line is not to
author the accounting policy; the CFO's finance team does that
in coordination with the external auditor. The head's role is
to (a) understand the classification the CFO's team is
applying to each vendor implementation, (b) negotiate the
timing where the head has legitimate substantive input (a
faster implementation with more expensed cost in year-1
versus a phased implementation with more capitalised cost
amortised over years 2-5, for example, is a substantive
choice), and (c) surface the classification decision to the
audit committee where the classification materially changes
the year-1 cost profile the committee is reading.

## Training and certification

The training and certification line funds the credentialing
that keeps the function's talent current with the field's
evolving practice. Chapter 03 of
[[mod-108-leading-the-governance-function]] runs the coaching-
and-development-plan discipline this budget feeds; chapter 01
of [[mod-112-certifications-career-path-community-leadership]]
runs the certification-portfolio discipline this budget also
feeds. This chapter carries the line-items.

- **IAPP AIGP cohort fees** — for team members progressing
  through the AI Governance Professional certification. The
  head budgets by named requisition against the development
  plans.
- **ISO/IEC 42001 Lead Implementer and Lead Auditor cohort
  fees** — through the accredited training providers (BSI,
  PECB, LRQA, and peers). The Lead Implementer path is
  appropriate for the level-50 architect and, in many
  enterprises, for the level-35 evaluator; the Lead Auditor
  path is appropriate for team members supporting the
  internal-audit interface.
- **NIST AI RMF training** — for team members developing the
  framework fluency the function's US-facing regulatory
  interface depends on.
- **ForHumanity Fellows and IAAIS auditor certification** —
  for team members developing the independent-assurance-craft
  fluency the function's IAAIS-engagement interface depends
  on.
- **BABL AI training and Algorithm Bias Auditor
  certification** — for team members developing the
  algorithmic-audit-craft fluency.
- **University programs** — degree, executive-education, and
  certificate programs where the head is funding a specific
  team member's progression against a specific development
  plan.

The head budgets training and certification against named
team members with specific development plans, not as a
generic "training budget" the function drains through the
year. The named-requisition discipline is what defends the
line against the CFO's compression instinct — a line that
carries "level-35 evaluator X is taking the ISO Lead
Implementer course in Q2 as agreed in the year-1 development
plan" is a line the CFO reads as specific and defensible; a
line that carries "training budget" is a line the CFO reads
as discretionary and cuttable.

## Incident-response reserve

The incident-response reserve is a contingent line — the head
does not spend it absent an incident, and the head does not
plan to spend it in a normal year. The reserve exists so that
when the incident arrives, the head unlocks external capacity
in hours rather than weeks. Cross-refer
[[mod-110-incident-leadership]] for the incident-leadership
discipline this reserve funds.

The reserve has two shapes running together:

- **The retainer that unlocks the reserve.** A retainer with
  an external DFIR provider with AI-incident competence, with
  external legal counsel with AI-regulatory competence, and
  with an external crisis-communications firm with technical-
  incident competence. The retainer is a modest recurring
  cost that the head budgets as a standing line. It is not
  the reserve itself; it is the mechanism that allows the
  reserve to be spent quickly when needed.
- **The contingent reserve capacity.** A specific dollar
  amount the CFO has authorised the head to draw against on
  incident declaration, without a fresh authorisation cycle.
  The reserve is not a line the head spends against in a
  normal year; it is a line the head has authorised, that
  the CFO's finance team has budgeted against, and that sits
  ready.

The distinction matters. A head who budgets an "incident
reserve" but has not funded the retainer that unlocks it is a
head who will discover in the incident that the DFIR provider
takes two weeks to onboard and the legal counsel takes ten
days to be scoped. A head who has funded the retainer but has
not budgeted the contingent capacity is a head who has to
open an emergency-spend authorisation with the CFO on
incident declaration — an authorisation the CFO reads as
absence-of-planning even where the incident itself is a
genuine surprise.

## External-benchmarking and community-leadership

The final aggregate cost category funds the function's
external presence — its participation in peer-benchmarking
exchanges, its subscription to industry-standard benchmarking
programs, its dues to the professional bodies whose
credentials the function's talent carries, and the travel and
speaking-slot offsets that keep the head and the function's
senior team visible in the community the function draws its
practice from.

Two subcategories run together:

- **External benchmarking** — participation in the maturity-
  model benchmarking exchanges referenced in
  [[mod-107-program-measurement-and-maturity]], sector working-
  group dues (where the enterprise participates in an
  industry-sector AI-governance working group), peer-
  institution benchmarking-exchange fees, and subscriptions
  to standardised AI-safety benchmarks (MLCommons AI Safety
  benchmarks, and adjacent) where applicable to the
  enterprise's AI portfolio.
- **Community leadership** — professional-body dues (IAPP,
  ForHumanity, BABL AI, OCEG, Partnership on AI where
  applicable to the enterprise), travel to the major
  AI-governance conference circuit (RSA AI-focused sessions,
  IAPP Global Privacy and AI Governance Summits, RAISE,
  TrustCon, and the AI-governance conference circuit), and
  the speaking-slot offsets that the head budgets against
  the function's community presence. Cross-refer chapters
  04–05 of
  [[mod-112-certifications-career-path-community-leadership]]
  for the substantive community-leadership discipline this
  budget funds.

The head runs this category as a substantive line, not as a
discretionary travel-and-entertainment bucket. A function whose
community-leadership presence is invisible is a function whose
credentialed-talent pipeline eventually dries up and whose
practice drifts from the community's evolving standard. The
category is compressible in a genuinely constrained year, but
it is not the first line to compress; the head defends it as
the substantive investment it is.

## The multi-year trajectory — why rolling, not annual

The rolling multi-year model is not documentation. It is the
substantive negotiating instrument.

An annual-only model gives the CFO the shape they prefer for
compression: each year is a fresh authorisation, each line is a
fresh negotiation, and the function's cost drift year-over-year
is a series of tactical concessions rather than a strategic
trajectory. The head who runs an annual-only model consents to
that shape and loses the strategic conversation.

A multi-year rolling model gives the head a different shape.
Year-1 is detailed at the same fidelity the annual model would
carry — line-item, quantity, per-line defence. Year-2 is
committed — the head has authored the year-2 shape at less line-
item detail but with substantive per-line trajectory, and the
CFO has (typically at the previous cycle) ratified the year-2
shape as the target. Year-3 is planning — the head has authored
the year-3 shape at higher-level granularity, as the trajectory
the function is on and the shape the CFO can plan against, with
the understanding that year-3 detail will emerge as year-3
becomes year-2 in the next cycle.

The trajectory has to be defensible on demand, not just at
budget-cycle open. The CFO can and will ask "what has changed
since we ratified this three months ago" at any audit-committee
or board cycle, and the head who cannot answer without a
scramble is a head who has authored the model as a cycle
artefact rather than a living instrument. The discipline is to
run the model as a live artefact — the head updates the model
at each quarterly forecast-refresh, notes the deltas, and
carries a running narrative of what has changed and why. The
narrative is what the CFO reads at the on-demand question, and
the CFO reads it as evidence the head is running the function
as a business rather than as a queue of asks.

## Line-item defence discipline

Every line in the model carries a specific defence-of-ask the
head can articulate on demand. The defence has four components,
each of which the head authors explicitly rather than assuming.

- **The first-order justification.** Each line is justified as
  a regulatory obligation (the line funds a capability without
  which the enterprise fails a specific regulatory obligation),
  a contractual obligation (the line funds a capability without
  which the enterprise breaches a specific customer or
  supplier contract), a board-adopted risk-appetite obligation
  (the line funds a capability without which the enterprise
  breaches the risk appetite the board has adopted), or a
  strategic-imperative obligation (the line funds a capability
  the enterprise has committed to as strategic priority, not
  as compliance obligation). The head is specific about which
  of the four each line is, and the head is specific about
  the source authority the justification rests on. A line
  whose justification is "we've always spent this" is a line
  the head has not defended.
- **The peer-benchmark reference.** Where a defensible peer
  benchmark is available (a comparable-enterprise
  AI-governance function's spend on the same category, drawn
  from a specific benchmarking source), the head carries the
  reference. Where a defensible benchmark is not available,
  the head names the absence rather than inventing one. A
  fabricated benchmark is worse than no benchmark — the CFO's
  finance team will run its own benchmarking against the head's
  claim, and a claim that does not survive their check
  damages every other line in the model.
- **The growth driver.** Each line's year-over-year trajectory
  is driven by something specific — AI portfolio growth (the
  observability line scales with model-inference volume; the
  GRC platform's per-AI-system charges scale with inventory
  size), regulatory change (a new regulatory obligation drives
  a line-item into existence or expands one), incident-load
  history (a category with rising incident-response invocation
  drives the reserve line up), maturity trajectory (a function
  moving from level-2 to level-3 on a maturity model drives
  the training and external-assurance lines up). The head
  names the growth driver so the CFO reads the trajectory as
  driven by a mechanism rather than by wishful escalation.
- **The compression cost.** For each line, the head carries a
  substantive answer to "what capability breaks if the CFO
  takes a knife to this line by 10%, 25%, 50%". The
  compression-cost answer is not "the function fails" for
  every line — that is the reflexive defence the CFO discounts.
  The compression-cost answer is specific: at 10% compression
  on training, the level-35 evaluators do not complete the ISO
  Lead Implementer track this year and the CB audit will find
  an unqualified-lead-auditor gap that reads as a finding; at
  50% compression on the incident reserve, the DFIR retainer
  is cancelled and the enterprise's incident-response window
  extends from hours to weeks. The specific compression cost
  is what the CFO reads as the head's substantive engagement
  with the trade-off.

## The budget-cycle calendar

The head sits inside the enterprise's annual CFO cycle at
specific windows and interfaces at specific cadences. The
specific calendar varies by enterprise; the pattern is
consistent enough to name.

- **Q4 budget authoring.** The enterprise's annual budget
  cycle typically authors the coming year's budget in the
  final quarter. The head brings the multi-year model to the
  cycle with year-1 rolled into the coming year and year-2
  and year-3 rolled forward. The head negotiates the year-1
  detail against the CFO's compression posture and rates the
  year-2 shape as committed at the ratification cycle's end.
- **Mid-year forecast refresh.** Roughly halfway through the
  year, the CFO runs a forecast refresh to reforecast year-end
  actuals against the ratified budget. The head brings the
  forecast-refresh update to the cycle — where lines are
  running ahead of budget, where lines are running behind,
  and what the year-end forecast looks like. The head also
  brings any re-baselining requests the mid-year refresh
  supports — a re-baseline for a mid-year vendor renewal that
  came in materially above expectation, a re-baseline for an
  incident that has drawn against the reserve, a re-baseline
  for a hiring plan that has moved.
- **Quarterly forecast refresh.** Between the mid-year and Q4
  cycles, most CFO organisations run a lighter quarterly
  refresh. The head participates as the forecast-provider for
  the function's line and as the substantive read on trajectory
  changes.
- **Mid-year re-baselining triggers.** The head does not wait
  for the mid-year cycle to raise a re-baseline where a
  substantive change has happened. A material vendor
  renegotiation, a material regulatory change, a material
  incident that has drawn the reserve, or a material change
  in the AI portfolio's shape are all triggers for a
  re-baselining conversation with the CFO on the timing the
  substantive change warrants. The head carries the
  re-baselining conversation as a substantive-change
  discussion rather than as an emergency ask.

## Interfaces upstream and downstream

The budget model sits at the centre of a set of interfaces the
head runs substantively rather than as a paperwork flow.

- **Upstream to the CFO's finance team.** The finance team
  owns allocation, cost-centre coding, capex/opex
  classification, and — in multinationals — transfer-pricing
  where the AI-governance function's cost is allocated across
  legal entities. The head engages the finance team's
  substantive contact (typically a business-partner finance
  lead assigned to the head's function, and where the function
  is larger, a dedicated FP&A analyst) at the model-authoring
  and forecast-refresh cadences.
- **Downstream to procurement.** Procurement runs the vendor-
  renewal cycles, the PO discipline, and the invoice-approval
  flow the vendor lines run through. The head engages
  procurement's category managers (the technology-vendor
  category manager for GRC-for-AI, observability, and runtime
  security; the professional-services category manager for
  assurance and audit) at the vendor-renewal cadences and at
  the annual budget-authoring cycle. Chapters
  [03](03-grc-for-ai-vendor-pnl.md) through
  [06](06-assurance-body-relationship-pnl.md) develop the
  vendor-P&L discipline the head runs at the head's level; the
  head-to-procurement interface is where the discipline
  meets the enterprise's own commercial machinery.
- **Downstream to HR.** HR ratifies headcount against the
  hiring plan. The head engages HR at the annual hiring-plan
  cycle and at the quarterly headcount-tracking cadence.
- **Downstream to the CAE.** The Chief Audit Executive
  coordinates the external-audit fees across the enterprise's
  overall external-audit budget; the head engages the CAE on
  the AI-audit-fee coordination the assurance-and-audit
  category rolls up into.
- **Upstream to the audit committee.** The audit committee
  reads the annual budget disclosure that includes the
  AI-governance function's line. The head does not present
  the line-item detail to the audit committee directly (that
  is the CFO's disclosure); the head does support the CFO on
  the substance behind the disclosure and answers substantive
  audit-committee questions on the line where they arise.

## Anti-patterns the head does not permit

**The line-item-first, no-model posture.** The head has a set
of lines but no model that ties them to portfolio growth,
regulatory obligation, or maturity trajectory. Each line is
authored in isolation as an ask; the CFO reads the aggregate
as a shopping list rather than as a plan. The compression is
line-by-line and drifts toward whatever the CFO's finance team
finds hardest to defend externally. The remedy is the multi-
year model discipline this chapter describes — the model that
ties the lines to the substantive drivers.

**The "vendor spend is procurement's problem" abdication.**
The head has never opened the vendor spend as a portfolio and
has left the vendor P&L to procurement's category managers.
Individual vendor renewals are negotiated on procurement's
category-management posture (typically compress-the-line-item),
without the head's substantive engagement on what the vendor
category actually purchases the function. The vendor category
drifts — capabilities get compressed at renewal, price
increases get accepted where they should be resisted and
resisted where they should be accepted, and the function's
tooling posture erodes invisibly. The remedy is the vendor-P&L
discipline chapters [03](03-grc-for-ai-vendor-pnl.md) through
[06](06-assurance-body-relationship-pnl.md) develop.

**The one-year horizon posture.** The head negotiates only the
current year and has no committed year-2 shape. Year-2 becomes
a green-field re-negotiation each cycle, and the head loses
the strategic conversation because the head has consented to
having only tactical conversations. The CFO's compression
target moves higher each cycle because the head has no
ratified year-2 shape to defend against. The remedy is the
multi-year rolling model with the ratified year-2 shape and
the year-3 planning shape.

**The absent incident reserve.** The budget carries no
reserve for the incident that will arrive. When it arrives,
the head has to open an emergency spend authorisation the
CFO reads as absence-of-planning even where the incident
itself is a genuine surprise. Worse, the retainer that would
have unlocked the reserve in hours does not exist, so even
the emergency spend takes weeks to route. The remedy is the
paired retainer-and-reserve discipline above and the
composition with [[mod-110-incident-leadership]].

**The community-and-training-cost soft-cut pattern.** Training
and community-leadership lines are the first the CFO
compresses because they are the lines the head has not
defended substantively. The team's credentialing pipeline
degrades over one or two cycles; the enterprise's community-
leadership presence goes silent; the head does not notice
because the effect is invisible in the year the compression
happens. The remedy is the named-requisition discipline on
training (specific team members, specific development plans,
specific credentials against specific timelines) and the
substantive-community-line discipline on community leadership
(named conferences, named speaking commitments, named
professional-body dues, named benchmarking exchanges).

**The "budget as inflation" defence.** The head's year-over-
year narrative is "same as last year plus X%", and the CFO
reads it correctly as absence-of-thought. The inflation
argument is a placeholder the head has substituted for
substantive per-line trajectory reasoning, and the CFO
compresses on the placeholder rather than engaging with the
substance. The remedy is the growth-driver discipline in the
line-item defence above — each line's year-over-year change
is driven by a specific mechanism, and the head names the
mechanism rather than defaulting to inflation.

**The disclosure-cycle-only forecast posture.** The head
updates the model only at the mid-year forecast-refresh and
the Q4 budget-authoring cycle, and lets the intervening
quarters drift. When the CFO asks the on-demand question at
an audit-committee cycle three weeks after a material vendor
renegotiation, the head cannot answer because the model has
not been updated. The CFO reads the delay as evidence the
head is running the function as a paperwork exercise rather
than as a live P&L. The remedy is the live-artefact discipline
described in the multi-year-trajectory section — the model
is updated at the substantive-change cadence, not only at
the CFO cycle's authored refresh points.

## Summary

- The head runs the AI-governance function budget as a
  P&L-carrying executive, not as a queue-managing operator;
  "just fund the team" and "annual line-item negotiation
  without a multi-year model" are the two failure states this
  chapter refuses.
- The model is a three-year rolling shape — year-1 detailed,
  year-2 committed, year-3 planning — updated at the
  substantive-change cadence and defensible on demand at any
  audit-committee or board cycle.
- Nine cost categories: headcount, GRC-for-AI vendor spend,
  AI-observability vendor spend, AI-runtime security vendor
  spend, external assurance and audit spend, tooling
  depreciation, training and certification, incident-response
  reserve, and external-benchmarking-and-community-leadership.
- Each line carries a four-component defence — first-order
  justification (regulatory / contractual / risk-appetite /
  strategic), peer-benchmark reference where available,
  growth driver, and compression cost.
- The head runs the budget-cycle calendar (Q4 authoring,
  mid-year refresh, quarterly refresh, mid-year re-baselining
  triggers) and the substantive upstream and downstream
  interfaces (CFO's finance team, procurement, HR, CAE, audit
  committee) as the substantive negotiating machinery the
  model sits inside.
- Six anti-patterns the head refuses: line-item-first with no
  model, vendor-spend abdication to procurement, one-year
  horizon, absent incident reserve, community-and-training
  soft-cut, and budget-as-inflation defence — plus the
  disclosure-cycle-only forecast posture the head does not
  permit either.
- Chapter [02](02-multi-year-investment-case.md) authors the
  multi-year investment case that sits on top of this
  model; chapters [03](03-grc-for-ai-vendor-pnl.md) through
  [06](06-assurance-body-relationship-pnl.md) develop the
  vendor-P&L discipline that populates the vendor lines;
  chapter [07](07-roi-case-modelling.md) authors the ROI
  case that turns the model into a modelled-value argument;
  chapter [08](08-caio-and-cxo-budget-envelope-coordination.md)
  puts the model into proportion inside the enterprise-wide
  AI budget envelope.
