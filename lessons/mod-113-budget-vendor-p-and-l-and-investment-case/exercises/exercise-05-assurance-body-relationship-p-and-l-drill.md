# exercise-05: Assurance-Body Relationship P&L Drill

**Estimated effort:** 2 hours

## Objective

Author the enterprise's **assurance-body relationship P&L** —
the per-body P&L for each assurance-body sub-category the
enterprise runs (or plans to run), the multi-body coordination
map that keeps their scopes, timing, and findings from
colliding, the auditor-independence overlay that governs any
Big Four engagement, and the CFO- and audit-committee-facing
coordination artefacts. For each of the five sub-categories in
chapter [06-assurance-body-relationship-pnl](../06-assurance-body-relationship-pnl.md),
the head produces a per-body P&L: the ISO/IEC 42001 CB (BSI,
PECB, TÜV Rheinland, TÜV SÜD, DNV, DEKRA, or the accredited
equivalent), the ForHumanity IAAIS auditor, BABL AI, the Big
Four AI-assurance firm (whichever of Deloitte, EY, PwC, KPMG is
engaged), and any specialist AI-audit firm. Each P&L names the
current-state incumbent where the enterprise already runs the
relationship, or the planned-state shortlist where the
enterprise is scoping the relationship for the first time.

The pack is what the head takes into the audit-committee annual
assurance-plan discussion ([[mod-103-board-and-audit-committee-reporting]]),
what the head hands to the CFO's controller as the assurance-fee-
line substantiation, and what the head coordinates with the CAE
against ([[mod-108-leading-the-governance-function]] chapter 05).
The pack has to survive three stress tests. First, the CFO's
controller asks why the enterprise is running four simultaneous
assurance bodies — the multi-body coordination map has to show
non-overlapping scope, deliberate stacking where it exists, and
the specific role each body plays in the enterprise's assurance
posture. Second, the audit committee chair asks whether the Big
Four engagement raises an independence concern given the
enterprise's financial-statement auditor — the auditor-
independence overlay has to answer with the specific rule set
that applies (SEC / PCAOB, FRC, ESMA-adjacent, APAC-equivalent)
and the specific eligibility check the head ran. Third, the CAE
asks how the head decided the boundary between the CAE's
internal-audit scope and the head's external-assurance scope —
the coordination map has to name the boundary with the CAE, not
just among the external bodies.

The pack is the head's negotiating instrument with each body,
the discipline that keeps the multi-body programme from
compounding into an unmanaged fee escalation, and the artefact
the audit committee reads as evidence that the assurance spend
is substantive rather than performative.

## Steps

1. **Inherit the enterprise context.** Confirm the exercise-01
   context one-pager is current and reference it. The pack's
   assurance-body specifics depend on the sector, the
   jurisdictional and listing footprint (which fires the
   auditor-independence rule set), the AI portfolio scope
   (which fires the sub-categories in play), and the maturity
   stage (which fires whether the enterprise is running one,
   two, three, or all five sub-categories).

2. **The per-body P&L — ISO/IEC 42001 CB.** Author the P&L for
   the enterprise's ISO/IEC 42001 CB relationship — the
   accredited certification body running or contemplated to run
   the enterprise's ISO/IEC 42001 certification cycle. Name
   either the incumbent CB (from BSI, PECB, TÜV Rheinland, TÜV
   SÜD, DNV, DEKRA, or the accredited equivalent for the
   enterprise's national accreditation body — UKAS, ANAB,
   DAkkS, or peer) or the planned shortlist if the relationship
   is not yet contracted. For each candidate or the incumbent:
   - The accreditation body the CB holds accreditation from
     (with a `needs-research` marker where the head has not
     yet confirmed the accreditation for ISO/IEC 42001
     specifically against the IAF-member register and against
     ISO/IEC 42006).
   - The **contracted fee envelope** — the commercial shape
     (fixed-scope, fixed-fee, multi-year), the cycle shape
     (Stage 1, Stage 2, surveillance year 1, surveillance year
     2, re-certification), and the fee driver (audit-days-per-
     cycle at the CB's standard rate). Do not populate
     specific dollar figures unless the enterprise has a
     signed engagement letter; leave the envelope populated as
     a range or as a `needs-research` marker against the
     specific proposal the head has in hand.
   - The **scope** — the AIMS boundary (in-scope AI systems,
     sites, functions, external interfaces), the criteria set
     (ISO/IEC 42001 and any sector-overlay standards the CB
     assesses against), and any deliberate scope exclusions
     the head has negotiated.
   - The **timing** — the specific week or month for Stage 1,
     Stage 2, each surveillance audit, and the re-certification
     audit, coordinated against the AIMS operating calendar
     and the other assurance bodies' calendars (per step 4).
   - The **deliverable shape** — the certification against
     ISO/IEC 42001 (positive certification, certification with
     minor non-conformities, certification with major non-
     conformities requiring closure, certification withheld),
     the accompanying audit report, and the findings letter.
   - The **substantive-competence note** — the head's
     assessment of the CB's AI-competent auditor bench (the
     named lead auditor and technical reviewer where the CB
     shares that at contracting), with `needs-research`
     markers where the head has not yet confirmed the
     specific auditor assignment.

3. **The per-body P&L — ForHumanity IAAIS.** Author the P&L for
   the enterprise's ForHumanity IAAIS relationship — the
   ForHumanity-certified auditor (individual or firm) the
   enterprise retains or plans to retain. Populate:
   - The named auditor / firm (or the shortlist), with the
     ForHumanity certification status and the specific IAAIS
     criteria (algorithmic risk, ethics, bias, cybersecurity,
     trust, sector-specific overlays) the auditor is
     qualified against. `needs-research` marker where the
     head has not yet confirmed the auditor roster and
     criteria set at forhumanity.center.
   - The **contracted fee envelope** — the retained-scope
     shape (retainer plus per-engagement fees), the engagement
     types the enterprise expects to commission in the coming
     year, and the fee driver (engagement scope, engagement
     duration, deliverable shape) rather than a fee-per-hour.
   - The **engagement pipeline** — the specific IAAIS
     engagements the enterprise will commission in the coming
     year (an engagement scoped to a specific high-impact AI
     system; an engagement scoped to a sector-specific IAAIS
     overlay; an AIMS-adjacent socio-technical assessment),
     with the specific AI system or overlay each engagement
     covers.
   - The **timing** per engagement — start, interim-
     checkpoints, deliverable date, coordinated against the
     CB surveillance-audit calendar and the other bodies.
   - The **deliverable shape** per engagement — written
     report, executive summary, findings-and-recommendations
     letter, public-disclosure-suitable report — named for
     the specific audience (audit committee, regulator,
     customer tender, public trust posture).

4. **The per-body P&L — BABL AI.** Author the P&L for the
   enterprise's BABL AI relationship — the algorithmic-audit
   or advisory engagements the enterprise runs or plans to run.
   Populate:
   - The specific BABL AI service the enterprise engages (an
     algorithmic-audit engagement against fairness criteria on
     a specific high-impact AI system, a sector-specific
     algorithmic audit, advisory work on algorithmic risk).
     `needs-research` marker against the current BABL AI
     service catalog at babl.ai.
   - The **contracted fee envelope** — the retained-scope-
     per-engagement shape, with the fee driver (engagement
     scope, system complexity, evidence-production burden)
     rather than a fee-per-hour.
   - The **engagement pipeline** — the specific AI systems
     BABL AI will audit in the coming year, prioritised by
     the AI system inventory's risk tiering.
   - The **timing** — coordinated against the ForHumanity
     engagement calendar so the two independent-assurance
     bodies do not stack evidence-production demands on the
     same six-week window.
   - The **deliverable shape** — the algorithmic-audit report,
     the fairness-assessment findings, any accompanying
     public-disclosure summary — named for the specific
     audience.

5. **The per-body P&L — Big Four AI-assurance.** Author the
   P&L for the Big Four AI-assurance engagement the enterprise
   is running or contemplates running. Populate:
   - The specific firm engaged or under consideration (whichever
     of Deloitte, EY, PwC, KPMG is not the enterprise's
     financial-statement auditor, subject to the independence
     overlay in step 7). `needs-research` marker where the
     head has not yet confirmed the firm's current AI-
     assurance service positioning.
   - The **contracted fee envelope** — the SOW shape, the fee
     driver (partner-and-manager assignment, engagement hours,
     deliverable shape), and the year-scoped fee (Big Four
     engagements are typically annual, not multi-year).
   - The **scope** — the specific AI-risk-management-framework
     alignment work (NIST AI RMF, ISO/IEC 42001), the specific
     AI-model risk management work, the specific AI-governance-
     programme assessment, and any adjacent advisory work.
   - The **partner-and-manager assignment** — the named AI-
     competent partner and manager per the chapter-06 Big
     Four sub-section discipline, with the specific minimum
     share of engagement hours committed in the SOW and the
     escalation-for-substitution process.
   - The **deliverable shape** — one of the four (reasonable-
     assurance report, limited-assurance report, agreed-upon-
     procedures report, advisory work), selected for the
     specific audience (audit committee, regulator, customer
     tender).
   - The **audit-committee-facing note** — how the deliverable
     will land at the audit committee (the specific cycle it
     is scoped to, the specific audit-committee ask it answers).
   - The engagement is authored as one file per firm engaged
     (typically only one of the four is engaged in a given
     year); if the enterprise runs no Big Four AI-assurance
     engagement, this file records the head's specific
     recommendation and the specific reason no engagement is
     scoped this year.

6. **The per-body P&L — specialist AI-audit firms.** Author the
   P&L for any specialist AI-audit engagement the enterprise
   runs or plans to run — the boutique algorithmic-audit firm,
   the sector-specialist AI-audit firm, the red-team-plus-
   assurance hybrid provider. `needs-research` marker on the
   current specialist landscape before naming specific firms
   in the enterprise's assurance dossier. Populate:
   - The specific firm(s), with the specific AI-audit craft
     the firm is engaged for.
   - The **contracted fee envelope** — the SOW-per-engagement
     shape at the specialist firm's scale (typically lower
     than Big Four scale, often with faster cycle-time).
   - The **scope** and **timing** as with the other
     sub-categories.
   - The **deliverable shape** and audience.
   - The **substantive-fit note** — why the specialist is
     the right fit for this specific scope, and what the
     specialist's engagement adds that the CB, the
     independent-assurance bodies, and the Big Four do not.

7. **The multi-body coordination map.** Author the coordination
   map that keeps the per-body P&Ls from compounding into an
   unmanaged programme. The map has three layers per the
   chapter-06 discipline:
   - The **scope-overlap map** — a matrix whose rows are AI
     systems (or risk domains, at the head's discretion) and
     whose columns are the assurance bodies engaged. Each cell
     records whether the body covers the system, at what
     depth (surface / substantive), against what criteria, on
     what cadence. Overlaps are named as deliberate (where
     the head is intentionally stacking bodies for audit-
     committee purposes) or accidental (where the head will
     remove the overlap in the next negotiation cycle). Gaps
     are named as intentional (the system is low-risk; the
     system is covered by adjacent enterprise assurance) or
     accidental (which the head closes).
   - The **timing coordination** — the assurance calendar
     alongside the AIMS operating calendar
     ([[mod-102-running-the-program]] chapter 02) and the
     audit-committee calendar
     ([[mod-103-board-and-audit-committee-reporting]] chapter
     02). Surveillance audits, IAAIS engagements, BABL AI
     audits, Big Four SOWs, and specialist engagements are
     staggered so evidence-production demands do not cluster
     in the same six-week window and so audit-committee
     deliverables arrive in the cycle they are scoped to.
   - The **CAE boundary** — the scope-boundary between the
     CAE's internal audit (third line) and the head's
     external assurance. Which risk domains the CAE covers
     internally, which the head covers externally, where the
     overlaps are deliberate, and where the coverage handoffs
     are documented jointly with the CAE per
     [[mod-108-leading-the-governance-function]] chapter 05.
   The coordination map is the artefact the CFO's controller
   will read against the four-body fee stack; it is what turns
   a compounding fee into a defensible portfolio.

8. **The auditor-independence overlay for Big Four.** Author
   the auditor-independence overlay that governs any Big Four
   engagement. The overlay names:
   - The **specific rule set that applies to the enterprise**
     — SEC and PCAOB rules for a US-registered issuer, FRC
     rules for a UK premium-listed issuer, ESMA-adjacent
     rules for an EU public-interest entity, and equivalent
     frameworks in Australia, Canada, Singapore, Hong Kong,
     or wherever the enterprise's footprint fires an
     independence-rule set. `<!-- needs-research: verify the
     specific auditor-independence rule set applicable to
     [the enterprise's jurisdictional / listing footprint]
     against the current SEC/PCAOB / FRC / ESMA / equivalent
     rulebook -->` markers wherever the head has not yet
     confirmed the specific applicable rule.
   - The **enterprise's financial-statement auditor** — named,
     with the specific classes of non-audit service the firm
     is restricted from providing under the applicable rules.
   - The **eligibility check per Big Four firm** — for each of
     the four, whether the firm is eligible for the specific
     AI-assurance engagement scope, with the specific reason
     (financial-statement-auditor restriction, prior-engagement
     conflict, or the firm is eligible).
   - The **coordination discipline** — the specific process
     the head runs before scoping a Big Four engagement
     (controller consultation, audit committee chair
     consultation, firm's own independence attestation in the
     SOW). A Big Four engagement scoped without the process
     is the auditor-independence-violation anti-pattern from
     chapter 06 and is not permitted.

9. **The scope-and-fee negotiation posture per body.** Author
   the head's specific negotiation posture for each body,
   using the chapter-06 discipline. For each body:
   - What the head **does** negotiate — scope, engagement
     mechanics (timing, evidence-access process, response-
     window, deliverable format, audiences), fee for the
     scoped work, partner-and-manager assignment (where the
     body has partner-and-manager structure).
   - What the head **does not** negotiate — the opinion, any
     consideration that would compromise the body's
     independence, the fee-per-hour on the fixed-scope-fixed-
     fee CB engagement (where a talk-down on fee-per-hour
     usually produces a talk-down on audit-days).
   - The specific negotiation posture per body — the head's
     opening ask, the head's fallback, and the specific
     escalation channel with the body if the negotiation stalls.

10. **The finding-remediation tracking file.** Author the
    findings-remediation tracking discipline underneath the
    assurance-body P&L. The file composes the findings-tracking
    discipline from [[mod-102-running-the-program]] chapter 04
    and chapter 06 with the assurance-body-specific overlay
    from chapter 06 of this module:
    - Every finding from every assurance body enters the same
      tracking file within the head's committed response
      window, regardless of source.
    - Findings functionally identical across bodies are
      tracked once with sources attributed, not twice.
    - The audit committee sees a single consolidated findings-
      status report per cycle
      ([[mod-103-board-and-audit-committee-reporting]]) with
      each finding attributed to its source body and its
      remediation status named.
    - Findings not closed on the committed timeline trigger
      the escalation defined in the running-programme
      discipline; they do not carry silently into the next
      assurance cycle where the follow-up engagement re-audits
      the un-closed finding at additional cost (the finding-
      not-remediated escalating-fee anti-pattern from chapter
      06).

11. **The CB rotation trade-off case.** Author a specific case
    that walks the CB rotation decision honestly, using the
    chapter-06 P&L discipline and the corresponding relationship
    discipline in [[mod-108-leading-the-governance-function]]
    chapter 06. The case names:
    - The **continuing-CB case** — continuity, deep
      familiarity with the AIMS, lower fee in the continuing
      cycle, but a weakening independence signal as the CB
      and the enterprise co-evolve over multiple cycles.
    - The **rotating-CB case** — refreshed independence
      signal, but higher first-cycle cost (the new CB
      re-runs Stage 1, Stage 2, and an inaugural certification
      cycle), discovery-cycle time in the function's calendar,
      and the possibility that the new CB is materially more
      or less rigorous than the incumbent.
    - The **decision framing for the audit committee** — the
      audit committee owns the rotation decision at high-level;
      the head owns the P&L analysis underneath. The case names
      the specific trade-offs the audit committee has to weigh
      and the head's specific recommendation with the specific
      reason.

12. **The CFO / audit-committee coordination artefact.** Author
    the two-page pack the head takes to the CFO's controller
    and (in adjusted form) to the audit committee chair. The
    pack compresses the per-body P&Ls, the multi-body
    coordination map, and the independence overlay into:
    - The **assurance-fee line** — the total assurance spend
      by sub-category, the specific role each sub-category
      plays in the enterprise's assurance posture, and the
      trajectory across the next three years.
    - The **classification note** — for each engagement, the
      controller's classification (external audit versus
      advisory), with the specific reason.
    - The **independence-posture note** — the auditor-
      independence overlay in one paragraph, with the specific
      rule set that applies and the specific eligibility
      conclusion per Big Four firm.
    - The **audit-committee-facing note** — how the
      assurance-body deliverables land at the audit committee
      across the coming year, aligned to the audit-committee
      calendar.
    - The head's **specific asks** — the assurance-line
      ratification, the specific SOW authorisations required
      for each engagement, the specific coordination the head
      needs from the controller (fee-line ownership) and the
      audit committee chair (independence-posture ratification,
      CB rotation decision framing).

13. **The failure-mode audit.** Author a short section — one
    paragraph per failure mode — naming how the pack detects
    and recovers from each of the seven chapter-06 anti-
    patterns:
    - Single-assurance-body over-dependence.
    - Audit-fee compression.
    - "One-of-each because we should" without coordination.
    - Audit-friendly-body selection.
    - Auditor-independence violation.
    - Finding-not-remediated escalating-fee.
    - Stealth-scope-creep.

## Acceptance criteria

- Every per-body P&L names the **contracted fee envelope
  structure** — fixed-scope-fixed-fee-multi-year for the CB,
  retained-scope-per-engagement for ForHumanity and BABL AI,
  SOW-per-engagement for Big Four and specialists. The
  envelope structure is populated; the specific dollar figures
  are populated only where the enterprise has a signed
  engagement letter, and are otherwise left as ranges or
  `needs-research` markers against the specific proposal in
  hand. A P&L that populates specific dollar figures without a
  signed engagement letter (or that populates them from a
  guess) fails the criterion.
- Every per-body P&L names the **scope**, the **timing**, and
  the **deliverable shape** as specific artefacts. A P&L with
  a scope populated as "the enterprise's AI systems" without
  the specific AIMS boundary, or a timing populated as "in
  the coming year" without the specific week or month, or a
  deliverable populated as "the audit report" without the
  specific report shape and audience, fails the criterion.
- The **auditor-independence overlay** names the specific rule
  set that applies to the enterprise — SEC / PCAOB, FRC,
  ESMA-adjacent, APAC-equivalent, or the specific national
  framework — with `<!-- needs-research: ... -->` markers
  where the head has not yet confirmed the specific applicable
  rule against the current rulebook. An overlay that names
  "auditor-independence rules" generically without the
  specific rule set fails the criterion. An overlay that
  names a specific rule with certainty where the head has not
  actually confirmed it fails equally — the `needs-research`
  marker is what makes the artefact honest.
- The **multi-body coordination map** names the non-
  overlapping-scope discipline explicitly. Deliberate overlaps
  are named as deliberate (with the specific audit-committee
  or regulator-facing reason); accidental overlaps are named
  as accidental and carry a specific removal action for the
  next negotiation cycle; gaps are named as intentional (with
  the specific reason) or accidental (with the specific
  closure action). A coordination map that does not enumerate
  overlaps and gaps explicitly fails the criterion.
- The **CB rotation trade-off case** includes both sides
  honestly — the continuing-CB case with its independence-
  signal weakening acknowledged, and the rotating-CB case
  with its first-cycle cost and discovery-cycle burden
  acknowledged. A case that presents rotation as unambiguously
  correct without the discovery-cycle cost on the table, or
  presents continuation as unambiguously correct without the
  independence-signal weakening on the table, fails the
  criterion.
- The **CFO / audit-committee coordination artefact** names
  the classification per engagement (external audit versus
  advisory), the assurance-fee-line trajectory across three
  years, and the head's specific asks. An artefact that
  presents the assurance spend as a single undifferentiated
  line without the per-engagement classification fails the
  criterion.
- The **failure-mode audit** names, for each of the seven
  chapter-06 anti-patterns, at least one specific detection
  signal and one specific recovery action. An anti-pattern
  paragraph that names the pattern without a detection signal
  and a recovery action fails the criterion.
- Any factual claim about a specific assurance body's
  accreditation status, service catalog, credential structure,
  or firm-level positioning carries a `<!-- needs-research:
  verify [specific fact] against [body's published source] -->`
  marker where the fact is not independently verified. The
  chapter itself carries such markers; the exercise inherits
  the discipline.
- No specific audit-fee dollar range appears anywhere in the
  pack unless it is drawn from a signed engagement letter the
  enterprise holds. Ranges from a proposal in negotiation, a
  peer-set anecdote, or an industry-report figure are marked
  as such or omitted. A P&L that claims a specific dollar
  range as if it were an established benchmark fails the
  criterion.
- No claim that any assurance body is best, cheapest, or most
  rigorous appears in the pack. The head selects on
  substantive competence, market standing, and fit with the
  enterprise's assurance need; the head does not rank bodies
  as a category. A pack that ranks bodies (BSI over TÜV
  Rheinland, EY over PwC, ForHumanity over BABL AI) fails
  the criterion.

## Stretch goals

- Author the **audit-committee annual assurance-plan slide** —
  the specific one-page slide the audit committee chair reads
  as the annual view of the enterprise's assurance posture.
  The slide names each body engaged, each body's specific
  role in the posture, the trajectory across the coming year,
  the independence-posture note, and the head's specific
  recommendations for audit-committee ratification (the
  assurance-plan approval, the CB rotation decision framing,
  the Big Four scope authorisation).
- Draft the **regulator-facing assurance dossier extract** —
  the extract of the pack that the head can share with a
  regulator on request (typically in a supervisory dialogue
  per [[mod-104-regulator-engagement]]). The extract preserves
  the per-body engagements and the coordination discipline
  but does not disclose the fee envelopes, since fees are
  commercially sensitive and not typically shared with
  regulators; the extract names the enterprise's independent-
  assurance investment as substantive without disclosing the
  commercial terms.
- Author the **customer-tender assurance response template** —
  the specific template the head hands to the CRO's tender-
  response team when a prospective customer's tender requires
  an assurance-posture statement. The template names the
  per-body engagements, the specific deliverables the customer
  can rely on (a public-disclosure-suitable ForHumanity IAAIS
  summary, a BABL AI algorithmic-audit summary, the ISO/IEC
  42001 certification), and the specific engagements the
  customer would need a supplementary letter for
  (typically the Big Four SOW is not shared verbatim; a
  summary letter is authored per tender).
- Draft the **assurance-body-underperformance escalation
  runbook** — the specific runbook the head runs where an
  assurance body underperforms (the CB's assigned auditor is
  materially less AI-competent than the sales cycle indicated;
  the ForHumanity auditor's engagement runs materially over
  timeline without justification; the Big Four's assigned
  partner is substituted without approval; the specialist's
  deliverable is materially thinner than the SOW committed).
  The runbook covers the escalation channel with the body,
  the specific commercial remedy the head negotiates
  (partner-substitution rework, fee-reduction on the sub-
  substantive deliverable), and the specific decision point
  at which the head recommends terminating the relationship.
- Author the **five-year assurance-body trajectory** — the
  head's specific view of how the mix of assurance bodies
  evolves across the next five years. Which bodies the
  enterprise will continue to run, which the enterprise will
  rotate (the CB in year three or year four, per the rotation
  trade-off case), which the enterprise will retire (a
  specialist engagement that has served its purpose), and
  which the enterprise will add (a sector-specific
  independent-assurance body that emerges as sector regulation
  matures per [[mod-111-sector-program-leadership]]). The
  trajectory is a running artefact the head updates annually.

## Notes and starter guidance

The most common first-cut failure is a pack that treats the
assurance-body relationship P&L as an ordinary vendor P&L. The
head who applies the discount-negotiation discipline from the
GRC-for-AI vendor P&L (chapter 03), the observability vendor
P&L (chapter 04), or the runtime-security vendor P&L (chapter
05) to the assurance-body engagement has misread the category.
Assurance bodies are judgement vendors; the head who negotiates
aggressive discount has weakened the credibility of the
opinion the enterprise is paying for and has therefore reduced
the return on the entire assurance spend. Force the inverted
discipline throughout the pack — the head negotiates scope,
engagement mechanics, and fee-for-scoped-work, not opinion or
fee-per-hour on the CB engagement or leniency on the deliverable.

The second common failure is the temptation to negotiate the
assurance-body opinion. The head who signals to a CB that a
minor non-conformity would be an inconvenient finding, or who
signals to an independent-assurance auditor that a scoped
system's exclusion would be commercially convenient, or who
signals to a Big Four partner that the audit committee expects
a reasonable-assurance opinion where the substantive evidence
supports only limited assurance, has crossed the line the head
does not cross. The head does not negotiate the opinion. The
head negotiates scope, timing, mechanics, and fee. The
distinction is what preserves the assurance body's
independence — which is what preserves the value of the
opinion the enterprise is paying for. A pack that reads as if
the head is arranging the opinion is a pack the audit
committee, the regulator, and the enterprise's customers will
eventually discount.

The third common failure is the temptation to shop for the
cheapest CB (or the cheapest independent-assurance body, or
the cheapest Big Four SOW). The audit-friendly-body selection
anti-pattern from chapter 06 warns against selecting a body
because the head expects a lenient opinion; the cheapest-CB
temptation is the same anti-pattern from a different angle.
The head has budget pressure. The CFO's controller reads the
assurance-fee line as a category to be managed. But the
cheapest CB is not necessarily the CB whose certification the
market reads as substantive, and the discount on the fee is
recovered many times over in the discount on the certification's
market weight. Force the substantive-competence-and-rigour
selection discipline — the head selects on the CB's AI-
competent auditor bench, the CB's market standing, and the
fit with the enterprise's assurance need. Fee is a floor, not
the driver.

The fourth common failure is the auditor-independence trap.
The head has a Big Four SOW to scope. The head reaches out to
a partner the head has a good working relationship with.
The partner is at the enterprise's financial-statement auditor.
The engagement is scoped, the SOW is signed, and the audit
committee chair raises the engagement as an independence
concern the next month. Either the engagement is unwound at
cost, or the head's judgement is questioned, or both. The
overlay in step 8 is the specific discipline that prevents
this. Every Big Four engagement runs through the controller-
consultation, the audit-committee-chair-consultation, and the
firm's own independence-attestation in the SOW. The head does
not scope a Big Four AI-assurance engagement to the enterprise's
financial-statement auditor without the eligibility check
completed and documented. The temptation to skip the check for
speed is what produces the anti-pattern; the discipline is
what prevents it.

The fifth failure is the compounding multi-body programme.
The enterprise runs a CB, a ForHumanity engagement, a BABL AI
engagement, and a Big Four SOW in the same year with
overlapping scopes and no coordination. The total spend is
high, the findings duplicate across engagements, the function's
operating capacity is consumed producing evidence rather than
closing findings, and the audit committee receives four
separate reports with overlapping substance. The multi-body
coordination map in step 7 is what turns the compounding into
a defensible portfolio. Author the map genuinely — the scope-
overlap map with the deliberate versus accidental overlaps
named, the timing coordination that staggers evidence-
production demands, and the CAE boundary that keeps the head
from paying for external assurance in a domain the CAE is
already covering internally. The coordination discipline is
what the CFO's controller reads as evidence that the four-body
programme is a portfolio rather than a compounding fee stack.

The pack is a living artefact. Version it, date it, refresh it
at the annual audit-committee assurance-plan cycle, update the
per-body P&Ls when scope changes materially or fees adjust,
update the coordination map when a body's scope moves or a
new body is added, update the independence overlay when the
enterprise's listing footprint or financial-statement auditor
changes, and update the failure-mode audit when a new anti-
pattern surfaces in the enterprise's practice or in adjacent
enterprises' post-mortems. A pack that has not been touched
since it was authored is a pack the assurance programme's
evolution has already outrun.
