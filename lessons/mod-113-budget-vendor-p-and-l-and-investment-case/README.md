# mod-113 — Budget, Vendor P&L, and Investment Case

**Estimated effort:** 14 hours
**Position on the track:** the thirteenth module — the
budget-and-P&L discipline underneath the function shape from
[[mod-108-leading-the-governance-function]], the cross-enterprise
CxO relationships from
[[mod-109-cross-enterprise-leadership]], the measurement backbone
from [[mod-107-program-measurement-and-maturity]], the running
program from [[mod-102-running-the-program]], and the audit-
committee reporting posture from
[[mod-103-board-and-audit-committee-reporting]]. This module is
what turns the head from a functional leader who runs a program
into a P&L-carrying executive who negotiates the function's
existence with the CFO, the CEO, the audit committee, and — where
the enterprise carries one — the Chief AI Officer.

## What this module is for

The AI-governance function is a cost centre. It does not sell a
product. It does not book revenue. Its budget is negotiated
annually against a CFO who is looking for reasons to compress
cost, an audit committee who is looking for reasons to demand
more assurance, and a business who is looking for reasons to
route around the function when a release is under time pressure.
The head who cannot defend the budget line — headcount, vendor
spend, external audit, training and certification, incident
reserve, benchmarking, community leadership — with a defensible
multi-year plan is a head who watches the function get compressed
in the next cost cycle, watches vendor renewals get denied on
"we already spend enough on GRC" arguments, and watches the
external-assurance investment get treated as optional theatre.

The failure mode this module opens with is not a head without
a budget. It is a head who has a budget line but has never
authored the multi-year investment case that makes the line
defensible — programme case, transformation case, incident-
preparedness case — and who consequently negotiates the line
each year on the CFO's terms rather than on the head's. It is a
head who signs a GRC-for-AI vendor contract at RFP without
running the vendor as a P&L over the term. It is a head who
carries the same ISO/IEC 42001 certification-body relationship
across a decade without ever surfacing the cost-and-value
trade-off. It is a head who cannot answer "what is the ROI on
your function this year" with a modelled number.

This module answers eight connected questions.

1. How does the head own the AI-governance function budget line
   across all its cost categories — headcount (feeding out of
   the plan authored in
   [[mod-108-leading-the-governance-function]] chapter 02),
   GRC-for-AI vendor spend, AI-observability vendor spend, AI-
   runtime security vendor spend, external assurance and audit
   spend, tooling depreciation, training and certification
   spend, incident-response reserve, external-benchmarking
   spend, and community-leadership spend (travel, dues,
   speaking) — with a defensible multi-year plan?
2. How does the head author the multi-year investment case to
   the CFO, the CEO, and the audit committee — the programme
   case (headcount + tooling + external audit + maturity
   progression + regulatory-change readiness), the
   transformation case (moving off spreadsheets onto a
   certifiable AIMS runtime, replacing point tools with a
   governance-of-record platform), and the incident-
   preparedness case (contingency reserve, incident-response
   retainer, tabletop exercise budget)?
3. How does the head manage the GRC-for-AI vendor P&L at CxO
   scope — Credo AI, Holistic AI, ModelOp, Monitaur,
   ServiceNow AI Control Tower, IBM watsonx.governance, Fairly
   AI, Enzai, Trustible, Verify AI, and adjacent — with the
   consolidation-or-expansion decisions, the renewal
   negotiation posture, and the escalation discipline with the
   vendor account executive when SLAs slip?
4. How does the head manage the AI-observability vendor P&L —
   Fiddler AI, Arthur AI, WhyLabs, Evidently, and adjacent —
   in coordination with `ai-evaluation-engineer` (level 35) on
   the tooling requirements the observability stack has to
   meet?
5. How does the head manage the AI-runtime security vendor
   P&L — Robust Intelligence, Lakera Guard, Calypso AI,
   HiddenLayer, Protect AI, HydroX AI, Cranium, TrojAI, and
   adjacent — in coordination with `ai-infra-security` (level
   35) on the runtime tooling the security stack has to
   deliver?
6. How does the head manage the assurance-body relationship
   P&L — ISO/IEC 42001 certification body (BSI, PECB, TÜV,
   DNV, DEKRA), ForHumanity IAAIS auditor, BABL AI Algorithm
   Bias Auditor, external Big Four audit engagement (Deloitte,
   EY, PwC, KPMG), specialist AI-audit firms — as the
   assurance-cost P&L that the audit committee reads as the
   independence-and-rigour signal?
7. How does the head build the ROI case with defensible
   modelling — avoided-loss (regulator-fine-avoidance,
   incident-cost-avoidance, third-party-defence cost
   avoidance), deal-enablement (trust-margin, regulator-
   approval-speed premium, tender-eligibility uplift,
   enterprise-customer AI-trust requirements coverage), trust-
   margin (customer-retention delta, employee-attraction
   delta), and transformation (spreadsheet-elimination FTE-
   recovery) — so the function is defended on modelled value
   rather than on narrative?
8. How does the head coordinate with the Chief AI Officer
   (level 70) on the enterprise-wide AI budget envelope — the
   AI-governance function budget is a component of a larger AI
   budget, and the head negotiates the governance-function
   line as a proportion of the whole; where a CAIO does not
   exist, the head negotiates directly with the CIO, CTO,
   CDO, and CFO?

The module produces the operating artefacts the head carries
across the annual budget cycle — the multi-year budget model,
the investment-case pack, the six vendor P&L files (GRC-for-AI,
observability, runtime security, ISO 42001 CB, independent
assurance, Big Four), the ROI model, and the CAIO/CxO
negotiation dossier.

## How to work through this module

The module is designed to be worked linearly. Chapter 01 opens
with the budget line itself, because everything else in the
module either produces a line item in the budget or defends one.
Chapter 02 authors the multi-year investment case — the shape
the CFO, CEO, and audit committee read the budget against, and
the discipline that turns a single-year ask into a defensible
multi-year story. Chapters 03 through 06 walk the four vendor
P&L categories the head runs — GRC-for-AI, AI observability,
AI runtime security, assurance and audit bodies — each with the
distinct commercial shape, the distinct coordination surface
(with the level-35 evaluator and the level-35 security engineer
in chapters 04 and 05, with the audit committee and the CRO in
chapter 06), and the distinct escalation path when a vendor is
materially underperforming. Chapter 07 authors the ROI case
that turns the budget from a cost defence into a modelled-value
argument. Chapter 08 closes with the coordination discipline
that puts the governance-function budget into its correct
proportion inside the enterprise-wide AI budget envelope — with
the Chief AI Officer where one exists, and with the CIO / CTO /
CDO / CFO directly where one does not.

Exercises follow the same order. Exercise 1 produces the
function's multi-year budget model. Exercise 2 produces the
investment-case pack. Exercise 3 produces the GRC-for-AI
vendor P&L for the enterprise's specific incumbent (or, if
none, its shortlist). Exercise 4 produces the paired
observability-and-runtime-security vendor P&L in coordination
with the level-35 evaluator and level-35 security engineer.
Exercise 5 produces the assurance-body relationship P&L.
Exercise 6 produces the ROI case with the four modelling axes.

By the end of the module, a working head-of-role has produced:

- A multi-year budget model with every named cost category as
  a line item, per-line multi-year trajectory, and per-line
  defence-of-ask; feeding the annual CFO budget cycle and the
  quarterly forecast-refresh cycle.
- An investment-case pack with the programme case, the
  transformation case, and the incident-preparedness case,
  each authored for the CFO, the CEO, and the audit committee
  distinctly.
- Six vendor P&L files (GRC-for-AI incumbent or shortlist,
  observability incumbent or shortlist, runtime-security
  incumbent or shortlist, ISO 42001 CB, independent-assurance
  body, Big Four external audit) with per-vendor total cost of
  ownership, per-vendor SLA position, per-vendor renewal
  disposition, and per-vendor escalation path.
- An ROI model with the four axes (avoided-loss, deal-
  enablement, trust-margin, transformation), each modelled with
  the defensible-input discipline, cited assumptions, and the
  sensitivity band the CFO's finance team will re-run against.
- A CAIO/CxO coordination dossier that positions the
  governance-function budget as a proportion of the enterprise
  AI budget envelope, with the CAIO-scope negotiation stance
  where a CAIO exists and the CIO/CTO/CDO/CFO scope
  negotiation stance where one does not.

These artefacts extend the operating substrate from
[[mod-102-running-the-program]] and the measurement backbone
from [[mod-107-program-measurement-and-maturity]] into the
budget-and-P&L discipline the CFO and audit committee read as
the signal that the function is being run as a business rather
than as a cost queue. They compose with the CxO peer
relationships in [[mod-109-cross-enterprise-leadership]], the
incident-leadership discipline in [[mod-110-incident-
leadership]], the sector-program leadership in
[[mod-111-sector-program-leadership]], and the community
investments in
[[mod-112-certifications-career-path-community-leadership]].
