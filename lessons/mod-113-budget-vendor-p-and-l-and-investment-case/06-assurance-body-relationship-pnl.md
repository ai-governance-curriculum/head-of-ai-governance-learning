# The Assurance-Body Relationship P&L — ISO/IEC 42001 CB, ForHumanity, BABL AI, Big Four, Specialist AI-Audit Firms

## Motivation

The assurance-body relationship is different in kind from every
other vendor category this module runs. The GRC-for-AI vendor
(chapter [03](03-grc-for-ai-vendor-pnl.md)), the AI-observability
vendor (chapter [04](04-ai-observability-vendor-pnl.md)), and the
AI-runtime security vendor (chapter
[05](05-ai-runtime-security-vendor-pnl.md)) are *enabler* vendors
— they produce the system of record, the drift and evaluation
signal, and the runtime control surface the function operates
against. The head runs their P&L on the ordinary commercial
disciplines — total cost of ownership, renewal negotiation,
consolidation-versus-expansion, escalation-when-SLA-slips. The
head who negotiates a firmer discount on the observability
vendor has produced better value for the function.

Assurance bodies are *judgement* vendors. They produce the
independent opinion the audit committee reads as the enterprise's
assurance posture, the regulator reads as the enterprise's
willingness-to-be-scrutinised signal, the enterprise's customers
read in the tender response, and the market reads in the trust
posture. The commercial discipline is inverted. The head who
negotiates aggressive discount from an assurance body has
weakened the credibility of the assurance the enterprise is
paying for and reduced the return on the entire assurance spend.
A discounted independent opinion is not a bargain; it is a
devalued opinion.

The P&L is real. External audit and assurance fees are a
material line — often the second- or third-largest line in the
function's non-headcount spend, after the GRC-for-AI platform —
and they escalate rapidly with scope. Multi-body assurance
programmes compound: an ISO/IEC 42001 certification cycle running
in parallel with a ForHumanity IAAIS engagement running in
parallel with a Big Four AI-assurance engagement running in
parallel with a specialist algorithmic-audit engagement produces
a total assurance spend the CFO scrutinises as a category even
where each line individually looks defensible.

The head therefore runs the assurance-body P&L with a discipline
distinct from the enabler-vendor P&L. The head negotiates
*scope*, *engagement mechanics*, and *deliverable shape*. The
head does not negotiate the *opinion*. The head runs the
coordination across multiple simultaneous bodies so their scopes
do not duplicate. The head coordinates with the CFO's controller
on the audit-fee line, with the audit committee chair on the
independence signal the mix of engagements produces, and with
the Chief Audit Executive on the boundary between the internal
audit the CAE owns and the external assurance the head owns.

The relationship discipline itself — how the head runs the
account, the standing cadence, the escalation channel where a
body underperforms — lives in
[[mod-108-leading-the-governance-function]] chapter 06. This
chapter carries the commercial and P&L shape underneath: scope
negotiation, engagement-mechanics negotiation, multi-body
coordination, the Big Four auditor-independence overlay, and the
renewal-and-scope-adjustment cadence that turn the assurance-
body spend into a defensible line the CFO and audit committee
read as substantive.

## Core concept — the assurance-body category map

The assurance-body category has five sub-categories, each with
its own commercial shape, its own cadence, and its own
contribution to the enterprise's assurance posture.

### ISO/IEC 42001 certification body

The ISO/IEC 42001 certification body (CB) is an accredited
conformity-assessment body. Accreditation runs from an IAF-member
accreditation body — UKAS in the United Kingdom, ANAB in the
United States, DAkkS in Germany, and the equivalent national
accreditation body wherever the enterprise operates. Named CBs
that hold or are pursuing accreditation for ISO/IEC 42001 include
BSI, PECB, TÜV Rheinland, TÜV SÜD, DNV, and DEKRA.
<!-- needs-research: verify current accredited-CB roster for ISO/IEC 42001 against ISO/IEC 42006 and IAF-member accreditation registers before citing at CFO or audit-committee depth. -->

The CB runs the certification cycle: an initial certification
audit at year zero (Stage 1 followed by Stage 2), annual
surveillance audits in years one and two, and a re-certification
audit at year three, repeating thereafter. The commercial shape
is fixed-scope, fixed-fee, multi-year — the CB scopes the audit
in audit-days-per-cycle at contracting, and the fee is a proxy
for those days. The head does not negotiate fee-per-hour; the
head negotiates scope, audit-team assignment, and cycle timing.

The CB's certification is the ISO/IEC 42001 conformity signal.
The audit committee, the regulator, and the enterprise's
customers read it as an accredited third-party opinion that the
enterprise operates an AI Management System (AIMS) that conforms
to the standard. A CB whose certification the market discounts —
because the CB's competence is thin, its reputation is that of
an audit-friendly body, or its accreditation itself is contested
— is a CB whose certification produces less value per dollar
spent.

### ForHumanity IAAIS auditor

ForHumanity is an independent AI-audit body whose Independent
Audit of AI Systems (IAAIS) criteria cover algorithmic risk,
ethics, bias, cybersecurity, and trust, with sector-specific
overlays. The audit is delivered by ForHumanity-certified
auditors — practitioners who have completed the ForHumanity
audit-preparation programme and are qualified to deliver IAAIS
engagements.
<!-- needs-research: verify current IAAIS criteria set and auditor roster at forhumanity.center. -->

The commercial shape is retained-scope-with-per-engagement-fee.
The enterprise establishes a retained relationship with a
ForHumanity-certified auditor and commissions specific IAAIS
engagements against the retainer — scoped to a specific
high-impact AI system, to a sector-specific IAAIS overlay, or to
an AIMS-adjacent socio-technical assessment. Each engagement has
its own scope-and-fee negotiation; the retainer runs the
relationship.

### BABL AI Algorithm Bias Auditor and algorithmic-audit provider

BABL AI is an independent AI-audit provider anchored in fairness
and algorithmic-audit craft. Its service catalog spans
algorithmic-audit engagements, credentialed auditor training
(Algorithm Bias Auditor and adjacent credentials), and advisory
services on algorithmic risk, with engagements scoped to specific
high-impact AI systems (hiring, lending, healthcare, education,
insurance) and to sector-specific overlays.
<!-- needs-research: verify current BABL AI service catalog at babl.ai. -->

The commercial shape resembles ForHumanity's — retained-scope
with per-engagement fee — although engagements are typically
anchored in the algorithmic-bias-audit craft (a scoped audit of
a specific AI system against fairness criteria) rather than in a
broad management-system criteria set.

### Big Four external audit engagement

Deloitte, EY, PwC, and KPMG each operate an AI-assurance practice
within their broader assurance line of business, typically
covering AI-risk-management-framework alignment (NIST AI RMF,
ISO/IEC 42001), AI-model risk management, AI-governance-programme
design and assessment, and adjacent advisory work.
<!-- needs-research: verify current AI-assurance service positioning at each of Deloitte, EY, PwC, KPMG. -->

The commercial shape is statement-of-work per engagement. The
head, the CFO's controller, and (where relevant) the audit
committee chair negotiate the specific SOW — scope, partner and
manager assignment, deliverable shape, timing, and fee — for
each engagement. The Big Four engage in the enterprise's
ordinary procurement process; the SOW is the substantive
artefact.

Big Four engagements carry an overlay unique in this category:
the *auditor-independence overlay*. If any of the Big Four is
the enterprise's financial-statement auditor, that firm is
generally restricted from providing certain non-audit services
to the enterprise under auditor-independence rules — SEC and
PCAOB rules in the United States, FRC rules in the United
Kingdom, and equivalent regulator frameworks in the EU,
Australia, Canada, Singapore, and elsewhere.
<!-- needs-research: verify the current applicable auditor-independence rule set (SEC/PCAOB, FRC, ESMA/IAASB, equivalent) against the enterprise's specific jurisdictional and listing footprint before scoping any Big Four AI-assurance engagement. -->
The head does not scope a Big Four engagement without confirming
which firms are eligible under the enterprise's independence
posture.

### Specialist AI-audit firms

A growing category of specialist AI-audit boutiques offers
algorithmic audit, sector-specific AI audit, and red-team-and-
assurance hybrid engagements. The specialist category is
heterogeneous — firms differ substantially in scope, methodology,
and depth. The head evaluates each on substantive AI-audit
craft, named-partner assignment, case history, and the market
weight the firm's opinions carry.
<!-- needs-research: verify the current specialist AI-audit firm landscape — including boutique algorithmic-audit firms, sector-specialist AI-audit firms, and hybrid red-team-plus-assurance providers — before naming specific firms in the enterprise's assurance dossier. -->

The commercial shape is typically statement-of-work per
engagement, similar to the Big Four SOW pattern though at lower
firm scale and often with faster cycle time.

## The distinct commercial shape of assurance-body engagements

Each sub-category has a distinct commercial shape the head runs
against.

### Fixed-scope, fixed-fee, multi-year cycle (CB)

The ISO/IEC 42001 CB engages on a fixed-scope, fixed-fee, multi-
year cycle. At contracting, the CB assesses the scope of the
enterprise's AIMS (the AI systems in scope, the sites, the
functions, the interfaces with the broader enterprise), scopes
the required audit-days per cycle, and commits to a fee that is
a proxy for those days. The head negotiates:

- **Scope.** The specific boundary of the AIMS to be audited.
  Over-scoping produces excess audit-days and inflated fees;
  under-scoping produces a certification the market discounts
  because it does not cover the AI systems the market cares
  about.
- **Audit-team assignment.** The specific auditor(s) and, where
  the CB uses one, the technical committee reviewer(s) assigned
  to the certification. AI-competent auditors produce a
  substantive audit; generic management-systems auditors without
  AI depth produce an audit the head and audit committee find
  shallow.
- **Cycle timing.** The initial audit, the surveillance audits,
  and the re-certification, timed to avoid concurrent evidence-
  production demands from other assurance engagements (see the
  multi-body coordination sub-section below).

The head does not negotiate fee-per-hour. The fee is what the
audit-days-per-cycle produces at the CB's standard rate. A head
who talks the CB down on fee-per-hour has usually talked the CB
down on audit-days-per-cycle, and has therefore reduced the
substantive audit depth.

### Retained-scope with per-engagement fee (ForHumanity, BABL AI)

Independent-assurance providers typically engage on retained-
scope with per-engagement fees. The head establishes a retained
relationship — confirming the provider's competence, the
enterprise's substantive assurance appetite, and the shape of
engagements the enterprise expects to commission — and then
commissions specific engagements against it. Per-engagement
negotiation covers:

- **Engagement scope.** The specific AI system, sector overlay,
  or criteria set the engagement covers. Scope creep in
  independent-assurance engagements is common and expensive.
- **Timeline.** Start and end dates, interim-checkpoint dates,
  and the deliverable date.
- **Evidence-access commitments.** What evidence the enterprise
  makes available, on what timeline, and with what personnel
  access.
- **Deliverable shape.** Written report, executive summary,
  findings-and-recommendations letter, or public-disclosure-
  suitable report, and the audiences it is authored for.
- **Fee.** Typically fixed-fee for a scoped engagement, sometimes
  with a time-and-materials contingency clause for scope
  adjustments.

### Statement-of-work per engagement (Big Four and specialist)

Big Four and specialist AI-audit firms engage on per-engagement
statements of work. The SOW is the substantive commercial
artefact. Beyond the fee, the head negotiates:

- **Partner-and-manager assignment.** The AI-competent partner
  accountable for the engagement, and the manager running it
  day-to-day. The firm's brand is a proxy; the specific
  assignment is the substantive commitment.
- **Deliverable shape.** Limited-assurance report, reasonable-
  assurance report, agreed-upon-procedures report, or advisory
  work — see the Big Four sub-section below.
- **Timeline and evidence-access commitments.** As with the
  independent-assurance providers.
- **Auditor-independence attestation.** For Big Four engagements,
  the SOW includes the firm's attestation that the engagement
  does not violate the auditor-independence rules that apply to
  the enterprise.

## The independence-preservation P&L discipline

The commercial inversion in this category is the discipline that
distinguishes it from every other vendor category in the module.
The head runs the P&L with three specific disciplines that do
not apply — or apply less sharply — elsewhere.

### The head negotiates scope and engagement mechanics, not the opinion

The head does not negotiate the assurance opinion. The head does
not signal, imply, or arrange for the assurance body to reach a
particular conclusion. The head does not offer, imply, or accept
any consideration that would compromise the assurance body's
independence. What the head does negotiate is *scope* (the
specific boundary of what the engagement covers), *engagement
mechanics* (timing, evidence-access process, response window for
management responses to findings, deliverable format, audience),
and *fee* — negotiated as ordinary commercial terms for the
scoped work, not as a lever on the opinion.

The distinction is substantive. A head who negotiates scope
down to remove the AI systems the head suspects will produce
adverse findings has not saved money; the head has produced an
assurance opinion that omits the material risk area and is
therefore worth less. A head who negotiates timing so the
assurance body cannot adequately audit within the window has
produced an opinion the audience reads as time-pressured rather
than substantive.

### The head does not shop assurance bodies for leniency

[[mod-108-leading-the-governance-function]] chapter 06 names the
"audit-friendly CB" anti-pattern at the relationship level. This
chapter reinforces the same pattern at the P&L level: the head
who selects the CB, the independent-assurance body, or the Big
Four firm expected to produce a lenient opinion has spent
assurance budget on a discounted-value signal. The audience
learns which bodies produce substantive opinions and which
produce weak ones, and the certification, IAAIS report, or Big
Four deliverable is discounted accordingly. The nominal fee is
the same; the value produced by the opinion is materially lower.

### The head coordinates with the CAE and the CFO on assurance-line ownership

The head does not own the assurance-line spend in isolation. Two
adjacent CxO relationships shape how the line is run.

- **The Chief Audit Executive
  ([[mod-108-leading-the-governance-function]] chapter 05).**
  The CAE owns internal audit — the third line of defence. The
  external assurance the head commissions complements the
  internal audit but does not replace it. The head coordinates
  with the CAE on scope boundaries — which risk domains the CAE
  covers internally, which the head covers externally, and where
  the overlaps are deliberate versus accidental. A joint scope
  map prevents duplicated spend and gap-in-coverage alike.
- **The CFO's controller and the audit committee chair.**
  External audit spend — including AI-assurance spend classified
  as external audit — is typically carried on the finance-
  controller's audit-fee ledger and scrutinised by the audit
  committee separately from the head's operating budget. The
  head coordinates with the controller on classification (is
  this spend external audit or advisory?) and with the audit
  committee chair on the assurance posture the spend produces.

The head who runs the assurance-body P&L without engaging the
CAE and the audit committee chair has run half of the
discipline.

## Multi-body coordination — the coordination overhead most enterprises under-invest in

Most enterprises with mature AI governance run multiple
simultaneous assurance-body engagements. A common shape:

- An ISO/IEC 42001 CB running the three-year certification
  cycle with annual surveillance audits.
- A ForHumanity-certified auditor delivering one or two IAAIS
  engagements a year against sector-specific overlays.
- A BABL AI algorithmic-audit engagement on one of the
  enterprise's highest-impact AI systems annually.
- An occasional Big Four AI-assurance engagement — perhaps every
  two or three years — driven by a specific audit committee ask,
  a specific regulator response, or a specific customer-facing
  assurance requirement.
- A specialist AI-audit firm engagement on a specific system,
  event, or event-cluster where the specialist's craft is the
  right fit.

Running these in parallel without coordination produces three
predictable pathologies: duplicated scope, colliding evidence-
production demands, and duplicated or missed findings-tracking.
The head runs the coordination.

### The scope-overlap map

The head maintains a scope-overlap map — a matrix whose rows are
AI systems (or risk domains) and whose columns are assurance
bodies, and whose cells record whether the body covers the
system, at what depth, against what criteria, on what cadence.
The map lives in the same evidence store the running-programme
artefacts live in ([[mod-102-running-the-program]] chapter 04
and chapter 06).

The scope-overlap map answers three questions:

- **Where are the overlaps?** Two bodies covering the same
  system against similar criteria produces duplicated evidence-
  production demand and duplicated findings. Overlaps are
  sometimes deliberate — a Big Four AI-assurance engagement over
  a ForHumanity IAAIS engagement on the same system may be an
  intentional stacking for audit-committee purposes — but the
  head decides that intentionally.
- **Where are the gaps?** A system covered by no assurance body
  is a system the head has left unscoped. Where the head has
  left a gap intentionally (the system is low-risk; the system
  is covered by adjacent enterprise assurance), the head names
  the gap; where the head has left a gap accidentally, the head
  closes it.
- **Where does the head want the scope to move?** The map is
  authored forward as well as backward — where does the head
  want each body's scope to be at year two, year three, and
  onward?

### Timing coordination

Surveillance audits, IAAIS engagements, BABL AI algorithmic
audits, Big Four SOWs, and specialist engagements each produce
evidence-production demands on the function. Uncoordinated,
these demands cluster on the function's calendar — three bodies
requesting the same evidence in the same six-week window — and
either the function's operating capacity is consumed producing
evidence, or the evidence is produced hastily and the assurance
opinions are correspondingly thin.

The head coordinates the calendar. Surveillance audits usually
have some flexibility on scheduling within the certification
cycle. IAAIS and BABL AI engagements are commissioned by the
head and are therefore fully in the head's control. Big Four
SOWs are timed jointly with the CFO's controller. The
coordination produces an assurance calendar the head runs
alongside the AIMS operating calendar
([[mod-102-running-the-program]] chapter 02) and the audit-
committee calendar ([[mod-103-board-and-audit-committee-reporting]]
chapter 02).

### Findings-tracking coordination

Findings from every assurance body flow into a single tracking
file, composed with the evidence-store and findings-tracking
discipline in [[mod-102-running-the-program]] chapter 04 and
chapter 06. The head ensures that:

- The same finding is not tracked twice under different
  wordings — a finding on model-inventory completeness raised by
  the CB and a functionally identical finding raised by the
  ForHumanity auditor is one finding with two sources, not two
  findings.
- No finding is missed because it appeared in one body's report
  and not another's — findings are entered into the tracking
  file within the head's committed response window regardless
  of source.
- The audit committee sees the consolidated view — a single
  findings-status report at each audit-committee cycle
  ([[mod-103-board-and-audit-committee-reporting]]) with each
  finding attributed to its source body.

Findings-tracking coordination is the discipline that keeps the
enterprise from paying twice for the same finding — first at the
engagement that produced it, and again at the follow-up
engagement that re-audits the un-closed finding — and from being
surprised at the audit committee by a finding the head has not
seen consolidated with the others.

## Big Four engagement discipline

The Big Four sub-category warrants its own sub-section given the
auditor-independence overlay and the distinct commercial and
audit-committee-signal shape.

### The financial-statement-auditor-independence overlay

If the enterprise's financial-statement auditor is one of the
Big Four, that firm is generally restricted from providing
certain non-audit services to the enterprise under the applicable
auditor-independence rules. The specifics differ by jurisdiction
and by the enterprise's listing footprint — SEC and PCAOB rules
in the United States for SEC-registered issuers, FRC rules in
the United Kingdom for premium-listed issuers, ESMA-adjacent
rules in the EU for public-interest entities, and equivalent
frameworks in Australia, Canada, Singapore, Hong Kong, and
elsewhere.
<!-- needs-research: verify the specific auditor-independence rule set applicable to the enterprise — its jurisdictional footprint, listing status, public-interest-entity status — before scoping any Big Four AI-assurance engagement. -->

The head coordinates with the CFO's controller and the audit
committee chair before scoping any Big Four AI-assurance
engagement. The specific question:

- **Which Big Four firm is the enterprise's financial-statement
  auditor?** The financial-statement auditor is generally
  restricted from certain non-audit services.
- **Which Big Four firms are eligible for the AI-assurance
  engagement under the applicable independence rules?** The
  three non-audit-firm Big Four firms are typically eligible;
  the head confirms specifically before scoping.
- **Which Big Four firm has the strongest AI-competent partner
  and manager availability at the timing the engagement
  requires?** The eligibility filter is a floor; the substantive
  choice is competence and availability.

The audit committee chair reads the choice as an independence
signal. A head who scopes a Big Four engagement to the
enterprise's financial-statement auditor without the
independence check has produced an audit-committee-level
independence concern.

### Partner-and-manager assignment

The Big Four brand is a proxy for competence, not a substitute.
The specific AI-competent partner and manager assigned to the
engagement is the substantive commitment. A firm that pitches
with a senior AI-competent partner in the sales cycle and
delivers with a junior partner and a green manager has produced
a deliverable that will read as junior at the audit committee.

The head negotiates the assignment explicitly:

- **Named partner.** The specific partner accountable for the
  engagement, named in the SOW, with a specified minimum share
  of engagement hours.
- **Named manager.** The specific manager running the engagement
  day-to-day, named in the SOW, with a specified minimum share
  of engagement hours.
- **Escalation for substitution.** The specific process the firm
  follows if the named partner or manager cannot deliver — head
  approval required, replacement subject to the head's review
  of the substitute's AI competence, fee adjustment for a
  material substitution.

### Deliverable shape

Big Four AI-assurance engagements produce distinct deliverables
depending on the SOW. The head selects the shape appropriate to
the audience:

- **A reasonable-assurance report.** The highest-confidence
  attestation — a positive assurance opinion that management's
  assertions are, in all material respects, fairly stated. The
  most expensive engagement type; the strongest audit-committee
  and market signal. Reserved for engagements where the audience
  demands the highest-confidence deliverable.
- **A limited-assurance report.** A moderate-confidence
  attestation — an opinion that nothing has come to the auditor's
  attention that would cause them to believe management's
  assertions are materially misstated. Lower fee than reasonable
  assurance; correspondingly lower signal.
- **An agreed-upon-procedures (AUP) report.** The auditor
  performs specific procedures the head and the auditor agree
  on and reports the findings; the auditor does not opine on the
  assertions. Useful for scoped, specific reviews where a full
  assurance opinion is not the audience's ask.
- **Advisory work.** Not an assurance engagement — the firm
  provides analysis, benchmarking, or recommendations without a
  formal assurance opinion. Different independence and
  deliverable rules apply.

The head does not accept whatever deliverable shape the firm
proposes as the default. The head selects the shape that fits
the specific audience — audit committee expects one shape,
regulator expects another, customer tender may expect a third —
and negotiates the SOW accordingly. A firm that pitches an
advisory engagement where the audit committee expects a limited-
assurance report has under-scoped the deliverable; a firm that
pitches a reasonable-assurance report where an AUP would suffice
has over-priced the deliverable.

## Renewal-and-scope-adjustment discipline

Each sub-category renews on a distinct cadence, and the head's
discipline shifts accordingly.

### The CB rotation trade-off

The ISO/IEC 42001 CB relationship is typically continuous across
multiple three-year certification cycles. [[mod-108-leading-the-governance-function]]
chapter 06 covers the relationship-side of the CB rotation
decision — the independence signal a rotation produces, the
substantive-competence trade-off, the audit-committee
coordination. This chapter carries the P&L-side of the same
decision.

Rotation is not free. A new CB re-runs the discovery cycle —
Stage 1, Stage 2, an inaugural certification cycle where the CB
develops its substantive understanding of the enterprise's AIMS.
The first cycle with a new CB typically costs more than a
continuing cycle with an incumbent CB, both in CB fees (the
CB scopes more audit-days for the initial cycle) and in the
function's own evidence-production and coordination cost. The
head presents the trade-off honestly:

- **Continuing CB.** Continuity, deep familiarity with the
  AIMS, lower fee in the continuing cycle, but a weakening
  independence signal as the CB and the enterprise co-evolve
  over multiple cycles.
- **Rotating CB.** Refreshed independence signal, but higher
  first-cycle cost, discovery-cycle time in the function's
  calendar, and the possibility that the new CB is materially
  more or less rigorous than the incumbent.

The rotation decision is a genuine trade-off — the head does
not present it as "the right thing to do" without the trade-offs
on the table, and does not defer it indefinitely because the
short-term cost is inconvenient. The audit committee owns the
decision at high-level; the head owns the P&L analysis
underneath.

### The ForHumanity and BABL AI renewal discipline

ForHumanity-certified auditor and BABL AI relationships are
typically retained-with-per-engagement rather than long-cycle.
Renewal is less about the annual fee negotiation and more about
whether the enterprise continues to commission engagements at
the pace and scope it has committed to.

The head's discipline:

- **Retainer renewal.** Does the enterprise continue to retain
  the same auditor / firm, or does it rotate — for the same
  independence-signal reasons that apply to the CB, and for the
  same trade-offs?
- **Engagement pipeline.** What engagements will the enterprise
  commission in the coming year? The head plans the engagements
  as line items in the budget model (chapter
  [01](01-governance-function-budget-line.md)) and coordinates
  them with the scope-overlap map above.
- **Fee trajectory.** Where a retained auditor's fee is
  escalating faster than the scope, the head engages the
  auditor commercially at the same substantive discipline the
  head engages an enabler vendor with — but without compromising
  the independence discipline that distinguishes this category.

### The Big Four scope-year discipline

Big Four engagements are scoped year-by-year rather than on a
multi-year cycle. Each year the head, the CFO's controller, and
(where the engagement is audit-committee-facing) the audit
committee chair engage the firm on the coming year's scope.

The head participates in the negotiation substantively but does
not lead it. The CFO's controller and the audit committee chair
own the negotiation with the Big Four in the same way they own
the negotiation with the enterprise's financial-statement
auditor. The head owns the substantive AI-assurance-scope input
— what the engagement should cover, what deliverable shape it
should produce, what partner and manager assignment is required.
The head does not run the fee negotiation alone.

Where the enterprise runs an annual Big Four AI-assurance
engagement, the head produces the head's own scope-and-partner
recommendation each year and hands it to the controller and
audit committee chair as input. Where the enterprise runs Big
Four engagements only occasionally, the head produces the
recommendation at the time the engagement is commissioned.

## Anti-patterns the head does not permit

**The single-assurance-body over-dependence pattern.** The
enterprise runs only ISO/IEC 42001 CB assurance and has never
engaged an independent assurance body. The assurance surface is
thin — a single accredited third-party opinion against a
management-system standard, without the socio-technical,
algorithmic, or sector-specific independent assessment. The
audit committee reads the mono-assurance surface as under-
invested, and the market discounts the enterprise's assurance
posture accordingly. The remedy is the multi-body assurance
posture — the CB for accredited management-system conformity,
one or more independent-assurance bodies for the broader
socio-technical and algorithmic-audit signal, and periodic Big
Four engagement for the audit-committee-facing external-audit-
quality signal.

**The audit-fee compression pattern.** The head has negotiated a
Big Four AI-audit scope at a compressed fee. The firm has
responded by assigning a less-competent partner, a green manager,
and a lower-cost engagement team. The deliverable is thin. The
audit committee reads it correctly — the deliverable does not
substantively assess the enterprise's AI-risk posture — and the
head has spent audit budget on a signal the audit committee
discounts. The remedy is the partner-and-manager-assignment
discipline in the Big Four sub-section above — the head
negotiates the specific assignment as an SOW commitment, not the
firm's brand as a proxy.

**The "one-of-each because we should" pattern.** The enterprise
runs a CB + a ForHumanity IAAIS engagement + a BABL AI
algorithmic-audit engagement + a Big Four AI-assurance
engagement in the same year with overlapping scopes and no
coordination. The total fee is high, findings duplicate across
engagements, evidence-production is doubled or tripled, and the
audit committee receives four separate reports with overlapping
substance. The function's operating capacity is consumed
producing evidence rather than closing findings. The remedy is
the multi-body coordination discipline above — the scope-overlap
map, the timing coordination, the findings-tracking
coordination.

**The audit-friendly-body selection pattern.** The head has
selected the CB, the independent-assurance body, or the Big Four
firm because the head expected the body to produce a lenient
opinion. The resulting opinions carry less market weight than
they should. The audit committee, the regulator, and the
enterprise's customers discount the assurance signal in
proportion to the market's read on the body's substantive
independence and rigour. The head has spent full assurance
budget on a discounted signal. The remedy is the substantive-
competence-and-rigour selection discipline — the head selects
the body on competence, on market standing, and on the fit with
the enterprise's substantive assurance need, and does not select
on the head's expectation that the body will produce a
favourable opinion. [[mod-108-leading-the-governance-function]]
chapter 06 carries the relationship-side of the same discipline.

**The auditor-independence violation pattern.** The head has
engaged the enterprise's financial-statement auditor for AI-
assurance work without confirming the auditor-independence rules
that apply, and without coordinating with the CFO's controller
and the audit committee chair. The audit committee raises the
engagement as an independence concern — either the engagement is
unwound at additional cost, or the audit-committee-scope trust
in the head's judgement is damaged, or both. The remedy is the
independence-overlay discipline in the Big Four sub-section
above — the head does not scope a Big Four engagement to the
enterprise's financial-statement auditor without confirming
eligibility with the controller and the audit committee chair.

**The finding-not-remediated escalating-fee pattern.** The head
has not closed findings from prior assurance engagements. The
follow-up engagement — the surveillance audit, the next IAAIS
cycle, the follow-up Big Four scope — re-audits the same issue
at additional cost. The findings-tracking file
([[mod-102-running-the-program]] chapter 04) shows the same
finding open across multiple cycles. The audit committee reads
it as a remediation-discipline failure — the enterprise pays
repeatedly to be told the same thing. The remedy is the
findings-remediation discipline in [[mod-102-running-the-program]]
composed with the assurance-body P&L discipline here — findings
close on committed timelines; the assurance-body engagement is
scoped to advance to the next issue rather than to re-audit the
prior one.

**The stealth-scope-creep pattern.** An independent-assurance
engagement or a Big Four SOW that started at a defined scope has
expanded quietly across the engagement — the auditor has
requested access to adjacent systems, the deliverable has grown
in ambit, the fee has escalated through change orders. The head
did not push back at each individual expansion because each was
small; the cumulative expansion is material. The remedy is the
engagement-mechanics discipline above — the SOW specifies the
scope and the process for scope adjustments; adjustments are
negotiated explicitly rather than absorbed silently.

## Summary

- The assurance-body relationship P&L is inverted relative to
  the enabler-vendor P&L in chapters
  [03](03-grc-for-ai-vendor-pnl.md) through
  [05](05-ai-runtime-security-vendor-pnl.md). Assurance bodies
  are judgement vendors; the head who negotiates aggressive
  discount has reduced the credibility of the opinion the
  enterprise is paying for.
- Five sub-categories: the ISO/IEC 42001 CB (BSI, PECB, TÜV
  Rheinland, TÜV SÜD, DNV, DEKRA), the ForHumanity IAAIS
  auditor, BABL AI, Big Four AI-assurance (Deloitte, EY, PwC,
  KPMG), and specialist AI-audit firms. Each has a distinct
  commercial shape.
- The commercial shape is fixed-scope-fixed-fee-multi-year for
  the CB, retained-scope-per-engagement-fee for ForHumanity and
  BABL AI, statement-of-work per engagement for the Big Four and
  specialists.
- The head negotiates scope, engagement mechanics (timing,
  evidence access, response window, deliverable shape), and fee
  as ordinary commercial terms for the scoped work. The head
  does not negotiate the opinion, does not shop for leniency,
  and does not compromise the independence discipline that
  distinguishes this category from the enabler categories.
- The head coordinates with the CAE ([[mod-108-leading-the-governance-function]]
  chapter 05) on the boundary between internal audit and
  external assurance, and with the CFO's controller and audit
  committee chair on the audit-fee-line classification and the
  independence posture the mix of engagements produces.
- Multi-body coordination is a distinct discipline — a scope-
  overlap map, timing coordination, and findings-tracking
  coordination — that keeps the enterprise from duplicating
  spend, colliding evidence-production demands, or receiving
  overlapping findings uncoordinated.
- Big Four engagements carry the auditor-independence overlay
  (SEC/PCAOB, FRC, EU-equivalent, APAC-equivalent). The head
  does not scope a Big Four AI-assurance engagement to the
  enterprise's financial-statement auditor without confirming
  eligibility. The head negotiates the AI-competent partner-and-
  manager assignment explicitly, and selects the deliverable
  shape (reasonable assurance / limited assurance / agreed-upon
  procedures / advisory) to fit the audience.
- Renewal shape differs by sub-category — CB rotation is a
  multi-year trade-off, ForHumanity and BABL AI retainers are
  engagement-by-engagement, Big Four scope is annual and led
  by the CFO's controller and audit committee chair with the
  head as substantive input.
- Six anti-patterns the head refuses: single-assurance-body
  over-dependence, audit-fee compression, "one-of-each because
  we should" without coordination, audit-friendly-body
  selection, auditor-independence violation, finding-not-
  remediated escalating-fee, and the stealth-scope-creep
  pattern.
- Chapter [07](07-roi-case-modelling.md) turns the assurance-
  body spend from a cost defence into a modelled-value argument
  — the avoided-loss and deal-enablement return the assurance
  investment produces. This chapter's P&L discipline and that
  chapter's ROI discipline compose.
