# mod-112 — Certifications, Career Path, and Community Leadership

**Estimated effort:** 12 hours
**Position on the track:** the twelfth module — the head's own
professional-development arc, sitting alongside
[[mod-108-leading-the-governance-function]] (which builds the
function the head runs) and
[[mod-113-budget-vendor-p-and-l-and-investment-case]] (which builds
the resource envelope the head defends). This module builds the
*head's own* portfolio — credentials, career-route preservation,
community-leadership seats, thought-leadership cadence, and the
rehearsed hard conversations that shape the head's next role.

## What this module is for

Every other module in the track builds craft the head applies at
the enterprise. This module builds craft the head applies to their
own career. The head who has authored a strong AIMS but a weak
credentials portfolio, or who has strong regulator counterparts but
no standards-body seat, or who has strong enterprise-internal
standing but no visible thought leadership, is a head whose seat
outlasts their own trajectory. The module makes the professional-
arc discipline explicit so the head is running it deliberately
rather than by default.

## What this module answers

1. Which credentials does a head-of-AI-governance hold, in what
   sequence, and for which reasons? IAPP AIGP as the near-universal
   baseline; ISO/IEC 42001 Lead Implementer / Lead Auditor at
   leadership scope; the privacy adjacency cluster (CIPP + CIPM +
   CIPT); the governance-risk-audit adjacency cluster (ISACA CGEIT
   / CRISC / CISM; IIA CIA); the independent-audit-community
   cluster (ForHumanity Fellow, BABL AI Fellow); the sector overlay
   cluster from [[mod-111-sector-program-leadership]]. The
   sequencing decision: which credentials are earned before
   promotion into head-of-role and which are earned after.
2. What are the career paths *into* head-of-AI-governance? Seven
   routes: senior AI governance architect (level 50); Head of
   Model Risk Management with AI/ML scope; Chief Compliance
   Officer with AI-heavy remit; Chief Privacy Officer with AI-heavy
   remit; Chief Audit Executive with AI-heavy remit; senior legal
   counsel with AI-regulatory specialisation (rarer); consulting
   Big Four partner with AI-governance practice (increasingly
   common). Each route brings a distinct substrate and a distinct
   set of year-one gaps the enterprise's onboarding investment
   closes.
3. What are the career paths *out* of head-of-AI-governance? Five
   routes: Chief AI Officer elevation (level 70); Chief Risk
   Officer / Chief Compliance Officer / Chief Privacy Officer
   cross-functional elevation; non-executive-director / board-
   portfolio route; public-sector CAIO under OMB M-25-21 (or
   equivalent); industry-body chair or academic-affiliation route.
   Each has a distinct trigger, mechanics, prerequisite, and
   anti-pattern.
4. What is the head's *community-leadership scope*? A two-to-four-
   seat portfolio drawn from five clusters: international standards
   bodies (ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 42, CEN-CENELEC JTC 21, IEEE, ITU-T);
   public-sector and cross-sector consortiums (AISIC, NIST AIRC,
   Federal CAIO Council); industry bodies (Frontier Model Forum,
   Partnership on AI, MLCommons, IAPP, IIA, ISACA); academic and
   policy institutions; independent-audit-community working groups
   (ForHumanity, BABL AI). The head negotiates the joint-seat
   protocol with the CEO and General Counsel — attribution,
   confidentiality, publication, time.
5. What is the head's *public-speaking and thought-leadership
   discipline*? An annual editorial calendar at three layers:
   two-to-four flagship pieces (position papers, long-form
   articles, chapter contributions, flagship keynotes); four-to-
   eight sector- or community-specific pieces; six-to-twelve
   responsive or short-form pieces. Keynote cadence at 2-4
   flagship plus 4-6 sector-specific per year, with total keynote-
   and-panel appearances under 15 per year. Media strategy under
   coordination with the Head of Communications, disciplined about
   on-record / background / off-record posture. Board-of-advisors
   composition kept below a five-position ceiling under conflict-
   of-interest review.
6. How does the head rehearse the *difficult career conversation*?
   Two specific rehearsals: declining a scope-broadening
   compliance elevation that would dilute the AI focus, and
   declining a scope-stripping CAIO offer whose seat lacks the
   risk-and-governance mandate. Both use a six-step conversational
   structure — name what you hear, name the recognition, state the
   decline, explain the substrate reasoning, offer alternatives,
   close on the relationship — rehearsed with an executive coach
   and peer head-of-role before the offer arrives.

## Learning objectives

- Position the certifications portfolio for a head-of-AI-governance
  — IAPP AIGP (near-universal baseline), ISO/IEC 42001 Lead Auditor
  / Lead Implementer (required at leadership scope when running an
  AIMS), IAPP CIPP/US or CIPP/E + CIPM + CIPT (privacy adjacencies
  where the reporting line touches privacy), ISACA CISM / CGEIT /
  CRISC (governance / risk / audit adjacencies), IIA CIA (internal-
  audit adjacency for audit-path heads-of-role), ForHumanity Fellow
  (independent-audit adjacency), BABL AI Fellow (bias-audit
  adjacency) — with the sequencing decision (which to earn before
  promoting into head-of-role, which to earn after).
- Map the career paths into head-of-AI-governance — from senior-
  ai-governance-architect (level 50) as the natural technical
  route; from Head of Model Risk Management with AI/ML scope
  (banking); from Chief Compliance Officer with AI-heavy remit;
  from Chief Privacy Officer with AI-heavy remit; from Chief Audit
  Executive with AI-heavy remit; from senior legal counsel with
  AI-regulatory specialisation (rarer); from consulting Big Four
  partner with AI-governance practice (increasingly common).
- Map the career paths *out* of head-of-AI-governance — into Chief
  AI Officer (level 70) where the role opens up; into Chief Risk
  Officer / Chief Compliance Officer / Chief Privacy Officer in
  cross-functional promotions; into board / non-executive-director
  seats (AI-governance NEDs); into public-sector CAIO under OMB
  M-25-21; into industry-body chair or academic-affiliation
  positions.
- Design the community-leadership scope — chair or working-group
  lead at ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 42, CEN-CENELEC JTC 21, Frontier Model
  Forum, Partnership on AI, MLCommons AI Safety, IAPP AIGP body-of-
  knowledge committee, IIA IPPF working groups, US AISI Consortium;
  position-paper authorship cadence; conference-keynote cadence;
  academic-affiliation decisions.
- Design the public speaking / thought-leadership discipline —
  annual conference cadence, industry-panel participation, board-
  of-advisors positions, media strategy (in coordination with
  Communications), position-paper editorial calendar.
- Rehearse the difficult career conversation — declining a lateral
  move into a broader-scope compliance seat that would dilute the
  AI focus; defending the choice to stay a head-of-AI-governance
  instead of moving into a Chief AI Officer role that lacks the
  risk mandate.

## How to work through this module

The module is designed to be worked in order. The chapters compose:

- Chapter 01 sets the credentials substrate. The credentials are
  the entry ticket to specific communities and the signing
  authority for specific artefacts; they read forward into every
  subsequent chapter.
- Chapters 02 and 03 map the career paths in and out of the seat.
  The routes are read against the credentials portfolio (which
  credentials the route brings; which the head earns to open the
  next route), against the community-leadership scope (which
  community-standing the route relies on), and against the
  thought-leadership cadence (which visibility the route
  compounds).
- Chapters 04 and 05 build the *external* substrate of the head's
  tenure — community-leadership seats at operational depth, and
  thought-leadership output at cadence. These chapters are what
  keep the head's onward routes (chapter 03) open.
- Chapter 06 rehearses the specific hard conversations that
  determine whether the head's onward move is authored or
  reactive. It is the tactical close.

The module deliberately does not have an eighth chapter on
"executing the career move." That work belongs to the head, their
executive coach, and their trusted counsel. The module builds the
substrate that makes the move well-executed; the move itself is
the head's own.

The module composes with the rest of the track:

- The credentials portfolio references the AI-governance craft
  built by [[mod-102-running-the-program]] and
  [[mod-107-program-measurement-and-maturity]] (credentials
  without operational depth read as thin), the AIMS operator
  discipline in [[mod-101-role-scope-and-leadership-shape]], and
  the sector-specific overlays in [[mod-111-sector-program-leadership]].
- The career paths in and out reference the function-leadership
  discipline in [[mod-108-leading-the-governance-function]]
  (succession planning), the cross-CxO coordination pattern in
  [[mod-109-cross-enterprise-leadership]] (the peer-CxO
  relationships that shape the routes), and the board- and
  regulator-engagement disciplines in
  [[mod-103-board-and-audit-committee-reporting]] and
  [[mod-104-regulator-engagement]] (the standing the routes are
  built on).
- The community-leadership scope references the enterprise's
  external-positioning portfolio in
  [[mod-106-external-positioning-and-voluntary-codes]] (the
  institutional stance the head's personal engagement
  operationalises).
- The thought-leadership discipline references the enterprise
  regulatory-change discipline in
  [[mod-105-regulatory-change-management]] (which regulatory
  developments the head has substantive positions on) and the
  incident-response discipline in
  [[mod-110-incident-leadership]] (which crisis-communication
  disciplines the head applies).
- The difficult-career-conversation chapter references the
  onward-path chapter and closes the module by tying the head's
  external-portfolio work back to the head's own next step.
