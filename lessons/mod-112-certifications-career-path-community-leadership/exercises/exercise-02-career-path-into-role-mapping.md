# exercise-02: Career Path Into Role Mapping

**Estimated effort:** 2 hours

## Objective

Instantiate the seven-route framework from chapter
[02-career-paths-into-role](../02-career-paths-into-role.md) against
your own enterprise (or a specified hypothetical enterprise). The
deliverable is two paired artefacts: a *route-map* for the head's
own path in (which of the seven routes brought the head into their
current seat, what substrate they brought, what year-one gaps the
onboarding investment addressed) and a *succession-slate* for the
head's own function (which internal candidates on each of the
seven routes could plausibly succeed the head, what specific
development each candidate needs).

The deliverable is not a career biography. It is the head's own
CHRO-standing succession artefact and the head's own reflective
read on their path in, both authored to a discipline the
enterprise's board would credit if the board asked.

## Prerequisites

- Chapter [02-career-paths-into-role](../02-career-paths-into-role.md)
  — read and internalised. In particular, the seven routes and
  the per-route substrate / year-one-gap / onboarding-investment /
  anti-pattern structure; and the closing succession-slate
  discipline.
- Chapter [[mod-108-leading-the-governance-function]] chapter 08
  on the successor-readiness dimension of function leadership.
- Chapter [01-certifications-portfolio](../01-certifications-portfolio.md)
  on the credentials each route brings and the credentials the
  onboarding investment adds.
- A specific scenario. Options:
  - Your own enterprise (if working the exercise in role or in
    transition into role).
  - A specified hypothetical enterprise with a defined sector
    (from [[mod-111-sector-program-leadership]]), a defined
    reporting line for the head, a defined AI portfolio
    maturity (early / mid / mature), a defined size of AI-
    governance function (small: 3-5; mid: 6-9; large: 10+), and
    a defined visible level-50 architect presence (yes / no).

## Steps

1. **Enterprise-context one-pager.** Half-page scoping: sector,
   enterprise size, AI portfolio scale and maturity, head's
   current reporting line, size and composition of the AI-
   governance function, presence or absence of a visible level-50
   architect on the succession slate. The scoping is the
   substrate for both artefacts.

2. **Route-in read for the sitting head (or the exercise's
   subject head).**
   - Identify which of the seven routes the head came in on.
   - Name the substrate the head brought at entry — prior
     credentials, prior professional community, prior regulator
     relationships, prior operational depth.
   - Name the specific year-one gaps the head faced (drawn from
     the per-route gaps section of chapter 02, and any additional
     gaps the specific enterprise created).
   - Name the specific onboarding investments the enterprise
     made — the credentials added in year one, the coaching
     relationships arranged, the shadowing and co-attendance
     arrangements, the executive-coach retention. Include what
     was budgeted for, what was actually done, and where the
     onboarding under-delivered.
   - Name the specific anti-pattern from chapter 02 that
     applies to the head's route, and describe how the head and
     the enterprise avoided it (or, honestly, where they did not
     avoid it and what the consequence was).

3. **Succession-slate authoring — internal candidates.** For each
   of the seven routes, name internal candidates who could
   plausibly succeed the sitting head. Two readiness horizons:
   - **Ready now** — candidates who could take the seat within
     12-24 months with a standard onboarding investment.
   - **Developmental** — candidates who could take the seat
     within 24-48 months with a defined development plan.
   - Per candidate: current role and level; the route they would
     come in on (which of the seven); the substrate they bring;
     the specific development gap the candidate would need to
     close before promotion; the specific development
     investments (credentials, coaching, community-leadership
     placement) that would close it.
   - Some routes may have no internal candidates. Record the
     null and analyse — is the null an intentional gap the
     enterprise accepts, or a gap the enterprise should close
     with deliberate hiring?

4. **External-market succession read.** For the routes where
   internal succession is not viable within either horizon, name
   the external-market population the enterprise would reach
   into. Per external-market entry:
   - Which of the seven routes; the sector the enterprise would
     target external candidates from; specific enterprises whose
     talent pool is a known source for the route (naming
     specific enterprises is acceptable and expected).
   - The typical compensation range and the enterprise's
     willingness to pay it.
   - The typical time-to-hire for the route (external retained
     search takes 6-12 months; recruiter-network sourcing is
     faster but with weaker vetting).

5. **Route-and-sector fit read.** For the enterprise's sector,
   read the seven routes against sector fit — which routes are
   natively preferred (banks prefer MRM route; frontier labs
   prefer technical / architect route; health systems prefer CCO
   or CPO route; public sector prefers legal or audit route).
   Where the enterprise's actual sitting head is on a route the
   sector does not natively prefer, name the reasoning that made
   the choice defensible.

6. **Anti-pattern audit.** For each of the internal candidates on
   the succession slate, name the anti-pattern from chapter 02
   they would be at risk of, and the specific onboarding
   investment that would prevent it. The purpose is to make the
   anti-pattern explicit before the promotion decision arrives,
   not after.

7. **Refresh-cadence protocol.** Author the specific mechanism by
   which the succession slate is refreshed annually with the
   CHRO. Include: the calendar timing (typically after the
   enterprise's annual talent-review cycle); the specific artefact
   handed to the CHRO (this exercise's deliverable, updated); the
   named candidates re-confirmed or re-scoped; the specific
   changes to development plans. Compare against
   [[mod-108-leading-the-governance-function]] chapter 08's
   succession-readiness cadence.

8. **Peer-review preparation.** Package the deliverables for
   review by a specific reader. Two options — one internal
   (typically the CHRO or the head's own executive sponsor), one
   external (a peer head of role at a non-competing enterprise
   whose function has succession discipline you can pattern-match
   against). The exercise deliverable is the request; the
   response is out of scope.

## Deliverables

- `enterprise-context.md` — the half-page scoping.
- `route-in-read.md` — the sitting head's route-in analysis.
- `succession-slate.md` — the seven-route succession slate with
  ready-now and developmental horizons.
- `external-market-read.md` — the external-market succession
  posture for the routes without viable internal candidates.
- `route-and-sector-fit.md` — the sector-fit read.
- `anti-pattern-audit.md` — the per-candidate anti-pattern read.
- `refresh-cadence.md` — the CHRO-cadence protocol.
- A peer-review request drafted for a named reviewer.

## Acceptance criteria

- The route-in read for the sitting head names one of the seven
  routes explicitly. "Some combination of routes" is not an
  acceptable answer — the exercise is a discipline read, and the
  head names the primary route with any secondary influences
  noted.
- The succession slate has an entry (or an explicit null) for
  each of the seven routes. Silent gaps ("we have no candidate
  for this route" without further analysis) fail the exercise.
- Each named internal candidate has a stated route, a stated
  substrate, and a stated development plan with specific
  credentials, coaching, and community-leadership investments.
  Placeholder "senior person on the team" entries fail the
  exercise.
- The external-market read is specific to routes without viable
  internal candidates. If every route has an internal
  candidate, the external-market read is a shorter contingency
  section rather than a null.
- The route-and-sector-fit read names the sector's default
  preference and defends any deviation the enterprise's specific
  choice represents.
- The anti-pattern audit names the specific anti-pattern per
  candidate and names the specific preventative onboarding
  investment. Generic ("we would give them an executive coach")
  answers fail the exercise; specific ("we would arrange
  shadowing of the CRO through one full audit-committee cycle
  before the candidate independently presents") answers pass.
- The refresh-cadence protocol names the calendar timing, the
  artefact handed to the CHRO, and the review discipline. Where
  the enterprise does not yet have an annual talent-review cycle
  that hosts this cadence, name the compensating discipline.
- The peer-review request is drafted with a named reviewer and a
  specific ask.

## Stretch goals

- Add a *retention-risk* dimension to the succession slate. Per
  ready-now candidate, name the retention risk (they are being
  recruited elsewhere; they are approaching a life stage where
  their retention is not certain; their compensation is below
  peer benchmark). Name the retention move the enterprise is
  making to hold each candidate through the readiness window.
- Add a *concurrent-succession* dimension. If the head is on
  path 1 (CAIO elevation) or path 2 (cross-functional
  elevation), the head's departure produces a succession event
  the sitting head still owes support to. Author the specific
  concurrent-succession discipline — how the head coordinates
  their own move with the successor's readiness so the
  enterprise's AI-governance discipline does not lapse during
  the transition.
- Add a *cross-sector-pull* dimension. Which of the seven
  routes' candidates could plausibly be recruited *out* of the
  enterprise's sector into another sector's head-of-role seat?
  This is the head's own read on their function's talent
  vulnerability to competitive recruitment.

## Notes and starter guidance

- The most common failure mode is producing a route-in read that
  is celebratory rather than honest. "I came in through the
  architect route and the onboarding investment was excellent and
  I had no gaps" is not an acceptable answer. The exercise is
  professional-discipline reading; the honest year-one-gap
  identification is where the exercise earns its keep.
- Chapter 02 is specific that some routes are rare (path 6 —
  legal counsel; path 7 — Big Four partner is becoming more
  common but is still a minority pattern). Do not force every
  route to have an internal candidate; the null with defended
  reasoning is often more accurate than a forced entry.
- The anti-pattern audit is the exercise's most operationally
  useful step. Boards that read succession slates read them for
  anti-pattern awareness — the CHRO or the audit committee wants
  to know that the enterprise has thought about how each
  candidate could fail in role and how the failure would be
  prevented. Skipping the audit fails the exercise's most
  practical purpose.
- The exercise composes with exercise 01 (credentials portfolio)
  and exercise 04 (community-leadership portfolio). The
  credentials the succession-slate candidates need are the
  credentials from exercise 01's plan; the community-leadership
  investments are drawn from exercise 04's portfolio. Working
  the three exercises in sequence produces a coherent
  professional-development architecture.
