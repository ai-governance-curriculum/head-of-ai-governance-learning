# exercise-05: Public Speaking and Thought Leadership Cadence

**Estimated effort:** 2 hours

## Objective

Author the head's *annual editorial calendar* against the
three-layer discipline from chapter
[05-thought-leadership-cadence](../05-thought-leadership-cadence.md).
The deliverable is a specific 12-month calendar covering flagship
pieces, sector- and community-specific pieces, and responsive
short-form pieces; the keynote cadence for the year with named
target conferences; the board-of-advisors portfolio composition;
the media-strategy protocol with Communications; and the position-
paper editorial calendar with drafting-cycle milestones.

The deliverable is not a wishlist of what the head might write.
It is a specific calendar the head could take to their CEO, their
Head of Communications, and their General Counsel at the start of
the enterprise's planning cycle, and defend as a coherent editorial
programme composing with the head's community-leadership seats
(exercise 04) and preserving the head's onward routes (exercise 03).

## Prerequisites

- Chapter [05-thought-leadership-cadence](../05-thought-leadership-cadence.md)
  — read and internalised. In particular, the four intertwined
  objectives (institutional positioning; regulator- and policy-
  shaping; community and peer-network compounding; team-signal
  and coaching credibility); the three-layer editorial calendar
  (flagship / sector-and-community / responsive-short-form); the
  keynote cadence discipline; the board-of-advisors composition
  rules; the media-strategy protocol; and the position-paper
  editorial-calendar drafting cadence.
- Chapter [04-community-leadership-scope](../04-community-leadership-scope.md)
  and the exercise-04 portfolio — the community-leadership seats
  are the substrate the flagship and sector-and-community pieces
  are drawn from.
- Chapter [[mod-109-cross-enterprise-leadership]] chapter 04 on
  the CHRO/Comms coordination pattern — the media protocol here
  operates under that relationship.
- Chapter [[mod-110-incident-leadership]] chapter 06 on public-
  disclosure discipline — the media-strategy piece tightens
  during incidents.
- A specific scenario. Options:
  - Your own current head-of-role tenure (if working in role).
  - A specified hypothetical head with a defined enterprise
    sector, a defined community-leadership portfolio (from
    exercise 04), a defined onward-route preservation stance
    (from exercise 03), and a defined regulatory / standards-
    body calendar for the target year.

## Steps

1. **External-context read.** Half-page inventory of the
   substantive external context the calendar operates against:
   - **Regulatory-calendar events.** Which regulator consultations
     close in the target year? Which implementing-act deadlines
     fall in the year? Which sector-supervisor examination cycles
     are known? (Draws on [[mod-105-regulatory-change-management]]
     horizon-scanning.)
   - **Standards-body milestones.** Which ISO SC 42, CEN-CENELEC
     JTC 21, or IEEE working-group ballots or publications fall
     in the year?
   - **Sector-conference calendar.** Which flagship conferences
     (IAPP Global Privacy Summit, RSA Conference, Money 20/20,
     HIMSS, RAPS, DIA, Frontier Model Forum-hosted convenings,
     RegTech Association events, etc.) are on the year's
     calendar? Which invite-only convenings the head has
     access to?
   - **Community-leadership working-group outputs.** From
     exercise 04, which working-group position papers or comment
     letters are scheduled to be authored in the year?

2. **Flagship-piece calendar authoring — layer 1.** Author the
   two-to-four flagship pieces the head will publish in the
   year. Per piece:
   - Format (position paper, long-form by-lined article, chapter
     contribution, flagship keynote).
   - Topic (specific, not generic — "the interpretation of ISO/IEC
     42005 impact-assessment scope for cross-jurisdictional AI
     deployments" not "AI impact assessment").
   - Target publication venue or conference.
   - Target publication date (aligned with regulatory or
     standards-body-milestone timing per chapter 05).
   - Co-authorship arrangement (single-author; co-authored with
     named peer or team member; working-body imprimatur).
   - Pre-clearance protocol (CEO sign-off timing; GC review
     timing; Communications review timing).
   - The layer-1 objective it primarily serves (institutional
     positioning; policy-shaping; peer-network compounding;
     team-signal).

3. **Sector-and-community-piece calendar authoring — layer 2.**
   Author the four-to-eight sector- or community-specific pieces
   for the year. Per piece:
   - Publication venue (specific: IAPP Privacy Tech Q2 issue;
     ISACA Journal Q3 issue; RMA Journal of Risk Management
     summer edition; ISO SC 42 working-group technical note; etc.).
   - Topic, tied to the venue's readership.
   - Target publication date.
   - Communications review posture (routine review; escalation
     to full pre-clearance if the piece takes an
     enterprise-unratified position).
   - The community or credential body the piece maintains
     standing with.

4. **Responsive-short-form-cadence authoring — layer 3.** Author
   the framework the head will use for the six-to-twelve
   responsive or short-form pieces:
   - Trigger events (specific regulatory rulings; specific
     enterprise or peer public actions; specific technical
     developments) the head will respond to.
   - Format choices (blog post; LinkedIn long-form; podcast
     guest appearance; op-ed; threaded social post).
   - Delegated-review protocol with Head of Communications —
     the specific low-stakes topics that can be published
     without piece-by-piece review, and the high-stakes topics
     that always route through review.
   - Anticipated frequency per format across the year.

5. **Keynote-cadence calibration.** Author the specific keynote
   plan:
   - **Two to four flagship keynotes.** Named conferences and
     tentative or confirmed acceptance status. Preparation-hour
     estimate per keynote. Companion published piece per
     keynote.
   - **Four to six sector-specific keynotes.** Named
     conferences.
   - **Six to twelve expert-panel appearances.** Named panels
     or panel-invitation types.
   - Total keynote-and-panel count for the year (chapter 05's
     ceiling is under 15). If the count is at or over 15,
     specify which conferences are being declined or deferred.
   - Preparation-hour total for the year and calendar-slot
     reconciliation.

6. **Board-of-advisors composition.** Author the current advisory-
   position portfolio:
   - Each currently-held position (start-up advisory board;
     consultancy advisory council; research non-profit advisory
     board; academic institution advisory board; publication
     editorial board).
   - Per position: named body; time-per-year commitment;
     compensation shape; conflict-of-interest posture.
   - Portfolio total against the five-position ceiling.
   - Any position under review for renewal, exit, or
     replacement in the coming year.

7. **Media-strategy protocol drafting.** Formalise the
   coordination pattern with the Head of Communications for the
   coming year:
   - Standing reporter-routing protocol (reporter identifies to
     enterprise press desk; Communications does pre-interview
     brief; Communications joins on-record calls).
   - Standing on-record / background / off-record posture
     defaults per typical scenario.
   - Standing rules for social-media publication (which platforms;
     which topics; what personal-capacity vs enterprise-capacity
     distinction, understanding that the distinction is often
     read by peers and regulators as institutional regardless of
     the head's disclaimer).
   - Standing rules for podcast-appearance acceptance.
   - Incident-time posture tightening: the specific changes to
     the standing protocol during a live incident, referencing
     [[mod-110-incident-leadership]] chapter 06.

8. **Position-paper editorial calendar with drafting-cycle
   milestones.** For each of the flagship-layer position papers,
   author the six-month drafting-cycle discipline:
   - Month 1: outline and evidence-gathering.
   - Month 2: first draft; peer reviewers identified and
     circulated to.
   - Month 3: revised draft after peer feedback; Communications
     and GC review begins.
   - Months 4-5: Communications and GC review, revisions, CEO
     sign-off (where required).
   - Month 6: publication and follow-through (accompanying blog
     post, LinkedIn announcement, media outreach, responses to
     commentary).
   - Named peer reviewers per paper.

9. **Composition audit — calendar-vs-portfolio-vs-substrate.**
   Verify the calendar composes:
   - Each flagship piece ties to a community-leadership seat
     (from exercise 04) or a preserved onward route (from
     exercise 03) or a regulatory / standards-body timing
     window.
   - Each sector-and-community piece ties to a credential-
     community from exercise 01 or a working-body from exercise
     04.
   - The keynote calendar composes with the flagship-piece
     calendar (companion published pieces exist per flagship
     keynote).
   - The total time cost of the calendar (drafting + review +
     rehearsal + delivery + follow-through) fits inside the
     head's available thought-leadership time budget alongside
     the community-leadership hours from exercise 04. Chapter 05
     estimates flagship-keynote preparation at 20-60 hours each
     and position-paper drafting at 3-6 months elapsed with
     substantial writing hours; the calendar's total budget is
     reconciled explicitly.

10. **Pre-clearance and CEO / Comms sign-off.** Package the
    calendar for review by the CEO, the Head of Communications,
    and the General Counsel. The exercise deliverable is the
    calendar packaged for review; the actual sign-off is out of
    scope.

## Deliverables

- `external-context-read.md` — the half-page inventory of the
  substantive external context.
- `layer-1-flagship-calendar.md` — the flagship-piece calendar
  with format, topic, venue, date, co-authorship, pre-clearance
  protocol, and objective per piece.
- `layer-2-sector-community-calendar.md` — the sector- and
  community-specific-piece calendar.
- `layer-3-responsive-short-form-framework.md` — the responsive-
  short-form framework with triggers, formats, delegated-review
  protocol, and frequency.
- `keynote-cadence-plan.md` — the specific keynote plan for
  flagship, sector, and expert-panel appearances.
- `board-of-advisors-portfolio.md` — the advisory-position
  portfolio.
- `media-strategy-protocol.md` — the coordination protocol with
  Communications.
- `position-paper-editorial-calendar.md` — the six-month
  drafting-cycle discipline per flagship position paper.
- `composition-audit.md` — the calendar-vs-portfolio-vs-substrate
  composition audit.
- Packaged review request for the CEO, Head of Communications,
  and General Counsel.

## Acceptance criteria

- The three-layer editorial calendar contains between two and
  four flagship pieces, between four and eight sector-and-
  community pieces, and a framework for six-to-twelve
  responsive-short-form pieces. Counts below or above these
  ranges require defended reasoning; silent deviation fails.
- Every flagship piece has a specific topic, a specific venue,
  a specific date, and a specific stated primary objective.
  Vague topics ("AI governance trends") fail; specific topics
  pass.
- The keynote calendar respects the sub-15 total ceiling or
  defends deviation. Preparation-hour estimates are specific.
  Companion published pieces per flagship keynote are named.
- The board-of-advisors portfolio respects the five-position
  ceiling or defends deviation. Every position has a stated
  time commitment, compensation shape, and conflict-of-interest
  posture.
- The media-strategy protocol is executable. A Communications
  colleague could follow it as-written. Incident-time posture
  tightening is included, referencing chapter 06 of
  [[mod-110-incident-leadership]].
- The position-paper editorial calendar per flagship piece runs
  the six-month drafting cycle with named peer reviewers, named
  Communications and GC review windows, and named CEO sign-off
  timing where required.
- The composition audit demonstrates that each piece and each
  keynote ties to a substrate seat, a preserved onward route, or
  a regulatory / standards-body timing window. A piece without a
  tie is either removed or has the tie explicitly added.
- The total time budget of the calendar is reconciled against the
  available thought-leadership hours alongside the community-
  leadership portfolio from exercise 04. Where the total exceeds
  available time, the specific cuts are named. Deferring to
  "we'll see how the year goes" fails.
- The packaged review request has specific named reviewers, a
  specific ask, and specific dates on which the review is
  scheduled.

## Stretch goals

- Add a *year-two projection*. Chapter 05 notes that position
  papers take 3-6 months to author well and that the compounding
  of a thought-leadership programme runs across years, not
  quarters. Sketch the year-two calendar in outline, showing how
  year-one pieces compound (year-two pieces build on year-one
  positions; year-two keynote invitations arrive because of
  year-one flagship-piece visibility).
- Add a *team-visible-portfolio* dimension. Which pieces the
  head is authoring, and which pieces the head is coaching a
  team member to author (drawing on the coaching cadence in
  [[mod-108-leading-the-governance-function]] chapter 03). A
  head who takes every piece for themselves is a head not
  developing the team's next-generation external portfolio.
- Add a *disclosure-and-conflict* dimension. Where a flagship
  piece or a keynote could produce disclosure obligations (SEC
  Regulation FD, sector-supervisor communications restrictions,
  standards-body confidentiality) or conflicts (positions the
  enterprise's counterparty could read as adversarial), name
  the specific pre-clearance safeguards.

## Notes and starter guidance

- The most common failure mode is producing a calendar that
  looks like a list of ambitions. A calendar that has no venue,
  no date, no reviewer, and no time budget is a wishlist. The
  exercise's discipline is specificity — every piece lands on
  a named venue at a named date.
- Chapter 05 is explicit that thought leadership is a discipline
  rather than a vanity practice. The calendar reflects this by
  requiring that each piece has a stated primary objective and
  that the pieces tie to substrate seats, onward-route
  preservation, and regulatory timing. Pieces that do not tie
  are either removed or given a tie.
- The media-strategy protocol is often the exercise's most
  operationally novel work. Many heads engage media without an
  explicit protocol. The exercise makes the protocol explicit
  and negotiable. The specific coordination pattern with
  Communications — reporter routing, pre-interview brief,
  posture defaults, incident-time tightening — is the head's
  standing-order to Communications for the year.
- The exercise composes tightly with exercises 03 and 04. The
  onward-route preservation shapes flagship-piece topic choices;
  the community-leadership portfolio provides the substrate
  seats and working-group outputs. Working the exercises in
  order produces a coherent external portfolio; the exercise-05
  calendar is the operational compilation of the substrate work.
- The pre-clearance-and-sign-off step is where the calendar
  becomes real. A calendar not signed off by the CEO and
  Communications is a calendar the head is executing at their
  own risk; a calendar signed off is a calendar the enterprise
  has ratified as institutional programme. The exercise
  deliverable is the packaged review request; the actual
  sign-off is a follow-up conversation the exercise prepares
  for.
