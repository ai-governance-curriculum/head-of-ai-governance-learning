# exercise-01: Certifications Portfolio Sequencing Drill

**Estimated effort:** 2 hours

## Objective

Instantiate the credentials taxonomy from chapter
[01-certifications-portfolio](../01-certifications-portfolio.md)
against your own career state (or a specified hypothetical head-
of-role's career state). The deliverable is a *sequenced credentials
plan* — which credentials the head holds, which the head will earn
before an anticipated promotion or move, which the head will earn
after, and which the head has explicitly declined to pursue with a
defended reason for each decision.

The deliverable is not a list of credentials. It is the head's own
professional-development plan the head could take to their CEO or
their CHRO in a career conversation, and could take to a peer head
of role at a non-competing enterprise for peer review.

## Prerequisites

- Chapter [01-certifications-portfolio](../01-certifications-portfolio.md)
  — read and internalised. In particular, the four reasons the
  head holds credentials, the six-cluster taxonomy, and the
  sequencing framework (before promotion / within 12 months / within
  24 months / deferred or declined).
- Chapter [02-career-paths-into-role](../02-career-paths-into-role.md)
  — the route context the credentials plan is built for. The
  credentials sequencing depends on which of the seven routes the
  head is on or is preparing to make.
- Chapter [03-career-paths-out-of-role](../03-career-paths-out-of-role.md)
  — the onward-path context. The credentials plan preserves one
  or more onward routes; naming them is part of the plan.
- Chapter [[mod-108-leading-the-governance-function]] chapter 03
  on the development portfolio the head funds for the team. The
  head's own credentials plan composes with the team's credential
  progression.
- A specific scenario. Options:
  - Your own current career state (if working the exercise in
    role or in transition into role).
  - A specified hypothetical head with a defined route in (from
    the seven in chapter 02), a defined tenure length in the
    seat, a defined onward route or routes being preserved (from
    the five in chapter 03), and a defined sector context (from
    [[mod-111-sector-program-leadership]]).

## Steps

1. **Career-state one-pager.** Half-page scoping the head's
   current position: current role and tenure; route in (the seven
   from chapter 02); enterprise sector context; enterprise
   reporting line (CRO / CCO / CPO / CIO / CEO / other); onward
   route or routes being preserved (the five from chapter 03);
   personal financial and life-stage context relevant to the plan
   (whether a materially larger next-role compensation is a
   binding constraint; whether relocation is possible; whether
   time-availability for community-leadership commitments is
   constrained). The scoping is the substrate every subsequent
   step refers to.

2. **Current-portfolio inventory.** List every credential the head
   currently holds. Per credential: the issuing body; the current
   in-force status (active, lapsed, in-progress); the date first
   earned; the maintenance requirement (CPE hours per cycle,
   renewal fee, continuing-education requirement). The list
   includes credentials the head is midway through (exam
   scheduled, training completed, exam not yet sat) and
   credentials that have lapsed (with a note on whether the
   lapse is intentional).

3. **Cluster-by-cluster read.** Against the six-cluster taxonomy
   in chapter 01 — AI-governance baseline, AIMS operator, privacy
   adjacency, governance-risk-audit adjacency, independent-audit
   community, sector overlay — record the head's current position
   in each cluster and the head's stated stance (in-portfolio, in
   progress, planned within specific window, deferred with
   reason, declined with reason). No cluster is skipped; every
   cluster gets a stated stance.

4. **Sequencing plan.** Author the sequencing plan against the
   framework in chapter 01:
   - **Before promotion / next move.** Credentials the head will
     earn (or must have earned) before the anticipated next
     move. Per credential: the credential; the accrediting body
     or accredited training provider; the time-to-completion
     estimate; the current status (registered / in study /
     scheduled).
   - **Within 12 months in current role.** Credentials the head
     will earn during the next 12 months in the current role.
     Per credential: credential; provider; time estimate; the
     rationale ("this credential opens the [community] I am
     joining to preserve the [onward route] path").
   - **Within 24 months.** The 12-24 month horizon.
   - **Explicitly deferred.** Credentials the head could earn but
     is deferring, with a defended reason (not the same as
     declined — deferred is "later, when it makes operational
     sense"; declined is "not at all").
   - **Explicitly declined.** Credentials the head is not
     pursuing, with a defended reason. Chapter 01 names the three
     categories of declined credentials: insufficient
     accreditation of the provider; body-of-knowledge unaligned
     with the enterprise stance; credential is on the team
     rather than the head. Every declined credential is
     categorised.

5. **Route-preservation check.** For each of the onward routes
   the head is preserving (from chapter 03), verify the plan
   preserves it:
   - Path 1 (CAIO): does the plan include the strategy- and
     governance-of-IT credentials (CGEIT or MBA-adjacent) and
     the visible thought-leadership pieces the CEO reads as
     strategic vocabulary?
   - Path 2 (CRO / CCO / CPO): does the plan include the
     non-AI-adjacency credentials (CRISC / CGEIT / CISM for CRO;
     CGEIT + IIA CIA for CCO; CIPP + CIPM + CIPT for CPO) at
     depth?
   - Path 3 (NED): does the plan include director-education
     programme credentials (IoD Chartered Director, NACD
     Directorship Certification, INSEAD IDP-C, or jurisdictional
     equivalents) at an appropriate cadence?
   - Path 4 (public-sector CAIO): does the plan include the
     public-service network-building credentials or fellowships
     and the FedRAMP / NIST AI RMF community engagements the
     path relies on?
   - Path 5 (industry-body chair / academic): does the plan
     include the community-standing credentials and academic
     affiliations the path relies on?

6. **Team-progression alignment.** For the head's own function,
   map the credential progression the head funds against the
   levels in [[mod-108-leading-the-governance-function]] chapter
   03: level-15 (analyst), level-25 (engineer), level-35
   (evaluator), level-50 (architect). Verify the head's own
   credentials pattern models the discipline the head is asking
   the team to build. A head who has never held Lead Auditor
   asking the level-35 evaluator to earn it is a head coaching
   from an uncredible position.

7. **Signal-calibration check.** Read the current portfolio and
   the planned portfolio against the three anti-patterns in
   chapter 01:
   - **Wall-of-letters.** Does the portfolio hold twelve
     credentials without alignment? If so, prune to the
     substantive six-to-eight.
   - **Credential-holding without operational depth.** Does the
     portfolio include a credential the head has not exercised
     operationally (Lead Auditor without audit experience;
     ForHumanity Fellow without ForHumanity working-group
     contribution)? If so, name the operational-depth
     commitment that will accompany the credential.
   - **Credential-holding without community contribution.** Does
     the portfolio include a Fellow-level community credential
     without a scheduled community contribution? If so, name the
     specific commitment (working-group participation, BOK
     contribution, methodology co-authorship).

8. **Peer-review preparation.** Package the plan for peer review.
   Identify the peer — either a sitting head of AI governance at
   a non-competing enterprise, or an executive coach with
   head-of-role experience, or a mentor who has run analogous
   career-plan discipline. Send the plan with a specific request
   ("please stress-test the sequencing decision and the deferred /
   declined reasoning; please suggest one credential I have not
   considered that fits my route preservation").

## Deliverables

- `career-state.md` — the half-page scoping.
- `current-portfolio.md` — the credentials inventory.
- `cluster-read.md` — the six-cluster stance record.
- `sequencing-plan.md` — the four-horizon plan (before promotion;
  within 12 months; within 24 months; deferred; declined) with
  defended reasoning per entry.
- `route-preservation-check.md` — the onward-route preservation
  check per preserved route.
- `team-progression-alignment.md` — the team-credentials progression
  map compared against the head's own pattern.
- `signal-calibration.md` — the three-anti-pattern read.
- A peer-review request drafted for a named peer, with the
  material sent as an attachment.

## Acceptance criteria

- Every cluster has a stated stance. No cluster is skipped.
  Explicitly declined clusters are called out with defended
  reasoning.
- Every credential in the sequencing plan has a named provider
  (accredited or otherwise noted), a time-to-completion estimate,
  and a rationale that ties it to at least one of the four reasons
  from chapter 01 (signing authority, external-engagement
  standing, body-of-knowledge membership, team-signal / coaching-
  credibility). Credentials without a rationale attached are
  removed from the plan.
- Deferred credentials are distinguished from declined
  credentials, and both are distinguished from "planned within
  the horizon." All three stances are represented in the plan.
- The route-preservation check names the onward routes and
  verifies the plan preserves each. Where a route is not being
  preserved, the choice is explicit ("path 4 is not being
  preserved because personal financial constraints do not
  support the compensation differential").
- The team-progression alignment shows the credentials pattern
  the head funds for the team and identifies any gap where the
  head is asking the team to hold a credential the head has
  never held ("I ask level-35 evaluators to hold Lead Auditor;
  I do not hold it; my plan for closing the gap is [named]").
- The signal-calibration check produces a specific pruning
  recommendation (if wall-of-letters) or a specific commitment
  augmentation (if operational-depth or community-contribution
  gap).
- The peer-review request is drafted with a named peer, a
  specific ask, and an authored covering note (not "please
  review, thanks"). The exercise deliverable is the request; the
  peer's response is out of scope for the exercise.

## Stretch goals

- Add a *cost-of-plan* dimension: for the credentials the head
  will pursue, estimate the direct cost (exam fees, training
  provider fees, membership fees) and the indirect cost (paid
  study time). Reconcile against the credential-funding budget
  line in [[mod-113-budget-vendor-p-and-l-and-investment-case]].
- Add a *maintenance-risk* dimension: for each in-force
  credential, list the maintenance requirement, the last
  successful renewal, and the specific mechanism by which the
  head's continuing-education hours are being logged. A head
  whose Lead Auditor certification is about to lapse because
  they have not logged the CPE hours is a head whose plan does
  not survive contact with maintenance-time reality.
- Add a *contingency* dimension: what changes to the plan if
  the head's preserved onward route becomes unviable (the CAIO
  seat does not open at the enterprise; the enterprise's
  financial-services acquisition falls through, removing the
  MRM-adjacency need). Pre-decide before the change arrives.

## Notes and starter guidance

- The most common failure mode is producing a plan that is a
  wishlist rather than a decision. A plan without deferred and
  declined entries is a plan that has not made choices. Chapter
  01 is explicit that some credentials the head does not pursue;
  the plan reflects this.
- The rationale-per-credential requirement is not busywork. The
  head who cannot write a defended rationale for a credential is
  a head who has not decided whether to earn it — they are
  drifting toward earning it because it is a credential.
- The peer-review step is where the plan actually improves. A
  plan authored alone is a plan whose blindspots are the head's
  own. A plan reviewed by a peer surfaces the deferred credential
  the peer thinks is a mistake, or the declined credential the
  peer thinks is worth reconsidering, or the sequencing choice
  that a peer at another enterprise has already found to be
  problematic. The exercise's stated deliverable is the peer-
  review request; the actual value is in what comes back.
- The team-progression alignment step is often where new heads
  discover a coaching-credibility gap. If the plan has never
  aligned the head's own credentials against the team-
  progression discipline, the head is asking the team to invest
  where the head has not invested. Fixing the gap does not
  necessarily mean the head has to earn every team credential;
  it means the gap is explicit and the coaching pattern accounts
  for it.
- The exercise does not produce final decisions. It produces a
  plan that is ready to be tested — against peer review,
  against the head's executive coach, against the CHRO or CEO in
  a career conversation, and against the head's own annual
  self-review cadence. The plan is a living document; the
  exercise instantiates the first version.
