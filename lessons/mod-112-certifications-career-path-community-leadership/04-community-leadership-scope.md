# Designing the Community-Leadership Scope

## Motivation

The head of AI governance is not an enterprise-internal role in
full. The head who tries to run the seat as an internal executive
alone is the head whose external inputs go stale, whose standards-
body positions are set by peers who did engage, and whose
credibility on regulator engagements is thinner than the head who
was in the room when the guidance was drafted. Chapter
[[mod-106-external-positioning-and-voluntary-codes]] mapped the
enterprise's external-positioning portfolio at the *institutional*
scale — which voluntary codes the enterprise signs, which industry
bodies the enterprise joins, which position papers the enterprise
publishes. This chapter is the analogous discipline at the *head's
personal* scale: which working-group seats the head holds, which
committee memberships the head accepts, which working-group outputs
the head co-authors.

The two levels compose. The enterprise's external-positioning
portfolio is the vehicle; the head's community-leadership scope is
the *engine*. Enterprises do not participate in ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 42
in any operational sense — a specific delegate does. That delegate
is either the head, a senior architect the head has deputised, or
no one at all. When it is no one at all, the enterprise's stated
external-positioning posture is a fiction; when it is the head, the
enterprise's posture is under the head's active hand.

The chapter builds on three prior threads:

- The credentials portfolio ([[01-certifications-portfolio]]) is the
  entry ticket to specific communities — IAPP AIGP membership
  opens the IAPP body-of-knowledge committee; ISO/IEC 42001 Lead
  Auditor opens the certification-scheme-development discussion;
  ForHumanity Fellow opens the Fellows-only working groups.
- The career-paths-out chapter ([[03-career-paths-out-of-role]]) is
  where the community-leadership investment repays. Paths 3
  (NED), 4 (public-sector CAIO), and 5 (industry-body chair or
  academic affiliation) all turn on visible community leadership.
- The enterprise's external-positioning portfolio
  ([[mod-106-external-positioning-and-voluntary-codes]]) sets the
  *institutional* stance; the head's community-leadership scope is
  where the stance is operationalised week-to-week.

The chapter's practical output is the head's own *community-
leadership portfolio* — a named list of seats, with specific
commitments, cadences, and time budgets. The exercise
([[exercises/exercise-04-community-leadership-portfolio-authoring]])
walks the portfolio-construction discipline.

## Core concept — the community landscape

The AI-governance community landscape divides into five clusters.
No head participates in all five at operational depth; every head
reads the taxonomy and picks two-to-four seats that compose with
their enterprise's external-positioning portfolio and their own
career-path preservation.

### Cluster 1 — International standards bodies

The formal international standards ecosystem publishes the
frameworks the head cites at the audit committee, the regulator
engagement, and the certification-body audit. Working-group seats
here are where the frameworks *are drafted*.

- **ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 42 — Artificial Intelligence.** The joint
  ISO/IEC technical committee on AI. Publishes ISO/IEC 42001, ISO/IEC
  23894, ISO/IEC 42005 (impact assessment), ISO/IEC 42006
  (certification-body requirements), ISO/IEC TR 24028, and the
  broader family. SC 42 is organised into working groups (WG 1
  foundational standards; WG 2 data; WG 3 trustworthiness; WG 4 use
  cases and applications; WG 5 computational approaches and
  characteristics). Head participation runs through national
  mirror committees — the US TAG (Technical Advisory Group) hosted
  by ANSI; the UK BSI committee; the Canadian SCC committee;
  Germany's DIN NA 043 committee; Japan's JISC delegation. The
  head who wants voice in the international drafting joins the
  national mirror committee first; the mirror committee sends
  delegates to the international plenaries.
- **CEN-CENELEC JTC 21 — Artificial Intelligence.** The European
  standards committee developing the harmonised standards
  supporting the EU AI Act. JTC 21 is drafting the EN standards on
  risk management, transparency, human oversight, robustness,
  cybersecurity, and quality-management-system requirements that
  Notified Bodies use to certify AI Act conformity. Participation
  runs through the national standards bodies (BSI in the UK, DIN
  in Germany, AFNOR in France, UNI in Italy, NEN in the
  Netherlands, and others). Head participation at JTC 21 is
  materially important for enterprises with EU AI Act obligations.
- **IEEE Standards Association — AI standards series.** IEEE has
  published the 7000-series ethical-AI standards, the P2801-series
  algorithmic-decision-system standards, and the P3110-series on
  AI system testing. Participation is by individual membership;
  the head who wants to contribute joins the working group
  directly.
- **ITU-T Focus Group on AI and multimedia.** The International
  Telecommunication Union's AI-adjacent standardisation activity.
  Relevant where the enterprise operates in the telecommunications
  or media sector; head participation is via ITU-T member-state
  delegations.

### Cluster 2 — Public-sector and cross-sector consortiums

The public-sector consortiums are where the head engages with
government AI-safety and AI-governance bodies at working depth.
These seats are typically the head's substrate for path 4 (public-
sector CAIO) in [[03-career-paths-out-of-role]].

- **US AI Safety Institute Consortium (AISIC), hosted by NIST.**
  The consortium of enterprise, academic, and civil-society
  organisations engaged with the US AISI on evaluation
  methodology, model testing, and AI-risk research. Enterprise
  membership is by application; the head is typically the named
  enterprise representative, and specific working-group
  contributions (safety cases, capability evaluations, watermarking
  and provenance, red-team methodology) run through named
  workstreams.
- **UK AI Safety Institute engagement.** The UK AISI's evaluation
  partnerships and its AI Safety Summit-descended coordination
  activity engage frontier labs and other enterprises directly.
  Head participation is by named-enterprise engagement rather than
  formal consortium membership.
- **NIST AI Resource Center (AIRC) working groups.** The NIST AI
  RMF community engages through working groups on generative AI
  (produced NIST AI 600-1), on adversarial ML (NIST AI 100-2), on
  synthetic content (NIST AI 100-4), and on additional profiles in
  development. Participation is by individual application.
- **Federal CAIO Council (US federal-only) or the Chief AI Officer
  Council equivalents in other governments.** These are federal-
  agency-heads-of-role fora. Enterprise-side heads engage through
  invited participation in specific workstreams rather than
  standing membership.
- **International governmental AI Safety Institute Network.** The
  network of national AISI-equivalents (US, UK, Canada, Japan,
  Singapore, EU, France, Kenya) has begun coordinating on
  evaluation methodology and cross-border safety information
  sharing. Enterprise participation is at the intersection of
  frontier-lab engagement and public-sector consultation.

### Cluster 3 — Industry bodies (multi-stakeholder and sector-specific)

Industry bodies convene enterprise-side heads in cross-cutting
forums that publish position papers, host convenings, and shape
policy discourse.

- **Frontier Model Forum (FMF).** The industry body founded by
  Anthropic, Google, Microsoft, and OpenAI in July 2023 to promote
  responsible development of frontier AI. Membership has expanded;
  workstreams include safety research, best-practice publication,
  and policy engagement. FMF seats are for enterprises building
  frontier models; enterprise-side heads at frontier labs
  participate directly. See [[mod-111-sector-program-leadership]]
  chapter 06 for the frontier-lab context.
- **Partnership on AI (PAI).** The multi-stakeholder AI-governance
  body founded in 2016. Membership includes technology enterprises,
  academic institutions, and civil-society organisations; working
  groups publish practices on synthetic media, on model deployment,
  on labour and AI, and on fairness. PAI seats are typical for
  heads whose enterprises have a substantial cross-sector public
  posture.
- **MLCommons — AI Safety Working Group.** The industry-body
  benchmarking consortium's AI Safety working group publishes
  MLPerf AI-safety benchmarks and evaluation methodology. Head
  participation is via enterprise membership and specific
  benchmark-authoring commitments.
- **IAPP — International Association of Privacy Professionals.**
  The credential-issuing body for AIGP, CIPP, CIPM, and CIPT. Head
  participation includes the AIGP body-of-knowledge committee
  (which shapes the credential's exam scope), IAPP conference-
  program committees, and IAPP working groups on specific
  regulatory topics.
- **IIA — Institute of Internal Auditors.** Publishes the IPPF
  (International Professional Practices Framework) and coordinates
  the internal-audit profession globally. Head participation is
  via IPPF working groups on AI audit (where the head has
  internal-audit credentials or standing), and via national
  chapter engagement.
- **ISACA.** The credential-issuing body for CGEIT, CRISC, CISM,
  and CISA. Head participation includes ISACA working groups on
  AI governance and AI audit, and ISACA conference programs.
- **Sector-specific industry bodies.** Named in each sector's
  chapter in [[mod-111-sector-program-leadership]]:
  - Banking: Risk Management Association (RMA), PRMIA, GARP, Bank
    Policy Institute, Institute of International Finance (IIF).
  - Insurance: Casualty Actuarial Society (CAS), Society of
    Actuaries (SOA), NAMIC, APCIA, ACLI.
  - Healthcare: American College of Healthcare Executives (ACHE),
    American Medical Informatics Association (AMIA), HIMSS.
  - Pharmaceutical: Regulatory Affairs Professionals Society
    (RAPS), Drug Information Association (DIA), PhRMA, BIO, EFPIA.
  - Public sector: Federal CAIO Council, GSA / OFPP working groups.
  - Frontier lab: Frontier Model Forum (already listed).
  - Critical infrastructure: sector ISACs (FS-ISAC, E-ISAC,
    WaterISAC, others).

### Cluster 4 — Academic and policy institutions

Academic and policy-institution engagement provides research-depth
inputs the head reads into program design and provides the substrate
for path 5 (academic affiliation) in
[[03-career-paths-out-of-role]].

- **Executive-in-residence, distinguished-fellow, or research-
  affiliate positions.** Stanford HAI, MIT CSAIL, CMU CyLab,
  Oxford Institute for Ethics in AI, Cambridge Leverhulme Centre
  for the Future of Intelligence, Georgetown Center for Security
  and Emerging Technology (CSET), and jurisdictional equivalents.
- **Advisory-board positions at AI-safety research non-profits.**
  Institute for Advanced Study, Center for AI Safety, Machine
  Intelligence Research Institute, Alignment Research Center, and
  the analogous non-profits in Europe and Asia. These positions
  are typically unpaid or nominal-honorarium; participation is
  by invitation.
- **Think-tank non-resident-fellow positions.** Brookings, RAND,
  CSIS, CFR (US); Chatham House, RUSI, Ada Lovelace Institute
  (UK); the European Council on Foreign Relations, ECIPE (EU);
  CIGI (Canada).
- **Academic-conference program committees and journal editorial
  boards.** NeurIPS, ICML, ICLR, AAAI, FAccT, AIES, and the
  journal Nature Machine Intelligence, AI & Ethics, Big Data &
  Society, and jurisdictional equivalents. Head participation is
  as reviewer, area chair, or editorial board member.

### Cluster 5 — Independent-audit-community leadership

The independent AI audit community has scaled to a point where
working-group seats and BOK-contribution roles are meaningful. The
credentials in [[01-certifications-portfolio]] (ForHumanity Fellow,
BABL AI Fellow) are the entry tickets.

- **ForHumanity working groups on specific audit-scheme
  methodology.** ForHumanity organises working groups on the EU AI
  Act audit certification scheme, on the Colorado AI Act audit
  scheme, on NYC LL 144 bias-audit methodology, and on emerging
  sector schemes. Fellow-level contributors participate in scheme
  drafting and BOK maintenance.
- **BABL AI faculty and content-contribution roles.** Certified
  Auditor and Fellow-level contributors participate in bias-audit
  methodology development and training-content authoring.
- **AI audit-scheme accreditation-body working groups.** Where the
  scheme is being accredited by a national accreditation body
  (UKAS in the UK, ANAB in the US, DAkkS in Germany), the working
  group drafting the certification-body accreditation requirements
  is a meaningful seat.

## Seat selection — the portfolio-construction discipline

The head does not join every body. The head reads their enterprise
context, their career-path preservation, and their time budget and
constructs a *specific* portfolio of two-to-four seats. The
construction rule is that the portfolio composes with the
enterprise's external-positioning portfolio
([[mod-106-external-positioning-and-voluntary-codes]] chapter 03),
that each seat's cadence fits the head's calendar, and that the
portfolio preserves at least one onward path from
[[03-career-paths-out-of-role]].

### Selection principles

- **One "anchor" seat.** Every head holds one seat that is central
  to their enterprise's posture — a national-mirror-committee seat
  on ISO/IEC SC 42 for the enterprise whose ISO/IEC 42001
  certification depends on the mirror committee's positions; a
  Frontier Model Forum working-group seat for the frontier-lab
  head; an AISI Consortium named-enterprise seat for the head
  whose enterprise has committed to public evaluation cooperation;
  a NAIC Big Data and AI (H) Working Group liaison seat for the
  insurance-sector head. The anchor seat is the seat the head
  reports on quarterly to the audit committee.
- **One "adjacency" seat.** A seat outside the anchor cluster that
  extends the head's perspective — a Partnership on AI working
  group for the frontier-lab head whose day work is
  frontier-only; an IIA IPPF AI-audit working group for the CRO-
  adjacent head whose day work is compliance-heavy; an academic
  advisory-board seat for the head whose day work is entirely
  operational.
- **One "credential-community" seat.** A seat that maintains the
  head's active membership in a credentialling community — the
  IAPP AIGP body-of-knowledge committee, an ISACA working group,
  an IIA chapter. This seat is the seat the head funds the team's
  credential progression against ([[01-certifications-portfolio]]
  team-progression section).
- **Optionally, one "onward-path" seat.** For the head who is
  preserving path 3 (NED) or path 4 (public-sector CAIO) or path
  5 (academic affiliation), a fourth seat specifically curated for
  the onward path — director-education programme membership for
  the NED-path head; Federal CAIO Council working-group liaison
  for the public-sector-path head; adjunct-teaching commitment for
  the academic-path head.

### Time budget calibration

Each seat has a time cost. The head calibrates:

- Working-group seats at international standards bodies typically
  cost 100-300 hours per year (working-group calls at monthly
  cadence, national-mirror-committee meetings at quarterly cadence,
  international plenaries at biennial cadence, contribution to
  drafting cycles).
- Public-sector consortium seats (AISIC, NIST AIRC) cost 60-200
  hours per year depending on workstream commitment.
- Industry-body working-group seats cost 80-200 hours per year.
- Academic and think-tank fellow positions cost 40-120 hours per
  year for the non-resident variants.
- Independent-audit-community working-group seats cost 60-150
  hours per year.

A four-seat portfolio at operational-contribution depth is
typically 400-800 hours per year — 8-15% of a working year. The
head who signs into more than four seats is a head running
underweight commitments and producing thin contributions; the head
who signs into fewer than two is a head whose community-standing
does not compound.

### The joint-seat protocol with the enterprise

The head's community-leadership seats are typically enterprise-
sponsored — the enterprise pays travel, conference fees, membership
dues, and reads the head's contributions as part of the enterprise's
external-positioning output. The head negotiates the joint-seat
protocol with their CEO and General Counsel:

- **Attribution.** Does the head attend as a named individual (with
  the enterprise disclosed), as an enterprise representative
  bound to the enterprise's ratified positions, or as a
  private-capacity individual? The three attributions carry
  different obligations; the negotiation is done in writing.
- **Confidentiality.** Working-group deliberations at SC 42, at
  the AISI Consortium, at the FMF are frequently pre-decisional
  and confidential. The head's participation is subject to the
  body's confidentiality rules; the enterprise's own disclosure
  obligations (SEC 8-K, sector-supervisory reporting, EU AI Office
  reporting) may conflict; the boundary is worked with the GC.
- **Publication.** Working-group outputs the head co-authors are
  published under the working body's imprimatur, sometimes with
  named-individual authorship. The head negotiates whether the
  enterprise is disclosed in the authorship line and whether the
  publication is pre-cleared by the CEO or the GC.
- **Time.** The enterprise treats the community-leadership hours as
  in-role time, not personal time. The head's job description
  and their annual objectives explicitly include the community-
  leadership commitments; the audit committee reads the community-
  leadership work as part of the head's assessed contribution.

## Working-group outputs — position papers and comment letters

The most operational output of community-leadership work is the
*co-authored working-group output*. Three formats recur:

### Position papers

A position paper is a 5-20 page document authored by the working-
group participants (often with a named lead and co-authors),
publishing a considered stance on an emerging topic. Position papers
frequently precede regulator consultations, shape professional-body
guidance, or set community expectations. The head reads and
contributes to two-to-six position papers per year across their
portfolio.

The head's contribution disposition:

- Lead-author role on one paper every 12-24 months. Lead-author
  implies substantive drafting, response-to-comment coordination,
  and public authorship attribution.
- Co-author or contributor role on two-to-four papers per year.
- Reviewer or comment role on the balance.

### Comment letters to regulator consultations

Regulator consultations — the EU AI Office's implementing-act
consultations, the FTC's AI-in-commerce inquiries, the CFPB's
algorithmic-credit-decisioning circulars, the SEC's cybersecurity-
rule proposals, the OMB's federal-AI memoranda — invite public
comment. The head participates in these through two channels:

- **The enterprise's own comment letter**, filed by the enterprise
  and typically co-signed by the CEO or the head as accountable
  official.
- **The working-body comment letter**, filed by an industry body,
  a standards body, or an audit-community body, and co-signed by
  the working-group participants. The head reads and edits these
  as a working-group member.

The two channels compose. The enterprise's own comment carries the
enterprise's specific stance; the working-body comment carries the
collective stance the enterprise has helped shape. Both are
citeable in the enterprise's regulator-engagement package
([[mod-104-regulator-engagement]] chapter 08).

### Standards-drafting text

Where the head participates in a standards body (SC 42, JTC 21,
IEEE), the working-group's output is the standard itself. The
head's contribution is at the specific-clause level — proposing
text, commenting on ballots, participating in Committee Draft (CD),
Draft International Standard (DIS), and Final Draft International
Standard (FDIS) review cycles. Standards-drafting is slower than
position-paper authorship (a standard typically takes 2-4 years from
first working draft to publication) but produces the most durable
output — the head's contribution to a published ISO/IEC standard
outlasts their tenure at their enterprise.

## Board and advisory positions — the specific composition

The head accumulates board and advisory positions over the tenure.
The composition distinguishes:

- **Non-executive-director seats** at other enterprises (the
  path 3 route in [[03-career-paths-out-of-role]]). These carry
  fiduciary duty and are governance seats.
- **Advisory-board seats** at start-ups, at consultancies, at
  research non-profits, at industry bodies. These are advisory
  rather than fiduciary and typically carry lower time commitment.
- **Board-of-visitors or trustee positions** at universities or
  policy institutes. These are governance seats at non-profits.

Each seat is negotiated under the joint-seat protocol with the head's
employer. The head who accepts more than two governance seats
outside their employer is a head running a portfolio that
approaches an NED-portfolio career; the enterprise's willingness to
support the portfolio depends on the enterprise's own read on the
head's continued employment intent.

## Conference-keynote cadence — the visibility-versus-substance calibration

Conference keynotes are the most visible form of community
leadership. The head calibrates the cadence carefully — every
keynote is a preparation cost of 20-60 hours (writing, rehearsal,
Q&A preparation, follow-up), and the enterprise reads keynote
frequency against substantive contribution.

The typical annual cadence:

- **Two to four "flagship" keynotes** per year at conferences whose
  audience is the head's peer community — IAPP Global Privacy
  Summit, RSA Conference, Money 20/20 (financial), HIMSS
  (healthcare), Frontier Model Forum-hosted convenings, national
  AI Safety Institute-hosted events, ISO SC 42-hosted plenaries.
- **Four to six "sector-specific" keynotes** per year at
  sector-specific conferences the enterprise's sector context
  requires — RMA and PRMIA Risk Management conferences for
  banking; CAS and SOA meetings for insurance; RAPS and DIA for
  pharma; ACHE and AMIA for healthcare.
- **A limited count of "expert-panel" appearances** — 6-12 per
  year, mixed between industry conferences and regulator-hosted
  workshops.

The head who exceeds this cadence is a head running a keynote
tour rather than a head-of-role; the enterprise's read on the
pattern turns unfavourable when the pattern crosses the
15-per-year threshold.

## Academic-affiliation decisions

The path-5 academic-affiliation route in [[03-career-paths-out-of-role]]
turns on a decision the head makes years in advance. The typical
progression:

- **Non-resident-fellow status** at a policy institute or think
  tank in the head's early tenure. Signals the head's contribution
  bandwidth and creates a research-affiliation record.
- **Adjunct-faculty status** teaching AI governance in a
  professional or executive-education programme. Signals the head's
  teaching bandwidth and creates a teaching record.
- **Executive-in-residence or distinguished-fellow appointment** in
  mid-tenure at a university's business school, law school, or
  policy institute. Signals substantive research contribution and
  is a natural next-step from the non-resident-fellow status.
- **Endowed-chair appointment** in late tenure or post-departure
  from head-of-role. This is the full-scale academic path and
  requires substantial prior visible academic contribution.

The head who intends the academic path builds it in that sequence.
The head who intends a different onward path may still hold
adjunct-faculty or non-resident-fellow status as adjacencies
without committing to the endowed-chair path.

## Summary

- The community-leadership scope is the head's *personal* external
  engagement, composing with the enterprise's *institutional*
  external-positioning portfolio
  ([[mod-106-external-positioning-and-voluntary-codes]]). The two
  compose; the enterprise's stated positioning is fiction without
  the head's operational engagement.
- The community landscape divides into five clusters — international
  standards bodies (SC 42, JTC 21, IEEE, ITU-T); public-sector and
  cross-sector consortiums (AISIC, NIST AIRC, Federal CAIO
  Council, AI Safety Institute Network); industry bodies (FMF,
  PAI, MLCommons, IAPP, IIA, ISACA, sector-specific bodies);
  academic and policy institutions (universities, think tanks,
  research non-profits, journal editorial boards); and
  independent-audit-community working groups (ForHumanity, BABL AI,
  scheme-accreditation working groups).
- The head constructs a two-to-four-seat portfolio using the
  selection principles — one anchor seat, one adjacency seat, one
  credential-community seat, optionally one onward-path seat.
  Each seat's time cost is calibrated against the head's calendar;
  the total portfolio is typically 400-800 hours per year.
- The joint-seat protocol with the enterprise (attribution,
  confidentiality, publication, time) is negotiated in writing
  with the CEO and General Counsel. The community-leadership
  hours are in-role time, not personal time.
- Working-group outputs — position papers, regulator-consultation
  comment letters, standards-drafting text — are the operational
  substance of community-leadership work. The head co-authors
  two-to-six position papers per year, contributes to comment
  letters at both enterprise-filed and working-body-filed
  channels, and where they participate in standards bodies
  drafts at the specific-clause level.
- Board and advisory positions accumulate under the joint-seat
  protocol; conference-keynote cadence is calibrated at 2-4
  flagship keynotes and 4-6 sector-specific keynotes per year, with
  a total ceiling under 15 keynote or panel appearances annually.
  Academic-affiliation decisions are made years in advance and
  progress from non-resident-fellow through adjunct-faculty to
  distinguished-fellow to endowed-chair as the head chooses the
  path 5 route.
