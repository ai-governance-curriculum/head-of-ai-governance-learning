# The Certifications Portfolio for a Head of AI Governance

## Motivation

Certifications do not make the head. The head's craft was built in
the years covered by [[mod-101-role-scope-and-leadership-shape]]
through [[mod-111-sector-program-leadership]], not in a two-day
review course. But the head still holds a specific set of
credentials, in a specific sequence, for four separable reasons —
and the head who cannot state the reasons ends up either
under-credentialed (unable to sign into the artefacts the
enterprise's AIMS or its audit-committee brief expects a credentialed
head to sign into) or over-credentialed (a wall of letters that
signals status-seeking rather than competence). This chapter is the
disciplined answer to *which credentials, in what order, and why*.

The four reasons the head holds credentials at all:

1. **Signing authority.** ISO/IEC 42001 requires the AI management
   system to designate individuals with defined competencies for
   specified roles. An unaccredited head signing an AIMS management
   review is a first-round audit finding an accredited head simply
   does not produce.
2. **External-engagement standing.** The regulator, the notified
   body, the industry-body counterpart, and the audit-committee peer
   read the head's credentials in the first thirty seconds of
   contact. A head with the credentials the counterpart expects is
   a head whose subsequent argument gets weighed on its merits; a
   head without them spends the meeting explaining themselves.
3. **Body-of-knowledge membership.** The credential earns entry to a
   professional community — the IAPP AIGP body of knowledge
   committee, the ISO SC 42 mirror-committee working groups, the
   ForHumanity Fellows programme, the ISACA / IIA member networks.
   That membership is the substrate the community-leadership
   chapters ([[04-community-leadership-scope]] and
   [[05-thought-leadership-cadence]]) build on.
4. **Team-signal and coaching credibility.** The head funds the
   team's certification progression as part of the development
   portfolio in [[mod-108-leading-the-governance-function]]
   chapter 03. A head who has not held the credentials they ask the
   team to earn coaches from an uncredible position. The head's
   own credentials are how the head models the investment.

The chapter opens the module because the credentials portfolio
shapes every subsequent question. The career-path chapters
([[02-career-paths-into-role]] and [[03-career-paths-out-of-role]])
turn on which credentials are earned before promotion and which
after. The community-leadership chapter ([[04-community-leadership-scope]])
draws its working-group seats from the credential bodies. The
public-speaking discipline ([[05-thought-leadership-cadence]]) is
supported by credential-anchored authority. The difficult career
conversation ([[06-difficult-career-conversation-rehearsal]])
sometimes turns on whether the head is holding a credential the
lateral role would strand.

## Core concept — the credentials taxonomy

Head-of-role credentials divide into six clusters. Not every head
holds every cluster; every head reads the taxonomy and knows which
they hold, which they are earning, which they have deliberately
decided not to earn, and why.

- **AI-governance baseline.** The near-universal credential for the
  role: IAPP AIGP (AI Governance Professional). This is the entry
  ticket; a head-of-AI-governance without AIGP in 2026 is unusual
  enough that it needs a defence.
- **AIMS operator credential.** ISO/IEC 42001 Lead Implementer, and
  where the head is directly involved in AIMS audit rather than
  running the operator side, ISO/IEC 42001 Lead Auditor. Required
  in substance where the head runs an ISO/IEC 42001-certified AIMS
  or is bidding to.
- **Privacy adjacency cluster.** Where the head's reporting line
  passes through or is peered with the Chief Privacy Officer, or the
  enterprise's AI programme has strong privacy-adjacent obligations
  (GDPR, CPRA, DPDPA, Quebec Law 25): IAPP CIPP/US or CIPP/E, plus
  CIPM (privacy management) and CIPT (privacy technology). The
  privacy trio is not required at every enterprise, but where it
  is required it is required in that combination.
- **Governance-risk-audit adjacency cluster.** Where the head's
  reporting line runs through the Chief Risk Officer or the head
  answers to the audit committee's risk-culture expectations:
  ISACA CGEIT (governance), ISACA CRISC (risk), ISACA CISM
  (information-security management, where the head owns the
  AI-security interface with the CISO). The IIA CIA (Certified
  Internal Auditor) applies where the head came into the role
  from the internal-audit function and retains audit-community
  standing.
- **Independent-audit-community adjacency cluster.** Where the head
  engages with independent AI audit firms as counterparty
  (commissioning independent audits) or as peer (contributing to
  the audit-scheme methodology): ForHumanity Fellow, BABL AI
  Fellow. These are community-standing credentials rather than
  regulatory prerequisites, but the community they admit the head
  to is a specific working population the head repeatedly
  encounters at conferences, in working groups, and in
  standards-body deliberations.
- **Sector-specific overlay cluster.** Where the head runs a
  sector-specific AI-governance programme ([[mod-111-sector-program-leadership]]):
  banking heads carry the sector's model-risk-management vocabulary
  and often the Global Association of Risk Professionals FRM or
  the PRMIA PRM (as risk-management community credentials rather
  than AI-specific); insurance heads carry the CPCU or SOA
  actuarial-adjacent credentials the sector expects; health-system
  heads carry an HIMSS credential where the enterprise's CIO
  organisation reads them as standing. These are enterprise-
  specific and read against the sector chapter's guidance rather
  than the AI-governance-baseline expectation.

The taxonomy is deliberately not organised as "which credentials to
earn." It is organised as "which clusters the head reads themselves
against, given their reporting line, sector, and career trajectory."
The next section runs the credential-by-credential detail; the
section after that returns to the sequencing question.

## The credentials in detail

### IAPP AIGP — the AI-governance baseline

The Artificial Intelligence Governance Professional (AIGP)
credential is offered by the International Association of Privacy
Professionals (IAPP). The body of knowledge covers the domains the
head is expected to be conversant in — AI foundations, AI impacts on
individuals and society, AI governance and risk management,
frameworks and standards (NIST AI RMF, ISO/IEC 42001, ISO/IEC 23894),
and law and policy (EU AI Act, US federal AI policy, sector
regulation).

The head sits AIGP early — typically before or shortly after
promotion into the role. The exam is knowledge-focused, and passing
it demonstrates the head has read the same body of knowledge every
credentialed peer has. AIGP is not the marker of the head's
seniority; it is the marker that the head speaks the field's shared
vocabulary. A head who has done the work of running an AIMS at scale
will find AIGP straightforward; a head who has not may find it
requires deliberate study on the standards and the law and policy
domains specifically.

The head who is investing in the team funds AIGP for the level-25
and level-35 team members ([[mod-108-leading-the-governance-function]]
chapter 03) as an early credential — it establishes the shared
vocabulary and it is inexpensive relative to its signalling value.

### ISO/IEC 42001 Lead Implementer and Lead Auditor

Where the enterprise operates an ISO/IEC 42001-certified AI
Management System, the head reads themselves against two credentials
in the ISO/IEC 42001 family:

- **ISO/IEC 42001 Lead Implementer.** The credential for the
  professional who leads the design, implementation, and
  maintenance of the AIMS. Where the head is running the AIMS
  operator side (working with the internal AIMS team, coordinating
  external certification), Lead Implementer is the head's
  operational credential.
- **ISO/IEC 42001 Lead Auditor.** The credential for the
  professional who audits an ISO/IEC 42001-certified AIMS. Where
  the head runs the ISO/IEC 42001 audit function (either as an
  internal-audit adjacency at large enterprises with an internal
  ISO auditor programme, or as a certification-body auditor at
  audit firms), Lead Auditor is the operational credential.

At most enterprise heads-of-role, Lead Implementer is the primary
credential the head personally holds. Lead Auditor is more common
among heads whose career path came from the internal-audit function
or the Big Four consulting practice.

The credential providers include the accredited training and
personnel-certification bodies for the ISO/IEC 42001 scheme — PECB,
BSI, and IRCA (the international register the professional-certification
of auditors runs against). The head reads accreditation of the
training provider carefully — a "Lead Implementer" course from an
unaccredited provider is not the same credential as one from an
IRCA-accredited or PECB scheme-accredited provider, and the
distinction shows up at the audit committee when the head is asked
to explain the credential.

### The privacy adjacency cluster — CIPP, CIPM, CIPT

The IAPP privacy credentials — Certified Information Privacy
Professional (CIPP), Certified Information Privacy Manager (CIPM),
and Certified Information Privacy Technologist (CIPT) — apply where
the head's role interfaces heavily with the enterprise privacy
programme. The interface is designed at
[[mod-109-cross-enterprise-leadership]] chapter 03 (CPO / DPO
coordination); the credentials are the working professional's marker
that they can hold that interface at pace.

- **CIPP** has jurisdictional variants: CIPP/US, CIPP/E (Europe),
  CIPP/C (Canada), CIPP/A (Asia). The head earns the variant
  matching the enterprise's primary privacy jurisdiction (or
  jurisdictions, at multinationals). CIPP is the substantive
  privacy-law credential.
- **CIPM** is the privacy-programme-management credential. Where
  the head runs an AI-governance function that also operates as
  the enterprise's AI-privacy interface (typical at mid-sized
  enterprises without a fully-scaled CPO function), CIPM is
  operationally direct.
- **CIPT** is the privacy-technology credential — where the head
  has significant hands-on interface with privacy-enhancing
  technologies (differential privacy, federated learning,
  privacy-preserving ML), CIPT reads as technical seriousness.

The head who has done privacy work before the AI-governance role
often carries the CIPP + CIPM combination already. The head who
came through the technical route (level-50 architect route) may earn
CIPP/E or CIPP/US as a post-promotion investment when the
enterprise's EU exposure or California exposure requires the head
to sign into privacy-adjacent artefacts.

### The governance-risk-audit adjacency cluster — ISACA CGEIT, CRISC, CISM; IIA CIA

The ISACA credential family covers three positions the head
frequently sits inside or peer-to:

- **CGEIT — Certified in the Governance of Enterprise IT.** The
  governance credential — how the enterprise's governance of IT
  (including AI) is structured. Where the head reports to a CIO or
  a Chief Digital Officer, or where the enterprise reads its AI
  governance as a specialisation of IT governance under ISO/IEC
  38500, CGEIT reads as substantive.
- **CRISC — Certified in Risk and Information Systems Control.**
  The risk credential — how the enterprise's information-systems
  risk is quantified and treated. Where the head reports to the
  CRO, or where the enterprise's AI risk programme reads through
  the parent enterprise risk framework (COSO ERM, ISO 31000, ISO/IEC
  27005), CRISC reads as substantive.
- **CISM — Certified Information Security Manager.** The
  information-security-management credential. Where the head owns
  the AI-security interface with the CISO ([[mod-109-cross-enterprise-leadership]]
  chapter 01), CISM anchors the interface at technical depth. CISM
  is not universal for the AI-governance head — the CISO holds the
  security programme — but a head who never opens CISM depth
  cannot productively coordinate with the CISO's team on OWASP LLM
  Top 10, MITRE ATLAS, or NIST AI 100-2 (adversarial ML) work.

The IIA credential — **CIA (Certified Internal Auditor)** — applies
where the head's career path came through internal audit or where
the head retains a coordinating role with the Chief Audit Executive
([[mod-109-cross-enterprise-leadership]] chapter 05). A head who
came from CAE-track carries CIA already; a head who came from
technical-track and now has substantial audit-committee interface
sometimes earns CIA mid-tenure to strengthen the third-line
coordination language.

### The independent-audit-community adjacency cluster — ForHumanity Fellow, BABL AI Fellow

The independent AI audit community has established two Fellow-level
credentials that carry weight in specific working circles:

- **ForHumanity Fellow of AI Ethics (FHF/AI Ethics).** ForHumanity
  is a non-profit that has authored a series of independent audit
  frameworks against specific AI regulations (EU AI Act,
  NIST AI RMF, NYC LL 144, Colorado AI Act, and a growing set of
  sector overlays). Fellow status is earned through their training
  and examination programme. Where the head commissions
  ForHumanity-affiliated independent audits, or contributes to
  ForHumanity working-group deliberations on audit-scheme
  methodology, the Fellow credential is the currency of the
  community. Fellow status also opens the ForHumanity BOK
  contribution channels — a professional route the head uses when
  the community-leadership chapter's position-paper cadence pulls
  them into audit-methodology authoring.
- **BABL AI Fellow (or Certified Auditor).** BABL AI runs
  algorithmic-audit training and certification with a bias-audit
  specialisation. BABL AI's bias-audit methodology has been
  operationally influential in the NYC LL 144 bias-audit surface
  and is being read into sector adaptations. The Fellow-level
  credential admits the head to the BABL AI faculty community and
  the algorithmic-bias-audit body of practice.

Neither is a regulatory prerequisite. Both are professional-community
credentials. The head who has strong community-leadership scope
([[04-community-leadership-scope]]) holds at least one; the head who
has scoped community engagement narrowly may hold neither.

### The sector-specific overlay cluster

The sector chapter ([[mod-111-sector-program-leadership]]) enumerates
the sector-specific credentials the head reads themselves against.
This chapter does not repeat that list; the sector-specific credentials
are read from the sector chapter and folded into the head's own
credential plan as sector-specific investments.

The one general principle: sector credentials do not substitute for
the AI-governance-baseline (AIGP) or the AIMS-operator (ISO/IEC 42001)
credentials. The head who is a banking specialist and holds FRM or
PRM still holds AIGP as the AI-governance-baseline marker. The
sector credential extends the head into the sector's own vocabulary;
it does not replace the head's AI-governance vocabulary.

## The sequencing decision — before promotion versus after

The sequencing question — which credentials to earn before
promotion into head-of-role, which to earn after — is where the
chapter earns its keep. The wrong sequencing costs the head either
a delayed promotion (they were not credentialed enough to be a
credible candidate) or a delayed year in role (they were promoted
but had to spend the first two quarters catching up on credentials
the audit committee expected).

The default sequencing:

**Before promotion into the head-of-role.**

- IAPP AIGP. The near-universal baseline. Almost every head-of-role
  candidate is expected to have earned it before entering the
  interview loop, or at minimum to have it in progress and to have
  scheduled the sitting.
- ISO/IEC 42001 Lead Implementer (or Lead Auditor, depending on
  route). Where the target enterprise runs an ISO/IEC
  42001-certified AIMS or intends to bid for one within 18 months,
  the head is expected to hold Lead Implementer at hire. Earning
  it after promotion is possible; a candidate arriving without it
  is at a disadvantage in a head-to-head competitive process.
- The route-specific credential the candidate brought from their
  prior role. A senior-ai-governance-architect (level 50) candidate
  brought CIPP/E or a technical-track credential from their prior
  work; a Head of MRM candidate brought FRM or PRM plus SR 11-7
  operational depth; a CCO-track candidate brought CGEIT + CRISC
  or CIA; a consulting-partner candidate brought their firm's
  quality-management credential plus AIGP.

**Within the first 12 months in role.**

- The privacy trio (CIPP + CIPM + CIPT) if the head's reporting
  line is privacy-adjacent and the head arrived without them.
- ISACA CRISC or CISM if the head's reporting line is CRO-adjacent
  or CISO-adjacent and the head arrived without them.
- ISO/IEC 42001 Lead Auditor as a second credential, if the head
  arrived with Lead Implementer and now coordinates with the
  external certification body at cadence.

**Within the first 24 months in role.**

- ForHumanity Fellow or BABL AI Fellow as the community-leadership
  scope in [[04-community-leadership-scope]] settles into a
  specific working-group commitment. Earning these before the
  community engagement is committed produces credentials the head
  does not use.
- IIA CIA if the head has grown a substantial audit-committee
  interface and the CAE coordination pattern
  ([[mod-109-cross-enterprise-leadership]] chapter 05) has scaled.

**Explicitly deferred or declined.**

Some credentials the head explicitly does not pursue. The head
decides deliberately and can defend the choice:

- Credentials from providers whose accreditation status the head
  reads as insufficient. A "Certified AI Governance Expert" from
  an unaccredited private provider is not a credential the head
  wants to hold, and an audit committee that reads the credential
  and reads the provider forms a specific view of the head's
  credential discipline.
- Credentials whose body of knowledge the head reads as unaligned
  with the enterprise's operating stance. A credential that treats
  AI governance as a marketing-communication discipline is not a
  credential the head-of-role at a supervised financial institution
  or a Frontier lab pursues.
- Credentials the head has decided the team should hold instead.
  A head-of-role does not need every technical credential on the
  team; the head funds the level-25 engineer's CISA / SANS
  security-analyst credential rather than earning it personally.

## The certification-signal calibration

Certifications are read for signal. The head calibrates the signal
they send by holding the *right combination*, not the *maximum
count*. Three anti-patterns the head avoids:

**Wall-of-letters.** Holding twelve credentials reads as
status-seeking. Six well-chosen credentials — AIGP + ISO/IEC 42001
Lead Implementer + CIPP + CIPM + CRISC + Fellow-status in one
community — read as disciplined portfolio construction. The audit
committee's implicit question is not "how many credentials" but
"do the credentials align with the role's substantive requirements";
alignment is what the audit committee credits.

**Credential-holding without operational depth.** The head who
holds Lead Auditor but has never audited an AIMS reads as thin. The
credential requires supporting evidence — the head who holds Lead
Auditor and can talk through the actual audits they contributed to
reads as credentialed; the head who holds it and cannot reads as
having sat an exam. Credentials without operational depth are
liabilities under sustained cross-examination.

**Credential-holding without community contribution.** A Fellow of
a community the head does not contribute to reads as
credential-shopping. The head who holds ForHumanity Fellow status
and appears on a ForHumanity working-group publication reads as
part of the community; the head who holds Fellow status and does
not contribute reads as having bought entry.

The signal calibration is the reason chapter 04's community-leadership
work and chapter 05's thought-leadership work compose with the
credentials chapter here. The credentials are the entry ticket; the
community and thought-leadership work is the visible evidence that
the head is using the ticket for its intended purpose.

## The head funds the team's credential progression

The final section closes the loop with the coaching cadence in
[[mod-108-leading-the-governance-function]] chapter 03. The head
does not just earn credentials themselves; the head funds a
credential progression for every team member as part of the
development portfolio.

The typical progression:

- **Level 15 (analyst).** IAPP AIGP within the first twelve months.
  Certified Information Privacy Professional (variant matching
  jurisdiction) within the second twelve months if the analyst is
  on the privacy-adjacent track.
- **Level 25 (engineer).** IAPP AIGP + CIPT within the first
  eighteen months. ISACA CRISC within the second twenty-four months
  if the engineer is on the risk-adjacent track.
- **Level 35 (evaluator).** ISO/IEC 42001 Lead Implementer within
  the first eighteen months. ForHumanity Fellow or BABL AI Fellow
  where the evaluator's community engagement is settling.
- **Level 50 (architect).** ISO/IEC 42001 Lead Auditor as a second
  ISO credential, plus at least one governance-risk credential
  (CGEIT or CRISC). ISACA CGEIT is the natural governance-of-AI
  credential for the architect who is authoring the enterprise's
  AI-governance-of-enterprise-IT stance.
- **Level 60 (head).** The portfolio detailed above.

The credential-funding budget is a line item in the function's
budget request ([[mod-113-budget-vendor-p-and-l-and-investment-case]]),
and the head defends it to the CFO as an operating investment in
capability rather than a discretionary benefit.

## Summary

- The head-of-role holds credentials for four separable reasons —
  signing authority, external-engagement standing,
  body-of-knowledge membership, and team-signal / coaching
  credibility. A credential that does not serve at least one of
  the four is a credential the head does not pursue.
- The credentials taxonomy has six clusters: AI-governance baseline
  (IAPP AIGP); AIMS operator (ISO/IEC 42001 Lead Implementer,
  Lead Auditor); privacy adjacency (CIPP + CIPM + CIPT); governance-
  risk-audit adjacency (ISACA CGEIT / CRISC / CISM; IIA CIA);
  independent-audit community (ForHumanity Fellow, BABL AI Fellow);
  and sector-specific overlays from [[mod-111-sector-program-leadership]].
- The sequencing discipline: AIGP and ISO/IEC 42001 Lead
  Implementer (plus route-specific credential) before promotion;
  privacy trio and ISACA credentials in the first 12 months in
  role; ForHumanity / BABL Fellow status and IIA CIA in the first
  24 months; some credentials explicitly deferred or declined
  where the accreditation is insufficient, the body of knowledge is
  unaligned, or the credential belongs on the team instead of the
  head.
- The signal calibration matters. Six well-chosen credentials read
  as portfolio discipline; twelve credentials read as
  status-seeking. Credentials without operational depth or
  community contribution read as thin. The credentials
  chapter composes with the community-leadership chapter
  ([[04-community-leadership-scope]]) and the thought-leadership
  chapter ([[05-thought-leadership-cadence]]) — credentials are
  the entry ticket, community and thought-leadership work is the
  visible evidence.
- The head funds the team's credential progression as part of the
  development portfolio ([[mod-108-leading-the-governance-function]]
  chapter 03) — every level in the ladder has a credential
  investment the head funds, defends to the CFO, and signals as
  part of the function's operating discipline.
