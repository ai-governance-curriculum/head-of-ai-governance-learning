# Coordinating with the Chief Audit Executive on the Third-Line AI Audit Programme

## Motivation

The head runs the second line. The Chief Audit Executive (CAE) runs
the third line. The two roles are neighbours in the IIA Three
Lines Model, and their relationship is often the single most
consequential relationship the head has for the credibility of
the AI-governance program as a whole. A working relationship
with the CAE produces audit engagements the head can defend and
audit findings the head can act on; a broken relationship
produces audit findings that read as adversarial in the audit-
committee record, and the head spends the following year
defending the second line's basic legitimacy rather than
building program capacity.

The load-bearing distinction in this chapter is simple to state
and easy to get wrong in practice. The head *does not run* the
third-line AI audit programme. The CAE runs it. The head is a
*subject* of the third-line audit, not its owner, not its
sponsor, and not its co-designer. Confusing that boundary — the
head "helping the CAE plan the AI audit programme", the head
"co-authoring the third-line's methodology", the head arranging
for a specific engagement to be paused because it would surface
findings inconvenient to the head — is the credibility failure
that will define the head's tenure if it is discovered by the
audit committee.

This chapter is how the head coordinates with the CAE inside
the correct boundary. The head is transparent, cooperative, and
substantively engaged. The head is not a co-owner, a designer,
or a controller of the third-line programme. The two dispositions
look similar from the outside and are qualitatively different
in practice, and the CAE reads the difference immediately.

## Core concept — what the CAE owns and what the head owes the CAE

The IIA International Professional Practices Framework (IPPF) —
the standard the CAE operates against — sets the CAE's authority
and independence at enterprise scope. The CAE is:

- **Independent of management.** The CAE reports functionally to
  the audit committee (or its equivalent) and administratively to
  the CEO or another senior executive; independence from the
  functions being audited (including the head's function) is a
  foundational IPPF requirement.
- **Authorised to audit any part of the enterprise.** The audit
  plan is the CAE's; the audit committee ratifies it; the CAE
  runs it against a risk-based methodology.
- **Owner of the third-line-audit methodology.** How an audit is
  planned, executed, tested, and reported is the CAE's craft,
  not the head's.
- **Responsible for the audit-committee-facing read on
  enterprise assurance.** The CAE reports on the state of
  assurance across the enterprise; the head's function is one
  of the subjects the CAE reports on.

Against this authority, what the head *owes* the CAE is a
specific set of transparent, timely, and substantive
contributions to make the CAE's work efficient without crossing
into ownership.

- **A current, accurate picture of the second-line function's
  shape and operating discipline.** The head provides the
  function's org chart (chapter
  [[01-function-org-shape-and-reporting-lines]]), the operating
  charter, the AIMS scope statement
  ([[mod-102-running-the-program]] chapter 04), the KRI/KPI
  portfolio ([[mod-107-program-measurement-and-maturity]]
  chapter 01), and the current control library and evidence
  index. Not because the CAE asked; because a working third-
  line-audit function needs the current picture and the head is
  the source.
- **Timely notification of material events.** Material incidents
  ([[mod-110-incident-leadership]]), material regulatory
  change ([[mod-105-regulatory-change-management]]), material
  audit-committee-scope events (a KRI breach, a stop-ship call,
  a material third-party finding). The head does not wait for
  the CAE to ask; the head notifies proactively.
- **Access to the function's personnel for interview.** When the
  CAE's audit team wants to interview a level-15 analyst, a
  level-25 engineer, or a level-50 architect, the head makes
  the person available on the audit team's timeline, not the
  head's. Delays here are read as obstruction.
- **Substantive engagement on findings.** When the CAE
  surfaces a finding, the head engages substantively — agreeing
  where the head agrees, disagreeing where the head disagrees,
  and doing so through the response-and-remediation channel the
  CAE runs. The head does not respond politically; the head
  does not treat the finding as personal.
- **No influence on the audit plan.** The head does not
  campaign for specific engagements to be run or not run, does
  not suggest scopes, does not offer to "help" with methodology.
  If the CAE asks the head for a view — which they may, for
  planning inputs — the head shares the view honestly and
  makes clear it is input, not direction.

What the head *does not owe* and does not provide:

- **Advance approval or veto over audit findings.** The head
  does not review a draft finding for the CAE's clearance
  before it goes into the record. The CAE's finding is the
  CAE's; the head responds through the channel.
- **Suggestions on which team members should be interviewed.**
  The CAE picks; the head produces the interviewees.
- **"Preferred timing" for engagements.** The head does not
  tell the CAE that a specific quarter is inconvenient for a
  particular engagement. The CAE runs the plan against
  enterprise risk; the head's convenience does not enter.
- **A cover story to the audit committee.** The head does not
  align with the CAE on a joint narrative to the audit
  committee. The CAE reports independently. The head presents
  the head's own material independently. Both are heard by the
  committee; where they diverge, the committee weighs both.

## The head's disposition toward the CAE — the working stance

Beyond the transactional contributions above, the head runs a
specific working stance with the CAE. Three properties.

**Transparent by default.** The head does not curate what the
CAE sees. If the second line has a specific concern about a
first-line practice, the CAE hears about it in the head's
routine engagement with the CAE, not in a defensive posture
after the CAE has surfaced it independently. The head who curates
is the head who is one CAE inquiry away from a finding on the
head's own transparency.

**Cooperative but not chummy.** The head builds a working
relationship with the CAE — regular meetings, mutual respect,
professional trust — without becoming social intimates. The CAE
is a professional peer, not a friend; the CAE's independence
depends on the ability to render findings that are unwelcome
to the head without personal cost to the relationship. A head
who has cultivated intimacy with the CAE is a head who has
made the CAE's independence harder to run.

**Substantively engaged, not politically engaged.** The head
engages with the CAE on the substance of findings, on the
substance of the enterprise's assurance state, on the substance
of what the third-line audit programme is telling the audit
committee. The head does not engage on which findings will be
"received well" by the audit committee, on which findings the
head would prefer to see re-scoped, or on which CAE-team
members are "easy to work with". The substantive-engagement
stance is legible; the political-engagement stance produces
the credibility failure the second-line function will not
recover from.

## The regular coordination cadence

The head and the CAE run a regular coordination cadence. Not
ad hoc. Regular, predictable, minuted.

**A quarterly working meeting.** Ninety minutes to two hours,
scheduled six-plus months ahead. Standing agenda:

- The CAE's read on where the third-line AI audit programme is
  in its cycle (engagements complete, in flight, on the plan
  for the following quarter). The CAE speaks; the head listens
  and asks substantive questions.
- The head's read on where the second-line function is (major
  workstreams, KRI/KPI trends the CAE should be aware of,
  substantive changes to the function's shape or operating
  discipline). The head speaks; the CAE listens and asks
  substantive questions.
- Cross-cutting themes — enterprise-scope AI risk developments
  (regulatory, technological, incident-shape) that both roles
  are tracking, upcoming audit-committee-scope events, cross-
  second-line-and-third-line coordination on specific issues.
- Discussion of any material findings from the CAE's completed
  engagements since the last meeting, the head's remediation
  responses, and the outstanding items.
- Discussion of any material second-line issues the head has
  raised at the audit committee or risk committee since the
  last meeting, the CAE's read on how they landed.

The meeting is minuted (by the CAE's team, typically) and the
minute is shared with the CRO. This is not a private meeting;
this is a professional cadence between two enterprise-scope
functions with third-party visibility.

**Ad hoc notifications, both directions.** Between the standing
cadence, the head notifies the CAE proactively on material
events (an incident of Article-73-adjacent severity, a
material regulatory-response deadline, a stop-ship call that
went to the CAIO on appeal). The CAE notifies the head
similarly (a finding that will land at the next audit-
committee cycle, a scope decision on the audit plan that
affects the head's function, an external-audit-body
engagement the CAE is coordinating that the head will need to
support).

**A joint annual reflection with the audit-committee chair.**
Once a year — typically before the audit committee's annual
plan-ratification cycle — the head, the CAE, and the audit-
committee chair meet to reflect on the working relationship,
on the state of the enterprise's assurance, and on the
following year's coordination shape. The meeting is the audit-
committee chair's cadence; the head and the CAE attend and
contribute; the head does not use it to lobby the chair, and
the CAE does not use it to lobby the head.

## Preparing for a third-line AI audit engagement

Once or twice a year, the CAE's audit plan will put a specific
AI-governance engagement on the calendar. The engagement
scope varies — a specific control-cluster audit, a specific
process audit (the AIA process, the third-party-vendor
process, the incident-response process), an audit of the
function's own effectiveness, or a targeted audit against a
specific regulatory obligation. The head prepares specifically.

**On engagement notification, the head does not renegotiate
scope.** The CAE tells the head an engagement is coming, with
the scope and the target timeframe. The head asks clarifying
questions (what evidence set will the audit request, what
personnel interviews are anticipated, what the timeline for
draft findings looks like) but does not push back on the
scope itself unless the scope is factually mis-specified
(referencing a control set the enterprise has retired, for
example, or a system the enterprise has decommissioned).

**The head runs an internal readiness pass.** Not to hide
findings from the audit; to know what the audit is likely to
find, so that the head's response is informed rather than
surprised. The readiness pass typically involves the level-50
architect and the level-35 evaluators running the same
evidence review the audit would run, against the audit's
stated scope, to a first-cut read. Findings surfaced in the
readiness pass are not concealed from the audit; they are
addressed in the head's remediation plans and (where they are
already remediated) are on the record with the CAE from the
start.

**The head produces the requested evidence on the audit's
timeline.** The audit team asks for evidence artefacts,
personnel interviews, and access. The head produces them
promptly and completely. Late production, incomplete
production, "we can't find that document" — these are the
signals that turn a routine audit into an escalated audit.
The head runs the evidence-production discipline as a level-15
analyst's specific responsibility, with the head available
for CAE-team questions about escalations.

**The head engages substantively on draft findings.** The CAE's
process typically involves sharing draft findings for the
head's factual review before the findings are finalised. The
head reviews for factual accuracy — not for tone, not for
severity re-classification, not for finding withdrawal — and
responds substantively. Where the head disagrees on the facts,
the head marshals the evidence and makes the substantive case
through the CAE's channel. Where the head agrees, the head
says so.

**The head authors the remediation response formally.**
Findings that stand after the factual-review round get a
remediation response from the head — what the head will do,
by when, with what success signal. The remediation response
is the head's public commitment to the audit committee; the
head takes it seriously and honours it. Missed remediation
dates are findings the CAE will revisit at the next
engagement cycle, and repeated misses become audit-committee-
level concerns.

**The head's team is coached on how to be audited.** New team
members — particularly those coming from first-line adjacent
sources (product, engineering) — may not have prior
experience being audited. The head coaches them: what an
interview looks like, how to answer substantively without
volunteering material beyond the question, how not to
speculate, how to handle a question the person genuinely does
not know the answer to (say so; commit to a follow-up),
what an audit relationship looks like as a normal part of the
function's operating rhythm.

## Third-line audit findings — how the head responds

When findings land, the head responds substantively rather than
politically. The response has a specific shape.

**Read the finding for what it says, not for what the head
wants it to say.** The finding is the CAE's professional read
on the second-line function. If the finding surfaces a control
weakness, the finding is naming a control weakness; the head
does not read it as the CAE being difficult, as the audit team
being new, or as the audit-committee-facing agenda being
somebody's political preference. The head reads the substance.

**Distinguish acknowledged from disagreed findings clearly.**
For findings the head accepts, the head says so directly in
the response. For findings the head disputes on the facts, the
head disputes on the facts specifically. For findings the head
accepts factually but considers over-severity-rated, the head
addresses the severity classification separately from the
facts. Distinguishing carefully is what makes the head's
disagreement credible; global "we accept the finding but
disagree with the framing" postures read as head-losing-the-
argument-diplomatically, and the audit committee sees through
it.

**Author the remediation plan against the substance of the
finding.** Not against the finding-narrative language, and not
against a broader "improvement program" umbrella. Specific,
concrete, dated, owner-named remediation actions. See chapter
[[mod-102-running-the-program]] chapter 05 on maturity-
assessment corrective-action discipline; the shape composes.

**Publish the remediation status on cadence.** The head reports
progress on outstanding audit findings at each audit-committee
cycle
([[mod-103-board-and-audit-committee-reporting]] chapter 02).
The CAE reports independently. Both reports name the same
findings; both name the current status; where the two diverge,
the audit committee sees the divergence and asks the head and
the CAE to reconcile.

**Learn from the finding, in the operating discipline.** A
finding on the AIA turnaround process should surface as a
change to the AIA process
([[mod-102-running-the-program]] chapter 06 adjacent). A
finding on the evidence-completeness pipeline should surface
as a change to the pipeline. A finding on function shape or
resourcing should surface in the multi-year hiring plan
(chapter [[02-multi-year-hiring-plan]]) or the succession
picture (chapter [[01-function-org-shape-and-reporting-lines]]).
The finding-to-change loop is what turns findings from cost to
value.

## When the head disagrees with the CAE materially

Occasionally, the head and the CAE will genuinely disagree on
a substantive finding or a methodology question. The disagreement
is legitimate — the head has second-line context the CAE does
not, and the CAE has third-line-audit craft the head does not.
Handling the disagreement well is a specific discipline.

**Have the disagreement first with the CAE, not with the
audit committee.** The head raises the disagreement in the
head-CAE working meeting, or in a specific escalation session
between the two. The head does not lobby the audit-committee
chair to overturn the CAE's finding; that path is the
credibility failure that ends the working relationship.

**Marshal substantive evidence for the head's position.** A
disagreement based on the head's professional judgment against
the CAE's professional judgment is a disagreement the CAE will
typically win, because the audit committee weights the third-
line's independence heavily. A disagreement based on
substantive evidence the CAE did not have when the finding was
drafted is a disagreement that can genuinely reshape the finding
or its severity.

**Accept the CAE's final call if the substantive engagement
does not resolve the disagreement.** The CAE is the third-line
authority; the CAE's finding is the finding of record. The head
can note the disagreement in the remediation response — "the
head's view remains that the finding overstates the severity;
the head accepts the finding of record and will remediate as
described" — but the head does not continue to litigate the
finding after it is finalised.

**If the head believes the CAE has genuinely erred at a
material level, the head raises it with the audit committee
chair privately and formally, once.** This is a rare escalation.
The head describes the substantive concern, offers the head's
evidence, and asks the chair for the chair's read. The chair
either supports a re-examination or supports the CAE. The head
respects the chair's decision either way.

## The head-CAE relationship at audit-committee scope

At the audit committee, the head and the CAE appear together
on the agenda. The head presents the second-line function's
material; the CAE presents the third-line's read on the same
material. The two presentations are independent by design; the
audit committee reads them side-by-side.

The head's discipline in the room:

- **The head does not preview the CAE's material.** The head
  presents the second-line material as if the CAE's material
  is unknown to the head; the CAE presents independently.
- **When the CAE surfaces a finding the head has already
  addressed in remediation, the head responds substantively.**
  "The finding is noted; the remediation described in the pack
  is on track; the head will report progress at the next
  cycle." No spin, no attempt to steal the CAE's thunder, no
  attempt to soften the finding retroactively.
- **When the CAE surfaces a finding that comes at the head as
  news in the room, the head takes the finding seriously and
  commits to a substantive response by the next cycle.** The
  head does not push back in the room on a finding they were
  not prepared for; that pushback reads as defensive. The head
  hears the finding, notes it, and comes back with substance.
- **The head does not use the audit committee to grade the CAE
  or the CAE's team.** Feedback on the CAE's craft goes
  through the CAE (or, at the audit committee's request,
  through the audit-committee chair); it does not go into the
  audit-committee record as the head's editorial.

## Anti-patterns the head does not permit

**The "collaborative audit planning" pattern.** The head
proposes to "help" the CAE plan the year's audit engagements,
suggests scopes, offers preferred timing. The CAE's independence
is compromised in appearance if not in fact, and the audit
committee's read on both roles suffers. The remedy is the
disposition described above — the head does not shape the plan,
period.

**The "pre-clearing" pattern.** The CAE shares draft findings
with the head for factual review; the head uses the round to
negotiate finding language and severity. The CAE either folds
(and the audit committee eventually notices findings soft-
pedalled compared to peer institutions) or holds (and the
working relationship deteriorates). The remedy is the head
engaging on facts, not on tone.

**The "friendly audit committee" pattern.** The head cultivates
the audit-committee chair as an ally and lobbies the chair
between meetings to shape how the CAE's material will be
received. The chair notices; the CAE notices; the head loses
both. The remedy is the head's independent relationship with
the chair, which is legitimate and appropriate, run without
using the chair against the CAE.

**The "audit-fatigue" pattern.** The head treats the CAE's
audit engagements as burdensome distractions from the head's
"real work" and communicates the frustration through delayed
evidence production, missed personnel-interview availability,
and slow remediation. The CAE eventually escalates; the audit
committee eventually intervenes. The remedy is the head
running the third-line-audit engagement as part of the head's
own operating discipline — the level-15 analyst on evidence-
production time, the head's own calendar available for CAE-
scheduled interviews, the remediation as first-order work.

**The "no relationship" pattern.** The head and the CAE have
minimal contact between audit engagements. When an engagement
lands, the head is unprepared and the CAE is proceeding
against a stale picture of the function. Both suffer. The
remedy is the quarterly cadence and the ad hoc notification
discipline — the working relationship is the enabling
condition for productive audit engagements.

**The "co-authored narrative" pattern.** The head and the CAE
present a joint story to the audit committee, aligned in
substance and framing. The audit committee reads this as the
second and third lines having lost their independence from
each other, which is a governance failure across both lines.
The remedy is the parallel-independent-report shape — the
head's material and the CAE's material are separately
authored and separately presented, even when they concur.

## Summary

- The CAE runs the third-line AI audit programme; the head does
  not. The head is a *subject* of the programme, not its owner
  or co-designer.
- What the head owes the CAE: current shape and operating-
  discipline artefacts, timely notification of material events,
  personnel access, substantive engagement on findings, no
  influence on the audit plan.
- What the head does not owe and does not provide: advance
  approval or veto over findings, suggestions on interviewees,
  preferred engagement timing, joint audit-committee
  narratives.
- The head's working stance with the CAE: transparent by
  default, cooperative but not chummy, substantively engaged
  rather than politically engaged.
- Regular coordination cadence: a quarterly working meeting,
  ad hoc bidirectional notifications, and a joint annual
  reflection with the audit-committee chair.
- Preparing for a third-line audit engagement: no scope
  renegotiation, internal readiness pass, prompt evidence
  production, substantive factual engagement on draft
  findings, formal remediation response, ongoing team
  coaching on how to be audited.
- Responding to findings: read the substance, distinguish
  acknowledged from disputed clearly, remediate concretely,
  publish status on cadence, learn from the finding in the
  operating discipline.
- Handling material disagreement: raise with the CAE first,
  bring substantive evidence, accept the CAE's final call,
  reserve the audit-committee-chair escalation for genuinely
  material material errors and use it once.
- At audit committee: independent parallel presentations,
  substantive response to CAE findings without spin or
  defensiveness, no editorialising on CAE's craft in the
  record.
- Six anti-patterns the head refuses: collaborative audit
  planning, pre-clearing, friendly audit committee against
  the CAE, audit-fatigue, no relationship, and the co-
  authored narrative.
- Chapter [[06-cxo-vendor-relationships]] runs the head's
  vendor relationships at CxO scope (a distinct discipline
  from the CAE relationship); chapter
  [[07-difficult-people-leadership-conversations]] runs the
  personal difficult-conversation discipline the head applies
  when disagreements — with the CAE, with a peer CxO, or
  within the head's own team — reach the difficult level.
