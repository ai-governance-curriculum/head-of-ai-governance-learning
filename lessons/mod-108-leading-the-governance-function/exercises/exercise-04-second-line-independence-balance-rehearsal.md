# exercise-04: Second Line Independence Balance Rehearsal

**Estimated effort:** 3 hours

## Objective

Author the enterprise's **second-line independence-balance
operating discipline** against the function shape produced in
exercise 01, the hiring plan produced in exercise 02, and the
coaching cadence produced in exercise 03. The discipline is a
set of written, rehearsable artefacts the head carries into any
specific pressure moment — a joint operating agreement with the
Chief Risk Officer on stop-ship escalations, three scripted
pressure-pattern rehearsals (release-timeline, relationship-
capture, CxO-adjacent), a stop-ship operating procedure, an
operational-distance detection panel of five tests with their
cadence and threshold rules, and an annual first-line-partner
interview and second-line-team survey instrument. The deliverable
is the artefact the head uses to coach the team through pressure
moments, the artefact the CRO reads as the operating agreement
between the two of them, the artefact the audit committee reads
as the written discipline that answers "how do you know your
second line is both close enough and independent enough", and
the artefact the CAE reads as one of the inputs on the third-
line audit engagement
([05-cae-and-third-line-coordination](../05-cae-and-third-line-coordination.md)).

The discipline has to survive three stress tests. First, a
level-35 evaluator comes under direct pressure from a product
VP to soften a fairness finding on a hot launch — the
release-timeline pressure rehearsal has to carry the evaluator
through without the head authoring the next move in the
moment. Second, an embedded analyst has been with the same
product team for twenty-two months and the head's level-50
architect flags that the analyst's recent reads are
consistently more sympathetic than the rest of the function —
the relationship-capture rehearsal, the bounded-embed rotation
discipline from chapter 03, and the second-line-team survey
have to produce an evidence-grounded recovery plan rather than
an awkward reassignment. Third, the CAIO routes a stop-ship
conversation around the head to the CRO — the joint operating
agreement and the CRO 1:1 cadence have to leave the CRO
already briefed by the time the CAIO's call lands.

The discipline is not the head's private read. It is a
published, ratified, periodically-reviewed artefact the
function and the CRO's office both operate against.

## Steps

1. **Inherit the enterprise context.** Confirm the exercise-01
   context one-pager is current and reference it. The
   discipline's specifics (where embeds sit, where the stop-
   ship escalation path routes, which CxO relationships
   create the acute pressure patterns) depend on the sector,
   operating-model shape, portfolio shape, and current CRO's
   operating model on the one-pager.

2. **The proximity / independence / trust operating
   statement.** Author the half-page operating statement the
   head publishes to the function and to the CRO. The
   statement names:
   - What proximity means in the enterprise's context —
     specifically what shared context the second line
     maintains with the first line (participation in which
     meetings, read-in on which artefacts, cadence of
     relationship conversations). Avoid the generic "we're
     close to product" formulation; name the specific
     mechanisms.
   - What independence means in the enterprise's context —
     specifically which incentive structures the head owns
     and refuses to compromise (performance-review
     authorship, compensation determination, assignment
     allocation, promotion decisions). Reference the
     reporting-line-decision table from exercise 01 as the
     operating artefact that fixes these.
   - What trust is being measured against — not announced,
     measured. Specifically which behaviours the first line
     reads as the signal (early engagement before the
     council review, confidential-until-escalated advice,
     the head backing team members publicly after
     well-founded concerns).
   The statement is signed by the head and ratified by the
   CRO; it is the head's public commitment the team can hold
   the head accountable to.

3. **The CRO joint operating agreement on stop-ship
   escalations.** Author the specific joint operating
   agreement the head and the CRO co-sign on how a stop-ship
   call escalates. The agreement answers:
   - Who is notified when the governance council calls stop
     (CAIO, business-line CxO, GC, CRO, head), on what
     timeline (immediately, within the same business day,
     before the following council session), by whom (the
     head personally for CxO-scope notifications, the
     level-50 architect for GC and CRO notifications where
     appropriate).
   - Who has authority to overrule the council and under
     what written standard (typically the CAIO or CRO with
     a defined-standard appeal; the standard is cited
     verbatim in the agreement).
   - How the decision is minuted — the specific wording
     template the council minute uses to record the stop-
     ship call and any appeal disposition.
   - How the decision is communicated to the audit
     committee — through the standing audit-committee cycle
     report ([[mod-103-board-and-audit-committee-reporting]]
     chapter 02), through an interim notification where the
     severity warrants it, or both.
   - The post-call-review protocol — who convenes, who
     attends, when (typically 2–4 weeks after the call
     closes), the standing questions, the publication
     discipline.
   - The review cadence of the agreement itself (annually
     at minimum; sooner on a material incident or
     organisational change). The agreement is minuted and
     signed; a verbal operating agreement is chapter 04's
     implicit-coordination anti-pattern.

4. **The CRO standing cadence shape.** Author the standing
   1:1 cadence with the CRO:
   - Frequency (weekly for most enterprises; fortnightly
     where the CRO's bandwidth is genuinely limited and the
     head has standing access through other channels).
   - The standing-hold discipline (same day, same time,
     rarely rescheduled; the chapter is explicit that this
     is not optional).
   - The standing agenda: material active engagements and
     active pressure moments; the head's preview of items
     the head expects to reach CxO or committee level; the
     CRO's enterprise-context download (regulator
     conversations, audit-committee-chair concerns, peer-CxO
     dynamics); any specific coordination asks either
     direction.
   - The note-taking and action-capture discipline.
   - The quarterly inclusion of the cross-second-line
     calibration (cyber, financial crime, operational risk,
     model risk, privacy, ethics) convened by the CRO.
   The cadence is published to the head's own calendar and
   to the function's rituals — not a private head-CRO
   secret.

5. **Rehearsal 1 — the release-timeline pressure.** Author
   the specific rehearsal of pressure pattern 1 from chapter
   04. Pick a concrete scenario — a specific product launch,
   a specific substantive finding (a fairness regression,
   an evidence-completeness gap, a supply-chain provenance
   issue), a specific launch timeline that creates the
   pressure. For the scenario:
   - The evaluation engineer's prepared move: the specific
     wording of the substantive concern the engineer takes
     to the governance council (anchored in evidence; see
     chapter 04 on evidence-anchored versus narrative-
     anchored concerns).
   - The head's prepared move: who the head calls before
     the council session (the CRO; the CAIO if adjacency
     warrants a pre-brief; the GC if legal exposure is
     material), what the head says, in what order.
   - The council session's prepared move: the chair
     discipline (chapter 04 says the head attends in
     person and chairs; name the specific framing the
     chair uses), the stop-ship-call standard the chair
     cites verbatim, the minute template the council runs
     against.
   - The appeal-path prepared move: if the CAIO or CRO
     invokes the appeal path, the head's prepared
     substantive case (not the head's personal
     disappointment at the appeal; chapter 04 is explicit
     that the appeal path is a feature, not a bug).
   - The post-call public backing move: the specific
     language the head uses in the team's rhythm to back
     the evaluation engineer publicly, calibrated for the
     outcome (concern was well-founded; concern turned out
     to be wrong; concern was partially right).
   The rehearsal is written out, not sketched. The head
   should be able to read the rehearsal cold and know what
   to say at each step.

6. **Rehearsal 2 — the relationship-capture pressure.**
   Author the specific rehearsal of pressure pattern 2.
   Pick a concrete scenario — a Shape B embedded analyst
   (or an equivalent embed under the function's own shape)
   who has been with the same product team long enough
   that the capture risk is live, and specific signals
   the level-50 architect has flagged (first-person-
   plural language in weekly reports; consistently
   sympathetic risk reads against the function's own
   calibration; declining attendance at the extended-
   function all-hands). For the scenario:
   - The signal review protocol: the specific signals the
     head looks at in the embedded analyst's 1:1s, in the
     level-50 architect's calibration reads, in the second-
     line-team survey (step 10 below), in the first-line-
     partner interview (step 10 below).
   - The coaching move: the specific 1:1 session the head
     runs with the analyst — the opening, the specific
     observations the head names (chapter 04 is explicit
     that the "we're worried about the launch window"
     first-person-plural usage is coachable; the head
     names it specifically rather than vaguely), the
     recalibration the head asks for, the follow-up
     cadence.
   - The rotation move: if coaching does not produce
     recalibration within the agreed window, the head
     reassigns the analyst to a different embed or to the
     centralised function per the bounded-embed-rotation
     discipline from chapter 03.
   - The communication move to the product team: the
     specific framing the head uses when the embed rotates
     out (the product team reads a rotation as a signal
     and may misread it; the head frames the rotation as
     the planned bounded-embed discipline, not as the head
     disciplining the analyst).
   - The post-rotation debrief with the analyst: the
     specific debrief the head and the level-50 architect
     run with the analyst to translate the embedded
     experience into function-centre craft.
   The rehearsal addresses the quietest, slowest, most
   emotionally awkward of the three pressure patterns; the
   writing is the discipline that gets the head through it.

7. **Rehearsal 3 — the CxO-adjacent pressure.** Author the
   specific rehearsal of pressure pattern 3. Pick a concrete
   scenario — the CAIO or a business-line CxO responding to
   a substantive second-line concern by routing around the
   head to the CRO (or above the CRO) rather than engaging
   substantively. For the scenario:
   - The head's prepared posture: substantive, not
     defensive; the specific opening sentences the head
     uses in the meeting the CxO requests, the specific
     questions the head asks to surface whether the CxO
     has substantive context the head did not have, the
     specific lines in the sand the head holds.
   - The pre-CRO briefing move: the specific CRO 1:1 the
     head takes the brief into (chapter 04 is explicit
     that the CRO hears the head's version first), the
     specific framing the head uses, the specific CRO
     coordination the head asks for.
   - The pack-and-minute discipline: the specific wording
     the head holds in the audit-committee pack and the
     council minute against the pressure to soften the
     concern. "Some concerns exist that may warrant
     consideration" versus "the fairness evaluation shows
     a substantive disparate impact that requires
     mitigation before release" — the head cites both
     formulations and names which is the head-in-the-room
     formulation.
   - The nuclear-option move: if the CRO's own posture
     becomes untenable, the specific audit-committee-chair
     direct-access session the head runs (chapter 04 is
     explicit that this is a once-per-tenure escalation
     the head does not deploy carelessly). The rehearsal
     names the specific conditions under which the head
     would deploy it, and the specific preparation the
     head does before doing so.
   This rehearsal is the hardest to write honestly. Force
   the honesty: the head who cannot author the nuclear-
   option move in rehearsal is the head who cannot
   execute it in the live moment.

8. **The stop-ship-call operating procedure.** Author the
   specific operating procedure the AI Governance Council
   runs when a stop-ship call is on the table:
   - The written standard the council applies (per
     chapter 04 — some formulation of "a substantive risk
     that cannot be adequately mitigated on the planned
     launch schedule and that materially exceeds the
     enterprise's risk appetite for the system's class";
     the enterprise's exact formulation goes here).
   - The evidence-not-narrative discipline — the specific
     evidence the council cites (evaluation outcome,
     control-testing result, impact-assessment finding)
     and the specific minute template that anchors the
     call in evidence.
   - The appeal path — how the appeal is invoked, by whom,
     within what window, under what standard, with what
     disposition shape (reversed, upheld, varied) and
     minuting.
   - The post-call review protocol — the specific
     conveners, participants, questions, timeline, and
     publication discipline from step 3's joint operating
     agreement, carried through at the procedural level.
   - The KRI binding — the stop-ship-call frequency, the
     stop-ship-call appeal-disposition rate, the
     stop-ship-call-leading-to-release-delay rate — on the
     head's KRI dashboard
     ([[mod-107-program-measurement-and-maturity]]
     chapter 01), with the standing threshold rules
     (chapter 04: rising rate signals design-time
     upstream issues; falling rate ambiguous between
     maturity and capture; spike signals specific
     systemic issue).

9. **The operational-distance detection panel.** Author
   the specific instrumentation of each of the five tests
   from chapter 04. For each test:
   - **The intake-source test.** The specific definition
     of ambient-discovered versus formal-intake additions
     (what counts as which; who records the attribution;
     where the attribution lives), the quarterly review
     protocol, the threshold rule the head reads against
     (falling ratio over two consecutive quarters is a
     signal; falling ratio over three is a finding; name
     the specific actions the head takes).
   - **The referred-issue test.** The specific tracking
     mechanism (where "we're thinking about X, what do
     you think" conversations are logged; who logs them;
     the per-level-50 architect weekly rhythm the
     tracking fits into), the quarterly review protocol,
     the threshold rule.
   - **The stop-ship-call frequency and shape.** The
     specific metric definition (per-quarter rate; per-
     release-portfolio rate; the appeal-disposition
     distribution), the KRI binding, the standing read
     the head and CRO review together.
   - **The first-line-partner interview.** The specific
     cadence (annual; or event-triggered on head
     suspicion); the sampling protocol (which product and
     engineering leaders; across which product lines;
     from which levels); the interview-guide shape (open
     structured questions, not survey; chapter 04 is
     explicit on this); the publication discipline (the
     head publishes an anonymised aggregation of what
     they heard and what the head is doing about it).
   - **The second-line-team survey.** The specific
     cadence (annual; or event-triggered on head
     suspicion); the anonymity-and-trust discipline; the
     survey-instrument shape (the specific questions,
     with a Likert-scale backbone and open-text follow-
     ups); the publication discipline inward (the head
     reads the survey substantively and acts on
     substantive findings).
   The panel is read in aggregate, not test by test. Author
   the aggregation protocol: the quarterly head-and-level-
   50-architect read, the semi-annual head-and-CRO read,
   the annual head-and-audit-committee-chair read.

10. **The first-line-partner interview guide and
    second-line-team survey instrument.** Flesh out the two
    instruments the detection panel depends on:
    - The interview guide — six to ten open structured
      questions (not a survey). Starter: what has worked in
      the second line's engagement with your team this year;
      what has not; what do you need from the second line
      that you are not getting; what does the second line do
      that you find unhelpful; where would you want the
      second line to engage earlier; where would you want
      the second line to engage less. Add two to four
      enterprise-specific questions calibrated to the
      operating-model shape.
    - The team survey instrument — ten to fifteen Likert
      items with open-text follow-ups. Starter items from
      chapter 04's explicit set: whether team members feel
      they can raise concerns without pressure to soften;
      whether they feel backed when they do; whether they
      feel captured by their product embeds (where
      applicable); whether they feel the head is present
      enough to back them. Add items on the specific
      pressure patterns the function is likely to see given
      the exercise-01 shape.
    Both instruments are versioned and dated; both carry a
    publication discipline (the head publishes an
    aggregated read with the head's intended actions, inside
    an agreed timeline).

11. **The CxO adjacency map.** Author the specific CxO
    adjacency map — the CRO (solid line), the CAIO (strongest
    peer adjacency; chapter 04 identifies CAIO capture as a
    specific anti-pattern the head refuses), the GC, the
    CFO, the CIO/CTO, the Chief People Officer or HR
    equivalent, the business-line CxOs the function most
    materially interacts with, the audit-committee chair.
    For each, name:
    - The specific adjacency cadence the head runs (standing
      1:1, quarterly working session, episodic).
    - The specific independence-exposure the adjacency
      creates (CAIO: strategic-priority capture; CFO:
      budget-cycle pressure; business-line CxO: release-
      timeline pressure through the product org; etc.).
    - The specific counter-discipline the head runs against
      the exposure (the chapter-04 CRO 1:1 as the check on
      the CAIO relationship; the audit-committee-chair
      direct access as the ultimate check on CRO alignment
      with CxO pressure).

12. **The failure-mode audit.** Author a short section — one
    paragraph per failure mode — naming how the discipline
    detects and recovers from each of chapter 04's six
    anti-patterns:
    - The check-the-box second line.
    - The permanent-embed pattern.
    - The head's own capture (particularly through the
      CAIO relationship).
    - The soft-language pattern on substantive findings.
    - The over-frequent stop-ship pattern.
    - The isolation posture.

## Acceptance criteria

- The proximity / independence / trust operating statement
  names specific mechanisms rather than generic claims. A
  statement that says "we are close to product and
  independent of product" without naming the specific
  mechanisms underneath each fails the criterion.
- The CRO joint operating agreement names, specifically, who
  is notified, on what timeline, by whom, under what standard
  the appeal path operates, how the decision is minuted and
  communicated to the audit committee, and the review cadence
  of the agreement itself. A section with a TBD or an "as
  appropriate" placeholder fails the criterion.
- Each of the three pressure-pattern rehearsals is written
  out as a specific scenario with the head's prepared moves
  at each step. A rehearsal that sketches the pattern in
  the abstract without a concrete scenario fails the
  criterion — the head cannot execute an abstract rehearsal
  under pressure.
- The stop-ship operating procedure cites the written
  standard verbatim (the enterprise's specific formulation,
  not a placeholder). A procedure that defers the standard
  to "as the council sees fit" is the head-loses-the-room
  pattern chapter 04 warns against and fails the criterion.
- The operational-distance detection panel instruments all
  five tests with a specific cadence, a specific threshold
  rule, and a specific action protocol. A test instrumented
  only as "the head will keep an eye on it" fails the
  criterion.
- The first-line-partner interview guide and the
  second-line-team survey instrument are both authored at
  the question level (not just the topic level). An
  instrument that lists topics without specific questions
  fails the criterion.
- The CxO adjacency map covers every CxO the function
  materially interacts with and names the specific exposure
  and counter-discipline per CxO. A missing CxO or an
  "exposure unknown" entry fails the criterion.
- The failure-mode audit names, for each of the six
  failure modes, at least one specific detection signal and
  one specific recovery action.
- Any factual claim about a specific peer enterprise's
  stop-ship rate, a specific regulator's expectation on
  second-line independence, or a specific sector's survey
  benchmark carries a `<!-- needs-research: verify
  [specific fact] against [official source] -->` marker
  where the fact is not independently verifiable. The
  discipline is designed; the citations are evidenced.

## Notes and starter guidance

The most common first-cut failure of the discipline is the
head who authors a strong operating statement and a strong
CRO joint operating agreement and stops there. The statement
and the agreement are necessary; they are not sufficient. The
pressure-pattern rehearsals and the operational-distance
panel are the operating-side artefacts; without them, the
statement is aspiration and the agreement is paper. Force
the full set.

The second common failure is the pressure-pattern rehearsal
authored in the abstract. "The head will respond
substantively, not defensively" is not a rehearsal; it is a
summary of the chapter. Force the concrete scenario — a
specific release, a specific finding, a specific CxO relationship,
specific prepared sentences the head will say. The head who
has not written the sentences in rehearsal will not say the
sentences in the live moment; the head will say something
less firm, and the balance will drift.

The third common failure is the operational-distance panel
authored without threshold rules. "The head will keep an eye
on the intake-source ratio" is not instrumentation; it is a
promise to pay attention. Force the specific threshold rule
— falling ratio over two consecutive quarters is a signal,
falling ratio over three is a finding, with specific actions
at each threshold. The threshold rule is what makes the
panel operate as detection rather than vibe.

On the first-line-partner interview and the second-line-team
survey — the temptation is to design them as satisfaction
surveys. A satisfaction survey is not what either instrument
is for. The interview is for the head to hear what the first
line genuinely needs that they are not getting; the survey
is for the head to hear whether the team can genuinely do
the second-line job without carrying pressure the head is
not seeing. Design the instruments against the specific
discipline chapter 04 names, not against generic engagement
survey templates.

On the stop-ship-call rate as a KRI — the temptation is to
target a specific rate ("one stop-ship call per quarter is
healthy"). Resist the target. Chapter 04 is explicit: zero is
a signal, every quarter is a signal, the shape of the
disposition over time is the signal. Author the metric as a
trend-and-threshold instrument, not a target.

The discipline is a living artefact. Version the artefacts,
date them, refresh the operating statement and the joint
operating agreement at the annual CRO review cycle, refresh
the pressure-pattern rehearsals when a live pressure
episode produces learning, refresh the detection panel at
