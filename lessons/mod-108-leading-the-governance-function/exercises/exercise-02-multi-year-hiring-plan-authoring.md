# exercise-02: Multi-Year Hiring Plan Authoring

**Estimated effort:** 3.5 hours

## Objective

Author the enterprise's **three-year AI-governance hiring plan**
against the org shape produced in exercise 01. The plan carries
year-one headcount detail (the base-case shape with sector
overlays where applicable), year-two and year-three trigger-
contingent trajectory, a two-population sourcing strategy with
channels per population, an interview loop per role level with
written rubrics, a sign-off flow per level, and a compensation-
band positioning statement ratified annually. The deliverable
is the artefact the head takes into the annual budget cycle
([[mod-113-budget-vendor-p-and-l-and-investment-case]]), the
artefact the CRO's second-line coordinator reviews for coherence,
the artefact the CAE reads for capacity-to-cooperate on third-
line audit engagements ([05-cae-and-third-line-coordination](../05-cae-and-third-line-coordination.md)),
and the artefact the recruiter operates against.

The plan has to survive three stress tests. First, the CFO
challenges the year-two headcount ask — the trigger-contingent
trajectory has to name the specific signals that fire the
expansion, not a straight-line growth assumption. Second, the
CRO's second-line coordinator asks how the sourcing strategy
handles the second-line-independence risk for product- and
engineering-source hires — the sourcing artefact has to name
the onboarding investment specifically. Third, an incoming
level-15 analyst asks what their progression to level 25 looks
like — the interview loop, the compensation band, and the
progression documentation from exercise 01 have to answer
without redefinition.

The plan is the head's negotiating instrument in the budget
cycle and the discipline that turns reactive hiring into
planned hiring.

## Steps

1. **Inherit the enterprise context.** Confirm the exercise-01
   context one-pager is current and reference it. The plan's
   headcount specifics depend on the sector, jurisdictional
   footprint, AI portfolio scope, and maturity stage on the
   one-pager.

2. **The year-one detailed headcount.** Author the specific
   year-one headcount by level and by position (using the
   position-level distinction from exercise 01). For each
   position:
   - The role title, level, and archetype.
   - The status (filled by named incumbent, filled with
     specified transition timing, vacant with active
     requisition, vacant with recruitment planned in a
     specific quarter, or vacant contingent on trigger).
   - The specific first-year priority for the role (per
     chapter-02 guidance — the level-50 architect's first-
     year priority is authoring the control library and the
     policy taxonomy; the level-35 evaluator's first-year
     priority is the release-assurance methodology; and so on).
   - The specific funding line the position sits on in the
     year-one budget.

3. **The year-two and year-three trigger-contingent trajectory.**
   For each year, author the specific role additions the plan
   contemplates, and for each addition author the specific
   trigger signal that fires it (chapter 02 taxonomy — AI
   portfolio growth, regulatory obligation change, third-line
   audit engagement, incident-load rise). For each trigger:
   - The specific measurement that fires the trigger (a
     specific system count threshold on the AI system
     inventory, a specific regulation moving from horizon-
     scanned to in-force, a specific audit engagement on the
     CAE's calendar, a specific incident-volume threshold on
     the incident register).
   - The specific role that fires on the trigger (role title,
     level, position, and reporting shape).
   - The pre-negotiated budget authorisation shape (a
     contingent line in the year-two budget so the head does
     not have to reopen the budget conversation when the
     trigger fires).
   The trigger-contingent trajectory replaces straight-line
   headcount growth. A plan that adds "two level-15 analysts in
   year two and two more in year three" without trigger
   signals fails the discipline.

4. **The two-population sourcing strategy.** Author the sourcing
   strategy per population per role level. Population A (career
   switchers from audit, privacy, MRM, legal, product /
   engineering) and Population B (early-career practitioners
   from IAPP AIGP, ISO/IEC 42001 LI / LA, BABL AI, ForHumanity,
   NIST AI RMF, university programs) are treated distinctly:
   - For each source discipline in Population A, name the
     natural entry level, the specific onboarding investment
     (what the switcher has to learn), and the specific
     retention risk (what the switcher expects that the
     enterprise has to deliver).
   - For each credential in Population B, name the entry
     level, the onboarding rotation the hire runs before
     productive delivery (referenced to exercise 03), and
     the specific progression-path expectation.
   - For each population, name the sourcing channels the head
     runs (recruiter partners, internal-transfer channel,
     professional-society talent boards, conference-sourcing
     shape, inbound applications). Enumerate at least three
     channels per population so the pipeline is not single-
     source-dependent.

5. **The interview loop per role level.** Author the interview
   loop for each of the four role levels below the head, using
   the chapter-02 templates as starting point:
   - Level-15 analyst loop (~5 stages, 4–6 weeks).
   - Level-25 engineer / level-35 evaluator loop (~6 stages,
     6–8 weeks; add a deep-technical panel and a prior-work
     working session).
   - Level-50 architect loop (~7 stages, 8–10 weeks; add the
     design-authorship exercise, the CxO panel, and the
     formal reference-check protocol).
   - Level-60 head successor loop (at CRO or CEO scope; the
     head-in-post authors the succession one-pager the CRO
     takes into the loop, per chapter 02).
   For each stage, author:
   - The stage name and purpose.
   - The panelist role (not name) and the specific rubric
     the panelist assesses against.
   - The debrief and decision cadence.
   The loop is designed once and run repeatedly; a stage
   without a written rubric is the ad-hoc-loop anti-pattern
   chapter 02 warns against.

6. **The rubric bank.** Author the specific written rubrics
   the interview stages assess against. At minimum:
   - The intake-scenario rubric for the level-15 analyst
     technical assessment.
   - The deep-technical rubric per craft (red-team, fairness,
     adversarial-ML, evaluation methodology) for the level-25
     / level-35 loop.
   - The design-authorship rubric for the level-50
     architect exercise.
   - The CxO-panel rubric for the level-50 architect CxO
     panel.
   Each rubric is a one-pager with the specific criteria the
   panelist scores against and the score scale.

7. **The sign-off flow.** Author the per-level sign-off flow
   from chapter 02, applied to the enterprise's own hiring
   process. Include:
   - Level-15 analyst: head signs off, recruiter processes.
   - Level-25 / level-35: head signs off, CRO's second-line
     coordinator notified.
   - Level-50 architect: head signs off with CRO ratification
     in writing.
   - Level-60 head successor: CRO or CEO signs off with
     audit-committee-chair notification and (in regulated
     sectors) regulator no-objection.
   Where the enterprise's sector adds an additional sign-off
   layer (fit-and-proper for banking regulated positions, HR
   Compensation Committee for outside-band offers, GC for
   specific-jurisdiction hires), name the layer explicitly.
   The sign-off flow is what the recruiter operates against;
   the flow is not a suggestion.

8. **The compensation-band positioning.** Author the specific
   positioning posture (at-market, above at senior /
   at-market at junior, above across) with the four-point
   defence (peer-set benchmark, offer-acceptance data, offer-
   decline data, regretted-loss data). Author the specific
   bands per level (base range, variable / bonus target,
   equity range where applicable, total range, positioning
   reference against the peer-set, locality adjustments).
   Name the recruiter's within-band authority and the
   outside-band sign-off flow.

9. **The plan-refresh discipline.** Author the annual refresh
   discipline. When the plan is revisited (typically the CFO
   budget-cycle preparation window), who convenes the review,
   what triggers a mid-year re-baselining (only material
   events — a regulatory change moving faster than planned,
   an incident-load rise past the trigger threshold, a
   sector-overlay adoption — not a difficult month), and how
   a re-baseline is ratified.

10. **The failure-mode audit.** Author a short section — one
    paragraph per failure mode — naming how the plan detects
    and recovers from each of the chapter-02 anti-patterns:
    - Reactive-hiring posture: requisitions open only when
      the current team is already burning out.
    - Single-population sourcing: the pipeline is thinner than
      it needs to be and the function's cognitive diversity is
      limited.
    - Ad-hoc interview loop: different candidates get
      different loops and the signal quality varies.
    - Premature architect hire: an architect with design
      craft but no operational discipline joins because the
      requisition was open.
    - Comp-band-negotiation-per-offer: the head is pulled
      into every offer because the band was never authored.
    - "No onboarding" pattern: hires close but no onboarding
      rotation is authored to receive them.

## Acceptance criteria

- Every year-one position has status, first-year priority, and
  funding line populated. A position with a blank status or
  priority fails the criterion — the year-one plan is the
  budget-cycle instrument and cannot carry TBDs.
- Every year-two and year-three role addition carries a
  specific trigger signal. A role added "as headcount grows"
  or "as needed" fails the criterion; the trigger has to be a
  specific measurable signal.
- The sourcing strategy names at least three channels per
  population. A single-recruiter-partner strategy fails the
  criterion.
- Every interview loop has a written rubric per stage that
  requires assessment. A loop with "candidate is assessed on
  fit" fails the criterion.
- The sign-off flow enumerates the sign-off layer per level
  and includes any sector- or jurisdiction-specific overlays.
  A sign-off flow that skips level-50 CRO ratification fails
  the criterion (chapter 02 is explicit that CRO ratification
  is required for level-50).
- The compensation-band positioning names one of the three
  postures with the four-point defence (peer-set, acceptance,
  decline, regretted-loss). A posture defended on narrative
  ("AI talent is scarce") without data fails the criterion.
- The failure-mode audit names, for each of the six failure
  modes, at least one specific detection signal and one
  specific recovery action.
- Any factual claim about a specific credential program's
  cohort size, tuition, or employer-recognition level carries
  a `<!-- needs-research: verify [specific fact] against
  [program's published statistics] -->` marker where the fact
  is not independently verifiable. The chapter itself carries
  such markers around credential-program numbers; the
  exercise inherits the discipline.

## Stretch goals

- Author the **CFO-facing budget-cycle pack** — the specific
  two-page pack the head takes to the CFO with the three-year
  plan compressed into: year-one headcount cost against the
  authorised budget line, year-two and year-three cost
  envelopes conditional on the trigger signals, per-population
  sourcing cost breakdown, and the head's specific asks (the
  year-one authorisation, the year-two contingent authorisation,
  the compensation-band ratification, the recruiter-authority
  ratification).
- Author the **recruiter-partner brief** — the specific brief
  the head hands to the external recruiter partners on the
  function's shape, the sourcing strategy per role, the
  interview loop, and the compensation-band framework. The
  brief is what enables recruiter productivity beyond ad-hoc
  requisitions.
- Draft the **onboarding-rotation shape** for each of the
  role archetypes. The rotation is authored fully in exercise
  03; this stretch sketches the shape so the interview loop
  and the onboarding are legibly connected artefacts.
- Draft the **outside-band-offer decision case** — a specific
  case study in which the enterprise extends an outside-band
  offer to a level-50 architect (the candidate is genuinely
  outstanding, the market for level-50 architects in the
  enterprise's geography is thin, the ordinary band cannot
  close). Walk the compensation-committee sign-off flow, the
  CRO briefing, and the specific band-review conversation
  that follows if outside-band offers become recurrent.
- Author the **third-year retrospective template** — the
  short template the head will run at the end of year three
  to assess whether the plan's trigger-contingent trajectory
  fired as planned, which triggers fired ahead of schedule,
  which triggers never fired, and what the year-four plan
  should look like given the trajectory data.

## Notes and starter guidance

The most common first-cut failure is a plan that presents year-
two and year-three as straight-line expansion (X more analysts
per year). Straight-line plans read to the CFO as budget
inflation and to the audit committee as absent forward planning.
Force the trigger-contingent shape — every year-two and year-
three role addition must carry a specific measurable trigger,
and the trajectory has to be defensible under the CFO's "what
if the trigger does not fire" question.

The second common failure is the sourcing strategy that treats
Population A and Population B as interchangeable. A career
switcher from banking model-risk management has very different
onboarding needs than an AIGP-credentialed early-career hire;
treating them the same produces mis-hires from both populations.
Force the per-population, per-source-discipline specificity —
what each source brings, what each source needs to learn, what
each source expects from the enterprise.

The third common failure is the interview loop with no rubrics.
"The panel decides" is not an interview loop; it is an ad-hoc
loop with theatre. Force the rubric per stage. The rubric is
what makes the loop repeatable across candidates and what
allows the head to calibrate the loop over time.

On the compensation-band positioning — the temptation is to
pick "above-market at senior levels" because it feels
generous. Above-market at senior levels is a specific decision
with specific consequences (progression-conversation friction
at the wedge; compensation-committee attention on the
outlier). Pick the posture the enterprise can actually defend
on data over multiple years, not the posture that feels
appealing in the first year.

On the sign-off flow — the temptation is to compress the flow
for speed. A hiring loop that skips the CRO ratification for a
level-50 architect is a loop that produces the architect the
CRO would not have hired, and the head loses months of
recovery when the architect turns out to be a mis-hire the CRO
saw coming. Run the sign-off flow exactly as authored.

The plan is a living artefact. Version it, date it, refresh
it at the annual CFO budget-cycle preparation window, update
the compensation bands at the annual compensation-committee
review, update the sourcing strategy when a credential cohort
moves materially (a new cohort emerges, an existing cohort
narrows), and update the trigger-contingent trajectory when
the KRI portfolio shows the trigger has fired. A plan that
has not been touched since it was authored is a plan the
enterprise's growth has already outrun.
