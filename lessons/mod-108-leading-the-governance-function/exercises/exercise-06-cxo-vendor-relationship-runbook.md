# exercise-06: CxO Vendor Relationship Runbook

**Estimated effort:** 3 hours

## Objective

Author the enterprise's **CxO-scope vendor-relationship
runbook** — four distinct vendor account plans (the GRC-for-AI
platform vendor, the ISO/IEC 42001 certification body, the
independent AI assurance and audit body, the standards-adjacent
training and credentialing partner portfolio), with the account-
owner named, the quarterly-review template and the annual
strategic-review shape per vendor, the vendor-satisfaction pulse
instrument, the four-stage escalation path per vendor, the
continuity-planning posture against single-vendor dependency,
and a selection-and-ratification rehearsal for the single
largest-cost relationship (typically the GRC-for-AI platform).
The deliverable is the account-side artefact the head carries
underneath the procurement-run contract (procurement runs the
RFP, the contract, the invoicing; the head runs the account),
the artefact the CFO reads as evidence the head is managing
vendor spend substantively, the artefact the CIO or CTO reads
as evidence the GRC-for-AI stack is in competent hands at the
business-owner level, and the artefact the audit committee
reads as evidence the enterprise's external assurance posture
(CB, independent-assurance body) is being run with the
substantive-engagement discipline chapter 06 names.

The runbook has to survive three stress tests. First, the
GRC-for-AI platform experiences a material-reliability event
during the audit-committee-cycle preparation window — the
escalation path and the continuity-planning posture have to
produce a credible response without the head either
over-reacting (threatening termination without genuine
willingness to execute) or under-reacting (treating the event
as a one-off that will resolve). Second, the ISO/IEC 42001
certification body informs the head that the surveillance-audit
team for the coming cycle will include an auditor whose AI
competence the head reads as thin — the CB-relationship
discipline has to carry the head through the substantive
engagement without crossing into the audit-friendly-selection
anti-pattern chapter 06 warns against. Third, an independent
AI assurance body produces an opinion that is materially less
favourable than the head expected — the substantive-engagement
discipline and the public-disclosure coordination have to
hold against the temptation to selectively disclose.

The runbook is not generic vendor management. It is the
specific account discipline chapter 06 names, authored to the
enterprise's own vendor portfolio.

## Steps

1. **Inherit the enterprise context.** Confirm the exercise-01
   context one-pager is current and reference it. The vendor
   portfolio's shape (which GRC-for-AI platform tier is
   appropriate; whether the enterprise carries ISO/IEC 42001
   certification at all; which independent-assurance-body
   engagement is substantively appropriate; which
   credentialing partners the hiring pipeline needs most)
   depends on the sector, operating-model shape,
   jurisdictional footprint, portfolio shape, and maturity
   stage on the one-pager.

2. **The four-category vendor portfolio inventory.** Author
   the half-page inventory of the enterprise's current
   vendor and assurance-partner relationships across the four
   chapter-06 categories:
   - **GRC-for-AI platform.** The current platform (named
     vendor; current contract term; current annual spend
     bucket at CFO-visible granularity; current executive-
     sponsor name on the vendor side; current account-owner
     name on the enterprise side).
   - **ISO/IEC 42001 certification body.** The current CB
     (named body; accreditation body — UKAS, ANAB, DAkkS, or
     equivalent; current certification status —
     uncertified / in-process / certified with cycle year;
     current account-principal name).
   - **Independent AI assurance and audit body.** The
     current engagement(s) (named body; scope of the
     current engagement; opinion-issuance history if any;
     publication status).
   - **Standards-adjacent training and credentialing
     partners.** The portfolio of partners the hiring plan
     and development portfolio reference (IAPP, BSI, PECB,
     LRQA, BABL AI, ForHumanity, Responsible AI Institute,
     university programs, NIST-recognised training
     providers), with the specific team members' active
     credential progression against each.
   The inventory is the artefact the CFO reads at the
   budget-cycle check-in and the audit committee reads as
   the baseline for any assurance-posture question. Keep it
   current.

3. **The GRC-for-AI platform account plan.** Author the
   specific account plan for the GRC-for-AI platform. The
   plan carries:
   - **The platform summary.** The vendor, the current
     contract term, the current executive sponsor and
     account team on the vendor side, the enterprise's
     account owner (the head), the enterprise's technical
     owner (the level-50 architect or a specific delegate),
     the specific scope the platform supports (AI system
     inventory, control library, evidence store, risk
     register, metric-production pipeline, workflow for
     the AIMS operating calendar — reference chapter 06's
     specific list).
   - **The quarterly-account-review template.** The
     standing agenda per chapter 06: product roadmap
     review; enterprise-side workstreams; escalation
     review; satisfaction pulse; peer-community
     discussion. For each agenda item, the specific
     prepared questions the head brings; the specific
     evidence or metrics the head cites; the specific
     action-capture discipline.
   - **The annual-strategic-review shape.** The half-day
     agenda per chapter 06: multi-year roadmap alignment;
     contract structure discussion; executive
     introductions across both organisations; contract
     renewal or replacement scoping where timing calls
     for it. Specify the frequency (annual at minimum;
     with mid-cycle session if a material contract event
     is approaching), the venue, the attendee set from
     both organisations.
   - **The ad hoc escalation channel.** The named vendor
     CxO contact (executive sponsor title and name), the
     specific channel (direct email / direct call), the
     expected response-time pattern, the escalation
     frequency discipline (chapter 06: weekly escalations
     are the wrong pattern; the right pattern is CxO
     engagement for material issues only).
   - **The joint-governance-rhythm.** Where the vendor
     offers a customer advisory board, a product-direction
     working group, or a user-community event, the head's
     participation posture (which the head attends; which
     the head sends a delegate to; which the head declines
     and why).
   The plan is signed by the head and reviewed by the
   level-50 architect before publication. Procurement sees
   a copy; the CIO or CTO sees a copy; the CFO sees the
   budget-relevant summary.

4. **The GRC-for-AI platform vendor-satisfaction pulse.**
   Author the specific pulse instrument chapter 06 names:
   - **The dimensions.** The five dimensions chapter 06
     specifies: reliability (uptime; pattern of downtime at
     critical operational moments); fitness for purpose
     (workflow support versus workaround labour);
     support responsiveness (SLA attainment; complex-
     ticket handling); roadmap alignment (committed items
     delivered on time; new items added to the roadmap);
     total cost of operation trajectory (licence +
     implementation + workaround labour + support
     engagements).
   - **The specific metric per dimension.** Not vibes;
     metrics. Uptime percentage over the quarter; count of
     downtime episodes at critical operational moments;
     count of material workarounds the function is
     running; SLA attainment percentage; count of complex
     tickets bounced back; count of committed roadmap
     items delivered; count of new items added to the
     roadmap; cost comparison against the prior-quarter
     and prior-year baseline.
   - **The data source per metric.** The specific place
     each metric comes from (the vendor's uptime report;
     the function's own incident log; the function's own
     workaround register; the ticket-system SLA report;
     the vendor's roadmap tracker; the finance system).
   - **The quarterly convening protocol.** The specific
     participants (chapter 06: the level-50 architect, the
     level-15 analysts who use the platform most, the
     level-35 evaluators), the specific session shape, the
     specific consolidated-read authoring discipline.
   - **The vendor-communication protocol.** The specific
     protocol for taking the pulse read into the quarterly
     account review — candid substance, not diplomatic
     gloss (chapter 06 is explicit on this).

5. **The GRC-for-AI four-stage escalation path.** Author
   the specific escalation path per chapter 06:
   - **Stage 1: to the vendor's account executive at the
     next quarterly review.** The specific trigger (pulse
     findings exceeding a threshold the head defines
     explicitly: repeated downtime at critical moments;
     roadmap items slipping past their commitments;
     support-responsiveness degrading; fit-for-purpose
     gaps the vendor is not addressing). The specific
     request language ("this is an escalation, not a
     routine review point"; the specific concrete request:
     fix the downtime pattern by Q-end; deliver the
     slipping roadmap item by Q-end; provide an
     implementation-support engagement to close the fit
     gap by Q-end). The agreed window for response.
   - **Stage 2: to the vendor's CxO sponsor by direct
     contact.** The specific trigger (stage-1 escalation
     does not produce substantive response within the
     agreed window). The specific wording of the direct
     contact; the specific ask (an executive-visibility
     remediation plan, a dedicated account-team
     assignment, a specific product-organisation
     commitment). The agreed window for response.
   - **Stage 3: internally, to the CFO and CIO, with the
     material-underperformance case.** The specific
     trigger (stage-2 escalation does not produce
     substantive change). The specific internal brief —
     the material-underperformance case, the vendor's
     response to date, the head's proposed path (invoke
     contract remedies — SLA credits, contract
     restructuring; begin the replacement-vendor
     evaluation; or a hybrid). The decision-authority
     mapping (the CFO's and CIO's authority is engaged;
     the head's role is to brief and recommend, not to
     execute contract action unilaterally).
   - **Stage 4: contract termination and replacement.**
     The specific trigger (stage-3 process concludes
     termination is warranted). The specific execution
     flow (head's recommendation; CFO's and CIO's
     execution; procurement's contract action; the
     replacement vendor's engagement; the data-portability
     execution against the continuity plan from step 6).
     Chapter 06 is explicit: the head does not threaten
     termination without genuine willingness to execute;
     the vendor reads the difference immediately. Author
     the specific internal preparation that must be
     complete before the stage-4 trigger is pulled.

6. **The GRC-for-AI continuity-planning posture.** Author
   the specific continuity plan the head maintains against
   the GRC-for-AI platform relationship:
   - **Data-portability discipline.** The specific export
     format(s) the contract guarantees (OSCAL, SPDX 3.0 AI
     profile, CycloneDX ML-BOM, or the platform's own
     well-documented export). The specific windows at
     which the head verifies portability (annually at
     contract review; at each material contract update).
     The specific test the head runs (a sample export, a
     sample re-import to a neutral staging environment,
     the specific completeness check against the AI
     system inventory, control library, and evidence
     store).
   - **Alternate-vendor awareness.** The specific competitor
     the head has evaluated at head-to-head scope in the
     last eighteen months (named); the specific emerging
     entrant the head has taken a briefing from (named).
     Where the chapter-06 named vendors are placeholders
     for the current landscape, carry `<!-- needs-research:
     verify current GRC-for-AI platform landscape against
     [specific analyst source or primary vendor site] -->`
     markers; the inventory itself is not guessed.
   - **Documented replacement plan.** The specific
     low-fidelity replacement plan — what a migration to
     an alternate vendor would look like (the data-
     portability execution; the parallel-run window; the
     cutover window; the stand-down of the incumbent),
     at what cost envelope, over what timeline. The plan
     is not detailed until replacement is genuinely
     contemplated; the plan exists so the head is not
     authoring from scratch in a crisis.

7. **The ISO/IEC 42001 CB account plan.** Author the
   specific account plan for the ISO/IEC 42001 certification
   body:
   - **The CB summary.** The named CB; the IAF-member
     accreditation body (UKAS / ANAB / DAkkS / equivalent);
     the current certification status; the current account
     principal and audit team lead on the CB side; the
     enterprise's account owner (the head); the enterprise's
     technical owner (the level-50 architect).
   - **The surveillance-audit cycle plan.** The specific
     cycle (chapter 06: certification and re-certification
     every three years typically; surveillance audit
     annually in between). The current cycle year; the
     next audit date; the engagement-preparation window
     the head blocks on the AIMS operating calendar
     ([[mod-102-running-the-program]] chapter 02); the
     internal readiness pass per chapter 05.
   - **The account-principal cadence.** The specific
     between-audits cadence (a couple of substantive
     conversations a year per chapter 06 — not quarterly
     at CB scope; CB engagement is not GRC-platform
     intensity). The specific topics (the enterprise's
     AIMS posture, the CB's read on the sector's AIMS
     practice, upcoming standard updates).
   - **The committee-work engagement.** Where the CB
     participates in ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 42 or related
     committee work, the specific head-side engagement
     (through the account principal or through the CB's
     technical committee members; voluntary and
     reciprocal; chapter 06 is explicit this channel is
     not used to shape audit outcomes).
   - **The CB-side escalation discipline.** The specific
     protocol for material CB-related escalations (a
     substantive finding disagreement; a scheduling
     conflict materially disrupting a cycle; an audit-
     team competence concern) — through the CB's own
     escalation channel (its technical committee or
     certification-decision authority), not through
     informal lobbying.
   - **The CB rotation posture.** The specific position
     the head carries on CB rotation (rotation on a
     defined cadence; continuation of the incumbent CB;
     coordinated decision with the CRO and audit
     committee). The specific rotation-decision trade-offs
     the head presents honestly rather than
     "rotation-is-the-right-thing-to-do" without the
     trade-offs on the table.

8. **The independent AI assurance body account plan.**
   Author the specific account plan for the enterprise's
   current (or planned) independent-assurance-body
   engagement:
   - **The engagement summary.** The named body
     (ForHumanity for the IAAIS criteria; BABL AI for a
     scoped algorithmic audit; or the specific body fit
     for the enterprise's assurance ask); the specific
     scope of the current engagement; the opinion-
     issuance history if any; the publication status.
   - **The engagement rationale.** The specific reason
     chapter 06 identifies — complementary assurance to
     ISO/IEC 42001 (criteria broader than the management-
     system focus); sector-specific or scoped assurance
     (a specific high-impact system); voluntary-code
     participation assurance ([[mod-106-external-positioning-and-voluntary-codes]]);
     regulator- or customer-facing assurance signal from
     a named body.
   - **The substantive-engagement discipline.** The
     specific head-side discipline per chapter 06 —
     substantive engagement on scope (not negotiating for
     a soft opinion); timely evidence production and
     personnel access (same discipline as the CB and
     CAE); substantive response to findings; public
     disclosure discipline coordinated with the
     enterprise's communications and legal functions.
   - **The public-disclosure coordination.** The specific
     coordination with communications and legal — who
     reviews the disclosure draft; the specific review
     window; the specific coordination of the disclosure
     timing with the vendor and with the audit committee
     cycle; the specific selective-disclosure refusal
     posture chapter 06 names.
   - **The relationship-posture discipline.** The specific
     posture the head holds — the cosy head-provider
     relationship is the anti-pattern chapter 06 names;
     the specific signals the head reads for cosiness
     drift (consistently favourable opinions over
     multiple engagements; social intimacy with the
     assurance-body principals; soft-engagement patterns
     on scope).

9. **The standards-adjacent training and credentialing
   partner account plan.** Author the specific portfolio-
   level plan — chapter 06 is explicit this discipline is
   lighter than the first three, but it is not absent:
   - **The credential-progression investment.** The
     specific per-level credential investments the head
     funds (IAPP AIGP or CIPP for level-15 through
     level-50 depending on role; ISO/IEC 42001 Lead
     Implementer / Lead Auditor for architects and
     evaluators; ForHumanity Fellows or BABL AI
     credentials as fit for role). The specific budget
     line this fits into (the coaching-and-development
     budget from exercise-03's stretch, coordinated with
     the finance business partner per
     [[mod-107-program-measurement-and-maturity]]
     chapter 05 and the vendor-spend line per
     [[mod-113-budget-vendor-p-and-l-and-investment-case]]).
   - **The curriculum-shift awareness protocol.** The
     specific ambient-awareness discipline — subscription
     to IAPP body-of-knowledge update notifications;
     tracking of ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 42 publication cadence
     against the ISO/IEC 42001 Lead Implementer course
     updates; attendance at credential-provider community
     events on the standing-attendance list.
   - **The employer-of-choice positioning posture.** The
     specific community-presence discipline — speaking
     commitments at cohort events; sponsorship of team
     members' body-of-knowledge-development participation;
     talent-partner engagement with the credentialing
     bodies. Reference
     [[mod-112-certifications-career-path-community-leadership]]
     for the deeper community-leadership shape.
   - **The partner-relationship-not-transactional
     discipline.** The specific protocol for refusing the
     talent-partner-as-transactional anti-pattern — the
     head's own attendance at partner-cohort events; the
     specific head-level conversations with the partner
     bodies' leadership on talent-pipeline shape; the
     specific feedback the head gives the partner bodies
     on curriculum fit against the enterprise's hiring
     needs.

10. **The selection-and-ratification rehearsal for the
    GRC-for-AI platform.** Author a scenario-based rehearsal
    of a GRC-for-AI platform selection cycle (either a
    fresh selection in year zero, or a replacement cycle
    on a mature program):
    - **Requirements authoring.** The specific
      consolidated requirements document — the level-50
      architect's inputs (control library shape; evidence
      store shape; integration surface with the
      enterprise's ISMS; workflow for the AIMS operating
      calendar); the level-35 evaluator's inputs
      (evaluation-harness integration; release-assurance
      workflow); the level-15 analysts' inputs (intake-
      queue ergonomics; evidence-collection ergonomics);
      the head's inputs (audit-committee-facing reporting;
      KRI production; CxO-adjacent workflow). The
      consolidated document the head takes forward.
    - **Vendor-CxO engagement during evaluation.** The
      specific session the head holds with each serious
      candidate's executive sponsor — the specific
      questions on roadmap direction, product depth
      investments, market positioning, financial
      stability. Not a demo; an account-relationship
      conversation.
    - **Head-to-head reference-checking.** The specific
      protocol for the head's own network — the two or
      three peer heads-of-AI-governance the head calls
      for candid substance; the specific question set
      (what has actually worked; what has not; what
      would they do differently; would they renew). The
      reference is from the head's own network, not the
      vendor's reference list.
    - **CIO / CTO ratification.** The specific
      ratification session the head arranges with the
      CIO or CTO before contract signing — the
      technology-stack fit question, the enterprise-scope
      integration question, the roadmap-dependency
      question, the security-review coordination.
    - **Contract execution and post-selection
      onboarding.** The specific head-side onboarding
      the head runs with the vendor — the kickoff
      session with the account team, the first
      quarterly-review calendaring, the first-six-
      months implementation-support engagement, the
      first-year contract-performance review the head
      has committed to with the vendor's executive
      sponsor.

11. **The vendor-portfolio-level governance cadence.**
    Author the specific cadence the head runs across the

    - **The annual vendor-portfolio review with the CFO

    - **The semi-annual vendor-portfolio review with the

    - **The audit-committee-cycle vendor-posture
      summary.** The specific short section the head

12. **The failure-mode audit.** Author a short section —
    one paragraph per failure mode — naming how the

## Acceptance criteria

- The vendor-portfolio inventory covers all four categories
  with the named vendor, contract term (where applicable),
  account owner, and current status per entry. A category
  with no entry (where the enterprise has no engagement) is
  flagged explicitly rather than omitted silently; the
  absence itself is a posture the head carries.
- The GRC-for-AI platform account plan carries the
  quarterly-review template at the per-agenda-item level
  (prepared questions, evidence, action-capture discipline
  per item). A plan that lists agenda items without the
  per-item preparation fails the criterion — chapter 06 is
  explicit the review is substantive, not routine.
- The satisfaction-pulse instrument carries specific metrics
  per dimension, with specific data sources per metric. A
  pulse that lists dimensions without metrics ("the head
  will assess reliability") fails the criterion — chapter
  06 names the vibes-based satisfaction read as a specific
  anti-pattern.
- The four-stage escalation path carries specific triggers,
  specific request language, and specific response windows
  per stage. A path that lists stages without the per-stage
  specifics fails the criterion — the head without specific
  wording cannot execute the escalation under pressure.
- The continuity plan names the specific data-portability
  format(s), the specific alternate-vendor awareness
  (named alternatives, specific evaluation window), and the
  specific low-fidelity replacement plan. A continuity plan
  that gestures at portability without naming formats fails
  the criterion.
- The CB account plan names the surveillance-audit cycle
  plan, the account-principal cadence, the committee-work
  engagement posture, the escalation discipline, and the
  rotation posture. A CB plan that treats the CB as a
  procurement-run engagement rather than an account-scope
  relationship fails the criterion.
- The independent-assurance account plan names the
  engagement rationale (which of chapter 06's four reasons),
  the substantive-engagement discipline, the public-
  disclosure coordination, and the relationship-posture
  discipline. An account plan that assumes a favourable
  opinion will issue fails the criterion — the discipline
  is substantive engagement with an independent party.
- The training-and-credentialing portfolio plan names the
  specific credential-progression investment, the specific
  curriculum-shift awareness protocol, the specific

- The portfolio-governance cadence covers the annual CFO/CIO

  committee-cycle summary. A cadence missing any of the

  governance of the portfolio at the three distinct
  accountability layers.
- The failure-mode audit names, for each of the seven
  failure modes, at least one specific detection signal and
  one specific recovery action.
- Any factual claim about a specific vendor's current
  product offering, a specific CB's accreditation status, a
  specific independent-assurance body's current
  methodology, or a specific credential's current body-of-
  knowledge version carries a `<!-- needs-research: verify

  landscape evolves rapidly; the `needs-research` markers

## Notes and starter guidance

The most common first-cut failure of the runbook is the
head who treats all four vendor categories as the same
discipline. The GRC-for-AI platform is a product vendor the
head manages at account scope with heavy ongoing cadence; the
CB is an assurance provider the head manages at account scope
with lighter but substantive cadence; the independent-
assurance body is a provider the head manages with
specifically-public-disclosure-coordinated cadence; the
credentialing partners are community partners the head
manages at a lighter talent-and-community scope. Force the
category-specific discipline; a one-size runbook mis-
calibrates all four.

The second common failure is the GRC-for-AI satisfaction
pulse authored as a satisfaction survey. A survey is not a
pulse; the pulse is the structured read on specific
dimensions chapter 06 names, with specific metrics per
dimension and specific data sources per metric. Force the
rigour — the five dimensions, specific metrics, specific
data sources, the specific convening protocol with the
level-50 architect and level-15 analysts and level-35
evaluators who actually use the platform.

The third common failure is the escalation path authored
without specific triggers or specific wording. "The head
will escalate if the vendor is underperforming" is not an
escalation path; it is a statement of intent. Force the
specific trigger per stage, the specific wording of the
request at each stage, the specific window for response.
The head who has not written the wording in rehearsal will
not say the wording in the moment; the escalation will
drift from substantive to apologetic and the vendor will
not treat it as the escalation it was meant to be.

On the CB rotation posture — the temptation is to default
to "we don't rotate; the incumbent is working well". A
default without the honest trade-off presentation is
chapter 06's "rotation is the right thing to do" pattern
in reverse — a cost without the trade-off on the table.
Force the honest reading in the artefact: here is what
continuation gives us; here is what it costs us; here is
what rotation would give us; here is what it costs; here
is where we are landing, and why.

On the independent-assurance-body relationship — the
temptation is to assume a favourable opinion will issue
and to run the relationship under that assumption. The
assumption is itself the anti-pattern. Design the
relationship for a substantive opinion that might be
materially unfavourable, and the head will be ready for
whichever outcome lands. Design the relationship for a
favourable opinion, and the head will be unprepared the
first time the opinion is anything else.

The runbook is a living artefact. Version the account
plans, date them, refresh the GRC-for-AI account plan at
the annual strategic review (and whenever the vendor's
executive sponsor changes), refresh the CB account plan at
the surveillance-audit cycle, refresh the independent-
assurance account plan at each engagement, refresh the
training-and-credentialing portfolio plan annually against
the development portfolio from exercise 03. A runbook that
has not been touched in a year is a runbook the vendor
portfolio has already moved past.
