# exercise-05: CAE and Third-Line Coordination Drill

**Estimated effort:** 3 hours

## Objective

Author the enterprise's **CAE coordination file** — the
quarterly working-meeting cadence and standing agenda, the
ad hoc bidirectional notification list, the audit-preparation
runbook, the finding-response protocol, the material-
disagreement handling discipline, the parallel-report shape at
the audit committee, and a full third-line AI-audit engagement
rehearsal — against the IIA Three Lines Model, the IPPF /
Global Internal Audit Standards, and the IIA's Auditing
Artificial Intelligence practice guidance. The deliverable is
the artefact the head carries into every CAE engagement, the
artefact the head's level-50 architect operates against on the
internal readiness pass, the artefact the audit committee
secretariat reads as evidence that the head is a substantive-
and-transparent audit subject rather than a co-designer, and
the artefact an incoming head inherits on day one of the
relationship with the CAE.

The file has to survive three stress tests. First, the CAE
calls on short notice to notify the head of a scope expansion
to the audit engagement already on the calendar — the audit-
preparation runbook has to carry the head through the response
without the head either renegotiating the scope (the "collaborative
audit planning" anti-pattern from chapter 05) or treating the
notification as adversarial. Second, a draft finding lands that
the head genuinely disputes on the facts, and a different finding
in the same audit lands that the head accepts — the
finding-response protocol has to produce distinct, substantive
responses to each, both legible and both resistable by the CAE
on the merits. Third, the audit committee asks the head and the
CAE together why their reports diverge on the same system — the
parallel-report shape has to hold up without the head and the
CAE having pre-aligned a narrative behind the committee's back.

The file is not a courtesy artefact for the CAE. It is the
head's own operating discipline against the single most
consequential external-to-the-function relationship the head
runs.

## Steps

1. **Inherit the enterprise context.** Confirm the exercise-01
   context one-pager is current and reference it. The CAE
   relationship's specifics (whether the enterprise's internal-
   audit function has an AI specialism, whether the CAE's
   charter explicitly references AI, whether the audit
   committee has an AI-specific sub-committee or works through
   the full committee) depend on the sector, operating-model
   shape, and maturity stage on the one-pager.

2. **The owed-vs-not-owed boundary document.** Author the
   half-page document that fixes, for the head's team and for
   the CRO's office, the exact boundary chapter 05 draws
   between what the head owes the CAE and what the head does
   not owe. Enumerate specifically:
   - Owed: a current org chart (exercise 01), a current
     operating charter, the AIMS scope statement, the KRI/KPI
     portfolio, the current control library and evidence
     index, timely notification of material events, personnel
     access on the audit team's timeline, substantive
     engagement on findings.
   - Not owed: advance approval or veto over draft findings,
     suggestions on interviewee selection, preferred timing
     for engagements, a joint audit-committee narrative with
     the CAE.
   The document is the operating reminder the head uses to
   catch the function (and the head themselves) drifting
   across the boundary. Pin it where the head reads it before
   every CAE interaction.

3. **The CAE working-stance one-pager.** Author the one-page
   working-stance document the head publishes to the function
   (and references in the extended-function all-hands when
   the CAE engagement is live). The document names the three
   properties from chapter 05:
   - Transparent by default — specifically what the head does
     not curate for the CAE (concerns about first-line
     practice, substantive challenges the function has
     raised, outcomes of the function's own self-assessment
     work).
   - Cooperative but not chummy — specifically what the head
     holds about professional distance with the CAE (regular
     working relationship; mutual respect; the specific
     social and relational lines the head does not cross).
   - Substantively engaged, not politically engaged —
     specifically which conversations the head holds with the
     CAE (substance of findings; substance of the function's
     operating discipline) and which conversations the head
     refuses to hold with the CAE (which findings will be
     "received well"; which CAE-team members are "easy").

4. **The quarterly working-meeting cadence.** Author the
   specific standing cadence with the CAE:
   - Frequency, duration, scheduling-horizon (ninety minutes
     to two hours; six-plus months ahead; the "scheduled
     ahead" discipline is chapter 05's).
   - The standing agenda from chapter 05: the CAE's read on
     the third-line AI audit programme cycle; the head's
     read on the function's state (major workstreams, KRI/KPI
     trends, substantive changes to shape or discipline);
     cross-cutting themes; discussion of material findings
     from the CAE's recent engagements and the head's
     remediation progress; discussion of material audit-
     committee-visible second-line issues the head has raised.
   - The minuting discipline (chapter 05: minuted by the
     CAE's team typically; shared with the CRO).
   - The participants-under-standing-exception discipline —
     when the head's level-50 architect joins (standing
     reviews of methodology-adjacent topics; audit cycles
     with evaluation-engineering focus) and when the CRO
     joins (annual joint reflection; cross-cutting enterprise
     risk themes).
   - The non-negotiability discipline — the meeting is not
     rescheduled casually; a cancellation is itself a signal
     the audit committee reads over time.

5. **The ad hoc bidirectional notification list.** Author
   the specific list of events on which the head notifies
   the CAE proactively and the specific list of events on
   which the head expects the CAE to notify the head. For
   the head-to-CAE direction:
   - Material incidents (incidents of Article-73-adjacent
     severity or equivalent sector trigger —
     [[mod-110-incident-leadership]]).
   - Material regulatory-change events (an in-force
     obligation change that materially reshapes the AIMS —
     [[mod-105-regulatory-change-management]]).
   - KRI breaches (a KRI breach on the metric ownership map
     with audit-and-independent-validation ownership —
     [[mod-107-program-measurement-and-maturity]] chapter
     01).
   - Stop-ship calls that went to appeal (and the appeal
     disposition, if resolved).
   - Material third-party-vendor findings (a CB-engagement
     finding, an independent-assurance-body opinion that
     materially diverges from the head's own read —
     chapter 06 of this module).
   - Material changes to the function's shape (chapter 01).
   - Material changes to the function's leadership (a
     level-50 architect departure, a succession-event
     escalation).
   For the CAE-to-head direction:
   - Findings that will land at the next audit-committee
     cycle (per chapter 05's standing cadence).
   - Scope decisions on the audit plan that affect the
     head's function.
   - External-audit-body engagements the CAE is coordinating
     that the head will need to support.
   - Material changes in the CAE's own methodology that
     affect the head's internal readiness pass.
   For each item, name the specific channel (named person,
   named mechanism — not "we'll figure out the right way to
   let each other know"; the discipline is written down).

6. **The audit-preparation runbook.** Author the step-by-
   step runbook the head and level-50 architect run on
   notification of a specific CAE engagement:
   - **On engagement notification (day 1–7).** The specific
     intake the head runs: clarifying questions on scope
     and evidence set (not scope renegotiation, per chapter
     05's explicit discipline); confirmation of the
     engagement window; identification of the lead
     evidence-production analyst (a specific level-15
     analyst role; chapter 05 names the discipline); the
     head's calendar block for CAE-team-scheduled interviews;
     the function's extended-function all-hands brief on
     the engagement.
   - **The internal readiness pass (weeks 2–4).** The
     specific protocol: the level-50 architect and the
     level-35 evaluators run the same evidence review the
     audit is likely to run, against the audit's stated
     scope, to a first-cut read. Findings surfaced in the
     readiness pass are not concealed; they are flagged in
     the head's remediation posture from the start. The
     readiness pass is minuted; the head reviews the minute
     against the CAE's eventual findings as the retrospective
     signal on whether the pass was substantive enough.
   - **Evidence production (continuous).** The specific
     evidence-production discipline: who hands over which
     artefacts, through which channel, within which window
     (chapter 05: "late production, incomplete production,
     'we can't find that document' — these are the signals
     that turn a routine audit into an escalated audit").
   - **Personnel access (continuous).** The specific access
     discipline: the head's own calendar availability, the
     calendar availability of each level-50 architect,
     level-35 evaluator, and level-25 risk engineer the
     audit may wish to interview, the level-15 analysts
     who may be asked for process-walkthrough interviews.
     Delays are flagged by the head to the CAE proactively,
     not reactively.
   - **Team coaching on "how to be audited" (pre- and
     during).** The specific coaching protocol for new
     hires — particularly product- and engineering-source
     hires who may have no prior audit-engagement
     experience. The coaching covers interview shape,
     substantive-not-volunteering answer discipline, "I
     don't know; let me follow up" protocol, and the
     normalisation of audit engagements as part of the
     function's operating rhythm (reference chapter 03's
     onboarding rotation; the head who adds "how to be
     audited" to the 90-day rotation is the head who does
     not scramble when the engagement lands).
   - **The engagement debrief.** The specific debrief the
     head and level-50 architect run after the fieldwork
     closes and before draft findings — captures the
     substantive observations from the fieldwork, the
     areas of agreement and areas of likely dispute, the
     evidence base for each.
   The runbook is written as a sequence the head's team
   actually executes, not as a checklist a procurement
   officer would recognise.

7. **The finding-response protocol.** Author the specific
   finding-response protocol the head runs when draft
   findings land:
   - **Factual-review window.** The specific window the CAE
     typically gives (named in the CAE's engagement letter
     or methodology; cite verbatim where available).
     Within that window, the head reviews each draft
     finding for factual accuracy only (chapter 05 is
     explicit: not for tone, severity, or finding
     withdrawal). The head's level-50 architect co-reads;
     the head signs off before response.
   - **The three-category response discipline.** For each
     draft finding, the head categorises the response:
     accepted factually and in severity; accepted
     factually but disputed on severity classification;
     disputed on the facts. For each category, author the
     specific response wording template chapter 05
     advises — e.g. "the finding is accepted factually; the
     head considers the severity classification
     overstated; the facts and the severity disagreement
     are addressed separately in the response".
   - **The remediation-plan authoring discipline.** For
     each finding that stands after factual review, the
     remediation plan is specific, concrete, dated, and
     owner-named. The authoring protocol references
     [[mod-102-running-the-program]] chapter 05 on the
     corrective-action discipline; the shape composes.
   - **The remediation-publication cadence.** The head
     publishes progress on outstanding audit findings at
     each audit-committee cycle
     ([[mod-103-board-and-audit-committee-reporting]]
     chapter 02); the CAE publishes independently; the
     reports name the same findings; where they diverge,
     the audit committee asks the two to reconcile. Author
     the specific template for the head's publication.
   - **The finding-to-change loop.** For each closed
     finding, the head authors the specific change that
     landed in the function's operating discipline (a
     process change; a control-library addition; a hiring-
     plan or succession-picture change). The loop is what
     turns findings from cost to value.

8. **The material-disagreement discipline.** Author the
   specific protocol for the (rare) case where the head
   genuinely disagrees with the CAE on a substantive finding
   or methodology question:
   - **The private-first discipline.** The head raises the
     disagreement in the head-CAE working meeting (or in a
     specific escalation session between the two) first.
     The head does not lobby the audit-committee chair or
     any other party against the CAE's finding; chapter 05
     is explicit this path is the credibility failure that
     ends the working relationship.
   - **The substantive-evidence-only discipline.** The head
     marshals the specific substantive evidence the CAE did
     not have when the finding was drafted (not the head's
     professional judgment against the CAE's judgment;
     chapter 05 is explicit the audit committee weights
     the CAE's independence heavily and the head loses
     that argument). Author the specific evidence-staging
     protocol.
   - **The acceptance-of-final-call discipline.** If the
     substantive engagement does not resolve the
     disagreement, the head accepts the CAE's finding of
     record and notes the disagreement on the remediation
     response in the specific wording chapter 05 suggests
     ("the head's view remains that the finding overstates
     the severity; the head accepts the finding of record
     and will remediate as described").
   - **The nuclear-option discipline.** If the head
     believes the CAE has genuinely erred at a material
     level, the head raises it with the audit-committee
     chair privately and formally, once, and respects the
     chair's decision. Author the specific preparation the
     head does before this escalation — the specific
     substantive concern, the specific evidence, the
     specific chair-ask wording. This escalation is a
     career-defining use of the direct-access line; the
     head authoring the preparation in rehearsal is the
     head preserving the right to use the line later.
   The disagreement discipline is deliberately uncomfortable
   to author. The discomfort is the point; the head who
   authors it well is the head who runs it well.

9. **The parallel-report shape at the audit committee.**
   Author the specific operating shape the head holds at
   the audit committee against the CAE's parallel report:
   - The head presents the second-line function's material
     independently; the CAE presents the third-line's read
     on the same material independently; the two
     presentations are not coordinated behind the committee's
     back.
   - When the CAE surfaces a finding the head has already
     addressed in remediation, the head's specific
     response ("the finding is noted; the remediation
     described in the pack is on track; the head will
     report progress at the next cycle"). No spin, no
     thunder-stealing, no retroactive softening.
   - When the CAE surfaces a finding that comes to the
     head as news in the room, the head's specific
     response (takes the finding seriously; does not push
     back in the room; commits to a substantive response
     by the next cycle). The head's prepared language is
     in the artefact.
   - The specific editorial-silence discipline — the head
     does not grade the CAE's craft or team in the
     committee record.
   - The specific divergence-handling discipline — where
     the head's report and the CAE's diverge, the head's
     prepared response to the committee's "how do we
     reconcile these" question. The response is
     substantive, not reconciling; it names where the two
     reports draw different inferences from different
     weightings of the same evidence, and invites the
     committee to weigh both.

10. **The full third-line AI audit engagement rehearsal.**
    Author a scenario-based rehearsal of a specific CAE
    engagement end-to-end. Pick the engagement scope
    (one of: a specific control-cluster audit on the AIA
    process; a process audit on the third-party-vendor
    assurance process; an effectiveness audit on the
    function itself; a targeted audit against a specific
    regulatory obligation — EU AI Act Article 9 risk
    management, NIST AI RMF implementation, sector-specific
    regulatory expectation). For the scenario:
    - **Day 0 — engagement notification.** The CAE's
      notification wording (as the head would expect to
      receive it; cite the IIA Global Internal Audit
      Standards section the CAE operates under where the
      standards specify notification shape — mark
      `<!-- needs-research: verify current standards section
      reference against theiia.org -->` if unsure of the
      current citation). The head's specific intake
      response.
    - **Day 1–7.** The specific scope clarification
      questions the head asks and the specific preparation
      moves the head makes.
    - **Weeks 2–4.** The internal readiness pass. The
      specific findings the readiness pass surfaces
      (invented for the rehearsal; the point is the
      shape).
    - **Weeks 5–8.** The audit fieldwork. The specific
      evidence requests, personnel interviews, and
      process walkthroughs.
    - **Weeks 8–10.** The draft findings. The head
      receives three draft findings in the rehearsal: one
      the head accepts factually and in severity; one the
      head accepts factually but disputes on severity; one
      the head disputes on the facts. For each, the head's
      specific response following the finding-response
      protocol.
    - **Weeks 10–12.** The final findings. The
      disposition of each finding (the two accepted
      findings stand; the disputed finding is varied by
      the CAE based on the substantive evidence the head
      provided, or stands and the head notes the
      disagreement on the response). The remediation plan
      authored.
    - **Weeks 12–14.** The audit-committee cycle. The
      head's and CAE's parallel reports; the committee's
      response; any follow-up asks.
    - **Months 3–12.** The remediation-execution window.
      The head's reporting on cadence; the CAE's follow-
      up as findings close.
    The rehearsal is written as a specific sequence with
    specific moves at each step. Reading cold, the head
    should know how to run the next engagement.

11. **The CAE-handoff pack for incoming heads.** Author
    the specific pack an incoming head inherits on day one
    of the relationship with the CAE:
    - A one-page introduction to the current CAE and the
      current audit-committee-chair relationship (named
      incumbents; standing cadences; most recent
      engagements).
    - A one-page summary of the owed-vs-not-owed boundary
      from step 2.
    - A one-page working-stance from step 3.
    - A pointer to the current quarterly working-meeting
      minute series.
    - A pointer to the ad hoc bidirectional notification
      list.
    - A pointer to the audit-preparation runbook and the
      finding-response protocol.
    - A pointer to the material-disagreement discipline.
    - A pointer to the parallel-report shape and the most
      recent audit-committee pack.
    The pack is the artefact that preserves the discipline
    across head transitions; without it, the discipline
    resets at every head change.

12. **The failure-mode audit.** Author a short section —
    one paragraph per failure mode — naming how the file
    detects and recovers from each of chapter 05's six
    anti-patterns:
    - The "collaborative audit planning" pattern.
    - The "pre-clearing" pattern on draft findings.
    - The "friendly audit committee" pattern against the
      CAE.
    - The "audit-fatigue" pattern (delayed evidence
      production; missed interview availability; slow
      remediation).
    - The "no relationship" pattern (minimal contact
      between engagements).
    - The "co-authored narrative" pattern at the audit
      committee.

## Acceptance criteria

- The owed-vs-not-owed document enumerates every item from
  chapter 05's owed list and every item from chapter 05's
  not-owed list, with the enterprise's specifics attached
  to each. A document that paraphrases the lists without
  attaching specifics fails the criterion.
- The quarterly working meeting is authored at the
  cadence, agenda, minuting, and participant level. A
  cadence sketched without the standing agenda — the five
  agenda items chapter 05 names — fails the criterion.
- The bidirectional notification list names the specific
  trigger events in each direction and the specific
  channel per event. A list that says "material events"
  without enumerating the material-event categories fails
  the criterion.
- The audit-preparation runbook sequences the preparation
  from notification through engagement debrief, with the
  specific personnel and the specific windows at each
  step. A runbook that skips the internal readiness pass
  or that fails to assign a specific evidence-production
  owner fails the criterion.
- The finding-response protocol produces three distinct
  response-wording templates (accepted factually and in
  severity; accepted factually but disputed on severity;
  disputed on facts). A protocol that offers one generic
  response template fails the criterion — chapter 05 is
  explicit the three categories are substantively
  different.
- The material-disagreement discipline names the
  nuclear-option protocol specifically and includes a
  rehearsed preparation. A discipline that gestures at the
  escalation ("the head may raise it with the chair") but
  does not author the preparation fails the criterion —
  the head who cannot author the preparation cannot
  execute the escalation under pressure.
- The parallel-report shape includes prepared language for
  the committee's divergence question. A shape that
  describes the parallel-presentation posture but does not
  prepare for the committee's reconciliation ask fails the
  criterion.
- The engagement rehearsal walks a specific scenario end-
  to-end with specific moves at each step. A rehearsal
  that sketches the engagement in the abstract fails the
  criterion.
- The CAE-handoff pack covers each of the file's components
  as a short, inherited artefact. A handoff pack missing
  any component fails the criterion — the discipline has
  to survive head transitions, which is what the pack
  tests.
- The failure-mode audit names, for each of the six
  failure modes, at least one specific detection signal
  and one specific recovery action.
- Any factual claim about a specific IIA standard section,
  a specific CAE's methodology, a specific peer
  enterprise's finding-response rate, or a specific
  audit-committee's reconciliation discipline carries a
  `<!-- needs-research: verify [specific fact] against
  [theiia.org or named primary source] -->` marker where
  the fact is not independently verifiable. The IIA
  references in `resources.md` carry standing
  `needs-research` markers; the exercise inherits them.

## Stretch goals

- Rehearse the **joint annual reflection with the audit-
  committee chair** — the specific agenda chapter 05 names
  for the once-per-year head-CAE-chair session. Pick a
  specific moment (end of the audit-plan year; before the
  committee's plan-ratification cycle); author the specific
  reflections the head brings (substantive observations on
  the year's engagements, the working-relationship state,
  the head's forward-plan commitments); the head's
  specific listen-mode discipline for the CAE's and the
  chair's observations.
- Draft the **CAE-notification letter templates** — the
  specific wording the head uses for each of the material
  events on the bidirectional notification list. The
  templates are short (one paragraph) and legible: the
  event, the material impact, the head's standing posture
  on the event, the next-review window. Pre-authored
  templates are what let the head notify the CAE promptly
  rather than deferring the notification to a drafting
  cycle.
- Author the **"how to be audited" team-training module**
  — the specific thirty-to-sixty-minute module the head
  (or a delegate) runs with new team members during the
  90-day onboarding rotation (chapter 03). The module
  covers interview shape, substantive-not-volunteering
  answer discipline, "I don't know; let me follow up"
  protocol, note-taking during interviews, and the
  normalisation of audit engagements. The module is
  authored at the agenda, exercise, and discussion-prompt
  level; it is a module the delegate can run cold.
- Draft the **CB-and-CAE coordination note** — the
  specific coordination the head holds between the ISO/IEC
  42001 certification body relationship (chapter 06) and
  the CAE relationship (this chapter). The two parties
  audit different things and the head is a distinct kind
  of subject for each; the note names the specific
  distinctions and the specific coordination the head
  runs to ensure the two engagements compose rather than
  collide.
- Author the **CAE-is-asking-about-the-head's-function
  rehearsal** — the specific rehearsal of the quarterly
  working meeting agenda item in which the CAE asks the
  head substantive questions about the function's
  operating discipline (not about findings; about the
  function). The head's prepared responses, calibrated to
  the owed-vs-not-owed boundary, carry the head through
  the conversation without either over-sharing (which
  compromises the owed boundary) or under-sharing (which
  compromises the owed-side substance).

## Notes and starter guidance

The most common first-cut failure of the file is the head
who authors the quarterly working-meeting cadence and the
audit-preparation runbook but does not author the material-
disagreement discipline or the parallel-report shape. The
first two artefacts are relatively comfortable to write; the
latter two are not. Force the latter two — the head who has
only authored the comfortable artefacts has authored the file
for the easy case and left the hard case to improvisation,
and the hard case is where the file matters.

The second common failure is the audit-preparation runbook
that reads as a project-management template. "Day 1–7:
engagement kickoff" is not a runbook; it is a schedule. The
runbook has to carry specific moves — specific scope-
clarification questions, specific evidence-production
assignment, specific coaching sessions with new team members
who may not have been audited before — written at the level
where the head's team can execute cold.

The third common failure is the material-disagreement
discipline authored as a sympathetic summary of chapter 05
rather than as a usable protocol. Force the usability: the
specific private-first conversation the head will have, the
specific substantive-evidence package the head will marshal,
the specific nuclear-option preparation the head will do.
The nuclear-option preparation is the hardest piece to
write; it is also the most important, because the head's
willingness to prepare for the escalation is what preserves
the right to use it later.

On the parallel-report shape — the temptation is to author
it as a courtesy ("the head and CAE present independently")
without preparing for the committee's reconciliation ask.
The committee will ask; the audit-committee-chair's job
includes asking. The head who has prepared the specific
substantive response to the ask is the head who holds the
divergence credibly; the head who has not prepared it is the
head whose divergence reads as muddled.

On the engagement rehearsal — the temptation is to pick a
sympathetic scenario (an audit where the function performs
well; findings all accepted; the committee is pleased). A
sympathetic scenario rehearses nothing. Pick a scenario with
genuine stress — a finding the head genuinely disputes; a
remediation window that genuinely strains the function's
capacity; a committee reconciliation question that is
genuinely hard. The hard rehearsal is where the discipline
is built.

The file is a living artefact. Version it, date it, refresh
the owed-vs-not-owed boundary at any material change in the
IIA standards or the CAE's charter, refresh the quarterly
working-meeting agenda when the enterprise's AI risk shape
materially shifts, refresh the bidirectional notification
list after each material event the list did not anticipate,
refresh the engagement rehearsal annually against the most
recent audit cycle. A file that has not been touched in a
year is a file the CAE relationship has already moved past.
