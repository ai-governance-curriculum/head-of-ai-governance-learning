# The CxO-Scope Vendor Relationships — GRC-for-AI, Certification Bodies, and Assurance Partners

## Motivation

The head of AI governance operates a function that is materially
enabled by external vendors and external assurance bodies. The
GRC-for-AI platform is the system of record the function runs
against. The ISO/IEC 42001 certification body is the external
conformity-assessment party the enterprise's AIMS is assessed by.
External independent-audit and assurance bodies — ForHumanity,
BABL AI, and peers — are the parties that produce the
independent-assurance signal the head takes into audit-committee
and market-facing conversations. Standards-adjacent training
providers are the source of the credentialed talent that feeds
the hiring plan (chapter [[02-multi-year-hiring-plan]]).

These relationships are not day-to-day procurement transactions.
They are enterprise-scope vendor and partner relationships that
the head owns at CxO account scope. A GRC-for-AI platform
selected in year one shapes the function's operating discipline
for five to seven years; a certification-body relationship is
often the same auditor firm across multiple three-year
certification cycles; a ForHumanity or BABL AI engagement
attaches the enterprise's assurance posture publicly. The head
who runs these relationships as generic vendor management is a
head who has under-invested in the relationships the enterprise
depends on for the function's operating capability.

This chapter is how the head runs those relationships. Not the
procurement flow — the enterprise's own procurement organisation
runs the RFP, the contract, the invoicing. What the head runs is
the *account* side: the strategic conversation about where the
vendor is going and where the enterprise is going, the
satisfaction-pulse discipline that keeps the account healthy,
the joint-planning cadence that keeps the enterprise on the
vendor's roadmap, and the escalation path when a vendor is
materially underperforming.

The chapter also names the discipline that keeps these
relationships from over-dependence. A function whose entire
operating discipline depends on one GRC platform, one
certification body, and one assurance partner is a function whose
continuity risk is high; the head runs the relationships and
also runs the continuity plan against them.

## Core concept — the four CxO-scope vendor relationships

The head owns four relationship categories at CxO account scope.
Each has its own working cadence, its own escalation path, and
its own contribution to the function's operating capacity.

**The GRC-for-AI platform vendor.** The platform is the system
of record for the AI system inventory
([[mod-102-running-the-program]] chapter 06), the control
library, the evidence store, the risk register, and the
metric-production pipeline that feeds the KRI/KPI portfolio
([[mod-107-program-measurement-and-maturity]] chapter 01). The
platform is chosen and refreshed on multi-year cycles; the
head is the CxO-account owner on the enterprise's side, engaging
directly with the vendor's CxO-account team (typically the
vendor's Chief Customer Officer, Head of Enterprise Accounts, or
the executive sponsor assigned to the account).

**The ISO/IEC 42001 certification body.** The external conformity
assessment body (CB) that audits the enterprise's AI Management
System (AIMS) against ISO/IEC 42001 and, where the enterprise
carries certification, issues the certification. The CB
relationship is typically a three-year certification cycle with
annual surveillance audits; the head is the enterprise-side
account owner, engaging with the CB's account principal or
technical committee lead. See
[[mod-102-running-the-program]] chapter 04 for the third-party
audit interface at operating scope; this chapter carries the
relationship at CxO scope.

**Independent AI assurance and audit bodies.** ForHumanity
(Independent Audit of AI Systems — IAAIS), BABL AI, and other
independent-assurance partners. These are the bodies the head
engages for external independent-assurance work that
complements the CB relationship — assurance against criteria
that are broader than ISO/IEC 42001 alone (algorithmic-audit
craft, socio-technical-impact assessment, sector-specific
independent assurance). The head owns the assurance-partner
selection and the enterprise-scope engagement.

**Standards-adjacent training and credentialing partners.**
IAPP (AIGP), BSI / PECB / LRQA (ISO/IEC 42001 Lead Implementer /
Lead Auditor training), BABL AI (auditor and practitioner
training), ForHumanity (Fellows program and audit
accreditation), Responsible AI Institute, NIST-recognised
training partners, and university programs. The head does not
manage each partner as a vendor account in the operational
sense; the head does maintain the strategic relationships that
keep the enterprise's talent pipeline current and that
position the enterprise's own team members as visible
contributors in the community. See
[[mod-112-certifications-career-path-community-leadership]]
for the deeper community-leadership discipline; this chapter
covers the head's own vendor-account posture toward these
partners.

Each of the four relationships has different economics,
different governance shape, and different escalation options.
The head runs them distinctly rather than under a generic
"vendor management" umbrella.

## The GRC-for-AI platform relationship

The GRC-for-AI platform is the largest single vendor investment
the function makes. Selection, ratification, and ongoing
relationship discipline are enterprise-scope work.

### Selection and ratification (year zero or at replacement)

Platform selection follows the enterprise's standard technology-
procurement flow (RFP, security-review, procurement-negotiation,
contract-execution) but the head is the CxO-account business
owner throughout. The head's specific responsibilities during
selection:

- **Author the requirements.** The functional requirements come
  from the level-50 architect (control library shape, evidence
  store shape, integration surface with the enterprise's ISMS,
  workflow shape for the AIMS operating calendar), the level-35
  evaluator (evaluation-harness integration, release-assurance
  workflow), the level-15 analysts (intake-queue ergonomics,
  evidence-collection ergonomics), and the head (audit-
  committee-facing reporting, KRI production, CxO-adjacent
  workflow). The head consolidates the requirements into a
  single ratified requirements document.
- **Engage vendor CxO scope in the evaluation.** For serious
  candidate vendors, the head arranges a session with the
  vendor's executive sponsor to discuss where the vendor is
  going strategically — roadmap direction, product depth
  investments, market positioning, financial stability. This
  is not a demo; this is an account-relationship conversation.
- **Reference-check at head-to-head scope.** The head talks to
  the heads-of-AI-governance at two or three peer institutions
  who use the candidate platform. Not the vendor's reference
  list; the head's own network. The conversations are candid,
  substantive, and cover what has actually worked and what has
  not.
- **Ratify with the CIO or CTO.** The GRC-for-AI platform runs
  on the enterprise's technology infrastructure and touches
  multiple enterprise-scope systems. The head brings the
  selection to the CIO or CTO for ratification before
  contracting.

The specific selection is not the chapter's business — the
head picks based on the requirements, the peer references, the
fit with the enterprise's technology stack, and the commercial
terms. What is the chapter's business is the discipline of
running the selection so the head's function does not inherit a
platform that was picked by procurement without the head's
substantive engagement.

### Ongoing account discipline

Once a platform is in place, the head runs a specific account
cadence with the vendor.

**A standing quarterly account review.** Ninety minutes,
scheduled six months ahead. Agenda:

- Product roadmap review — the vendor walks through what is
  landing in the next quarter and the following two, and how
  it maps to the enterprise's active requests.
- Enterprise-side workstreams — the head or the level-50
  architect walks through the function's coming quarter's
  major workstreams and where platform support matters.
- Escalation review — any open support tickets at severity that
  warrants CxO attention, any implementation-support
  engagements in flight, any pending contract or licensing
  decisions.
- Satisfaction pulse — the head shares an honest read on where
  the platform is performing well and where it is not; the
  vendor responds substantively.
- Peer-community discussion — where the vendor is willing, a
  short exchange on what the vendor is seeing across their
  customer base that might inform the enterprise's own
  practice.

The quarterly account review is minuted. Actions with owners
and dates go into both organisations' tracking. Repeat missed
actions become material at the next review.

**An annual strategic review.** Half a day, typically at the
vendor's location or at a neutral venue. Broader agenda:
multi-year roadmap alignment, contract structure discussion for
the coming term, executive introductions across both
organisations, and — if timing calls for it — early scoping of
contract renewal or replacement.

**Ad hoc escalation channel.** The head has a named vendor CxO
contact — the executive sponsor for the account — reachable
directly for material escalations. The head uses the channel
rarely (weekly escalations to CxO are the wrong pattern; the
right pattern is a competent implementation team handling
day-to-day and CxO-level engagement reserved for material
issues), and the vendor CxO responds materially when the head
does use it.

### The vendor-satisfaction pulse

The head runs a specific vendor-satisfaction pulse — a
structured internal read on how the platform is performing —
at a defined cadence. Quarterly is common. The pulse asks:

- **Reliability.** Is the platform up when the function needs
  it? Are there specific patterns of downtime or degradation
  that map to critical operational moments (month-end reporting,
  audit-committee cycles, regulator-response windows)?
- **Fitness for purpose.** Does the platform support the
  function's actual workflows well, or does the function work
  around the platform to get things done? Ambient workarounds
  are the signal the platform has drifted from fit.
- **Support responsiveness.** Are support tickets resolved on
  contracted SLAs? Are complex tickets handled substantively
  or bounced back and forth?
- **Roadmap alignment.** Is the vendor delivering roadmap items
  the enterprise depends on? Are dates slipping? Are new items
  the enterprise needs being added to the roadmap?
- **Total cost of operation trajectory.** Is the effective cost
  of the platform (licence plus implementation plus workaround
  labour plus support engagements) stable, rising, or falling
  against expectations?

The pulse is read structurally, not on head vibe. The head
convenes the level-50 architect, the level-15 analysts who use
the platform most, and the level-35 evaluators, and captures a
consolidated read. The read goes to the vendor at the quarterly
account review, in candid substance rather than diplomatic
gloss.

### Escalation when the platform is materially underperforming

When the pulse signals material underperformance — repeated
downtime at critical moments, roadmap items slipping past
their commitments, support-responsiveness degrading, or fit-
for-purpose gaps that the vendor is not addressing — the head
escalates through a defined path.

- **First escalation: to the vendor's account executive on the
  next quarterly review, with the specific pulse findings and
  a specific request.** The request is concrete: fix the
  downtime pattern by Q-end; deliver the slipping roadmap item
  by Q-end; provide an implementation-support engagement to
  close the fit gap by Q-end. The head names the escalation
  explicitly ("this is an escalation, not a routine review
  point") so the account executive routes it appropriately.
- **Second escalation: to the vendor's CxO sponsor by direct
  contact.** If the first escalation does not produce a
  substantive response within the agreed window, the head
  raises directly with the executive sponsor. The head names
  the pattern (not just the incident) and asks for CxO-scope
  engagement — an executive-visibility remediation plan, a
  dedicated account team assignment, or a specific commitment
  from the vendor's product organisation.
- **Third escalation: internally, to the CFO and CIO, with the
  material-underperformance case.** If the second escalation
  does not produce substantive change, the head engages the
  enterprise's own CFO and CIO with a proposal — invoke
  contract remedies (SLA credits, contract restructuring),
  begin the replacement-vendor evaluation (see continuity
  planning below), or a hybrid path.
- **Fourth escalation: contract termination and replacement.**
  The nuclear option, exercised rarely. Contract termination
  for material underperformance is the head's authority to
  recommend and the CFO's/CIO's authority to execute. The
  head does not threaten termination without genuine
  willingness to execute; the vendor reads the difference
  between real threat and posture immediately.

### Continuity planning against the platform relationship

The head does not run the function on the assumption that the
platform is permanent. Continuity planning is standing work.

- **Data-portability discipline.** The head confirms, at
  contract signing and at each material contract update, that
  the enterprise's data — the AI inventory, the evidence store,
  the control library — is portable in a standardised format
  (OSCAL, SPDX 3.0 AI profile, CycloneDX ML-BOM, or the
  platform's own well-documented export). A vendor whose
  contract does not guarantee portability is a vendor whose
  lock-in the head has to reduce through platform-neutral
  authoring elsewhere.
- **Alternate-vendor awareness.** The head maintains ambient
  awareness of the GRC-for-AI market — at least one competitor
  the head has evaluated at head-to-head scope in the last
  eighteen months, at least one emerging entrant the head has
  taken a briefing from. Continuity planning depends on the
  head knowing what alternatives exist.
- **Documented replacement plan.** The head maintains, at low
  fidelity, a documented replacement plan — what a migration
  to an alternate vendor would look like, at what cost, over
  what timeline. The plan is not detailed until the
  replacement is genuinely contemplated; the plan exists so
  the head is not authoring from scratch in a crisis.

## The ISO/IEC 42001 certification-body relationship

The certification body relationship is materially different from
the GRC-platform relationship. The certification body is an
*assurance provider* running an accredited conformity assessment;
the head's discipline is to maintain the CB's independence while
running a substantive working relationship.

### CB selection

Certification-body selection is typically a procurement-led
process constrained by accreditation (the CB must be accredited
to certify against ISO/IEC 42001 by an IAF-member accreditation
body — UKAS in the UK, ANAB in the US, DAkkS in Germany, and
so on). Beyond accreditation, the head's specific inputs to
selection:

- **AI competence.** The CB's team members auditing the AIMS
  need substantive AI-specific competence, not generic
  management-systems audit competence. The head asks about the
  audit team's qualifications, their AI-specific case history,
  and their engagement with the ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 42 committee
  work that informs the standard.
- **Sector fit.** For enterprises in regulated sectors, the CB's
  experience with sector-specific overlays matters. A CB with a
  strong medical-device track record is a better fit for a
  SaMD-heavy enterprise; a CB with a strong financial-services
  track record is a better fit for a bank.
- **Cross-standard capability.** Where the enterprise carries
  ISO/IEC 27001 and other management-system certifications, a
  CB that can run coordinated audits across the standards may
  produce operational efficiency.

The CB is not shopped on the head's preference for auditor
friendliness. A CB the head selects because the CB is expected
to be lenient is a CB whose certification the market will
discount, and the head has undermined the value of the
certification the enterprise is paying for.

### CB relationship discipline

The CB relationship runs on a distinct cadence.

- **The annual surveillance-audit cycle.** The CB runs a full
  audit at certification and re-certification (every three
  years typically) and a surveillance audit annually in between.
  The head prepares for each with the same discipline chapter
  [[05-cae-and-third-line-coordination]] describes for
  internal-audit engagements — timely evidence production,
  personnel access, substantive engagement on findings, formal
  response.
- **A standing account principal contact.** The CB assigns an
  account principal (or equivalent) who is the enterprise's
  primary CB contact between audits. The head has a working
  relationship with the account principal — a couple of
  substantive conversations a year on the enterprise's AIMS
  posture, on the CB's read on the sector's AIMS practice, on
  upcoming standard updates.
- **Committee-work engagement.** Where the CB participates in
  ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 42 or related committee work, the head
  engages the CB on developments — either through the account
  principal or through the CB's technical committee members.
  This is voluntary and reciprocal; the head does not use the
  channel to shape audit outcomes.
- **Escalation discipline.** Material CB-related escalations
  (a substantive disagreement on a finding, a scheduling
  conflict that materially disrupts an audit cycle, a
  perception that the audit team's competence is insufficient
  for the audit's scope) go through the CB's own escalation
  channel — usually to the CB's technical committee or
  certification-decision authority — and the head follows the
  CB's process rather than lobbying informally.

### CB rotation and independence maintenance

The CB relationship is subject to independence discipline.
Some CBs will not certify enterprises whose audit team has been
the same across too many cycles; the head respects that
discipline. Some enterprises prefer to rotate CBs on a defined
cadence to maintain the independence signal; the head takes the
rotation decision in coordination with the CRO and the audit
committee.

Rotation is not free — a new CB re-runs the discovery cycle,
takes a year or two to develop the depth of understanding of the
enterprise's AIMS the incumbent had, and typically costs more in
the first cycle than a continuing CB. The rotation decision is
a genuine trade-off; the head presents both sides honestly to
the CRO and audit committee and does not present rotation as
"the right thing to do" without the trade-offs on the table.

## The independent AI assurance and audit body relationship

ForHumanity, BABL AI, and other independent-assurance partners
occupy a different role again. They are neither the GRC platform
nor the accredited CB; they are independent-assurance parties the
head engages for specific assurance work that is broader or
different from the CB's scope.

### When the head engages an independent assurance body

- **Complementary assurance to ISO/IEC 42001 certification.** An
  independent audit against ForHumanity's IAAIS criteria, for
  example, covers criteria that extend beyond the ISO/IEC 42001
  management-system focus into socio-technical impact and
  algorithmic-audit craft. The head engages the independent
  auditor when the enterprise's assurance posture warrants that
  broader assessment.
- **Sector-specific or scoped assurance.** An algorithmic-audit
  engagement scoped to a specific high-impact AI system (a
  credit-decisioning system, a hiring-decisioning system, an
  educational-decisioning system) produces a focused
  independent read that neither ISO/IEC 42001 nor the CAE's
  internal audit would produce at the same depth.
- **Voluntary-code participation assurance.** Where the
  enterprise has signed on to a voluntary code
  ([[mod-106-external-positioning-and-voluntary-codes]]
  chapter 01) that includes an independent-assurance
  expectation, the head engages the qualified independent
  assurance provider.
- **Regulator- or customer-facing assurance signal.** Where a
  regulator or a material customer specifically values an
  independent-assurance signal from a named body, the head
  engages the body to produce the signal in the form the
  audience recognises.

### Independent-assurance-body relationship discipline

The head runs the independent-assurance relationship with the
same substantive discipline as the CB relationship:

- **Substantive engagement on scope.** The head is not
  negotiating for a soft assurance opinion. The scope is
  substantive; the assurance provider's independence is
  respected; the assurance opinion is what it is.
- **Timely evidence production and personnel access.** Same as
  the CB and CAE discipline.
- **Substantive response to findings.** Same discipline.
- **Public disclosure discipline.** Independent-assurance
  opinions are often intended for public disclosure (that is
  frequently their purpose). The head coordinates the
  disclosure with the enterprise's communications and legal
  functions and does not selectively disclose or edit the
  substance.

Because the assurance provider's opinion is often externally
visible, the head is especially attentive to running the
relationship in a way that survives external scrutiny. A cosy
head-provider relationship where the provider consistently
opines favourably is a relationship the market will discount;
the head does not seek that pattern.

## Standards-adjacent training and credentialing partners

The fourth relationship category is standards-adjacent training
and credentialing. IAPP, BSI, PECB, LRQA, BABL AI, ForHumanity,
Responsible AI Institute, university programs, and NIST-recognised
training providers.

The head's discipline here is lighter than the vendor-account
discipline in the prior three categories, but it is not absent.

- **Fund team members' progression through recognised
  credentials.** The head budgets for team members'
  certifications (IAPP AIGP or CIPP for level-15 through level-
  50 depending on role; ISO/IEC 42001 Lead Implementer /
  Lead Auditor for architects and evaluators; ForHumanity
  Fellows or BABL AI credentials as fit for role). This is a
  vendor spend and it is a talent-development investment; the
  head runs it as both. See
  [[03-coaching-and-career-pathing]] for the development-plan
  discipline the credential investment fits into.
- **Maintain awareness of curriculum shifts.** IAPP updates the
  AIGP body of knowledge as the field evolves; ISO/IEC 42001
  Lead Implementer courses update against standard revisions.
  The head keeps ambient awareness of what current cohorts
  are learning so the head's hiring pipeline and internal
  development stay current.
- **Position the enterprise as an employer of choice inside the
  credentialed community.** The head speaks at cohort events
  where appropriate, sponsors the enterprise's team members'
  participation in body-of-knowledge development work, and
  engages the credentialing bodies as talent partners. See
  [[mod-112-certifications-career-path-community-leadership]]
  for the community-leadership shape.

The head does not run these partners as a vendor-account
portfolio the same way as the GRC platform, the CB, and the
assurance body. The head does maintain the strategic
relationships that keep the enterprise inside the community
these partners serve.

## Anti-patterns the head does not permit

**The absent-CxO-owner pattern.** The head is nominally the
vendor-account business owner but has not actually engaged the
vendor's CxO account team in a year. The vendor treats the
account as a routine renewal, product roadmap does not reflect
the enterprise's requests, and the account drifts. The remedy
is the quarterly account review calendar with the vendor CxO
sponsor personally involved.

**The vibes-based satisfaction read.** The head thinks the
platform is fine because nobody has escalated recently, but
has never run the structured satisfaction pulse with the level-
50 architect and the level-15 analysts. When the platform is
genuinely underperforming, the head hears about it in the
crisis rather than in the pulse. The remedy is the quarterly
structured pulse discipline.

**The single-vendor dependency posture.** The head has not
maintained data-portability discipline, has not evaluated
alternatives in eighteen months, has no replacement plan.
When the vendor materially underperforms, the head has no
credible escalation because there is no credible replacement
option. The remedy is the continuity planning discipline
above.

**The audit-friendly CB selection pattern.** The head has
selected the CB expected to be lenient rather than the CB
whose competence fits the enterprise's AIMS. Certification
carries less market weight than it should; when the head
tries to use the certification in a material regulator or
customer conversation, the audience discounts it. The remedy
is the substantive-competence selection discipline and the
head's willingness to accept a rigorous CB.

**The soft independent-assurance pattern.** The head engages an
independent-assurance body with a nod-and-wink expectation of
a favourable opinion. The provider either delivers the
favourable opinion (and their own market standing eventually
suffers, and the enterprise's along with it) or delivers a
substantive opinion (and the head is unprepared for it). The
remedy is the substantive-engagement discipline — the head
engages independent assurance to hear a genuine
independent-assurance opinion.

**The talent-partner-as-transactional pattern.** The head treats
IAPP, BSI, PECB, ForHumanity, and university programs as
transactional certification vendors — the team pays for the
course, the team gets the certificate, transaction ends. The
enterprise's presence in the community these bodies serve is
weak; hiring pipeline suffers; the enterprise is not visible
as an employer of choice. The remedy is the community-
partner disposition described above and in
[[mod-112-certifications-career-path-community-leadership]].

**The complaint-only escalation pattern.** The head engages the
vendor CxO channel only to complain, never to strategise or to
give substantive positive feedback. The vendor CxO learns to
brace when the head calls, and the substantive signal degrades.
The remedy is the balanced quarterly cadence — substantive on
what is working and what is not, escalation as one component,
not the entirety.

## Summary

- The head owns four CxO-scope vendor relationships: the
  GRC-for-AI platform, the ISO/IEC 42001 certification body,
  independent AI assurance and audit bodies (ForHumanity, BABL
  AI, and peers), and standards-adjacent training and
  credentialing partners.
- The GRC-for-AI platform is the largest single vendor
  investment; the head runs selection, ongoing account
  discipline (quarterly account review, annual strategic
  review, ad hoc escalation channel), a structured vendor-
  satisfaction pulse, a four-stage escalation path when the
  platform materially underperforms, and standing continuity
  planning against platform lock-in.
- The ISO/IEC 42001 CB is an accredited assurance provider; the
  head selects on substantive competence rather than expected
  leniency, runs the CB relationship on the surveillance-audit
  cycle with a standing account principal, engages committee
  work reciprocally, and coordinates rotation decisions with
  the CRO and audit committee.
- Independent AI assurance bodies produce assurance broader
  than or different from the CB's scope; the head engages
  them with substantive-scope discipline and coordinates
  public disclosure with the enterprise's communications and
  legal functions.
- Standards-adjacent training and credentialing partners are
  the source of the credentialed talent that feeds hiring; the
  head funds team member progression, maintains curriculum-
  shift awareness, and positions the enterprise as an employer
  of choice in the community.
- Seven anti-patterns the head refuses: the absent-CxO-owner
  pattern, vibes-based satisfaction read, single-vendor
  dependency, audit-friendly CB selection, soft independent-
  assurance engagement, talent-partner-as-transactional, and
  the complaint-only escalation pattern.
- Chapter [[07-difficult-people-leadership-conversations]]
  runs the difficult conversations that can arise inside the
  team or at CxO scope, including the difficult conversation
  the head has with a vendor CxO when a material relationship
  breaks down; the vendor-escalation discipline in this chapter
  and the difficult-conversation discipline in the next
  compose.
