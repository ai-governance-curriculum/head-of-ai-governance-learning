# mod-108 — Leading the AI Governance Function as a People Leader

**Estimated effort:** 18 hours
**Position on the track:** the eighth module — the people-
leadership discipline underneath the running program built in
[[mod-102-running-the-program]], the reporting posture built in
[[mod-103-board-and-audit-committee-reporting]], the regulator
engagements from [[mod-104-regulator-engagement]], the
regulatory-change work from
[[mod-105-regulatory-change-management]], the external
positioning from
[[mod-106-external-positioning-and-voluntary-codes]], and the
measurement backbone from
[[mod-107-program-measurement-and-maturity]]. This module is
what turns the head from an accountable executive with a
program into a people leader who runs a function that survives
the head's absence.

## What this module is for

The AI-governance function is the head's own team, plus a set
of matrix and CxO-scope relationships the head owns. The program
lives inside the function; the function is what makes the
program repeatable, defensible, and survivable. A head who runs
the program well but has not built the function well is a head
whose program depends on the head's presence — the audit
committee's key-person-dependency finding waiting to be written.

The failure mode this module opens with is not a head without
people. It is a head who has hired capable people but has not
designed the function's *shape*, has not planned its multi-year
*trajectory*, has not authored the *coaching cadence* that
compounds craft over time, has not run the second line's
*independence balance* against the first line, has not built the
third-line *coordination discipline* with the CAE, has not
carried the leadership-scope *vendor relationships* at CxO
account depth, and has not rehearsed the *difficult
conversations* that arrive across each of those surfaces at some
point in any real tenure.

This module answers seven connected questions.

1. How does the head design the function's org shape — the five
   role archetypes on the level ladder (analyst 15, risk
   engineer 25, evaluation engineer 35, senior architect 50,
   head 60), the reporting-line pattern (fully-centralised,
   hub-and-spoke, federated by risk domain, or matrixed-from-
   source with a small hub), and the succession picture per
   critical role — so the function survives the head's absence?
2. How does the head author the multi-year hiring plan — the
   headcount trajectory across a three-year horizon, the two-
   population sourcing strategy (career switchers from adjacent
   second-line disciplines; early-career practitioners from
   credential cohorts), the interview loop, the sign-off flow,
   and the compensation-band positioning — so hiring is planned
   rather than reactive?
3. How does the head run the coaching and career-pathing
   cadence — the 1:1 discipline, the career-development plans,
   the rotation program (architect mentorship, evaluator
   shadowing, product-team embeds), the onboarding rotation, the
   team-level rituals — so the function's people compound in
   craft rather than plateau or leave?
4. How does the head hold the second line's independence from
   first-line product without the operational-distance failure
   the audit committee eventually names — close enough that
   product engineers see the second line as helpful, and
   independent enough that a stop-ship call is heard as the
   enterprise speaking rather than as a rival team obstructing?
5. How does the head coordinate with the Chief Audit Executive
   on the third-line AI audit programme — inside the IIA Three
   Lines Model and IPPF discipline that puts the CAE, not the
   head, in ownership of the third line — so the head is a
   substantive, transparent audit subject rather than a co-
   designer or an obstacle?
6. How does the head run the leadership-scope vendor
   relationships — the GRC-for-AI platform, the ISO/IEC 42001
   certification body, the independent AI assurance and audit
   bodies (ForHumanity, BABL AI, and peers), the standards-
   adjacent training and credentialing partners — at CxO account
   depth with the account-satisfaction pulse, the joint-planning
   cadence, and the escalation path when a vendor materially
   underperforms?
7. How does the head hold the difficult people-leadership
   conversation — reorganising the second line, escalating a
   matrixed peer for governance non-cooperation, terminating a
   senior team member, defending a hire who challenged product
   too aggressively — with preparation, in-person delivery,
   minuting, and follow-through disciplined enough that the head
   holds them well when they arrive?

The module produces the operating artefacts the head carries
across the tenure — the org chart, the multi-year plan, the
development portfolio, the balance discipline, the CAE
coordination artefacts, the vendor account plans, and the
difficult-conversation rehearsals.

## Learning objectives (from the plan)

- Design the AI-governance function's org shape — analysts,
  engineers, evaluators, architects — with the reporting-line
  decisions (directly reporting versus matrixed from product /
  engineering / risk / audit), the level-ladder mapping
  (`ai-governance-analyst` level 15 → `ai-risk-engineer` level 25
  → `ai-evaluation-engineer` level 35 → `senior-ai-governance-
  architect` level 50 → `head-of-ai-governance` level 60), and
  the succession-planning discipline.
- Design the hiring plan — the multi-year headcount ladder, the
  sourcing strategy (career switchers from audit / privacy /
  model-risk-management / legal / product / engineering; graduates
  from IAPP AIGP / ISO/IEC 42001 LI cohorts), the interview
  loop, the sign-off flow, the compensation-band positioning.
- Design the coaching and mentoring cadence — 1:1 discipline,
  career-development plans, technical-training rotation (through
  senior-AI-governance-architect mentorship, AI-evaluation-
  engineer shadowing, product-team embed rotations).
- Manage the second line's independence from first-line product
  without operational-distance failure — the head has to be
  close enough that product engineers see the second line as
  helpful, and independent enough that the second line can say
  "stop shipping" with a straight face; coordinate the balance
  with the CRO where the second line reports up.
- Coordinate with the Chief Audit Executive (IIA Three Lines
  Model + IIA CAE guidance + IIA IPPF) on the third-line AI
  audit programme — the head does not run third-line audit; the
  CAE does; the head is a third-line-audit *subject*, not its
  owner.
- Own the leadership-scope vendor relationship — the GRC-for-AI
  platform vendor at CxO-account scope; the assurance-body
  relationship (ISO 42001 certification body, ForHumanity, BABL
  AI); the vendor-satisfaction pulse; the escalation path when
  a vendor is materially underperforming.
- Rehearse the difficult people-leadership conversation —
  reorganising the second line, escalating a matrixed peer for
  governance non-cooperation, terminating a senior team member,
  defending a hire who challenged product too aggressively.

## How to work through this module

The module is designed to be worked linearly. Chapter 01 opens
with the org-shape decision because it is the load-bearing
design choice underneath everything else; without the shape, the
hiring plan has no target, the coaching cadence has no ladder to
run against, and the succession picture has no roles to plan
for. Chapter 02 fills the shape with a multi-year hiring plan
that has horizon, ladder, and shape as its three dimensions and
that survives the CFO budget cycle. Chapter 03 runs the coaching
cadence that turns hires into people who compound in craft over
time. Chapter 04 runs the second-line independence balance as an
operating discipline against the shape — proximity plus
independence plus trust, tested against three specific pressure
patterns and against the stop-ship discipline. Chapter 05 runs
the CAE coordination inside the IIA Three Lines / IPPF
discipline — the head as substantive, transparent audit subject
rather than co-designer. Chapter 06 runs the four CxO-scope
vendor relationships — the GRC-for-AI platform, the ISO/IEC
42001 CB, the independent AI assurance bodies, the training and
credentialing partners — with the quarterly account rhythm and
the four-stage escalation path. Chapter 07 closes with the four
difficult people-leadership conversations — reorganisation,
escalation, termination, and defence — and the five-element
discipline that runs each of them.

Exercises follow the same order. Exercise 1 produces the
function's org chart and reporting-line-decision table with a
succession-picture summary. Exercise 2 produces the three-year
hiring plan with year-one detail and year-two and year-three
trigger-contingent trajectory. Exercise 3 produces the coaching
cadence and development-portfolio artefacts. Exercise 4
rehearses the independence balance with three concrete pressure
scenarios. Exercise 5 produces the CAE coordination artefacts
and rehearses a full third-line audit engagement. Exercise 6
produces the four vendor account plans with the quarterly-review
template and the escalation pathway.

By the end of the module, a working head-of-role has produced:

- A canonical org chart with the shape decision (A / B / C / D)
  documented, a reporting-line-decision table (solid-line /
  dotted-line / performance-review-author) per role, a level-
  ladder mapping per role, and a succession picture (ready-now /
  ready-in-one-year / ready-in-three-years / interim on
  unexpected departure) per critical role.
- A three-year hiring plan with year-one headcount detail and
  year-two / year-three trigger-contingent trajectory, sourcing-
  strategy per candidate population, an interview loop per level
  with written rubrics, a sign-off flow per level, and a
  compensation-band-positioning statement ratified annually.
- A development portfolio with per-report 1:1 cadences, per-
  report career-development plans, a rotation program (architect
  mentorship, evaluator shadowing, product-team embeds) with
  named hosts and defined objectives, a per-role onboarding
  rotation, and a team-level ritual set.
- An independence-balance operating discipline with the
  proximity / independence / trust axes, the three pressure-
  pattern rehearsals, the CRO joint operating agreement, the
  operational-distance detection tests, and the stop-ship
  discipline.
- A CAE coordination file with the quarterly working-meeting
  cadence, the ad hoc bidirectional notification list, the
  audit-preparation runbook, the finding-response protocol, the
  disagreement-handling discipline, and the audit-committee
  parallel-report shape.
- Four vendor account plans (GRC-for-AI platform, ISO/IEC 42001
  CB, independent AI assurance body, training and credentialing
  partner) with the account-owner named, the quarterly review
  template, the annual strategic review shape, the satisfaction-
  pulse instrument, and the four-stage escalation path.
- A rehearsed pack for the four difficult people-leadership
  conversations — reorganisation, matrixed-peer escalation,
  termination, and defence-of-a-hire — with the five-element
  discipline (preparation, HR / CRO / GC pre-alignment, in-
  person delivery, minuting, follow-through) walked through each.
