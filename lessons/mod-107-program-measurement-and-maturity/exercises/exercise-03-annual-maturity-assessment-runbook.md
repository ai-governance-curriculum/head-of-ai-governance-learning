# exercise-03: Annual Maturity Assessment Runbook

**Estimated effort:** 2 hours

## Objective

Author the enterprise's **annual maturity assessment runbook**
— the operating discipline that turns the model authored in
exercise 02 into a plot the audit committee ratifies, and turns
the plot into a multi-year trajectory the CFO carries into the
investment planning cycle. The deliverable is the runbook the
head executes on the AIMS operating calendar, the artefact the
level-50 architect uses to plan the self-assessment window, the
artefact the second-line reviewer and the CAE work against, and
the pack the audit committee reads at the annual deep-dive
session.

The runbook has to survive three stress tests. First, the CAE
opens the annual audit engagement and can determine, from the
runbook alone, what evidence to test and against what tier
definitions; a runbook the CAE cannot follow is a runbook the
CAE will not use. Second, a peer head in a benchmarking
exchange asks how the enterprise runs the assessment — the
runbook has to describe the four-voice discipline in terms a
peer head recognises as defensible, not as terms bespoke to the
enterprise. Third, an incoming head-of-role inherits the
runbook mid-cycle and can pick it up without the
authoring head in the room; the runbook is calendar-anchored
and role-anchored, not incumbent-anchored.

The runbook is not a study of chapter 03. It is the operating
artefact that makes chapter 03's four-voice discipline
executable at the specific enterprise's cadence, with the
specific enterprise's peer set, the specific enterprise's
second-line and third-line functions, and the specific
enterprise's audit-committee calendar.

## Steps

1. **The assessment calendar for the enterprise.** Author the
   month-by-month calendar the assessment sits on, anchored to
   the enterprise's actual audit-committee calendar and AIMS
   operating calendar. Use chapter 03's Q4-anchored worked
   calendar as the reference shape, adapted to the enterprise's
   fiscal year and audit-committee cadence. Every entry names
   the specific work, the specific owner (as a role), the
   specific artefact produced, and the specific handoff to the
   next stage. Include the mid-year trajectory read at the
   six-month mark.

2. **The self-assessment window playbook.** Author the
   discipline the level-50 architect runs against during the
   self-assessment window (typically 4–8 weeks). Include:
   - The evidence-anchoring rule: every score at level 3 or
     above requires linked evidence held in a system of
     record. A score without linked evidence defaults to
     level 2.
   - The head's review discipline: the head reviews the
     architect's scoring for consistency across axes and
     challenges scores that appear to depart from tier
     definitions, but does not override the architect's
     scoring. Chapter 03 is explicit on this.
   - The down-move discipline: an axis or cell that regressed
     year-over-year is diagnosed against the three
     candidates (model changed, evidence base thinned,
     operating quality regressed) and named accordingly. A
     regression the runbook cannot explain is a regression
     the audit committee will read as unexplained, and the
     head will lose the room.
   - The draft-editability rule: draft scores stay editable
     until the second-line challenge closes. The head
     resists freezing the draft early because early-frozen
     scores produce a defensive posture during challenge.
   - The scoring workbook — the specific file, template, or
     GRC-tool artefact the architect scores in. Naming the
     workbook explicitly is what keeps scoring evidence-
     anchored rather than opinion-anchored.

3. **The second-line challenge process.** Author the
   discipline the CRO's function or the enterprise's
   operational-risk function runs against. Include:
   - Who convenes: the head convenes the challenge; the
     second-line function reviews.
   - What the challenge tests: whether the evidence linked
     from each level-3+ score is *the* evidence the score
     requires, and whether the tier definition is being
     applied consistently across axes.
   - What the output looks like: for each axis, a set of
     confirmed scores, contested scores (with the reviewer's
     alternative and reason), and evidence gaps (scores the
     reviewer would confirm if additional evidence were
     produced).
   - How contested scores are resolved: the head resolves
     with the architect and the second-line reviewer in the
     room. Either additional evidence is produced to hold the
     original score, or the score moves to the reviewer's
     alternative. The resolution is minuted.
   - The rule that a contested score does not go unresolved
     to the audit committee — the audit committee should not
     be adjudicating scoring disputes in the room.
   - The rule that second-line does not sign off on the plot
     — second-line challenges; the head authorises with
     audit-committee ratification. The distinction preserves
     second-line's independence for the next year's
     challenge.

4. **The third-line CAE engagement.** Author the discipline
   for engaging the CAE. Include:
   - The three CAE engagement depths (evidence assurance,
     methodology assurance, full audit) and the criteria the
     head uses to decide which depth is appropriate this
     year. Chapter 03 gives the discipline: light when the
     assessment is stable and the CAE has other priorities;
     moderate when the model has changed materially or the
     assessment is being established; heavy when the
     assessment is under external regulatory or examination
     scrutiny.
   - The specific handshake with the CAE at the start of the
     cycle: the head presents the model, the year's known
     changes, and a recommended engagement depth. The CAE
     decides — CAE independence is load-bearing.
   - The CAE's expected output: an assurance memorandum
     (light), an audit report against IIA IPPF (heavy), or a
     mixed output (moderate). Whatever the output, it becomes
     part of the assessment pack the audit committee reads.
   - The specific rule that the CAE does not set scores —
     the CAE assures the evidence and methodology; the
     scores are the head's function's, with second-line
     challenge and CAE assurance layered on top.

5. **The external-benchmarking process.** Author the
   discipline for the peer-benchmarking voice. Include:
   - The peer-set definition and ratification: the peer set
     is the enterprise's own strategic framing of who it
     competes with, who it is compared to by regulators and
     investors, and who it positions against publicly. The
     audit committee ratifies the peer set annually so the
     benchmarking is not the head's private definition.
     Chapter 03 is explicit on this.
   - The peer-set candidates: three to five peer institutions
     in the same sector and of comparable scale, industry-
     body benchmarking exchanges where they exist, certification-
     body-facilitated benchmarks, and public disclosure (peers'
     voluntary code signatory reports, frontier-lab public
     safety frameworks, sector-body public positions).
   - The exchange operations: formal peer-exchange forums are
     rare; in their absence, bilateral exchanges with peer
     heads on a "my scores in exchange for yours, under NDA"
     basis. The substance is score comparison, not program-
     detail disclosure — the head does not share the
     enterprise's evidence base or its target trajectory.
   - The output shape: a per-axis peer-position note that
     names the peer set and the position. The head does not
     adjust scores based on benchmarking; the head reports
     the position and lets the audit committee read the
     relative signal.
   - The gap-naming discipline: where benchmarking is
     unavailable for an axis, the runbook names the gap
     explicitly rather than fabricating a peer read. A
     benchmarking note reading "no functional peer
     benchmarking available for this axis in the enterprise's
     sector; the head is engaging with [named forum] to
     establish a benchmarking exchange for the following
     year" is defensible; a fabricated peer position is not.

6. **The output-pack shape.** Author the specific shape of the
   pack the audit committee reads at the deep-dive session.
   The pack has (chapter 03 gives the full list):
   - Per-axis maturity plot with current-year scores, prior-
     year scores, and the ratified two-year target overlaid.
   - Year-over-year delta commentary with regressions named
     specifically and diagnosed against the three candidates.
   - Composite view as a summary read *after* the per-axis
     view, with the model's composition-rule interpretation
     note.
   - Two-year trajectory with target levels and target-level
     rationales per axis (including deliberate under-
     investment).
   - Cost-of-move table for each planned move, feeding the
     budget cycle in
     [[mod-113-budget-vendor-p-and-l-and-investment-case]]
     and chapter 05.
   - Peer-benchmarking read per axis, with the peer set and
     the position note.
   - Independent-challenge and CAE assurance outputs.
   - Prioritisation recommendation (the two or three moves
     the head recommends the enterprise prioritise), with
     specific rationale.
   - Assumptions and limitations section.
   - Specific asks of the audit committee (endorsement of
     the trajectory, approval of resource requests,
     ratification of material model-version changes).
   The pack is a document, not a slide deck. It is packaged
   as a pre-read that the committee reads before the session.

7. **The trajectory flow into board reporting and multi-year
   investment planning.** Author how the ratified trajectory
   flows forward:
   - The board reporting flow: the board sees the maturity
     plot annually via the audit committee's board update
     ([[mod-103-board-and-audit-committee-reporting]] chapter
     01), with the trajectory line overlaid on the current
     plot.
   - The multi-year investment planning flow: each cost-of-
     move-table entry becomes a line item in the budget
     ([[mod-113-budget-vendor-p-and-l-and-investment-case]])
     with an identified funding source, a named owner, and a
     delivery date. The finance business partner (chapter 05)
     carries the line items into the budget cycle; the head
     defends them at the finance committee and at the CFO's
     own budget challenge.
   - The trajectory-drift rule: trajectory changes are
     minuted and ratified at the audit committee before the
     next annual assessment, not surfaced after the fact.
     Chapter 03 names trajectory drift as an anti-pattern.

8. **The mid-year trajectory read.** Author the shape of the
   lighter mid-cycle read at the six-month mark. The read is
   not a re-score; it is a read on evidence accumulation
   against the year's planned moves. If moves are on pace,
   the read reports "on trajectory"; if moves are behind,
   the read reports the specific slippage and what the head
   is doing about it. The read goes to the audit committee at
   the mid-year board update.

9. **The regression-and-under-performance discipline.** Author
   the specific discipline for reporting regressions and
   under-performance in the output pack:
   - Name the regression or under-performance in the delta
     commentary — no burying it in a footnote.
   - Provide the diagnosis (why it happened) with candour.
   - Provide the corrective in the trajectory — what the head
     is doing about it, whether the corrective is funded,
     and what the head is asking the committee for.
   - Name the specific resource ask if additional investment
     is required to close the gap.
   The rehearsal for the room is in chapter
   [06-difficult-measurement-conversations](../06-difficult-measurement-conversations.md);
   this runbook writes down the pack discipline that supports
   the rehearsal.

10. **The failure-mode audit.** Author a short section — one
    paragraph per failure mode — naming how the runbook
    detects and recovers from each of these failure modes:
    - The head runs the assessment as a solo exercise (self-
      assessment only, no second-line challenge, no CAE
      input, no benchmarking).
    - The CAE is engaged too late — after the pack is
      substantially drafted — so the CAE's assurance
      cannot influence the draft.
    - The peer set is redefined during the cycle to include
      peers whose disclosure makes the enterprise look
      favourable (post-hoc peer-set selection).
    - A contested score is left unresolved and appears in
      the pack as "under review", inviting the audit
      committee to adjudicate.
    - The trajectory ratified in the prior year has been
      quietly re-baselined and this year's performance is
      being measured against the new trajectory rather than
      the ratified one.

## Acceptance criteria

- The calendar names every stage on the AIMS operating
  calendar with a specific owner as a role, a specific
  artefact produced, and a specific handoff to the next
  stage. A calendar that names only "the assessment" as a
  single event fails the criterion.
- The self-assessment playbook enforces evidence-anchoring
  (level-3+ requires linked evidence) and the architect-
  scores / head-reviews discipline. A playbook that permits
  the head to override the architect's scoring fails the
  criterion.
- The second-line challenge process produces confirmed,
  contested, and gap outputs, and names the contested-score
  resolution before the audit committee. A process that
  permits contested scores to reach the audit committee
  unresolved fails the criterion.
- The CAE engagement discipline names the three depths and
  the criteria for choosing among them, and preserves CAE
  independence (CAE does not set scores). A discipline that
  has the CAE setting scores fails the criterion.
- The external-benchmarking process names the peer set as
  audit-committee-ratified, and names the gap-naming
  discipline for axes where benchmarking is unavailable. A
  process that permits fabricated peer positions fails the
  criterion.
- The output pack has every element chapter 03 names, and is
  packaged as a document (not a slide deck). A pack whose
  primary form is slides fails the criterion.
- The trajectory flow names how the cost-of-move table
  becomes budget line items and how the trajectory is
  ratified. A flow that permits trajectory drift (silent
  re-baselining) fails the criterion.
- The regression-and-under-performance discipline names the
  regression in the delta commentary, not in a footnote, and
  provides the diagnosis, corrective, and ask.
- The failure-mode audit names, for each of the five
  failure modes, at least one specific detection signal and
  one specific recovery action.
- Any factual claim about a specific peer institution's
  disclosure, a specific benchmarking-consortium
  publication, or a specific sector peer's stated maturity
  carries a
  `<!-- needs-research: verify [specific fact] against
  [official source] -->` marker where the fact is not
  independently verifiable. No peer position is guessed.

## Stretch goals

- Author the **CAE first-engagement letter** — the specific
  document the head hands the CAE at the start of the first
  cycle, framing the engagement without dictating it. The
  letter names the model, the year's known changes, the
  recommended engagement depth, and the head's ask (specific
  axes where the head would value assurance depth). The CAE
  decides scope; the letter is the head's input. See exercise
  01's stretch on the same shape.
- Draft the **peer-benchmarking exchange protocol** — the
  specific NDA-and-exchange protocol the head uses in
  bilateral peer-head exchanges. What is exchanged (scores,
  methodology-level context), what is not (evidence base,
  target trajectory, specific control implementations), how
  the exchange is documented, how the exchange output enters
  the assessment pack.
- Extend the runbook with a **certification-body benchmarking
  integration** — for the ISO axis specifically, the
  discipline for accessing the certification body's
  anonymised aggregate maturity data across its certified
  population as a peer-benchmarking source (see
  [[mod-102-running-the-program]] chapter 04). Not every
  certification body publishes such data; the discipline
  names where it is available and where it is not.
- Draft the **audit-committee deep-dive agenda** for the
  session the pack is presented at. The session is 45–90
  minutes; the pack is a pre-read; the agenda names the
  specific discussion points and the specific asks. Use the
  session-agenda convention from
  [[mod-103-board-and-audit-committee-reporting]] chapter 02.
- Author the **incident-driven re-timing rule** — the specific
  discipline for what happens when a material incident occurs
  mid-cycle. Options: re-time the assessment to give the
  incident-driven maturity signals time to accumulate; run
  the assessment on cadence and treat the incident as an
  input to the following year's model-version review; run a
  supplementary assessment on the incident-affected axes.
  Chapter 03 does not prescribe; the runbook makes the
  enterprise's choice explicit.
- Draft the **outgoing-head handover on the runbook** — the
  short note an outgoing head hands to an incoming head at
  transition, naming the current cycle's state, the peer set,
  the CAE engagement depth, the trajectory, and the known
  regressions.

## Notes and starter guidance

The most common first-cut failure of the runbook is the head
who writes the calendar without owners. A calendar entry
reading "Month –2: self-assessment complete" is a calendar
entry no one is accountable for. Every entry has an owner as
a role — the level-50 architect owns the self-assessment
window; the head owns the CAE handshake; the second-line
reviewer owns the challenge output; the finance business
partner owns the cost-of-move table's translation into
budget line items. The runbook that survives the first cycle
is the one where every stage has a named role that would show
up on the AIMS operating calendar's next revision.

The second common failure is the head who writes the runbook
around the pack and forgets the four-voice discipline. The
pack is the output; the four voices are the discipline. A
runbook that describes the pack but does not describe how the
second-line challenge closes, how the CAE handshake happens,
and how the peer set is ratified is a runbook that will
produce a pack from one voice only, and the audit committee
will read the pack as under-authored.

On the CAE engagement — resist the temptation to default to
the light engagement every year. The engagement depth is a
deliberate choice each cycle. A CAE who has run three years
of light engagements without ever running methodology
assurance is a CAE whose next engagement should be
methodology assurance, at minimum. The runbook names the
decision as a decision, not as a default.

On the peer set — the audit committee ratifies the peer set
annually. Do not treat the peer set as the head's private
list. Ratifying the peer set is a small ask but it is the
discipline that keeps the benchmarking honest — the head is
comparing against peers the audit committee has agreed are
comparable, not peers the head has selected to make the
enterprise look favourable.

On the regression discipline — the audit committee has
stronger tolerance for candid diagnosis than for evasive
explanation. A regression named clearly, diagnosed
specifically, and paired with a named corrective is what the
audit committee reads with confidence. A regression buried in
a footnote, or described as "an area of continuing focus", is
what the audit committee reads with distrust. The runbook
writes down the surfacing discipline, not because the head
enjoys reporting bad news, but because the discipline is what
carries the head's credibility across the years the news is
good.

The runbook is a living artefact. Version it, date it,
review it annually against the current year's cycle outcome,
update it when the CAE engagement depth changes or when the
peer set is revised, and treat runbook changes with the same
document-control discipline the head applies to the AIMS
operating calendar. A runbook that has not been touched in a
year is a runbook that no longer describes the discipline it
claims to describe.
