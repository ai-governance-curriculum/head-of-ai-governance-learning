**Estimated effort:** 14 hours

## What this module is for

The AI-governance program is legible to the CFO, the audit
committee, and the board through numbers. Not through
narrative, not through activity summaries, and not through the
head's own conviction that the program is well-run. The
numbers are KRIs and KPIs, maturity plots, ROI figures, and
cost lines. A program without them is a program that is
invisible in the enterprise's own governance forums; a program
with them but without discipline is a program whose numbers do
not survive challenge.

The failure mode the module opens with is the head who has
built a *program* (the running discipline in mod-102) and a
*posture* (the reporting shape in mod-103) but who has not yet
built the *measurement backbone* underneath either. Without the
backbone, the program's health depends on the head's presence
in the room; when the head is on holiday, absent, or replaced,
the program's health is not visible. The backbone is what
allows the program to be run by discipline rather than by the
head's personal attention, and to be defended by numbers rather
than by narrative.

This module answers six connected questions.

1. What KRIs and KPIs make up the head's portfolio at
   enterprise scope, and how are their target ranges calibrated
   to the enterprise's risk appetite so that a red metric
   actually means something.
2. How does the head author the enterprise's own AI-governance
   maturity model — informed by NIST AI RMF Playbook, ISO/IEC
   42001, IAPP AIGP body of knowledge, MLCommons AI Safety
   benchmarking, and ForHumanity IAAIS — with defensible tier
   definitions rather than off-the-shelf-borrowed language.
3. How does the head run the annual maturity assessment — four
   voices (self-assessment, second-line challenge, third-line
   audit input, external benchmarking against peer institutions
   in sector) — so that the plot feeds board reporting and
   multi-year investment planning defensibly.
4. How does the head build the ROI story to the CFO — avoided-
   loss, deal-enablement, trust-margin modelling — with the
   discipline that every claim carries a defensibility tier
   (anchored, modelled, illustrative) and every model is
   backtested against actuals.
5. How does the head run program-cost management — headcount,
   vendor spend, tooling depreciation, external-audit cost,
   training cost, certification cost, incident-response cost —
   with the finance business partner as the working interface,
   so the CFO's variance review is a working conversation
   rather than an adversarial one.
6. How does the head hold the difficult program-measurement
   conversation — telling the board a KPI is red, telling the
   CFO an ROI target was missed, defending a maturity tier that
   peer institutions have surpassed — without diminishing,
   deflecting, or re-baselining under fire.

The module produces the operating artefacts and rehearses the
discipline against the standing measurement surface as of the
research window.

## Learning objectives (from the plan)

- Design KRIs and KPIs for the AI-governance program at
  enterprise scope — control-testing pass rate, exception-
  waiver load, incident MTTR, board-report cycle time,
  regulator-response latency, third-party-vendor tier coverage,
  evidence-completeness rate, AIA turnaround time, maturity
  progression, cost-per-AI-system-governed — with target ranges
  calibrated to enterprise risk appetite.
- Author the enterprise AI-governance maturity model — informed
  by NIST AI RMF Playbook maturity dimensions, ISO/IEC 42001
  maturity dimensions, IAPP AIGP body-of-knowledge competence
  dimensions, MLCommons AI Safety benchmarking, ForHumanity
  IAAIS maturity criteria — with defensible tier definitions
  and an assessment cadence.
- Design the annual maturity assessment — self-assessment,
  second-line challenge, third-line audit input, external
  benchmarking against peer institutions in-sector — feeding
  board reporting and multi-year investment planning.
- Design the ROI reporting shape to CFO — avoided-loss
  modelling (regulator-fine avoidance, incident-cost avoidance,
  third-party-defence cost avoidance), deal-enablement
  modelling (trust-margin, regulator-approval-speed premium,
  tender-eligibility uplift), trust-margin modelling (customer-
  retention delta, employee-attraction delta) — with the
  discipline that ROI claims are defensible under scrutiny.
- Design the program-cost management discipline — headcount,
  vendor spend, tooling depreciation, external-audit cost,
  training cost, certification cost, incident-response cost —
  coordinated with the finance business partner.
- Rehearse the difficult program-measurement conversation —
  telling the board a KPI is red because the program is
  genuinely behind; telling the CFO an ROI target was missed
  because the incident-cost avoidance was lower than modelled;
  defending a maturity tier that peer institutions have
  surpassed.

## How to work through this module

The module is designed to be worked linearly. Chapter 01 opens
with the KRI/KPI portfolio because it is the load-bearing
running-cadence discipline underneath everything else; without
the portfolio, the maturity assessment has no telemetry, the
ROI pack has no source data, and the cost pack has no
performance context. Chapter 02 turns to the maturity model —
authored, not borrowed — because it is the model, not any
published reference, that the plot is scored against. Chapter
03 runs the assessment against the model, with the four-voice
discipline (self-assessment, second-line challenge, third-line
CAE, external benchmarking) that produces a plot the audit
committee ratifies. Chapter 04 turns to the ROI reporting
shape the CFO reads, with the three components (avoided-loss,
deal-enablement, trust-margin) and the defensibility hierarchy
(anchored, modelled, illustrative). Chapter 05 turns to the
cost side of the CFO conversation — the seven cost categories,
the finance business partner as the working interface, the
monthly and quarterly cadences. Chapter 06 closes with the
difficult conversation — the red KPI to the board, the missed
ROI target to the CFO, the peer overtake on a maturity tier.

Exercises follow the same order. Exercise 1 produces the
KRI/KPI portfolio with definition sheets. Exercise 2 produces
the enterprise's own maturity model with tier definitions.
Exercise 3 produces the annual maturity assessment runbook and
runs the first cycle end-to-end. Exercise 4 produces the ROI
pack with a backtest of a prior year's model. Exercise 5
produces the program-cost pack with the seven-category
discipline and the finance-business-partner interface.

By the end of the module, a working head-of-role has produced:

- A ten-metric KRI/KPI portfolio with a one-page metric-
  definition sheet per metric, RAG thresholds derived from the
  CRO's appetite framework, and named owners for definition,
  production, interpretation, escalation, and audit.
- An enterprise-authored AI-governance maturity model with
  five reference-anchored axes (NIST AI RMF Playbook, ISO/IEC
  42001, IAPP AIGP body of knowledge, MLCommons AI Safety,
  ForHumanity IAAIS), a common five-level scale, tier
  definitions per axis and per scoring cell, target-level-with-
  rationale annotations, and sector overlays as applicable.
- An annual maturity assessment runbook with the four-voice
  discipline, a Q4-anchored calendar, an output pack the audit
  committee reads, and a mid-year trajectory read.
- An ROI pack with the three components (avoided-loss, deal-
  enablement, trust-margin) and the defensibility-tier
  discipline (anchored, modelled, illustrative), backtested
  against the prior year, co-authored with the finance business
  partner.
- A program-cost pack with the seven cost categories
  (headcount, vendor spend, tooling depreciation, external-
  audit cost, training cost, certification cost, incident-
  response cost), a monthly cost-review cadence with the
  finance business partner, and a quarterly CFO-facing review.
- A rehearsed pack for the three difficult program-measurement
  conversations — the red KPI to the board, the missed ROI
  target to the CFO, the peer overtake on a maturity tier the
  enterprise had positioned as its strength.
