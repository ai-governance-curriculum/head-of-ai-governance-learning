# mod-110 — AI-Incident Response Leadership

**Estimated effort:** 14 hours
**Position on the track:** the tenth module — the discipline that
turns the cross-enterprise coordination pattern from
[[mod-109-cross-enterprise-leadership]] into live, time-pressured
execution when an AI-specific incident lands, and produces the
sector-scale confidence that carries into
[[mod-111-sector-program-leadership]] and the transparency
posture in [[mod-112-certifications-career-path-community-leadership]].

## What this module is for

The head of AI governance is not the operational incident
commander. That seat stays with the Chief Information Security
Officer, whose DFIR programme, SOC runbook, and enterprise
incident-bridge convention are the load-bearing infrastructure
every AI incident actually rides on. The head is the *substantive
incident commander for the AI dimension* — the seat on that
bridge who names the failure class, sets the AI-specific
containment posture, decides the Article 73 filing, owns the
US serious-incident disclosure map, sponsors the post-incident
learning cycle, and carries the difficult conversation with the
CEO, the board, the regulator, and the media. The operational
incident closes when containment holds; the head-shaped incident
closes only when the enterprise has learned from it.

The module is written against two failure modes. The first is the
**SOC-only response** — a technically competent DFIR operation
that contains the endpoint, preserves the logs, and never
surfaces the AI-substantive framing because no one on the bridge
has the level-60 shape to name a jailbreak from a training-data
leakage event from a silent model regression from an evaluator
corruption. The bridge produces a clean operational timeline and
a filing that is either technically wrong on the AI substance or
embarrassingly thin on it. The second is **positional collapse
at the top** — the CEO takes the audit-committee chair's phone
call, or the board-designated regulator question, with no head-
shaped view of what has actually happened on the AI side,
because the head was not incident-command-shaped inside the
response. Either edge produces the same downstream artefact and
the same reputational cost.

What the module produces is the working shape of the head's
incident seat: the incident-command shape at leadership scope,
the seven-surface coordination discipline that runs one bridge
with multiple pens, the EU AI Act Article 73 filing executed
under a running clock, the US serious-incident disclosure map
across the federal / state / sector patchwork, the sponsored (not
executed) post-incident learning cycle, the four rehearsed
difficult incident conversations, and the calibration discipline
that grounds the whole picture in public incident data and the
enterprise's own near-miss log.

## What this module answers

1. How does the head shape as substantive incident commander for
   the AI dimension while the CISO owns the operational IC seat —
   the eight AI-specific incident classes, the five substantive
   decisions, the single-bridge discipline, the presence pattern,
   the delegate pattern.
2. How does the head coordinate across the seven surfaces (SOC +
   DFIR, Communications, Legal, Regulator Relations, Government
   Affairs, Board, adjacent CxOs) under time pressure using pre-
   agreed contracts and the "one bridge, multiple pens"
   discipline.
3. How does the head own the EU AI Act Article 73 serious-
   incident filing at incident time — classification, multi-
   Member-State routing, filing content composition against the
   seven-question defence, filing-decision meeting shape, follow-
   up interaction posture, GPAI specifics for the EU AI Office.
4. How does the head own the US serious-incident disclosure map
   across the federal / state / sector patchwork (Colorado AI
   Act, NAIC bulletin, HHS OCR, FDA MDR, EEOC / DOL, CPPA / state
   AG, SEC 8-K Item 1.05, SR 11-7), and how does the head
   coordinate parallel filings when both EU and US obligations
   trigger.
5. How does the head sponsor (not execute) the post-incident
   learning cycle at program scope — RCA sponsorship, control-
   library and policy update commissioning, sector-share
   decision, audit-committee closure discipline.
6. How does the head rehearse the four difficult incident
   conversations (CEO, board on systemic failure, regulator on
   late filing, media with Communications), and what universal
   disciplines carry across all four.
7. How does the head calibrate incident readiness against public
   incident data (AIID, OECD.AI, MIT AI Risk Repository, ATLAS
   case studies, regulator enforcement actions) and against the
   enterprise's own near-miss log.

## Learning objectives

- Lead the enterprise AI-incident response — the head is the incident-command shape at leadership scope for AI-specific incidents (model failure, systemic bias event, prompt-injection compromise, model-extraction event, jailbreak-driven downstream harm, third-party model provider outage / withdrawal, dataset compromise, judge / evaluator corruption)
- Coordinate with SOC + DFIR (SOC runs the runbook; the head is on the incident bridge), Communications (public trust posture, media response), Legal (regulator-interpretation, disclosure obligations), Regulator Relations, Government Affairs, and the board (serious-incident notification)
- Own the EU AI Act Article 73 serious-incident filing at leadership scope — the senior-ai-governance-architect (level 50) designed the workflow; the head owns the filing decision, the filing content, and the follow-up regulator interaction; coordinate with the national CA per Member State and the EU AI Office where GPAI is in scope
- Own the equivalent US federal / state / sector serious-incident disclosure — where obligations exist (Colorado AI Act notification, NAIC bulletin notification, HHS OCR HIPAA breach notification where AI-triggered PHI exposure applies, FDA MDR filing for AI-enabled medical device malfunctions, EEOC / DOL notifications for AI-in-HR incidents, CPPA data-breach adjacencies)
- Lead the post-incident learning cycle at program scope — RCA sponsorship (`ai-risk-engineer` at level 25 does the RCA engineering; the head sponsors and reviews), control-library update commissioning (from `senior-ai-governance-architect` at level 50), policy update commissioning, sector-share of lessons where the enterprise chooses to share (Partnership on AI, Frontier Model Forum, MLCommons AI Safety, ISAC-equivalent for AI where sector membership exists)
- Rehearse the difficult incident conversation — briefing the CEO on a serious incident, briefing the board on a systemic failure, briefing a regulator on a filing that was late or incomplete, briefing the media in coordination with Communications
- Draw calibration lessons from the AI Incident Database, OECD.AI incidents monitor, and MIT AI Risk Repository — public incidents relevant to the enterprise scenario are the peer-benchmark for what the enterprise should be prepared to handle

## How to work through this module

The module is designed to be worked in incident-lifecycle order.
Chapter 01 establishes the *shape* — the head as substantive
incident commander for the AI dimension, the eight AI-specific
incident classes, the five substantive decisions the head owns,
the single-bridge discipline that keeps the head from running a
parallel AI call, the presence pattern that keeps the head on
the right bridge at the right severity, and the delegate pattern
that keeps the seat filled when the head cannot personally sit.
Chapter 02 opens the shape up across the *seven coordination
surfaces* — SOC + DFIR, Communications, Legal, Regulator
Relations, Government Affairs, Board, adjacent CxOs — and runs
each through the "one bridge, multiple pens" discipline so the
coordination pattern from
[[mod-109-cross-enterprise-leadership]] survives contact with
time pressure.

Chapters 03 and 04 are the *two filing regimes* the head executes
under a running clock. Chapter 03 takes the general Article 73
regime from [[mod-104-regulator-engagement]] chapter 02 and turns
it into the compressed, decision-forcing meeting shape the head
runs from awareness through classification through the seven-
question filing content through the post-filing follow-up posture,
including the multi-Member-State routing and the GPAI overlay
that puts the EU AI Office in the loop. Chapter 04 is the US
counterpart — the federal / state / sector patchwork the head
navigates in parallel, and the coordination discipline for
enterprises whose incident triggers filings on both sides of the
Atlantic simultaneously.

Chapter 05 is what happens *after containment* — the sponsored
post-incident learning cycle at program scope. The head does
not do the RCA (the AI Risk Engineer does) and does not commission
the control-library update (the Senior Architect does); the head
sponsors the cycle, reviews at depth, ratifies the artefact
updates, decides the sector-share posture, and closes the
incident on the audit-committee register with evidence rather
than with a declaration. Chapter 06 rehearses the four *difficult
conversations* the head owns end-to-end — briefing the CEO,
briefing the board on a systemic failure, briefing a regulator
on a late or incomplete filing, briefing the media in
coordination with Communications — and extracts the universal
disciplines that carry across all four. Chapter 07 closes the
module by grounding the whole picture in *public incident data* —
the AI Incident Database, the OECD.AI incidents monitor, the MIT
AI Risk Repository, the MITRE ATLAS case studies, and the
enforcement-action record — as the peer benchmark for what the
enterprise should be prepared to handle, cross-checked against
the enterprise's own near-miss log.

Exercises consolidate the chapters into working incident-response
artefacts. Exercise 01 produces the incident-command shape and
the delegate pattern. Exercise 02 produces the cross-function
coordination runbook against the seven surfaces. Exercise 03 is
the paired filing drill — Article 73 and the US disclosure map
executed under a compressed clock. Exercise 04 produces the
post-incident learning-cycle sponsorship record with the five
artefacts. Exercise 05 rehearses the four difficult
conversations against a scenario the head-of-role picks from the
enterprise's actual risk register.

The module extends the coordination pattern from
[[mod-109-cross-enterprise-leadership]] (the CISO, GC, CCO, and
adjacent-CxO interfaces become live under time pressure rather
than exchanged in peace-time cadence), the audit-committee
incident-and-near-miss cluster from
[[mod-103-board-and-audit-committee-reporting]] (which now
carries live filings and closure evidence), the general Article
73 regime from [[mod-104-regulator-engagement]] (which now gets
executed rather than described), and the voluntary-engagement
portfolio from
[[mod-106-external-positioning-and-voluntary-codes]] (with which
the chapter-05 sector-share decision composes). The module feeds
[[mod-111-sector-program-leadership]] (where the sector-specific
incident-response overlays — MDR-in-healthcare, model-risk-in-
banking, and their peers — attach to the shape authored here)
and [[mod-112-certifications-career-path-community-leadership]]
(where the transparency posture on public incident-database
participation and sector-share cadence is set as a standing
external-facing commitment).
