# exercise-05: Difficult Incident Conversation Rehearsal

**Estimated effort:** 3 hours

## Objective

Author the **rehearsal artefact set** that lets the head of AI
governance walk into any of the four difficult incident
conversations from chapter [[06-difficult-incident-conversations]]
— briefing the CEO on a serious incident, briefing the full
board on a systemic failure, briefing a regulator on a late or
incomplete filing, briefing the media in coordination with
Communications — and hold substance under time pressure, without
notes, without drafting on the fly, and without leaking the
uncertainty into the room. The artefact set is calibrated
against real public incident cases drawn from the three primary
sources named in chapter [[07-calibration-from-public-incident-data]]
(the AI Incident Database, the OECD.AI incidents monitor, the
MIT AI Risk Repository) so the counterpart cast is not
role-playing a caricature and the rehearsal is not sparring
against a straw regulator.

The artefact set has to survive three stress tests. First, the
head's delegate can pick up the rehearsal script cold, run the
rehearsal with the named counterpart-play, and produce a usable
critique — a rehearsal script that requires the head to explain
the intent is a rehearsal script that will not be used when the
head is unavailable. Second, the counterpart cast is calibrated
against public regulator, board, and media behaviour observable
in the primary sources — a rehearsal where the counterpart never
resists, never asks the awkward question, and never sits in
silence is not a rehearsal, it is a talking-points read-through.
Third, the annual rehearsal cadence is executable against the
head's diary and the delegate's diary — a cadence that reads
well in a document but never enters the calendar has no operational
value.

Why it matters: these four conversations happen under time
pressure, in a room the head does not control, in front of an
audience with its own agenda, and the head does not get to
draft as they go. The three universal disciplines from chapter
06 — own substance not delivery; do not fill silence; name what
you do not know — are habits, not talking points, and habits
are built by rehearsal against calibrated resistance. The
artefact set exists so the rehearsal is repeatable, so the
delegate can rehearse too, and so the head can be replaced —
by the delegate, by a successor, by the interim head during
handover — without the difficult-conversation capability walking
out of the enterprise with the incumbent.

## Prerequisites

- Chapter [[06-difficult-incident-conversations]] read and
  internalised: the four conversation shapes, the three
  universal disciplines (own substance not delivery, do not fill
  silence, name what you do not know), the specific counterpart
  postures the chapter names for each conversation.
- Chapter [[07-calibration-from-public-incident-data]] read and
  internalised: the three primary public incident sources (AI
  Incident Database, OECD.AI incidents monitor, MIT AI Risk
  Repository), the calibration discipline for reading regulator
  enforcement tone, board public statements, and media briefings
  that succeeded or failed publicly.
- [[mod-103-board-and-audit-committee-reporting]] chapter 04 —
  the decision-meeting shape the board expects when the head
  is briefing on a systemic failure (packet in advance, decision
  on the table, discipline in the room).
- [[mod-104-regulator-engagement]] chapters 06 and 07 — the
  engagement discipline the head brings to the regulator
  conversation and the over-explaining anti-pattern the head
  has to avoid when the filing was late or incomplete.
- [[mod-108-leading-the-governance-function]] — the
  difficult-conversation discipline the head practises with the
  team (naming underperformance, holding the promotion no, the
  hard coaching moment); the same three universal disciplines
  transfer.
- [[mod-106-external-positioning-and-voluntary-codes]] — the
  CCO / Communications relationship and the head's posture when
  the head is briefing the on-record spokesperson rather than
  speaking on record.
- Access to at least the three primary public incident sources:
  AI Incident Database (`incidentdatabase.ai`), OECD.AI
  incidents monitor (`oecd.ai/en/incidents`), MIT AI Risk
  Repository (`airisk.mit.edu`), plus regulator press-release
  pages relevant to the enterprise's sector and jurisdiction.

## Steps

1. **Scenario shortlist.** Pick THREE public incident cases from
   the primary sources — at minimum one from the AI Incident
   Database, one from the OECD.AI monitor, and one from a
   regulator press release (national CA under the EU AI Act, FTC,
   ICO, CNIL, the sector regulator for the enterprise, or
   equivalent). For each case, capture: the case title as the
   source names it; the source URL (do not invent — if the exact
   URL is uncertain, mark it `<!-- needs-research: verify [case
   name] against [source URL] -->` rather than guess); the AIID
   / OECD.AI incident identifier where the source assigns one;
   the incident class per the chapter 01 taxonomy from
   [[01-incident-command-shape-at-leadership-scope]]; and which
   of the four rehearsed conversations the case speaks to
   (a case has to speak to at least one). The shortlist is the
   raw material every subsequent artefact draws from.

2. **Conversation 1 rehearsal artefact — briefing the CEO on a
   serious incident.** Author the rehearsal script with the six
   required shape elements:
   - **Purpose statement.** One sentence naming why the CEO is
     being briefed and what the CEO is expected to decide or
     absorb (a decision on external notification timing; a
     decision on customer-communications posture; absorption
     of the fact that a serious-incident filing is imminent).
   - **Participants list.** Named roles in the room (CEO, head,
     GC, CCO, CISO if in scope), named roles on the bridge
     (SOC lead, incident commander).
   - **Opening frame.** The head's first two minutes: what the
     incident is, what class per chapter 01, what the current
     containment posture is, what the CEO is being asked for.
   - **The 3 to 5 substantive CEO questions with the head's
     answering discipline.** Anticipated CEO questions ("are our
     customers safe right now"; "who else knows"; "what does
     this cost us"; "why did we not catch this"; "when do we
     tell the regulator") — for each, the head's answering
     discipline (substance the head owns, what the head defers
     to whom, the specific "I do not know yet, the answer will
     be available at [time] from [source]" pattern where
     applicable).
   - **What the head does not do.** Explicit anti-patterns for
     this specific conversation — no filling silence, no
     over-explaining the technical taxonomy, no volunteering a
     forward commitment the RCA has not yet supported, no
     public-facing language the CCO has not seen.
   - **Closing frame and hand-off.** The head's last two
     minutes: what has been decided, what the next update is,
     who owns what between now and then.
   - **Cast for the counterpart-play.** Name the role that
     plays the CEO in the rehearsal (a peer head-of-role from
     another enterprise on a reciprocal basis, a former CEO
     acting as advisor, an external executive coach with CEO
     experience). The cast is named by role, not by
     individual, in the artefact.

3. **Conversation 2 rehearsal artefact — briefing the full board
   on a systemic failure.** Author the rehearsal script with the
   same six shape elements as conversation 1, adapted for the
   board setting per [[mod-103-board-and-audit-committee-reporting]]
   chapter 04:
   - Purpose statement (typically: the board absorbs that a
     class of failure is systemic rather than one-off, and
     endorses the remediation programme).
   - Participants (board chair, non-executive directors, audit-
     committee chair, CEO, GC, CFO where financial exposure
     is in scope, head).
   - Opening frame (what is systemic about it; what the
     evidence for the systemic claim is; what the remediation
     programme is; what the board is being asked for).
   - 3 to 5 anticipated substantive board questions ("did we
     know this class of risk existed before this incident";
     "what is our exposure now"; "what did the second and
     third lines see"; "why did the audit committee not see
     this earlier"; "when do we know it is fixed") with the
     head's answering discipline for each.
   - What the head does not do (no defensive posture, no
     blaming the second line, no rescuing the CEO on questions
     the CEO owns, no promising a completion date the
     remediation programme does not support).
   - Closing frame (what has been endorsed, what the next
     board touchpoint is, what the audit committee sees in
     between).
   - **Cast for the counterpart-play.** A former board chair
     acting as advisor, a governance-professional-body-
     nominated non-executive director (e.g., an ICSA /
     Chartered Governance Institute or IoD member willing to
     counterpart-play on a professional basis), a specialist
     board coach.

4. **Conversation 3 rehearsal artefact — briefing a regulator on
   a late or incomplete filing.** Author the rehearsal script
   with the six shape elements, adapted for the regulator
   setting per [[mod-104-regulator-engagement]] chapters 06 and
   07:
   - Purpose statement (typically: the head briefs the
     regulator on why the filing was late or incomplete, what
     the corrected filing timeline is, what the process
     remediation is, and absorbs the regulator's response —
     which may be an enforcement signal).
   - Participants (regulator's assigned case officer, the
     head, GC or external counsel where the regulator's
     posture warrants it, the head's regulator-engagement
     lead).
   - Opening frame (what the filing was, why it was late or
     incomplete, what has been corrected, what the process
     remediation is — in that order, no throat-clearing).
   - 3 to 5 anticipated regulator questions ("when did you
     first know this was a serious incident under Article 73
     / equivalent"; "why did the internal escalation not
     surface it to the filing decision earlier"; "who signed
     off on the initial filing"; "what has changed in your
     process"; "how do we know this will not happen again")
     with the head's answering discipline. The rehearsal
     script names explicitly the **over-explaining
     anti-pattern** the head has to avoid — the pattern where
     a defensive head fills regulator silence with context
     the regulator did not ask for and thereby volunteers
     material the regulator will treat as an admission (see
     [[mod-104-regulator-engagement]] chapter 07 and the
     related over-explaining discipline in
     [[mod-109-cross-enterprise-leadership]] chapter 06 on
     the head's cross-functional-forum posture).
   - What the head does not do (no volunteering, no
     apologising in a form the enforcement lawyer can quote,
     no promising a control the enterprise has not built,
     no speculating on other enterprises' filings).
   - Closing frame (what the corrected filing timeline is,
     what the next regulator touchpoint is, what the head
     will send in writing to follow up).
   - **Cast for the counterpart-play.** A former regulator
     from the relevant CA / FTC / ICO / sector regulator now
     in advisory practice, a specialist regulatory-affairs
     professional, external counsel with regulator-side
     experience. The counterpart-play uses the tone and
     silence discipline of the specific regulator, calibrated
     against the enforcement actions cited in step 1.

5. **Conversation 4 rehearsal artefact — briefing the media in
   coordination with Communications.** Author the rehearsal
   script with the six shape elements, adapted for the media
   setting per [[mod-106-external-positioning-and-voluntary-codes]]:
   - Purpose statement (typically: the head briefs the
     on-record spokesperson — usually the CEO or CCO — with
     the substantive content the spokesperson will convert
     into public statements, and coaches the spokesperson on
     what the head is willing to have said on the head's
     behalf).
   - Participants (CCO / head of Communications, on-record
     spokesperson, incident-communications lead, GC on
     disclosure adjacency, head).
   - Opening frame (what the incident is in public-facing
     language; what the enterprise is prepared to say; what
     the enterprise is not saying yet and why; what the
     first-24-hour posture is).
   - 3 to 5 anticipated Communications-lead or spokesperson
     questions ("what can we say about affected customer
     numbers"; "what can we say about root cause";
     "what can we say about who owns this internally"; "what
     do we say when a journalist asks about the regulator
     filing"; "what do we say when a journalist asks the head
     directly") with the head's answering discipline. Include
     explicitly the **two sub-cases**:
     - The common case where the head is briefing the on-
       record spokesperson and is not speaking on record.
     - The rare case where the head is publicly named — by
       a journalist, in a regulator's published notice, by
       an activist campaign — and is asked to speak on
       record. The rehearsal covers what the head says,
       what the head defers to the CCO / CEO, and the
       coordination discipline with Communications for the
       on-record moment.
   - What the head does not do (no going off Communications-
     approved language on any technical claim, no adopting
     the language of a specific journalist's framing, no
     answering the "what would you do differently"
     hypothetical in the moment).
   - Closing frame (what the spokesperson takes into the
     briefing, what the head is available for, when the next
     Communications-and-head sync is).
   - **Cast for the counterpart-play.** A media-training
     professional, a former journalist now in advisory
     practice, the CCO's incident-communications lead
     acting as counterpart-play in a rehearsed setting.

6. **Rehearsal replay note template.** Author the template — one
   template used for each of the four conversations post-
   rehearsal — the head and the delegate fill in after every
   rehearsal cycle. The template captures: what went well
   (specific moment, specific discipline held); what to change
   (specific moment, specific discipline dropped, specific
   corrective drill); what the delegate would do differently
   (the delegate's independent read, so the delegate's own
   rehearsal cycle draws on it); the three-universal-disciplines
   ratings for that specific rehearsal (see step 8); the date
   of the next rehearsal cycle.

7. **Calibration table.** Author the table that draws from at
   least the three public incident cases in step 1 (and any
   additional cases the head has drawn on) and calibrates the
   four rehearsal artefacts against public behaviour. Columns:
   - Incident source and reference (source name, incident
     identifier where assigned, URL — cited exactly, no
     invention).
   - Incident class per the chapter 01 taxonomy from
     [[01-incident-command-shape-at-leadership-scope]].
   - Which of the four conversations the case calibrates (1,
     2, 3, or 4 — a case may calibrate more than one).
   - The specific behavioural signal the case provides — for a
     regulator case, the regulator's tone in the enforcement
     action and the language the regulator quoted from the
     enterprise's own filing; for a board case, the board's
     public statement on the systemic failure; for a media
     case, a media briefing that went well or badly and what
     the enterprise's spokesperson did or said that made it
     so. The signal is specific and citable, not "this case
     was handled badly".
   - The rehearsal-artefact update the signal drives (a
     specific question added to the anticipated question set,
     a specific anti-pattern added to the what-the-head-does-
     not-do list, a specific counterpart-play tone
     calibration).
   Where the head cannot find a real public case for a given
   conversation, the row says so explicitly with the
   `<!-- needs-research: ... -->` marker; inventing a case is
   an exercise failure.

8. **Three-universal-disciplines self-assessment.** Author the
   written self-assessment against the three disciplines from
   chapter 06 — **own substance not delivery**, **do not fill
   silence**, **name what you do not know**. For each discipline:
   - A one-paragraph diagnostic — where the head has held the
     discipline recently under real pressure, where the head
     has dropped it, and the specific tell that signals the
     drop (rushing the closing frame; the "one more sentence"
     habit after the substantive answer; the confident
     answer to a question the head has not thought about).
   - A rating on a 1-to-3 scale (1 = habitual gap the head
     drops under pressure; 2 = held in low-stakes rooms,
     drops in high-stakes rooms; 3 = internalised across
     conditions).
   - A specific drill that addresses the gap where the rating
     is 1 or 2 (a silence-holding drill with the coach; a
     "name what you do not know" phrasing drill; a substance-
     versus-delivery review of a rehearsal recording).
   The self-assessment is diagnostic, not aspirational; the
   head names the gap by name.

9. **Annual rehearsal cadence plan.** Author the cadence plan
   naming:
   - Which conversations are rehearsed quarterly — typically
     conversations 1 (CEO) and 3 (regulator), because the
     stimulus for these is frequent enough that the head
     needs a fresh rehearsal in muscle memory.
   - Which conversations are rehearsed annually — typically
     conversations 2 (board systemic-failure) and 4 (media),
     because the stimulus is rarer but the consequence of
     underrehearsal is catastrophic.
   - The named counterpart-plays for each cadence slot and
     the professional-body / advisory / coach relationship
     that provides them.
   - How the delegate is included — the delegate observes at
     minimum, runs at least one rehearsal per year on the
     delegate's own account, and takes the replay note.
   - How the cadence composes with the delegate rehearsal
     cadence from chapter
     [[01-incident-command-shape-at-leadership-scope]] — the
     conversation rehearsal and the incident-command rehearsal
     alternate rather than compete for calendar time.
   The cadence is a diary commitment (dates in the head's and
   the delegate's calendars for the next four quarters), not
   a document.

10. **Distribution and confidentiality note.** Author the
    one-page note governing the artefact set. The set is
    confidential to the head, the delegate, and the named
    coach or counterpart-play cast. Distribution list: named
    by role (head, delegate, board coach where retained,
    executive coach where retained). Storage: where the set
    is kept (the head's function's secure store, not the
    laptop), access control, and versioning. Refresh cycle:
    quarterly review of the artefact set for changes in
    counterpart cast, changes in public incident calibration,
    changes in the enterprise's own incident history that
    should feed back into the rehearsal.

## Acceptance criteria

- Each of the four rehearsal artefacts has all six shape
  elements: purpose statement, participants list, opening frame,
  3 to 5 substantive counterpart questions with the head's
  answering discipline, an explicit "what the head does not do"
  section, closing frame and hand-off. A rehearsal artefact
  missing any of the six fails the criterion.
- Each of the four rehearsal artefacts names the cast for the
  counterpart-play by role (a specific individual is not
  required; the role — former regulator, board coach, media-
  training professional — is).
- The calibration table cites real public sources by URL against
  each row. An invented case, a paraphrased case with no source
  citation, or a source URL the reader cannot resolve is an
  exercise failure. Uncertain cases carry the
  `<!-- needs-research: verify [case] against [source URL] -->`
  marker rather than an inventive fill-in.
- The three-universal-disciplines self-assessment covers all
  three disciplines, gives each a 1-to-3 rating, and names a
  specific drill for each rating of 1 or 2. An aspirational
  self-assessment ("the head aims to hold silence better") that
  does not name the specific gap fails the criterion.
- The annual rehearsal cadence plan names which conversations
  are rehearsed quarterly, which are rehearsed annually,
  includes the delegate in at least one rehearsal cycle per
  year, and composes with the delegate cadence from
  [[01-incident-command-shape-at-leadership-scope]]. A cadence
  written as a policy without diary dates fails the criterion.
- The distribution and confidentiality note names storage,
  access control, and refresh cycle. An artefact set with no
  named refresh cycle fails the criterion.
- Standing needs-research criterion: any case cited anywhere in
  the artefact set has to link to a real source; where the
  source is uncertain, the artefact carries
  `<!-- needs-research: verify [case] against [source URL] -->`
  rather than invent. Invented cases fail the exercise.

## Stretch goals

- Extend conversation 2 with the specific sub-scenario where the
  board asks **"did we know this class of risk existed before
  this incident"** — the systemic-failure conversation where the
  answer is yes-with-qualification and the head has to hold the
  substance without collapsing into either false confidence or
  false humility. Rehearse against a board-coach counterpart
  calibrated against a real public case where the board's
  after-the-fact statement acknowledged prior awareness.
- Extend conversation 3 with the specific sub-scenario where the
  regulator has been handed a **competitor's late filing at the
  same time** and the two conversations are being compared side-
  by-side by the case officer. The rehearsal covers how the
  head holds the substance of the enterprise's own filing
  without speculating on the competitor, without being drawn
  into a comparative posture the regulator has invited, and
  without a defensive "we are not them" register.
- Author the **rehearsal-video review protocol** — the head
  watches the rehearsal recording (with the coach's consent)
  and calibrates the head's own on-camera posture, phrasing,
  and silence discipline against the calibration table. The
  protocol names the specific review checklist (substance-
  versus-delivery time ratio; longest held silence; number of
  times the head volunteered content unasked; number of
  "I do not know" statements against total questions) and the
  review cadence.
- Extend the media conversation to cover the **parliamentary-
  open-session sub-case** where a legislator (a select
  committee, a state assembly hearing, an EU Parliament ITRE
  or IMCO session) asks the head to speak on record in a
  public forum. The rehearsal covers the specific discipline
  differences from a media briefing — the record is legislative
  not journalistic, the audience is voters not readers, the
  counterpart is elected not commercial — and the coordination
  with Government Affairs.
- Author the **delegate's own rehearsal artefact set** — a
  delegate-shaped version of the artefact set (not a copy). The
  delegate is not a head, does not have the head's convening
  authority, and rehearses different conversations (typically
  briefing the head, briefing the SOC leadership, briefing the
  incident-communications lead, briefing the regulator-
  engagement lead) with different counterpart-plays and
  different disciplines. The artefact set names how the
  delegate's rehearsal composes with the head's rehearsal so
  the delegate is genuinely rehearsed for stand-in duty, not
  just informed of the head's rehearsal.

## Notes and starter guidance

The most common first-cut failure on this exercise is the
rehearsal script that reads as **Communications-approved
talking points** rather than as the head's substantive
positions. Talking points survive the CEO's first substantive
follow-up for about eight seconds. The remedy is to draft each
anticipated question against a substantive position the head
would hold under cross-examination — a position rooted in the
AIMS evidence, the RCA-in-progress, and the head's own
governance judgement — and to draft the "what the head does not
do" section as strictly as the substantive-answer section. The
rehearsal script is the head's, not the CCO's.

The second common failure is the **calibration table populated
with invented or paraphrased public cases**. The failure mode
is the well-meaning "there was a case a few years ago where
the board said…" without a citation. If the head cannot find
the real case for a given cell in the table, the cell says so
with the `<!-- needs-research: ... -->` marker; a rehearsal
artefact calibrated against invented behaviour is worse than a
rehearsal artefact calibrated against nothing, because it
trains the head against a caricature. Where the primary
sources genuinely do not have a case for a given cell — the
media briefing for the specific class of AI incident may
genuinely not have a public exemplar yet — the artefact set is
transparent about that and the counterpart-play draws on
adjacent domains (data-breach media briefings, safety-incident
board statements) with the adjacency called out.

The third common failure is the **cast that is peer-heads-of-
role only**. A rehearsal where the CEO counterpart is another
head of AI governance is a rehearsal against someone who thinks
like the head and who will not ask the CEO's actual question. A
rehearsal where the regulator counterpart is another head of AI
governance is a rehearsal against someone who has never sat on
the regulator's side of the table. Force the cast to include at
least one counterpart with genuine counterpart-role experience
(former CEO, former regulator, former board chair, former
journalist, media-training professional) — the resistance
matters, and the resistance is the point of the exercise.

The fourth common failure is the **annual rehearsal cadence
written as a document rather than as a diary commitment**. A
plan that names "quarterly rehearsal with the executive coach"
without dates in the head's and the delegate's calendars is a
plan that will not happen — the first quarter's incident load,
board cycle, or regulator engagement will absorb the time. The
cadence goes into the diary at the moment of authoring; the
document records what the diary already contains. The self-
assessment is diagnostic, not aspirational; the head names the
gap the head actually has, not the gap the head would prefer
to have.
