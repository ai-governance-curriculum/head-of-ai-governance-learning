# mod-103 — Board, Audit Committee, and Risk Committee AI Reporting

**Estimated effort:** 16 hours
**Position on the track:** third module — the reporting-discipline
module that carries the outputs of the running program (mod-102) up
to the governing bodies.

## What this module is for

Chapter 01 of mod-101 named the head of AI governance as a level-60
enterprise leader whose accountability includes making the AI
program legible to the board and its committees. mod-102 built the
running program — the AIMS operating calendar, the risk register at
portfolio cadence, the third-party audit interface, the AI system
inventory, the supply-chain evidence discipline. This module is how
those running outputs are *shaped for governing-body consumption*.

The module answers five overlapping reporting disciplines and one
rehearsal:

1. Designing the quarterly board-facing AI-risk pack — heat maps,
   KRI dashboards, incident summary, portfolio appetite tracking,
   regulator-engagement summary, third-party status — using the
   IoD's AI-governance guidance for boards and the NACD's AI
   Oversight publications as reference shapes.
2. Designing the audit committee's AI-risk deep-dive — first-line
   control effectiveness, second-line coverage, third-line audit
   findings and remediation, external audit and certification-body
   findings — aligned to the IIA Three Lines Model and the IIA
   professional practices framework.
3. Designing the risk-committee (or technology-committee where
   risk-committee scope does not include AI) AI-risk pack —
   portfolio appetite calibration, capability-tier posture,
   stop-shipping triggers — layered onto COSO ERM and ISO 31000
   as parent frameworks, with ISO/IEC 23894 as the AI overlay.
4. Coordinating content ownership and sign-off across the CxO peer
   group (CRO, CAE, GC, CISO, CPO/DPO, CTO) so the pack is jointly
   authored and signed off before it lands in front of a
   committee.
5. Running the reporting pipeline as a scheduled operational
   cadence — what evidence is produced by whom by when in what
   form — so pack production is composition of assembled evidence
   rather than heroic effort under time pressure.
6. Rehearsing the difficult-conversation pattern — telling the
   board about an appetite breach, a deteriorating regulator
   engagement, an Article 73 (or equivalent) serious-incident
   investigation, a stop-shipping decision — with defensible facts
   and clear next-step ownership.

The module closes with a chapter reading the frontier-lab tiered
risk frameworks (Anthropic RSP, OpenAI Preparedness Framework,
Google DeepMind FSF) as shape lessons for where enterprise
governing-body expectations are heading.

## Learning objectives (from the plan)

- Design the board-facing AI-risk report shape — quarterly cadence,
  heat maps, KRI dashboards, incident-summary decks, portfolio
  risk-appetite tracking, regulator-engagement summary, third-party-
  vendor status — using IoD AI-governance guidance for boards and
  NACD AI Oversight Blueprint as reference shapes.
- Design the audit-committee AI-risk deep-dive shape — first-line
  effectiveness, second-line coverage, third-line audit findings
  and remediation status, external audit findings — aligned to
  IIA Three Lines and IIA CAE guidance.
- Design the risk-committee (or technology-committee where the
  risk-committee scope does not include AI) AI-risk shape —
  portfolio appetite calibration decisions, capability-tier
  escalations, stop-shipping triggers — layered onto COSO ERM and
  ISO 31000 parents.
- Coordinate with the Chief Risk Officer (parent risk framework),
  Chief Audit Executive (third-line audit findings), General
  Counsel (regulator engagement summary), and CISO (AI-specific
  security signal) on report content ownership before circulation.
- Author the reporting-pipeline discipline — what evidence is
  collected from whom by when, who reviews, who signs, who
  circulates — so the report is not a last-minute scramble each
  cycle.
- Rehearse the difficult-conversation pattern — telling the board
  that the appetite has been breached, that a regulator engagement
  is deteriorating, that a serious incident is under Article 73
  investigation, that a stop-shipping decision has been made — with
  defensible facts and clear next-step ownership.
- Draw shape lessons from frontier-lab tiered risk frameworks
  (Anthropic RSP, OpenAI Preparedness, Google DeepMind FSF) —
  they are what a frontier-lab board sees; enterprise boards are
  heading toward comparable shape.

## How to work through this module

The module is designed to be worked in order. Chapters 01, 02, and
03 are the three governing-body pack shapes and can be read
in parallel once the shared vocabulary of chapter 01 is
internalised. Chapter 04 is the CxO sign-off discipline that all
three packs run through. Chapter 05 is the pipeline that produces
the evidence chapters 01-03 consume and chapter 04 signs off on.
Chapter 06 is the rehearsal chapter — the pattern the head brings
to the room when the pack surfaces a difficult conversation.
Chapter 07 reads the frontier-lab tiered frameworks as shape
lessons for the direction enterprise governing-body expectations
are heading.

Exercises follow the same order. Exercises 1, 2, and 3 produce the
three pack shapes for a specific enterprise. Exercise 4 produces
the pipeline and sign-off flow that carries all three. Exercise 5
rehearses the difficult conversation against one of the three
packs. Exercise 4 depends on exercises 1-3 being at least in
draft; exercise 5 depends on any one of the packs being drafted
enough to expose a scenario.

By the end of the module, a working head-of-role has produced:

- A quarterly board-pack template with the seven content clusters,
  a worked one-quarter example, and a comparability discipline for
  the archive.
- An audit-committee AI deep-dive pack template with the five
  clusters and the third-line-independence discipline in force.
- A risk-committee (or technology-committee) AI pack template with
  the five clusters, the appetite framework in force, and the
  capability-tier framework layered onto it.
- A CxO sign-off flow with named owners, timeline, and
  conflict-escalation discipline.
- A reporting-pipeline document naming every evidence input,
  producer, cadence, retention path, and quality bar.
- A rehearsed difficult-conversation script for one specific
  scenario, sufficient to run in a mock-committee setting.

These artefacts are the substrate for [[mod-104-regulator-engagement]]
(regulator engagement builds on the pack's regulator-engagement
cluster), [[mod-107-program-measurement-and-maturity]] (maturity
reporting lands in cluster 6 of the board pack), and
[[mod-110-incident-leadership]] (incident reporting into cluster 3
of the board pack and the addendum discipline in chapter 05).
