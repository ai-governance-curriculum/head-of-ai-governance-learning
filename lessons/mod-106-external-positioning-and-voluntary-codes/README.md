# mod-106 — External Positioning, Voluntary Codes, and Standards-Body Leadership

**Estimated effort:** 14 hours
**Position on the track:** the sixth module — the discipline
that takes the running program built in
[[mod-102-running-the-program]], the reporting posture built in
[[mod-103-board-and-audit-committee-reporting]], the regulator
engagements from [[mod-104-regulator-engagement]], and the
regulatory-change work from
[[mod-105-regulatory-change-management]], and extends them out
to the *voluntary* surface — the codes the enterprise signs, the
standards bodies the enterprise sits on, the public trust
posture the enterprise carries, the inbound requests the
enterprise responds to, and the shape lessons the enterprise
draws from the frontier labs.

## What this module is for

Regulator engagement (mod-104) is what the enterprise has to do.
External positioning is what the enterprise chooses to do. The
choice is not incidental: voluntary-code sign-ons, standards-body
seats, and public-trust postures are the durable artefacts the
enterprise builds its industry standing on, and the discipline
by which those artefacts are chosen determines whether they
build standing or leak credibility.

The failure mode the module opens with is not the enterprise
being absent from the voluntary surface. It is the enterprise
being *present without discipline* — signing codes without a
process, occupying standards-body seats without contribution,
speaking in public without substance, responding to inbound
requests as they arrive rather than as an integrated intake,
and imitating frontier-lab artefacts without extracting the
transferable shape. All of those are recoverable; none of them
is recoverable without pausing to build the disciplines this
module builds.

This module answers six connected questions:

1. How does the head decide, code by code, whether the
   enterprise signs on to a voluntary code — with the CEO / GC /
   CRO / CFO sign-off flow explicit, and with the register that
   answers "what codes has the enterprise considered" months and
   years later.
2. How does the head shape the enterprise's standards-body
   participation portfolio — at what depth on the engagement
   ladder per body, with what representative pattern (head
   personally, level-50 architect, contributor, industry-body
   proxy), and with what feedback loop into the enterprise's own
   operating disciplines.
3. How does the head build a public trust posture — public
   speaking, position papers, industry-body board seats, media
   strategy, and the enterprise's own AI-governance publishing
   channel — with substance owned by the head and delivery
   owned by Communications.
4. How does the head route inbound RFIs and RFPs — from
   regulators, from industry bodies, from academic and civil-
   society researchers, and from customers' procurement
   processes — with a five-category intake taxonomy and an
   internal drafting cycle that produces coherent responses.
5. How does the head make the harder decisions — declining a
   voluntary code, withdrawing from a code the enterprise had
   signed, and publicly disagreeing with a regulator's
   methodology — with the discipline that names the position
   substantively, frames the disagreement without adversarial
   tone, preserves the ordinary engagement surface, and
   coordinates internally before disclosing externally.
6. What shape lessons does the head extract from frontier-lab
   external positioning — Anthropic's Responsible Scaling
   Policy, OpenAI's Preparedness Framework, Google DeepMind's
   Frontier Safety Framework — that transfer to the enterprise's
   own scale without direct transplantation.

The module produces the operating artefacts and rehearses the
discipline against the specific voluntary surface as of the
research window.

## Learning objectives (from the plan)

- Design the internal decision process for signing on to a
  voluntary code — EU AI Office GPAI Code of Practice, G7
  Hiroshima Process Code of Conduct, White House Voluntary AI
  Commitments where applicable, Frontier Model Forum charter
  where frontier-lab scope applies, Partnership on AI
  membership, MLCommons AI Safety Working Group participation,
  US AISI Consortium (AISIC) membership — with the CEO / GC /
  CRO / CFO sign-off flow explicit.
- Design the standards-body participation posture at leadership
  scope — ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 42, CEN-CENELEC JTC 21, IEEE 7000-
  series, NIST public workshops (AI RMF, AISI publications,
  AISIC working groups), OECD.AI Expert Group; whether the head
  personally chairs a working group or the senior architect
  represents in technical body sessions.
- Design the public trust posture — public speaking, position
  papers, industry-body board seats, media strategy in
  coordination with Communications (this role owns the AI-
  governance substance; Communications owns the delivery).
- Design the enterprise's RFI / RFP response posture —
  regulator RFIs (CPPA, EU AI Office, NIST, US AISI), industry-
  body RFIs (Frontier Model Forum, Partnership on AI,
  MLCommons), academic RFIs — with the internal drafting cycle.
- Rehearse the difficult external decision — declining to sign
  a voluntary code and defending the position; withdrawing from
  a code and defending the position; publicly disagreeing with
  a regulator's methodology and framing the disagreement without
  burning the relationship.
- Draw shape lessons from frontier-lab external positioning
  (Anthropic RSP publication cadence, OpenAI Preparedness
  Framework updates, Google DeepMind FSF publication cadence) —
  the enterprise equivalent is emerging.

## How to work through this module

The module is designed to be worked linearly. Chapter 01 opens
with the sign-on decision because it is the load-bearing
external decision the head faces and because the decision
process — attachment analysis, cost estimate, appetite check,
legal exposure read, public-positioning read, head
recommendation, CxO sign-off — reappears in adapted form across
chapters 02, 03, 04, and 05. Chapter 02 turns the sign-on
discipline to standards-body participation, which has different
mechanics (procedural, sustained, technical) than the code-
signing decision but the same portfolio ownership pattern.
Chapter 03 turns to the public trust posture the sign-ons and
seats feed into, with the substance-versus-delivery boundary
between head and Communications as the load-bearing
distinction. Chapter 04 turns to the request-shaped inbound —
RFIs and RFPs — that runs adjacent to the outbound postures of
chapters 01 through 03. Chapter 05 rehearses the difficult
decisions where the ordinary disciplines are stress-tested.
Chapter 06 closes with the frontier-lab shape lessons that
inform where the enterprise-equivalent artefact is heading.

Exercises follow the same order. Exercise 1 produces the
voluntary-code sign-on runbook and the register that carries
the portfolio. Exercise 2 produces the standards-body
participation portfolio and the decision matrix behind it.
Exercise 3 produces the annual public-trust plan and the head-
and-CCO joint operating agreement. Exercise 4 produces the
RFI / RFP intake register, the answer library for customer
RFPs, and the head-and-CRO joint operating agreement. Exercise
5 is the capstone drill — a rehearsal of one decline decision,
one withdrawal decision, and one publicly-disagreeing-with-a-
regulator decision, each with the artefacts the head would
produce for the live event.

By the end of the module, a working head-of-role has produced:

- A voluntary-code register covering the standing portfolio
  (EU AI Office GPAI CoP, G7 Hiroshima Code, White House
  Voluntary Commitments where applicable, Frontier Model Forum,
  Partnership on AI, MLCommons AI Safety WG, AISIC) with a
  named stance per code, a recorded rationale, and, for signed
  codes, a named operating owner and withdrawal condition set.
- A standards-body participation portfolio with a specific
  depth-on-the-ladder decision per body, a named represented-by
  pattern, named seats and working-group roles, and a specific
  feedback loop into the enterprise's crosswalk, AIMS
  management review, and audit-committee cadence.
- An annual public-trust plan with a bounded speaking
  calendar, a considered position-paper agenda, a coordinated
  industry-body board-seat portfolio, a Communications-managed
  media strategy, and an enterprise AI-governance publishing
  channel — all under the head-and-CCO joint operating
  agreement.
- An RFI / RFP intake register covering the five categories,
  with a named intake owner, a settled routing rule per
  category, an answer library for customer RFPs, and the head-
  and-CRO joint operating agreement on the sales-and-head
  boundary.
- Three rehearsed difficult-decision packs — one decline, one
  withdrawal, one public-methodology disagreement — each with
  the substantive rationale, the framing discipline, the
  public-facing artefact, and the follow-up plan.
- A shape-lessons synthesis from Anthropic's RSP, OpenAI's
  Preparedness Framework, and Google DeepMind's FSF, mapped to
  the enterprise-equivalent public commitment framework the
  head is building toward.

These artefacts extend the running program from
