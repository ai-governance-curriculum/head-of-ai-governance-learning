# exercise-05: Difficult External Decision Rehearsal

**Estimated effort:** 4 hours

## Objective

Rehearse the module's three difficult external decisions —
**declining to sign a voluntary code that peers are signing**,
**withdrawing from a code the enterprise had signed**, and
**publicly disagreeing with a regulator's methodology** —
against three concrete scenarios. For each rehearsal, produce
the artefacts the head would produce if the decision were live:
the substantive rationale, the framing package, the public-
facing artefact, and the follow-up plan. The capstone exercise
of the module: by its close, the head has walked the four-move
disagreement discipline through all three scenario families and
has muscle memory before the live event.

The rehearsal is not a study of chapter 05. It is the head's
own dry-run. Live difficult decisions arrive on a compressed
timeline with adversarial pressure; a head who has never walked
the discipline in slow, deliberate form has to invent it under
those conditions. The three artefact sets produced here are
what the head reaches for when the live event lands.

The rehearsal has to survive three stress tests. First, an
audit-committee chair asks "if the enterprise had to decline
code X, what would we say" — the rehearsal produces the
substantive one-paragraph statement, the underlying rationale,
and the anticipated follow-up questions with prepared answers.
Second, a communications director simulating a hostile press
interview probes the framing for adversarial tone, for
inconsistency with prior public position, and for the head
having burned the underlying regulator relationship — the
framing has to hold under the probe. Third, an incoming head
inherits the rehearsals as templates for future live events,
adapting the shape rather than rebuilding the discipline from
scratch.

## Steps

1. **Inherit the enterprise context.** Confirm the exercise-01
   context one-pager is current. The rehearsal scenarios take
   their plausibility from the context — sector,
   jurisdictional footprint, AI-system portfolio scope,
   current voluntary-code posture, current standards-body
   posture, current regulator-engagement footprint.
2. **The disagreement-discipline crib sheet.** Author a
   one-page crib sheet the head carries into each rehearsal
   (and, later, into the live event). The four moves:
   substantive naming, non-adversarial framing, preservation
   of the ordinary engagement surface, and internal
   coordination before external disclosure. Each move,
   articulated with the specific failure mode it defends
   against (e.g. substantive naming defends against the
   "we're still reviewing" placeholder that ossifies into
   the rationale; non-adversarial framing defends against
   burning the underlying regulator relationship). The crib
   sheet is the head's own working reference; it is short
   enough to reread in five minutes on the way to the
   sign-off room.
3. **Scenario A — Decline to sign a voluntary code.** Select
   one voluntary code from the exercise-01 register (or a
   plausible new code) the enterprise would plausibly
   consider signing but that a considered decline would be
   the right answer for. Rehearse the decline:
   - **Substantive rationale.** Select the applicable
     rationale family from the chapter-05 taxonomy
     (attachment mismatch, evaluation-methodology gap,
     cost / opportunity conflict, governance-conflict with
     existing commitments, principled disagreement). Draft
     the rationale in the family's terms — specific,
     technical, defensible. Not "we are not signing because
     we have not completed our review" (procedural); "we
     are not signing because commitment C attaches to
     system class X in a way our current evaluation
     methodology cannot defend on the code's reporting
     cadence" (substantive).
   - **Register entry.** Author the register row in the
     voluntary-code register — code identifier, current
     state (declined with rationale), state-entry date, and
     the one-paragraph rationale. Cross-reference to the
     public-facing summary (if any) and to the monitoring
     commitment.
   - **Public-facing artefact.** Draft the one-paragraph
     statement (ready to be issued if a journalist,
     customer, or peer asks) covering the code, the fact
     that the enterprise considered and declined, the
     substantive rationale in one sentence, and the
     enterprise's continued engagement on the underlying
     subject matter through other surfaces.
   - **Explanatory position paper outline.** If the decline
     is material enough that the one-paragraph statement
     will invite follow-up, outline the longer explanatory
     position paper. Not required for every decline; if you
     judge this decline material, produce the outline; if
     not, record why not.
   - **Monitoring commitment.** Name the specific facts
     the head is watching for that would move the decline
     to a revisit (peer with similar operating shape signs;
     the underlying substance becomes regulatorily
     material; the code is materially revised) and the
     cadence of the watch (annual by default, faster if the
     code is moving).
   - **Sign-off routing.** Walk the CEO / GC / CRO / CFO
     sign-off room, applied to a decline (which CxO
     ratifications are required for a decline; typically
     the decline is a lighter sign-off than the sign-on).
     Name the specific accountabilities.
4. **Scenario B — Withdraw from a signed code.** Select one
   voluntary code from the exercise-01 register the
   enterprise is currently signed to (or a plausible signed
   code) that a considered withdrawal would be the right
   answer for. Rehearse the withdrawal:
   - **Trigger family.** Select the applicable trigger from
     the chapter-05 taxonomy (code has evolved beyond
     original commitment; enterprise operating shape has
     changed; regulatory context has changed; enterprise
     has failed to deliver on a commitment; signatory
     community has moved in a direction the enterprise
     disagrees with). Name the specific facts that satisfy
     the trigger. Where the trigger is failure-to-deliver,
     reference the incident-disclosure discipline in
     [[mod-110-incident-leadership]] chapter 05 for the
     disclosure shape.
   - **Unwind plan.** Author the unwind plan chapter 05
     defines. Sections: the last cycle (last reporting
     round, last committee meeting, last data-sharing
     round); the handoffs (what passes back to the body's
     secretariat or a successor entity); the public timing
     (when the withdrawal is announced, synchronised
     against the last cycle); the customer-facing
     communication (if customer contracts reference the
     enterprise's signatory status, the specific
     communication to those customers); and the public-
     facing withdrawal statement itself.
   - **Public-facing withdrawal statement.** Author the
     five-component statement chapter 05 requires:
     recognition of the code's value, the specific reason
     for withdrawal (substantively, not "recent strategic
     changes"), what the enterprise will continue to do,
     the unwind timing, and continued willingness to
     engage. One page, sometimes two. If the draft runs
     longer, compress; longer statements signal discomfort
     with the decision.
   - **Sign-off routing.** Walk the CEO / GC / CRO / CFO
     sign-off room, applied to a withdrawal. Withdrawal is
     a heavier sign-off than a sign-on because the sign-on
     is being reversed. The CEO signs; the audit-committee
     chair sees the statement before publication for a
     material code.
   - **Internal debrief plan.** Author the debrief plan
     the head will run after the withdrawal is public.
     Participants (CxO peer layer, audit-committee chair,
     level-50 architect, operating owner of the code),
     inputs (the sign-on decision record, the operating
     history, the withdrawal artefacts), outputs (register
     entry that carries the withdrawal history for the
     successor; lessons that improve the next sign-on
     decision).
5. **Scenario C — Publicly disagree with a regulator's
   methodology.** Select one regulator's methodology or
   proposal — plausibly, one the enterprise has already
   engaged with via the exercise-04 register (an NIST
   proposed measurement approach, an EU AI Office
   evaluation methodology, a CPPA proposed ADMT rule, a
   sector-regulator methodology on model risk or
   provider practice) — that a considered public
   disagreement would be the right posture on. Rehearse
   the disagreement:
   - **Threshold test.** Walk the three chapter-05
     questions and answer each explicitly for the chosen
     matter:
     - Is the enterprise's substantive position actually
       distinctive, or would an industry-body submission
       cover the same substance?
     - Is the disagreement substantive enough to warrant
       the relationship cost — the running regulator
       relationship the disagreement will cost some
       capital against?
     - Is the enterprise's alternative technically
       defensible under peer and regulator scrutiny?
     If any answer is no, the disagreement stays in the
     ordinary submission channel and this rehearsal ends
     with that recorded. If all three are yes, continue.
   - **Framing discipline.** Author the disagreement piece
     applying the chapter-05 five-point framing:
     - Speak to the substance, not the regulator.
     - Cite the enterprise's own work — evaluation
       methodology, operating experience, published
       research, standards-body contribution.
     - Offer a specific alternative proposal, not
       criticism-only.
     - Acknowledge the regulator's mandate and the
       enterprise's commitment to comply with the final
       rule.
     - Coordinate with peer disagreement where peers
       share the substantive position; explain internally
       why the enterprise still holds the position if
       genuinely alone.
   - **Public-facing artefact.** Choose the vehicle for
     the disagreement — a position paper on the
     enterprise's AI-governance channel (drawing on the
     exercise-03 discipline), a consultation submission
     under the [[mod-104-regulator-engagement]] chapter
     06 discipline, a keynote at a regulator-convened
     event (chapter 03 speaking discipline), or a
     combination. Justify the choice against the
     substance's characteristics (durability, technical
     depth, expected audience).
   - **Sign-off routing.** Walk the internal coordination
     step — CEO ratification, GC review (legal exposure
     specifically for public disagreement with a
     regulator), CRO review (relationship-cost read),
     Government Affairs coordination, level-50 architect
     technical review. The head does not go public without
     the full internal alignment.
   - **Follow-up cadence.** Author the follow-up cadence
     chapter 05 defines — the direct-engagement update
     within four to eight weeks (a private meeting or
     call with the regulator's counterpart), the
     methodology contribution (if the enterprise's
     alternative is technically substantive, contributing
     the technical documentation under an appropriate
     confidentiality arrangement, coordinated with GC),
     and the ongoing engagement surface (continuing to
     attend the regulator's workshops, respond to its
     RFIs, participate in standards work — the
     disagreement does not close the surface).
6. **The cross-scenario compression.** Author, in one page,
   the shared discipline the three scenarios rehearse. What
   is common (the four moves; the substantive-rationale
   requirement; the non-adversarial framing; the internal
   coordination) and what is different (the ratification
   weight, the public-facing artefact, the follow-up cost).
   The compression is the head's own synthesis and the
   handoff artefact for the successor.
7. **The stress-test walk-through.** Have a peer (or the
   study group, or the CCO in a real-enterprise setting)
   simulate a hostile press interview against one of the
   three rehearsed artefacts. Log where the framing held,
   where it wobbled, and what pre-cleared decline language
   or bridge language the head needed and did not have.
   Update the artefact accordingly. The stress test is the
   difference between rehearsal-as-writing and rehearsal-
   as-practice.
8. **The failure-mode audit.** Author a short section — one
    paragraph per failure — naming how the discipline
    detects and recovers from each of these failure modes:
    - **The procedural rationale ossifies.** A decline
      justified as "we have not yet completed our review"
      is still that rationale a year later; the head has
      not converted procedural to substantive.
    - **The withdrawal statement runs long.** The statement
      grows past one page; the length signals the
      enterprise's discomfort with the decision and invites
      the wrong inferences.
    - **The regulator disagreement becomes adversarial.**
      The head's framing drifts from substance to
      regulator-directed critique; the ordinary engagement
      surface starts to close; the head does not notice
      until the next scheduled meeting is quietly declined.
    - **The internal alignment breaks silently.** A CxO
      peer disagreed at the sign-off but did not speak;
      the disagreement surfaces when a journalist or a
      shareholder asks and the enterprise's answer
      wobbles.
    - **The follow-up cadence lapses.** The direct-
      engagement update never happens; the methodology
      contribution never lands; the disagreement reads to
      the regulator as obstruction because the enterprise
      did not do the follow-up work.

## Acceptance criteria

- Each scenario's substantive rationale is specific,
  technical, and defensible. A rationale that reads as
  procedural ("still under review") or brand-safe
  ("consistent with our commitment to responsible AI")
  fails the criterion. The chapter-05 taxonomies of
  rationale families and trigger families are used
  explicitly, not paraphrased.
- The decline artefact set includes a monitoring
  commitment with a specific revisit cadence or trigger.
  A decline without monitoring is the "decision that
  ossifies" failure mode chapter 05 warns against.
- The withdrawal artefact set includes an unwind plan
  with all five components (last cycle, handoffs, public
  timing, customer-facing communication, public-facing
  statement) and a five-component public statement that
  fits on one page (two at most).
- The regulator-disagreement artefact set includes
  explicit answers to all three threshold questions. If
  any answer is no, the rehearsal ends with that
  recorded and no public disagreement is drafted; if
  all three are yes, the framing discipline covers all
  five points and the follow-up cadence covers all
  three follow-up commitments.
- Each scenario's sign-off routing names the CxO peers
  and their specific accountabilities. A sign-off
  routing that reads as a rubber stamp fails the
  criterion — chapter 01's sign-off room discipline
  applies here in adapted form.
- The cross-scenario compression identifies what is
  common and what is different across the three
  scenarios. A compression that reads as three
  summaries stacked together has not done the synthesis
  work.
- The stress-test walk-through is documented. If the
  head has no peer or CCO to run the stress test in
  real terms, the head simulates it against an
  adversarial checklist and documents the artefact
  updates that resulted. The stress test is not
  optional; unstressed rehearsal is scriptwriting.
- The failure-mode audit names, for each of the five
  failure modes, at least one specific detection
  signal and one specific recovery action.
- The rehearsals compose with the exercise-01 register,
  the exercise-02 portfolio, the exercise-03 plan, and
  the exercise-04 runbook. A scenario that ignores the
  standing artefacts is a scenario disconnected from
  the discipline the module has built.

## Stretch goals

- Author a **fourth scenario**: a customer-facing decline,
  where a customer's RFP asks for a bespoke commitment
  the enterprise cannot honestly make (chapter 04's
  escalation trigger case). The rehearsal walks the
  substantive rationale (why the commitment cannot be
  made), the response draft to the customer (respectful,
  substantive, with the enterprise's standard commitment
  offered as the alternative), and the follow-up
  discipline (does the enterprise pursue the deal at
  the standard commitment, or accept losing the deal
  with the rationale on the record).
- Author a **fifth scenario**: a public disagreement not
  with a regulator but with a peer body's methodology —
  for instance, an evaluation benchmark published by an
  industry body the enterprise is a member of, where
  the enterprise has substantive disagreement. The
  four-move discipline applies; the "regulator"
  substitutes to "peer body" and the relationship-cost
  question shifts to the industry-body relationship.
- Draft the **journalist Q&A pack** for one of the three
  rehearsed scenarios — the anticipated questions a
  substantive reporter would ask, with the head's
  prepared substantive answers, the questions the head
  would decline (and the pre-cleared decline language),
  and the bridge phrases that pivot from an off-topic
  question back to the substantive posture.
- Author the **peer-head advance-notice protocol** —
  the discipline the head uses to give peer heads-of-role
  at named peer enterprises quiet advance notice of a
  material public position before it drops. The
  protocol is not collusion (GC watches the antitrust
  boundary) and it is not favouritism (the notice is
  factual, not persuasive); it is the courtesy that
  keeps the peer relationships intact when the head
  takes a position peers may find surprising.
- Draft the **audit-committee external-briefing update**
  the head would deliver at the next scheduled briefing
  if one of the three rehearsed scenarios became live in
  the intervening quarter. The update is short (a
  two-page briefing note), covers the decision, the
  artefacts produced, the CxO peer alignment, the
  external reception, and the follow-up cadence, and
  frames the audit committee's own ratification
  question.

## Notes and starter guidance

The most common first-cut failure of the rehearsal is a
substantive rationale that reads as brand-safe rather
than substantive — "we are not signing because our
approach to responsible AI is comprehensive and we
prefer to focus on our own program". That rationale
survives no probing. It reads as "we don't want to be
measured against public commitments"; the reader
supplies the missing reason. Force the rationale into
the chapter-05 family terms (attachment mismatch,
evaluation-methodology gap, cost / opportunity conflict,
governance-conflict, principled disagreement) and into
the specific facts that satisfy the family. Substantive
rationale is what makes the decision defensible; brand-
safe rationale is what makes it indefensible.

The second common failure is the withdrawal statement
that runs long because the enterprise is uncomfortable
with the decision. Chapter 05 is explicit that the
statement is one page, five components, and the
compression discipline is what signals the enterprise's
composure. Force the compression even where the draft
resists it; the discomfort is diagnostic.

On the regulator-disagreement threshold test — the pull
is to skip past the three questions because the head has
already decided to disagree. The point of the threshold
test is that not every disagreement is worth taking
public; the discipline is to answer each question
explicitly and honestly, and if any answer is no, to
record the disagreement in the ordinary submission
channel rather than escalate to public disagreement.
Skipping the threshold produces public disagreements
that read as reflexive.

On the framing — resist the pull toward regulator-
directed critique. Chapter 05 is explicit that the
enterprise disagrees with the *substance*, not with the
regulator or its motives. A draft that reads as "the
regulator has failed to appreciate X" has drifted; a
draft that reads as "the substantive methodology has a
specific limitation on system class Y that our evidence
addresses differently" has not.

On the stress test — do not skip it. Rehearsal in
writing is a fraction of rehearsal in dialogue. Even a
solo stress test (the head simulates the hostile
interview against an adversarial checklist) surfaces
framing weaknesses that the written draft concealed.
The head who has stress-tested is the head who does not
freeze on the live event.

The rehearsals are living artefacts. Retain them,
version them, update them when the standing regulator
relationships shift or when a code the enterprise is
signed to undergoes material revision. A rehearsal
retained from three years ago is a rehearsal against a
world that has moved; the discipline is to keep the
scenarios current so the head's muscle memory is
current.

The three scenarios rehearsed here are not exhaustive.
The head will face variations — the decline that
becomes a withdrawal because the enterprise signed and
regretted; the withdrawal that becomes a public
disagreement because the enterprise's rationale
attracts regulator questioning; the public disagreement
that becomes an ordinary submission because the head's
threshold-test answers shift as the substance is
better understood. The four-move discipline is the
common floor; the variations are what live events
produce.
