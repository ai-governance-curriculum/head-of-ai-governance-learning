# exercise-02: Standards-Body Participation Portfolio Drill

**Estimated effort:** 3 hours

## Objective

Author the enterprise **standards-body participation portfolio**
— the register that answers, for every standards body the
enterprise cares about, at what depth on the ladder the
enterprise sits, which of the enterprise's people represent it
there, which named seats and working-group roles the enterprise
occupies, and how the outputs of the body flow back into the
enterprise's own operating disciplines. The deliverable is the
portfolio the head defends at the audit committee's annual
external-engagement briefing and the artefact the level-50
senior AI governance architect executes against between
briefings.

The portfolio has to survive three stress tests. First, a
certification body auditing the enterprise's AI management
system under ISO/IEC 42006 asks "does the enterprise engage
with the standards work its AIMS is built against, and how" —
the portfolio has to answer with named seats, named
contributors, and a documented feedback loop. Second, an
incoming head of AI governance reads the portfolio on day one
and can tell, without reconstructing memory, why the enterprise
is at *contributor* in one body and at *observer* in another.
Third, a regulator who reads the enterprise's public
standards-body summary (chapter 02, public-facing artefact)
does not find claims the register cannot substantiate — no
"leadership" claim where the enterprise is at member, no
"active participation" claim where the enterprise cast one
ballot vote in 2024 and nothing since.

The drill is not a study of chapter 02. It is the artefact
that turns chapter 02's five-rung ladder and four-decision
matrix into an operating discipline any CxO peer or successor
head can follow.

## Steps

1. **Inherit the enterprise context.** Open the context
   one-pager from exercise 01 and confirm it is current. If
   the enterprise's sector, jurisdictional footprint, or
   AI-system portfolio scope has been refined since exercise
   01, update the one-pager first — the standards-body
   portfolio takes its shape from the context and works
   against the current version, not an older one.
2. **Portfolio register schema.** Author the register schema
   the drill maintains. One row per body. Columns:
   - Body identifier and canonical citation (name, full path
     — e.g. "ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 42", not just "SC 42" — and the
     official landing URL).
   - Depth on the ladder (observer / member / contributor /
     convenor-or-editor / chair).
   - Depth-choice rationale (one paragraph — why this rung and
     not the adjacent one).
   - Representation pattern (head personally / level-50
     architect personally / named technical contributor with
     architect backing / industry-body proxy).
   - Named seats and roles — the specific working groups,
     editor roles, convenor roles, and mirror-committee seats
     the enterprise occupies. Names, not counts.
   - Feedback loop — the specific artefact inside the
     enterprise that outputs from the body flow into (the
     level-50 architect's crosswalk, the AIMS management
     review, the risk register, the audit-committee pack, the
     regulatory-change register, the supply-chain evidence
     discipline).
   - National mirror committee(s), where applicable — the
     specific mirror at the enterprise's national body (BSI
     ART/1, ANSI INCITS AI, DIN NA 043-01-42 AA, AFNOR CN
     CEN/CLC JTC 21, JISC mirrors, and equivalents in other
     jurisdictions).
   - Annual cost estimate (headcount fraction of the named
     contributors' time; membership fees where the enterprise
     is a direct member; travel; convenor / editor overhead if
     the enterprise occupies a leadership rung).
   - Last-milestone note (the most recent governance
     milestone against this body — a ballot cast, a working-
     group meeting attended, a comment resolution submitted).
   - Public-facing summary text — the exact phrasing the
     enterprise uses on its AI-governance channel to describe
     its participation in this body, and the date the phrasing
     was last reviewed for accuracy.
   Name the storage location — the register lives in the
   GRC-for-AI stack alongside the voluntary-code register
   from exercise 01, not on the head's laptop. Name the
   access control (read for CxO peers, audit-committee chair,
   external auditors under NDA; write for head and level-50
   architect). Name the retention posture (retain per the
   enterprise's records-management schedule; the public-
   facing summary text is retained as a versioned history so
   the enterprise can defend prior public claims).
3. **Populate the register — the standing portfolio.** For
   each of the bodies chapter 02 names — ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 42,
   CEN-CENELEC JTC 21, IEEE 7000-series, NIST (workshops, AIRC
   comment windows, AISIC working groups where relevant),
   OECD.AI ONE AI, and the national mirror committees relevant
   to the enterprise's jurisdictional footprint — populate the
   register with a specific depth choice, a specific
   representation pattern, and a specific feedback loop. Where
   a specific fact about a body's current organisational
   status, work-item roster, or membership terms is not
   independently verifiable, mark with
   `<!-- needs-research: verify [specific fact] against
   [official source] -->` rather than guessing.
4. **The four-decision matrix, written down per body.** For
   every body on the register, author the four-decision block
   chapter 02 defines:
   - **Depth on the ladder** — the chosen rung, with one-
     sentence rationale (why this rung).
   - **Represented by** — the specific role or person. If the
     enterprise represents by "the level-50 architect", name
     the role, not the individual (the artefact outlives the
     individual). If the enterprise represents through an
     industry-body proxy, name the proxy body and the
     coordination discipline.
   - **Named seats and roles** — the specific working groups
     and roles. "One working group, TBD" is not a named seat.
     If the seat is aspirational (the enterprise wants to
     take a convenor role at the next opportunity), record
     the aspiration as a *target*, not as a current seat.
   - **Feedback loop** — the specific downstream artefact.
     "Outputs feed the AIMS management review" is only enough
     if the register also names the review cadence, the
     surface where the feedback lands on the review, and the
     owner who carries the input into the review room. Detail
     is the discipline.
5. **The head-versus-architect split, applied.** For each
   body, decide who represents the enterprise at leadership
   scope, applying the chapter-02 rule (head at chair-level
   plenaries and cross-body coordination; architect at
   working-group and technical editorial sessions; head
   personally chairs only where the head's public profile is
   a strategic asset, the head has specific technical depth,
   or continuity from a predecessor matters). Document, in
   one paragraph per body, the rationale for the split.
   Where the head personally chairs, name the specific
   working group and the multi-year commitment the head is
   accepting. Where the architect chairs, name the
   architect's role explicitly and the head's backing pattern.
6. **The three sustaining cadences.** Author, in a short
   companion document, the three chapter-02 cadences the
   portfolio runs against:
   - **Annual portfolio review** — the review meeting the
     head holds once a year against every row of the register.
     Name the participants (head, level-50 architect,
     Government Affairs, GC representative), the input pack
     (the register plus the quarterly digests from the year),
     the decision surface (adjust depth, adjust
     representation, adjust named seats, adjust feedback
     loop, retire the body if no longer relevant), and the
     output artefact (the updated register and the
     audit-committee external-engagement briefing input).
   - **Quarterly technical-contribution digest** — the
     one-page digest the level-50 architect produces every
     quarter. Name the format (one page maximum), the
     content (drafts commented on, ballots voted, working-
     group meetings attended, work items moving toward
     closure), the destination (the head reads it; the
     digest is also the enterprise's contemporaneous
     evidence for the ISO/IEC 42006 certification-body
     inquiry).
   - **On-milestone briefing** — the trigger-driven briefing
     the architect gives the head when a standard the
     enterprise is materially engaged with hits a Committee
     Draft ballot, a Draft International Standard ballot, a
     publication, or (for JTC 21 outputs) an OJEU citation.
     Name the trigger events, the SLA on the briefing
     (typically two weeks from milestone), and the head's
     downstream decisions (escalate to audit committee,
     update crosswalk, communicate to CxO peers).
7. **The public-facing summary discipline.** Author the
   discipline that keeps the enterprise's public standards-
   body summary (on the AI-governance channel, per chapter
   03) honest to the register. Include:
   - The mapping from private-register fields to public
     summary text (which fields go public, which do not —
     annual cost is private; named individual contributor
     identities are private absent the individual's consent;
     depth-on-the-ladder is public; named seats and roles are
     public at role level).
   - The update trigger (any change to depth, representation,
     named seats, or feedback loop triggers a summary review
     within two weeks).
   - The audit rule — an annual reconciliation between the
     public summary and the register, run alongside the
     annual portfolio review.
   - The "no overstatement, no understatement" test
     (chapter 02) — the summary describes the enterprise's
     depth honestly. A public claim of "leadership" where the
     register says the enterprise is at member is a
     credibility failure; a silent under-summary of a
     convenor role the enterprise actually holds is capital
     left on the table.
8. **The coordination interfaces.** Author the three short
   coordination contracts the portfolio depends on:
   - **With Government Affairs.** GA owns industry-body
     strategy and coordinates the enterprise's positions
     across trade associations. Where a standards-body
     seat is filled by an industry-body proxy (chapter 02's
     "represented by" fourth option), GA and the head
     coordinate on the proxy's mandate. Where a standards-
     body position could conflict with a GA-led policy
     position (rare but possible), the head convenes.
   - **With the level-50 architect.** The architect is the
     day-to-day operator of the portfolio and produces the
     quarterly digest; the head decides depth, ratifies
     representation, and holds the audit-committee
     accountability. The contract names the RACI split
     explicitly.
   - **With the certification body (where relevant).** For
     enterprises whose AIMS is built against ISO/IEC 42001
     and certified under ISO/IEC 42006, the certification
     body will inquire into standards-body engagement as
     part of the audit. The contract names the standing
     evidence the enterprise offers (the register, the
     digest history, the annual review outputs) and the
     head's ownership of the response.
9. **The portfolio walk-through.** Select one body on the
   populated register the enterprise currently sits at
   *observer* or *member* and could plausibly move to
   *contributor* over the next year. Walk the four-decision
   matrix against the target rung: what depth, what
   representation, what named seats, what feedback loop.
   Produce the one-page recommendation that would go to the
   sign-off room if the enterprise elected to move — parallel
   in shape to the chapter-01 head's-recommendation format,
   with the CFO cost line, the CRO capacity check, and the
   head's substantive rationale. The walk-through is the
   head's dry-run of a depth-adjustment decision.
10. **The failure-mode audit.** Author a short section — one
    paragraph per failure — naming how the portfolio detects
    and recovers from each of these failure modes:
    - **Subscription drift.** The enterprise pays the national-
      body fee, is listed as a member, but casts no ballots and
      submits no comments. The failure is invisible until an
      external audit surfaces it.
    - **Named-contributor departure.** A named technical
      contributor leaves the enterprise; the working-group
      seat is silently unoccupied for months.
    - **Public summary drift.** The register changes but the
      public-facing summary is not updated; the enterprise's
      public claim now overstates or understates the current
      participation.
    - **Feedback-loop breakage.** Outputs from the body no
      longer reach the crosswalk; the level-50 architect stops
      briefing the head on-milestone; the portfolio becomes
      subscription without the enterprise noticing.
    - **Head over-representation.** The head is attending
      technical working-group sessions the architect should be
      running; the head's calendar is consumed by detail work;
      the leadership-scope venues the head should be at are
      being missed.

## Acceptance criteria

- Every row of the populated register has a specific rung on
  the ladder (observer / member / contributor / convenor-or-
  editor / chair). "Under review" or "TBD" is not a rung.
- Every row has a named representation pattern — a role, not
  a person — and a rationale that connects to the head-
  versus-architect split.
- Every named seat is a specific working group or role, not
  a count ("one or two working groups"). Aspirational seats
  are marked as targets and separated from current seats.
- Every row has a specific feedback loop that terminates in a
  named downstream artefact inside the enterprise (crosswalk,
  AIMS management review, risk register, audit-committee
  pack, regulatory-change register, supply-chain evidence).
  "Outputs feed operations" without a named terminus fails
  the criterion.
- Any factual claim about a body's current organisational
  status, work-item roster, or membership terms carries a
  `<!-- needs-research: verify [specific fact] against
  [official source] -->` marker where the fact is not
  independently verifiable. No status is guessed.
- The head-versus-architect split is applied per body with a
  written rationale. A body where the head personally chairs
  a working group carries the specific multi-year commitment
  the chair role represents.
- The public-summary discipline names the mapping between
  private-register fields and public-summary text
  explicitly, and the summary passes the "no overstatement,
  no understatement" test.
- The walk-through recommendation is genuinely one page and
  has the CFO cost line, the CRO capacity check, and the
  head's substantive rationale. A longer recommendation is
  recorded as the head having failed the discipline.
- The failure-mode audit names, for each of the five failure
  modes, at least one specific detection signal (a KRI, a
  cadence trigger, an audit check) and one specific recovery
  action.
- The register composes with the voluntary-code register
  from exercise 01 as the enterprise's *external-engagement
  portfolio*. Cross-references from a standards-body row to
  a related voluntary-code row (e.g. from ISO/IEC 42001 to
  the EU AI Office GPAI Code of Practice where relevant)
  are explicit, not implied.

## Stretch goals

- Extend the register with a **three-year forward look** for
  each body — which work items the enterprise expects to
  reach OJEU citation, publication, or major-revision
  milestones in the next three years, and what the
  enterprise's depth-adjustment intention is against each
  milestone. The forward look feeds the annual external-
  engagement briefing.
- Author the **on-milestone briefing template** the level-50
  architect uses when a standard the enterprise is materially
  engaged with hits a Committee Draft ballot, DIS ballot,
  publication, or OJEU citation. One page, five sections
  (the standard, the milestone, the substantive implication
  for the enterprise, the recommended head decision, the
  audit-committee visibility question).
- Draft the **audit-committee external-engagement briefing
  pack** input drawn from the populated register — a
  two-to-three-page summary that a committee director can
  read in ten minutes: bodies where the enterprise leads
  (convenor / editor / chair roles); bodies where the
  enterprise contributes; bodies at member without upgrade
  intent; bodies dropped from the portfolio in the last year
  with rationale. Use the pack shape convention from
  [[mod-103-board-and-audit-committee-reporting]] chapter 01.
- Author the **certification-body evidence pack** for the
  next scheduled ISO/IEC 42006 audit cycle — the specific
  artefacts (register extract, digest history, annual review
  outputs) the enterprise offers to demonstrate standards-
  body engagement. The pack shape should let the certifier
  find each artefact without asking a follow-up.
- Draft the **head-personally-chairs decision packet** for
  one body the head is considering taking the chair or
  convenor role in. The packet is the substantive
  case — why this body, why the head and not a level-50
  architect chair, what the multi-year commitment costs the
  head's calendar, and what the enterprise gains — plus the
  chapter-01 sign-off routing (CEO, GC, CRO, CFO ratification
  of the head's committed time and the political capital
  spent).

## Notes and starter guidance

The most common first-cut failure of the portfolio is a
register that reads as a set of aspirational sentences
("the enterprise engages with SC 42 through participation in
its working groups") without the discipline that names the
working group, names the contributor, names the last-cast
ballot, and names the downstream artefact. Aspirational
narrative is the failure mode chapter 02 opens with —
subscription without participation. Force the register into
the structured form even where the entries feel thin; a thin
row is honest, and a thin row is a prompt to either upgrade
the participation or drop the body from the portfolio.

The second common failure is the head who fills the
representation column with "the head" across every row
because the head believes personal attendance signals
seriousness. Chapter 02 is explicit that the head at a
technical working-group session is signalling *the wrong
seriousness* — that the enterprise does not have the
technical depth to send. Force the head-versus-architect
split even where the head is uncomfortable ceding the seat;
the discipline is what allows the enterprise to occupy
leadership rungs credibly.

On the feedback loop — resist the pull to write "outputs
feed the risk register" without naming the specific input
the risk register accepts, the named owner who carries the
input in, and the cadence at which the input arrives. A
feedback loop that cannot be traced from the standards-body
output to a specific edit on a specific downstream artefact
is not a feedback loop; it is a claim.

On the national mirror committees — the pull is to skip the
mirror committee layer because the head assumes direct
international participation is "more serious". Chapter 02 is
explicit that the mirror committee is often lower-cost and
more responsive; the default posture is at least one mirror-
committee seat per body per material operating jurisdiction.
Skipping the mirror layer means skipping the pre-plenary
positioning of the enterprise's own national body — and the
national body is the entity whose vote the enterprise's
international position is carried through.

On the walk-through — pick a body where the depth-adjustment
decision is genuinely open, not one where the decision is
obvious. The exercise is calibration; the head learns most
by walking a decision the head is uncertain about.

The portfolio is a living artefact. Version it, date it,
review it at the annual external-engagement briefing, update
it when the standing portfolio shifts materially (a new
body emerges, a standard the enterprise is engaged with is
withdrawn or replaced, a peer's convenor role opens up and
the enterprise decides whether to bid). A portfolio that has
not been touched in a year is a portfolio that no longer
reflects the participation it claims to describe.
