# exercise-03: Public Trust Posture Authoring

**Estimated effort:** 2 hours

## Objective

Author the enterprise **annual public-trust plan** and the
underlying **head-and-CCO joint operating agreement** that the
plan runs against. The plan lays out the head's intended
public surfaces for the year — speaking calendar, position-
paper agenda, industry-body board-seat portfolio, media
strategy, and enterprise AI-governance publishing channel —
and the joint operating agreement codifies the substance-
versus-delivery boundary between head and Communications that
keeps the plan credible.

The plan has to survive four stress tests. First, a peer
enterprise's incident lands mid-year and the enterprise is
asked to comment; the head reaches for the plan and its
underlying response-to-events discipline rather than
improvising a first appearance. Second, an incoming Chief
Communications Officer reads the joint operating agreement on
day one and can tell where their delivery ownership begins
and where the head's substance ownership ends without
renegotiating from scratch. Third, an audit-committee chair
reviewing the annual external-engagement briefing asks "what
is the head's public-trust footprint and what does it cost
the enterprise in the head's time and Communications
resource" — the plan has to answer with specific numbers.
Fourth, a hypothetical future regulator reading the
enterprise's public archive over three years can trace a
coherent substantive arc through the head's speaking,
publishing, and media appearances, not a random walk.

The plan is not a study of chapter 03. It is the artefact
that turns chapter 03's five surfaces and its substance-
versus-delivery discipline into a governable rhythm.

  operating agreement codifies parallels the head-and-GC
  agreement structure.
- [[mod-104-regulator-engagement]] chapter 07 (engagement-
  versus-legal-opinion boundary) — the head does not opine
  on legal questions in public; the discipline transposes to

- [[mod-110-incident-leadership]] chapter 05 (incident
  disclosure discipline) — the response-to-events discipline

  plan's shape follows the context — a bank in the EU has a
  different plausible speaking calendar than a US medical-
  device manufacturer.

## Steps

1. **Inherit the enterprise context.** Confirm the exercise-01
   context one-pager is current. If the enterprise's sector,
   footprint, or scope has been refined since, update the
   one-pager. The public-trust plan is written for a specific
   enterprise; the more specific the context, the more the
   plan is testable.
2. **The five-surface inventory.** Author, for each of the
   five surfaces chapter 03 defines, a one-page inventory of
   the enterprise's current state:
   - **Public speaking.** The head's speaking track record
     over the last twelve months (venues, topics, audiences),
     with each appearance classified by the chapter-03
     venue-family taxonomy (industry-convened, regulator-
     convened, academic / civil-society, general audience).
     Where the head has no track record, record the absence
     honestly.
   - **Position papers.** The papers the head has published
     under their own name, with dates and topics. Where the
     head has not published a position paper in the head's
     current tenure, record the gap.
   - **Industry-body board seats.** The seats the head
     currently occupies, with the body's name, the seat
     designation (board, executive committee, advisory), the
     term, and the meeting cadence. Cross-reference to the
     voluntary-code register from exercise 01 where the seat
     is at a body the enterprise has also signed a code with.
   - **Media strategy.** The head's on-record media
     appearances in the last twelve months (publication or
     broadcast, topic, framing) and the standing relationships
     Communications maintains with named reporters. Where
     Communications does not yet maintain any standing
     relationships, record the absence.
   - **Enterprise AI-governance publishing channel.** The
     current state of the enterprise's AI-governance page(s)
     — URL, contents (transparency reports, position papers,
     sign-on summaries, standards-body summaries, regulator-
     facing filings), and last-update dates per content type.
     Where the channel does not yet exist, record what the
     plan needs to spin up.
3. **The annual public-trust plan.** Author the one-page
   annual plan chapter 03 defines. Four sections:
   - **Calendar of intended surfaces.** The speaking venues
     the head expects to accept over the year (typed by the
     four venue families, with the specific bounded number
     per family — e.g. "up to four industry-convened
     appearances, up to two regulator-convened, up to two
     academic / civil-society, up to one general audience");
     the position papers the head expects to publish (topic,
     approximate month, co-author or co-publish body if any);
     the industry-body board seats the head is committing to
     (with the seat term); the media relationships the head
     is cultivating (types of publication, no individual
     names in the artefact — the individual names live in
     Communications' working files).
   - **Budget.** The head's time in days per quarter, honestly
     — speaking-day count, position-paper drafting days, board-
     seat meeting days, media-prep days — and the Communications
     resource commitment (event coordination hours, editorial
     hours, media-training hours, channel-maintenance hours).
     Ranges are acceptable if the range is honest; padded
     estimates are not.
   - **Positioning arc.** The theme the head's substance
     builds through the year. A single, coherent arc across
     three to five related positions the head takes across
     the surfaces — for example, an arc on GPAI governance
     that connects a Q1 position paper, a Q2 industry-
     convened keynote, a Q3 academic panel, and a Q4
     enterprise-channel post; or an arc on AIMS discipline
     that connects standards-body work with regulator-
     convened workshop remarks. A random-walk arc is what
     chapter 03 warns against.
   - **Coordination points.** The specific interfaces with
     other executives' public postures — the CEO's own
     speaking calendar, the CTO's technical public presence,
     Government Affairs' policy-lobbying presence, GC's
     litigation-adjacent silence discipline. Name the
     synchronisation cadence (a monthly standing sync between
     head and CCO; a quarterly CEO-CCO-head calendar review;
     an ad-hoc sync on any event that touches multiple
     executives' surfaces).
4. **The head-and-CCO joint operating agreement.** Author the
   two-to-three-page joint operating agreement that governs
   the plan. Sections:
   - **Substance vs delivery.** The specific split the
     agreement codifies. Which decisions are the head's
     unilaterally (substantive positions the enterprise
     stands behind, the interpretation of the enterprise's
     own governance program, technical read of standards or
     regulations). Which decisions are the CCO's unilaterally
     (venue selection, timing, tone, medium, visual identity).
     Which decisions are joint (specific speaking-invitation
     accept / decline calls, position-paper publication
     timing against Communications' broader calendar,
     escalation to CEO on reactive events).
   - **Escalation ladder.** The path from ordinary decisions
     through joint decisions to CEO-and-CCO decisions to
     board-audit-committee-visible decisions. Chapter-01
     sign-off routing is the model.
   - **Media discipline.** The three prep steps chapter 03
     names — briefing, substantive prep, post-interview
     capture — codified as the standard cycle for every
     on-record interaction. On-record is the default; off-
     record and background are exceptions that require
     Communications approval per event.
   - **Reactive-event protocol.** The pause-layered-
     response-peer-coordination discipline written down as
     an operating protocol: the moment a triggering event is
     detected, who calls whom, in what window, and who
     drafts the first short statement; how the layered
     response cascades (first short statement, fuller
     enterprise-channel statement, position paper if
     warranted); who signs each layer; and what the antitrust
     boundary is on peer coordination (GC watches; head does
     not coordinate substantive positions with peers absent
     the boundary).
   - **Metrics.** The two or three specific measures the
     agreement carries — for instance, the head's on-record
     media appearance count against plan, the position-paper
     publication cadence, the enterprise-channel update
     frequency, and the CxO-peer-visible-issue count where
     the head's public posture required unplanned
     coordination.
   - **Ratification.** The CEO ratifies the agreement; the
     audit-committee chair sees it as part of the annual
     external-engagement briefing.
5. **The enterprise AI-governance publishing channel
   discipline.** Author the operating discipline for the
   channel:
   - The channel's editorial voice — the head's substantive
     register (technical, considered, non-marketing), the
     scope of what belongs on the channel (position papers,
     sign-on summaries, standards-body summaries, regulator-
     facing filings where public, transparency reports)
     versus what belongs on the enterprise's product page
     (product features, marketing content).
   - The submission cycle for any new content — the same
     seven-step process chapter 01 uses for voluntary-code
     sign-on, applied to publishing decisions, with sign-off
     routing to head, GC, CCO, and (for high-visibility
     items) CEO.
   - The versioning and archive discipline — every published
     item is dated, versioned, and retained on the channel;
     material revisions are noted with a version history so
     the enterprise's public record is not silently rewritten.
     A regulator or a journalist reading a two-year-old
     position paper sees the paper as originally published,
     with a link to any subsequent revision.
   - The channel's URL discipline — the channel lives at a
     URL distinct from the enterprise's product marketing
     page, and the two do not cross-link at the top level;
     the head's substantive register does not sit next to
     the CMO's product register.
6. **The positioning-arc worked example.** Choose one
   substantive theme the enterprise could plausibly build a
   positioning arc around over the next year (drawing from
   the sign-on register, the standards-body portfolio, or
   the enterprise's regulatory-engagement footprint —
   [[mod-104-regulator-engagement]]). Sketch the arc across
   the year: which surface carries which position at which
   quarter, how the positions build on each other, what the
   cumulative posture is the reader takes away at year-end.
   The arc is illustrative, not committing — but it has to
   be concrete enough to test whether the plan's positioning-
   arc section is authored well.
7. **The response-to-events dry-run.** Select one hypothetical
   event the enterprise's plan would face in a plausible year
   (a peer's serious AI incident with adjacent subject matter;
   a regulator's landing rule the enterprise had watched but
   not commented on going front-page; a civil-society report
   naming the enterprise in a broader industry critique).
   Walk the three-move reactive discipline against the event:
   the pause (who is called, in what window, to settle a
   position); the layered response (the first short
   statement's draft, the fuller enterprise-channel statement's
   outline, the position-paper question if the event opens
   a durable one); the peer coordination (which peer heads
   the head reaches out to, what the coordination is limited
   to, where GC watches the antitrust boundary).
8. **The failure-mode audit.** Author a short section — one
   paragraph per failure — naming how the plan detects and
   recovers from each of these failure modes:
   - **The head-becomes-brand drift.** The head's public
     posture accumulates personal following that no longer
     reads as the enterprise's substantive posture; the
     enterprise loses the signal a role-shaped posture would
     send.
   - **The Communications-owns-substance drift.** The CCO
     starts drafting the head's substance because the head
     is not producing it; the head's positions become
     communications-safe rather than substantive.
   - **The reactive-event scramble.** An event lands and the
     head fills the silence unprompted without the CCO and GC
     having settled; the enterprise's first statement is
     walked back.
   - **The publishing-channel drift.** The enterprise-
     governance channel drifts toward marketing content
     because the head is not populating it with substantive
     items on the plan's cadence.
   - **The plan-versus-calendar drift.** Communications'
     calendar accretes speaking invitations that the plan
     did not budget for; the head is on the road twice as
     much as the plan projected; the running program
     suffers.

## Acceptance criteria

- The five-surface inventory is honest about the
  enterprise's current state — absences (no speaking track
  record, no position papers, no board seats, no
  publishing channel) are recorded, not concealed by
  aspirational language. A plan built on inflated current
  state is a plan that fails on execution.
- The annual public-trust plan is genuinely one page. A
  longer plan has failed the compression discipline chapter
  03 requires. The four sections (calendar, budget,
  positioning arc, coordination points) are all present.
- The calendar section specifies bounded numbers per venue
  family, not open-ended acceptance rates. A head who
  reserves the right to accept "as many appearances as
  invitations arrive" has skipped the discipline.
- The budget section specifies days per quarter for the
  head and hours per quarter for Communications. "The head
  will spend appropriate time" is not a budget.
- The positioning arc is a single coherent theme across
  three to five related positions the head takes across
  the surfaces. Two disconnected themes across the year is
  a random walk; the discipline is to choose.
- The joint operating agreement has a specific, non-
  overlapping substance-vs-delivery split, an escalation
  ladder, the three-step media discipline, the reactive-
  event protocol, at least two metrics, and CEO
  ratification.
- The publishing channel discipline names the channel's
  editorial voice, the submission cycle (with sign-off
  routing), the versioning and archive rule, and the URL-
  distinct-from-marketing rule.
- The response-to-events dry-run walks all three moves
  (pause, layered response, peer coordination) with named
  participants, named windows, and the GC antitrust-boundary
  reference.
- The failure-mode audit names, for each of the five
  failure modes, at least one specific detection signal and
  one specific recovery action.
- The plan cross-references the voluntary-code register
  (exercise 01), the standards-body portfolio (exercise
  02), and — where applicable — the regulator-engagement
  register from [[mod-104-regulator-engagement]]. The plan
  is not a standalone artefact; it is the outward-facing
  presentation of the underlying registers.

## Stretch goals

- Extend the plan with a **three-year positioning horizon**
  — the theme the head is building substantive presence
  against over three years, with a sketch of how the annual
  arcs compose. A three-year horizon is what turns a
  well-executed annual plan into a role-shaped public
  posture the enterprise can point at.
- Author the **speaking-invitation triage rubric**
  Communications applies before the head reviews an
  invitation. The rubric compresses the four venue-family
  taxonomy plus the plan's calendar bounds plus the
  reactive-event calendar into a checklist that lets
  Communications route obvious accepts and obvious declines
  without the head's involvement.
- Author the **on-record media briefing template** the CCO
  uses to prepare the head for every on-record interaction —
  the reporter's parameters, prior related coverage, the
  reporter's known positions, the two-to-three substantive
  positions the head is prepared to speak to, and the two-
  to-three questions the head will decline to comment on
  (with the pre-cleared decline language).
- Draft the **enterprise-channel content calendar** for the
  next four quarters — the specific position papers,
  sign-on summaries, standards-body updates, transparency
  report items, and regulator-facing-filing indexes the
  channel will publish, with the head-owner and
  Communications-owner named per item.
- Author the **peer-coordination directory** — the specific
  peer heads-of-role at named peer enterprises the head
  maintains an ordinary-events relationship with (industry-
  body board colleagues, standards-body co-contributors),
  the standing meeting cadence, and the GC-cleared
  boundary on substantive coordination. Coordination
  without a directory is coordination that happens
  reactively; the discipline is standing.

## Notes and starter guidance

The most common first-cut failure of the plan is a
positioning arc that reads as a marketing calendar rather
than a substantive arc. The head or a Communications
partner drafting the plan reaches for the marketing register
because it is closer at hand — five speaking appearances
"on responsible AI" spread across the year, with no build.
Force the arc into specific, related positions the head
takes across the year, and against specific substantive
material (a standards-body work item, a code-of-practice
provision, a regulator's methodology), not abstract themes.
Substance is what accumulates; theme without substance is
random walk.

The second common failure is a joint operating agreement
that reads as a courtesy overlap of head and CCO
responsibilities rather than a hard split. Chapter 03 is
explicit that the split is substance-vs-delivery, and the
agreement's job is to enforce the boundary against the daily
pull to blur it. Force the agreement to say, for the specific
decisions that arise ("which speaking invitation to accept",
"which reporter to talk to", "what the position paper's
takeaway is"), which side owns and which side advises. An
agreement that lists both parties on every decision has done
the boundary no work.

On the budget — resist the pull to round down the head's
time. Chapter 03 warns that the head who accepts every
invitation is a head on the road not running the program.
An honest budget of "twelve speaking days per year, four
position-paper drafting weeks, three board-seat meetings
per year, twelve media prep hours per quarter" is more
defensible than a padded budget the head fails to hold to.

On the response-to-events dry-run — pick a hypothetical
event that the enterprise would actually face plausibly,
not the easiest one. The exercise is calibration; if the
event chosen is one where the pause discipline is obvious,
the dry-run teaches nothing. A trickier event (a peer's
incident with adjacent subject matter, or a regulator
action on a matter the enterprise had commented on
publicly) exposes where the discipline is under-authored.

The plan is a living artefact. Version it, date it, review
it quarterly at a standing head-and-CCO sync, update it
when the plan's assumptions no longer hold (a positioning
arc is overtaken by events; a board seat becomes
untenable; a reactive event pulls the calendar off track).
A plan that has not been touched since Q1 is a plan that
no longer reflects the discipline.
