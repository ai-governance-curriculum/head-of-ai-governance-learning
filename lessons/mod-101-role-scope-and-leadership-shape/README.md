# mod-101 — Head of AI Governance Role Scope and Leadership Shape

**Estimated effort:** 14 hours
**Position on the track:** first module — the level-60 scope
statement and the leadership-shape frames that every later module
operates inside.

## What this module is for

Every later module in the Head of AI Governance track — running the
program, board reporting, regulator engagement, incident leadership,
function leadership, budget, sector programs — presupposes that the
head has an internalised answer to four questions:

1. Where does level-60 leadership authority begin and end on the AI
   Governance level ladder?
2. What is the *leadership shape* of the second-line function under
   NIST AI RMF, ISO/IEC 38507, the IIA Three Lines Model, and the
   ISACA COBIT 2019 / NIST CSF 2.0 GOVERN sibling references?
3. Where is the AI governance function positioned against the CRO,
   CCO, CPO / DPO, CAE, GC, CISO, CIO / CTO / CDO, Chief
   Communications Officer, and Chief AI Officer scopes?
4. What are the two engagement contracts — downward to the level-50
   senior AI governance / risk architect and upward to the level-70
   CAIO — that turn the level ladder from a diagram into an
   operating discipline?

This module answers all four, produces the artefacts the head walks
into week one with, and sets the vocabulary the rest of the track
uses.

## Learning objectives (from the plan)

- Diagram this role's exact scope on the level ladder — what
  `ai-governance-analyst` (15) legwork, `ai-risk-engineer` (25)
  engineering craft, `ai-evaluation-engineer` (35) release-assurance
  methodology, `agentic-safety-engineer` (40) frontier-agent
  methodology, and `senior-ai-governance-architect` (50)
  architecture the head consumes; what `chief-ai-officer` (70)
  inherits or extends; where the head's leadership authority begins
  and ends.
- Read NIST AI RMF 1.0 GOVERN function (with the Playbook) as the
  leadership shape for the second line, rather than as a compliance
  checklist.
- Read ISO/IEC 38507 as the definitional shape for board-facing
  responsibilities the head owns.
- Read the IIA Three Lines Model at leadership scope — the head
  owns the second line, coordinates the first line, and interfaces
  to the third line without operational-distance failure.
- Position ISACA COBIT 2019 and NIST CSF 2.0 GOVERN as sibling
  references the AI governance function composes with at enterprise
  scale.
- Position the AI governance function against CRO / CCO / CPO /
  DPO / CAE / GC / CISO / CIO / CTO / CDO / Chief Communications
  Officer / CAIO scopes.
- Author the role's engagement contract with the level-50 architect
  below and the level-70 CAIO above.

## How to work through this module

The module is designed to be worked linearly. Chapter 01 draws the
level ladder; chapters 02–05 supply the four framework lenses that
define the *shape* of level-60 authority; chapter 06 draws the
horizontal CxO boundary; chapter 07 supplies the two contracts that
operationalise the vertical and upward boundaries.

Exercises follow the same order. Exercises 1, 4, and 5 are
prerequisites for exercise 6 — you cannot write the engagement
contracts without the level-ladder map, the Three-Lines position
statement, and the CxO adjacency boundary map already in hand.

By the end of the module, a working head-of-role has produced:

- A one-page level ladder scope and leadership map.
- A one-page NIST AI RMF GOVERN answer plus Playbook coverage CSV
  and Generative AI Profile overlay.
- A five-page ISO/IEC 38507 board-shape annotation with the
  evaluate → direct → monitor calendar.
- A one-page Three Lines position statement with a five-scenario
  drill.
- A nine-to-eleven-sheet CxO adjacency boundary map.
- Two engagement contracts (downward and upward) with an
  escalation decision matrix.

These artefacts feed forward into module 102 (running the program)
and module 103 (board and audit-committee reporting), and are
referenced across every subsequent module in the track.
