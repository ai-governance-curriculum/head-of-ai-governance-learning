# exercise-06: Role Engagement Contract Authoring

**Estimated effort:** 2 hours

## Objective

Author both **engagement contracts** for a target enterprise: the
downward contract with the level-50 senior AI governance / risk
architect, and the upward contract with the level-70 Chief AI Officer
(where the designation exists) or with the CEO plus delegated
CIO / CTO / CDO / CFO (where it does not). Each contract follows the
four-section Consume / Deliver / Defer / Escalate shape from chapter
07, is one to three pages long, uses concrete deliverables with
cadences, and is ratified by the appropriate body.

The two contracts are the artefacts that turn the level ladder from a
diagram into an operating discipline. Once signed, they are the head's
first move when the boundary is challenged in argument.

## Prerequisites

- All seven lecture chapters read, especially
  [01-level-ladder-and-leadership-boundary](../01-level-ladder-and-leadership-boundary.md)
  and [07-engagement-contract-authoring](../07-engagement-contract-authoring.md).
- Exercises 1, 4, and 5 completed. The level ladder scope map, the
  Three Lines position statement, and the CxO adjacency boundary map
  are prerequisites — the engagement contracts operationalise them.
- The target enterprise from exercise 1, including whether it has a
  CAIO designation.

## Steps

1. **Downward contract with the architect.** Draft the following
   sections:
   - Parties, dates, ratification body (audit committee).
   - Consume — the twelve architectural deliverables from chapter
     07's list, each with an update cadence (annual, on-material-
     change, quarterly, etc.). Note the delivery format required
     (machine-readable catalogue, not spreadsheet).
   - Deliver — the five upward signals from the head to the
     architect (regulatory-change signal, operating-fitness
     feedback, regulator-engagement signal, incident lessons-learned
     signal, CxO sponsorship), each with a cadence.
   - Defer — the enumerated list of what the head does not author.
   - Escalate — the three triggers (scope expansion,
     under-delivery, scope-drift attempt) with routing.
   - Signatures (head, architect, CAE observing, audit-committee
     chair) and next review date.
2. **Upward contract with the CAIO (or equivalent cluster).** Decide
   whether your target enterprise is Configuration A (CAIO exists) or
   Configuration B (no CAIO — delegations spread across CEO / CIO /
   CTO / CDO / CFO). Draft:
   - Parties, dates, ratification body (CEO).
   - Consume — AI strategy alignment, enterprise AI budget envelope,
     external positioning direction, escalation-and-appeal
     decisions, each with a cadence.
   - Deliver — the eight upward deliverables from chapter 07
     (second-line coverage, risk register, regulator-engagement
     summary, incident summary, voluntary-code posture, function
     budget, multi-year investment case, board-pack contribution),
     each with a cadence.
   - Defer — strategy authorship, portfolio P&L, CEO/board-seat
     external speaking, enterprise-wide AI budget management.
   - Escalate — strategy pivot, CEO-scope external disagreement,
     under-consumption of risk signal.
   - Signatures (head, CAIO or delegates, CEO) and next review date.
3. **Independent audit-committee access invariant.** State, in one
   sentence in each contract, that independent audit-committee
   access is preserved regardless of any provision above and is
   tested annually.
4. **Escalation table.** Draft a one-page decision matrix showing,
   for each of ten common decisions (stop-shipping, appetite
   breach, regulator-material issue, new-jurisdiction market entry,
   Article 73 filing, voluntary-code sign-on, function budget
   overshoot, headcount request, tool procurement, sector-blueprint
   update), who decides, who is consulted, and who is informed —
   with the two engagement contracts as the primary references.
5. **Ratify.** Assemble the packet — two contracts plus the
   escalation table plus a two-page audit-committee cover briefing
   — and present it as a first-thirty-days deliverable.

## Deliverables

- `downward-engagement-contract.pdf` — one to three pages,
  Consume/Deliver/Defer/Escalate structure, signed.
- `upward-engagement-contract.pdf` — one to three pages, same
  structure, signed under Configuration A or B.
- `escalation-decision-matrix.pdf` — one page, ten decisions,
  RACI-style routing.
- `audit-committee-briefing.md` — two pages, explaining what is
  ratified and why.

## Acceptance criteria

- Both contracts use the exact four-section shape (Consume,
  Deliver, Defer, Escalate) with no invented sections.
- Every line item is a concrete deliverable with a cadence. Vague
  verbs ("collaborate," "align," "partner") appear nowhere.
- The downward contract's Consume section lists all twelve of the
  architect's deliverables from chapter 07, none omitted.
- The upward contract identifies Configuration A or B explicitly
  and adapts accordingly.
- Independent audit-committee access appears as an invariant in
  both contracts.
- The escalation decision matrix references the two contracts (not
  a third document) as the source of authority for each decision.
- No contract line silently absorbs a scope that chapter 06's
  adjacency map assigned to another CxO.

## Stretch goals

- Draft the *dispute-resolution* addendum: when the head and the
  architect (downward) or the head and the CAIO (upward) disagree
  on a specific deliverable, what is the process to converge, and
  what is the timebox before escalation?
- Draft a *transition* addendum for the case where the CAIO
  designation is created (Configuration B → Configuration A) or
  removed. What changes in the upward contract? What does the
  audit committee re-ratify?
- Sketch a *quarterly review checklist* — three to five bullets —
  that the head and each counterparty use to open every review
  cycle, so the review is not free-form.

## Notes and starter guidance

- The commonest error is to treat "Deliver" as an aspirational
  wish-list. Every "Deliver" line must be something the head can
  produce every quarter without heroics.
- The commonest error on "Defer" is to hedge — writing "not
  primarily authored by the head" instead of "not authored by the
  head." Hedges will not survive an audit committee's read.
- Do not try to make the two contracts fit on the same page. The
  downward and upward relationships have different rhythms; force-
  fitting them makes both weaker.
- If your target enterprise is Configuration B (no CAIO), take
  extra care with the escalation-decision matrix — the appeal
  routing is more complex and the head's independent access to the
  audit committee matters more, not less.
