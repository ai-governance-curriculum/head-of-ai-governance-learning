# exercise-04: Three Lines for AI Leadership Position Drill

**Estimated effort:** 2 hours

## Objective

Position the Head of AI Governance role inside the IIA Three Lines
Model at leadership scope for a target enterprise, using a
**position drill**: a table-top written exercise in which you walk
through five concrete scenarios and, for each one, name who does
what across first line, second line, third line, and the governing
body. The drill's purpose is to make operational-distance failure
visible before it happens.

The deliverable is a signed **position statement** — one page — that
you would hand to the CAE and the audit-committee chair on day one,
plus a two-page walkthrough of the five scenarios showing the
position statement is operable.

## Prerequisites

- Chapter [04-three-lines-at-leadership-scope](../04-three-lines-at-leadership-scope.md)
  read and internalised.
- Access to the
  [IIA Three Lines Model (2020)](https://www.theiia.org/en/content/position-papers/2020/the-iia-three-lines-model/)
  and the [IIA IPPF Standards](https://www.theiia.org/en/standards/what-are-the-standards/).
- The target enterprise from exercise 1, including its parent risk
  framework (COSO ERM or ISO 31000) and the CAE's reporting line.

## Steps

1. **Author the position statement.** In one page, state:
   - The head at level 60 is the second-line accountable executive
     for the AI Governance family.
   - The seven-item second-line envelope (framework, AIMS, risk
     register, control operation, crosswalk, challenge, regulator
     interface, plus independent audit-committee access) is owned
     by the head.
   - The head coordinates with first line without absorbing first-
     line accountability.
   - The head interfaces with the third line under the CAE without
     reporting into or drafting the third-line audit plan.
   - Independent access to the audit committee is preserved
     regardless of solid-line management, and is tested annually.
2. **Walk the five scenarios.** For each, list who acts, in what
   order, and what evidence is generated:
   - **Scenario A — Stop-shipping challenge.** Second-line challenge
     to a first-line release. Follow the chapter 04 example; adapt
     to your enterprise.
   - **Scenario B — Third-line finding requires remediation.** The
     CAE's audit surfaces a control deficiency in the AIMS. Who
     owns closure, who reviews closure, who signs off?
   - **Scenario C — First-line disputes a risk-register entry.**
     First line believes a risk is over-scored. The disagreement is
     escalated. Where does it land?
   - **Scenario D — Regulator opens an examination.** A sector
     regulator or the EU AI Office opens an exam. What is the head's
     role vs GC's vs CAE's vs the first-line business owner's?
   - **Scenario E — Reporting-line change (annual walkaround).**
     The audit-committee chair calls the head directly, without the
     CAIO or CRO in the room, once a year to test independent
     access. What does the head report?
3. **Identify the operational-distance risk** in your target
   enterprise. Which of Form A (too close) or Form B (too far) —
   from chapter 04 — is the risk you are most likely to fall into,
   given the enterprise's culture? Draft two mitigations you will
   put in place in your first ninety days.
4. **Draft the boundary invariants** — three sentences on what
   never changes about the head's position under any organisational
   reconfiguration.
5. **Sign the statement** with the head's name (in the exercise, a
   placeholder), the CAE's countersignature line, and the audit-
   committee chair's ratification line.

## Deliverables

- `three-lines-position-statement.pdf` — the one-page signed
  statement.
- `three-lines-position-drill.md` — the two-page walkthrough of
  scenarios A through E.
- `operational-distance-mitigations.md` — the one-page mitigation
  plan for your enterprise's likely failure mode.

## Acceptance criteria

- The position statement covers all seven items of the second-line
  envelope, plus independent audit-committee access.
- Each of the five scenarios has a clearly named actor per action
  and a named evidence artefact generated.
- The scenario walkthrough demonstrates that neither Form A
  (rubber stamp) nor Form B (irrelevance) is possible under the
  stated position.
- The operational-distance mitigation plan names two concrete
  actions the head will take in the first ninety days,
  time-bounded and testable.
- The signature block names the head, the CAE, and the audit-
  committee chair as the three ratifying parties.

## Stretch goals

- Extend scenario A to a case where the CAIO overrides the
  stop-shipping recommendation on strategy grounds. What is the
  head's escalation path? What is the annual walkaround
  conversation the head has with the audit-committee chair as a
  result?
- Draft the coordination charter between the head and the CAE —
  three pages, ratified by the audit committee — covering the AI
  audit universe, evidence-access pattern, finding-remediation
  process, and the joint audit-committee appearance rhythm.
- Draft the head's *raise-a-concern* channel design: how a
  first-line contributor raises AI-risk concerns to the second line
  without going through the first-line management chain.

## Notes and starter guidance

- The commonest mistake in this exercise is writing the position
  statement in language that sounds independent but describes a
  role that in practice reviews its own work. Test each line
  against "if I authored the artefact I am reviewing, is that
  visible?"
- Reproduction of the CAE-and-head boundary matters more than
  most people realise. If your target enterprise has the CAE
  reporting through the CFO instead of the audit committee, note
  it — it is a red flag for third-line independence and it
  changes the head's job.
- Do not use the drill to argue for the head to *become* the CAE
  or to *absorb* first-line responsibilities. Both are role
  collapses; the drill is meant to prevent them.
