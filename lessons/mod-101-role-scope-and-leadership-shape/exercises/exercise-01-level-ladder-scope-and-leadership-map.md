# exercise-01: Level Ladder Scope And Leadership Map

**Estimated effort:** 2 hours

## Objective

Produce a defensible one-page **Level Ladder Scope And Leadership Map**
for a Head of AI Governance role you would take (or already hold),
naming — for each role on the AI Governance ladder from level 15 to
level 70 — what the head *consumes*, *sponsors*, *staffs and directs*,
*reports to*, and *does not do*. Ratify each verb against a concrete
example from your target enterprise.

The deliverable is not a diagram alone; it is a diagram plus a
one-paragraph justification per adjacency, so the map survives an
audit-committee challenge.

## Prerequisites

- Chapter [01-level-ladder-and-leadership-boundary](../01-level-ladder-and-leadership-boundary.md)
  read and internalised.
- Familiarity with the roles named in `CURRICULUM.md` "Ownership rule"
  — `ai-governance-analyst` (15), `ai-risk-engineer` (25),
  `ai-evaluation-engineer` (35), `agentic-safety-engineer` (40),
  `senior-ai-governance-architect` (50), `chief-ai-officer` (70).
- A concrete enterprise in mind (real or fictional but shaped like
  one you would join). Note the sector and whether a Chief AI
  Officer designation exists.

## Steps

1. **Diagram.** Draw the level ladder from 15 → 70 for the AI
   Governance family. Mark the head at level 60 and put a box around
   the level-60 scope.
2. **Consume list.** For each of levels 15, 25, 35, 40, 50, name in
   one line what the head *consumes* from that level. Aim for the
   noun of the delivered artefact, not a verb.
3. **Sponsor / challenge list.** For each of levels 35 and 40, name in
   one line what the head *sponsors and challenges* at that level,
   distinguishing sponsorship (advocacy at CxO scope) from challenge
   (independent second-line pushback).
4. **Staff and direct list.** For each of levels 15 and 25, name in
   one line what the head *staffs and directs* (hires, coaches,
   escalates gaps for) at that level.
5. **Upward.** Name what the head *reports to* or *matrixes with*
   at level 70. Decide whether your target enterprise has a CAIO
   designation and note the resulting configuration (A or B from
   chapter 07).
6. **Does not do.** Write down five specific tasks the head *does not
   do* even under pressure. Draw one each from strategy, portfolio
   P&L, external CEO-scope positioning, enterprise AI budget
   envelope, and one on the downward side (a level-25 or level-15
   task the head is never rescued into).
7. **Justification paragraph.** For each of the seven statements in
   steps 2–6, write one to three sentences justifying it against a
   concrete first-90-days scenario in your target enterprise.
8. **Independent audit-committee access invariant.** State, in one
   sentence, how independent audit-committee access is preserved
   under your reporting-line choice.

## Deliverables

- `level-ladder-scope-and-leadership-map.pdf` — one-page diagram plus
  a two-page justification. PDF preferred so it can be circulated to
  the audit committee unchanged.
- `level-ladder-scope-and-leadership-map.md` (or `.txt`) — the
  machine-readable text underlying the diagram, so it is diff-able
  in the AIMS document control.

## Acceptance criteria

- Every level from 15 to 70 (inclusive) appears on the diagram, with
  the head's verb (consume / sponsor / staff-and-direct / report /
  does-not-do) named for each adjacency.
- Every one of the seven statements has a written justification tied
  to a concrete first-90-days scenario.
- Independent audit-committee access is stated in writing as an
  invariant, not as a preference.
- No statement contains a vague verb ("collaborate with," "align
  with," "partner around"); every verb is one of the five above.
- The "does not do" list contains at least one item from each of
  strategy, portfolio P&L, external CEO-scope positioning, budget
  envelope, and level-25/15 execution.

## Stretch goals

- Add a second column to the diagram for the sector-specific
  adjacency (HoMRM in banking, CMO in health system, sector CAIO
  under OMB M-25-21 in public sector).
- Draft the level-60-to-level-70 escalation table: which decisions
  the head takes, which the CAIO takes, and which are appealed to
  the CEO or audit committee.
- Circulate the map to a peer in a level-50 or level-70 role and
  incorporate one round of critique.

## Notes and starter guidance

- Resist the urge to write the map from the outside in (looking at
  what regulators or standards require and inferring scope). Write
  it from the inside out — from what only the head at level 60 can
  do — and use standards to *ratify* the resulting map, not to
  derive it.
- Test your map against the running example in chapter 01: the
  stop-shipping decision. If your map does not clearly say who
  reproduces the exploit, who authors the harm model, who signs the
  release packet, and who owns the stop-shipping decision, revise.
- Do not attempt to solve the reporting-line question in this
  exercise. It comes back in exercise 6.
